## Personal Injury Law Firm Serving Warren, MI

Warren is Michigan’s third-largest city, about 137,000 people on a grid of mile roads and six-lane north-south arterials between Eight Mile and Fourteen Mile, Dequindre and Hayes. It is also the region’s manufacturing and engineering center: the General Motors Technical Center, Stellantis’s Warren Truck Assembly, the Army’s Detroit Arsenal and TACOM headquarters, and hundreds of tool-and-die and supplier shops line Van Dyke, Mound, and Groesbeck. The trucks, the shift changes, and the arterials produce Warren’s injuries. In 2024, the 11 Mile Road/I-696 intersection at Van Dyke in Warren/Center Line recorded 185 crashes, the highest intersection total in Michigan. The 18½ Mile Road intersection at Van Dyke in Sterling Heights ranked second, with 162 crashes.

## Where Warren injuries happen

**Van Dyke (M-53).** Eight lanes from Eight Mile to Fourteen Mile, past the Tech Center’s east gates and the Warren Truck plant, with the 11 Mile intersection ranking first statewide for crashes in 2024 and the 12 Mile, 13 Mile, and Nine Mile intersections close behind. Left-turn crashes across four lanes of oncoming traffic, red-light and late-yellow crashes, rear-end crashes in the stacked turn lanes, and pedestrian crashes between the strip retail on both sides. Van Dyke is MDOT’s road.

**Mound Road.** The plant corridor, rebuilt from I-696 to M-59 in the Innovate Mound project with new signals and turn lanes, carrying tractor-trailers into and out of the Tech Center, Warren Truck, and the Arsenal at every shift change. Turning-truck crashes at the plant gates, trucks stopped in lanes waiting for gate clearance, and rear-end crashes into slowing trucks are Mound’s cases; see our [Warren truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/warren).

**Groesbeck (M-97), Eight Mile (M-102), and I-696.** Groesbeck’s diagonal across the east side with its skewed intersections at the mile roads; Eight Mile along the Detroit line, with Detroit-edge traffic and coverage; and I-696 across the city’s center with interchanges at Dequindre, Mound, Van Dyke, Schoenherr, and Hoover in six miles, where the merging and lane-change crashes concentrate.

**The mile roads and the local arterials.** Nine, Ten, Eleven, Twelve, Thirteen, and Fourteen Mile; Dequindre, Ryan, Hoover, Schoenherr, and Hayes. The mile roads are the Macomb County Department of Roads’; the others are the city’s or the county’s by segment. Intersection crashes at the signals, and pedestrian and bicycle crashes near the schools and the senior housing.

**The plants and the shops.** The Tech Center, Warren Truck, the Arsenal, and the supplier and tool shops along Mound, Van Dyke, Groesbeck, and Stephens. Workers’ compensation is the exclusive remedy against the employer (MCL 418.131), but a worker injured by a contractor, a vendor’s driver, or a defective machine has a lawsuit as well. Certain injuries caused by negligent federal employees acting within the scope of their duties at the Detroit Arsenal may support claims against the United States under the Federal Tort Claims Act, subject to applicable exceptions. A claimant generally must present an administrative claim to the appropriate federal agency within two years under 28 USC 2401(b) and 2675. A separate six-month lawsuit deadline generally applies after a final written denial. Injuries involving independent contractors or other parties require separate analysis. See our [workers’ compensation](/personal-injury/workers-compensation/warren) and [product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability) pages.

**The neighborhoods.** Warren’s housing is mid-century, with a large senior population and a growing share of renters in the apartment complexes along Van Dyke, Twelve Mile, and Hoover. Falls on stairs and ice, rental-housing injuries under MCL 554.139, dog bites under MCL 287.351, and sidewalk injuries under the two-inch rule (MCL 691.1402a). See our [premises liability](/personal-injury/premises-liability), [tenant injury](/personal-injury/tenant-injuries), and [dog bite](/personal-injury/dog-bites/warren) pages.

**The senior-care facilities.** Warren has six Medicare-certified nursing homes and a number of assisted-living and senior apartment communities; falls, pressure injuries, and medication errors in them are a steady source of claims. See our [nursing home abuse page](/personal-injury/nursing-home-abuse/warren).

## What is different about a Warren claim

**Fault is the case.** Many Warren crash claims involve intersections on Van Dyke, Mound, Groesbeck, and the mile roads. In a left-turn or traffic-signal crash, fault may be disputed, and the other driver’s insurer may argue that you share responsibility. Under MCL 500.3135(2)(b), a person found more than 50 percent at fault generally cannot recover noneconomic damages. What decides it: the police report and diagram, any available signal-timing plans, controller records, or other traffic-signal data maintained by the Department of Roads or MDOT, the corridor cameras (dense along Van Dyke and at the plant gates, retained for days), the vehicles’ event data recorders, and the witnesses in the other lanes. We promptly assess available camera footage, traffic-signal records, and witness evidence and take appropriate steps to preserve relevant information after accepting a case.

**Coverage.** Warren’s at-fault drivers carry everything from the $50,000 minimum (MCL 500.3009(1)) to full coverage, with Detroit-edge traffic on Eight Mile and Van Dyke more often uninsured. Your own no-fault insurer pays your medical bills and lost wages regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107), or the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan does if no household policy applies (MCL 500.3172, capped at $250,000); the claim against the at-fault driver requires a threshold injury (MCL 500.3135) and is worth no more than the insurance available unless you carry uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage. We review every policy in the household before we value a Warren claim. See our [Warren car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents/warren), [no-fault insurance guide](about:blank), and [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Three road agencies, plus the federal government.** MDOT has jurisdiction over state trunklines such as Van Dyke (M-53), Groesbeck (M-97), Eight Mile (M-102), and I-696. Mound Road is under the Macomb County Department of Roads, which also maintains certain mile roads and other county roads. The City of Warren maintains local streets and other roads under municipal jurisdiction. Responsibility for individual mile-road and arterial segments should be verified against the applicable road-jurisdiction map. A qualifying defect in the improved portion of a public roadway may support a claim against the responsible governmental agency under the highway exception to governmental immunity, MCL 691.1402. Claims based solely on malfunctioning traffic signals, inadequate signage, or obstructed sight lines generally do not fall within that exception. Qualifying highway-defect claims generally require written notice within 120 days under MCL 691.1404, including claims against MDOT, for which notice must be filed with the Court of Claims as required by statute.

**Government vehicles.** Warren Police and Fire, SMART buses on Van Dyke, Mound, Eight Mile, and the mile roads, school buses serving Warren-area students, including those operated by Warren Consolidated, Van Dyke, Fitzgerald, Warren Woods, Center Line, and Eastpointe Community Schools, Department of Roads trucks, and the Arsenal’s federal vehicles. Crashes involving city, county, school district, or SMART vehicles may support claims under the Governmental Tort Liability Act’s motor vehicle exception, MCL 691.1405, when injuries result from negligent operation of a government-owned motor vehicle. Certain tort claims against SMART also require written notice within 60 days under MCL 124.419. Claims involving federal vehicles require separate analysis under the Federal Tort Claims Act. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

**The 37th District and the Sixteenth Circuit.** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims for vehicle damage (MCL 500.3135(3)(e), up to $3,000), are filed in the 37th District Court at 8300 Common Road, which serves Warren and Center Line. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court at 40 N. Main Street in Mount Clemens; wrongful death estates (MCL 600.2922) and minors’ settlements (MCR 2.420) to the Macomb County Probate Court in Clinton Township. For crashes near Eight Mile Road or Dequindre Road, or involving defendants from another county, venue is determined under the statutory order of priority in MCL 600.1629, generally beginning with the county where the original injury occurred and where a defendant resides, conducts business, or maintains a registered office.

**The hospitals.** Henry Ford Warren Hospital on Twelve Mile Road (formerly Ascension Macomb-Oakland Hospital, Warren campus) is a Level III adult trauma center serving Warren. Henry Ford Macomb Hospital in Clinton Township and McLaren Macomb in Mount Clemens are Level II trauma centers. Corewell Health and Detroit-area hospitals also serve injured patients from Warren and surrounding communities.

## Frequently asked questions

**How dangerous is Van Dyke in Warren?** The 11 Mile Road/I-696 intersection at Van Dyke recorded 185 crashes in 2024, the highest count of any Michigan intersection that year. The 18½ Mile Road intersection at Van Dyke in Sterling Heights ranked second, with 162 crashes. The 12 Mile, 13 Mile, and Nine Mile intersections are also high-crash locations.

**The other driver’s insurer says I was 50 percent at fault for a left-turn crash on Van Dyke. Is my claim over?** Not because an adjuster says so. Under MCL 500.3135(2)(b) you lose the pain-and-suffering claim only if you are found more than 50 percent at fault; at exactly 50 percent you recover half. The police diagram, signal data, cameras, and witnesses decide the number.

**I was hit by a truck turning into the Tech Center or Warren Truck. Who is responsible?** The driver and the carrier, and possibly the plant or its logistics provider if the gate layout, traffic control, or delivery schedule contributed. Plant-gate cameras are overwritten quickly; the preservation letter goes to the plant as well as the carrier.

**I was hurt at a Warren plant. Can I sue?** Not your employer, in most cases; workers’ compensation is the exclusive remedy. You can sue a third party whose negligence caused the injury and pursue both claims at once. If the injury was caused by a qualifying federal employee acting within the scope of federal employment at the Arsenal, an FTCA claim may be available, subject to applicable exceptions and a generally two-year administrative filing deadline.

**Where would my Warren injury case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, in the 37th District Court on Common Road; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court in Mount Clemens.

**Who pays my medical bills after a crash in Warren?** Your own no-fault insurer, regardless of fault, up to your PIP level, after you file an Application for No-Fault Benefits within one year; a household member’s insurer if you have none; the Assigned Claims Plan if no policy applies.

**How much does it cost to hire you?** Nothing up front, and no fee unless we recover money for you. Michigan caps contingency fees in personal injury cases at one-third of the recovery (MCR 8.121).

**How long do I have?** Three years for most injury suits, one year to notify your no-fault insurer in writing, and as little as 120 days for a claim against the City of Warren or Macomb County.
