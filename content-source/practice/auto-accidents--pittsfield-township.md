## Pittsfield Township Car Accident Lawyer

A car crash in Pittsfield Charter Township is a Michigan no-fault claim, and it usually happens in one of four places:

- **Carpenter Road,** past the big-box stores, shopping centers, apartment complexes, and bus stops on the township’s east side.
- **Washtenaw Avenue,** the five-lane commercial road between US-23 and Ypsilanti.
- **Michigan Avenue (US-12), State Street, and Ann Arbor-Saline Road,** the higher-speed roads to Saline and into Ann Arbor.
- **I-94 and US-23,** which meet at the township’s northeast corner.

In 2024, the Michigan State Police recorded **1,143 crashes** in the township. **3 people were killed,** 29 were seriously injured, and 215 crashes injured someone. Three of Washtenaw County’s ten highest-crash intersections that year were on Carpenter Road, and a fourth was at Hogback Road and Washtenaw Avenue.

Most township residents have an **Ann Arbor, Ypsilanti, or Saline mailing address.** A crash that seems to have happened “in Ann Arbor” is often a township crash, with a different police agency, a different road owner, and a different district court.

A Pittsfield Township crash claim usually turns on four questions:

- **Fault** at the corridor, the parking-lot exit, or the freeway ramp.
- **Who pays your no-fault benefits,** including for a pedestrian, a cyclist, or a bus rider without a car.
- **The other driver’s insurance,** and how your own uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage fits with it.
- **Whether a public body is involved,** and which one: the Washtenaw County Road Commission, MDOT, the township, or TheRide. The court and any notice deadline depend on which one.

## Where Pittsfield Township crashes happen

**Carpenter Road.** Carpenter runs north and south through the east side of the township, past big-box stores, shopping centers, apartment complexes, and bus stops. It crosses Packard, Ellsworth, and Michigan Avenue, and three of those intersections made the county’s top ten in 2024. In a 2026 request for state money to study the corridor, the township described Carpenter as one of the most dangerous roads in Washtenaw County for pedestrians, cyclists, and drivers, with multiple pedestrian deaths in 2025. A 2025 road safety audit of Carpenter and Packard found numerous risks to people on foot and on bikes. The crashes on Carpenter are:

- left turns across several lanes into shopping centers and side streets
- vehicles leaving parking lots and apartment drives (MCL 257.652)
- rear-end crashes in the queues at the signals
- red-light and left-turn crashes at Packard, Ellsworth, and Michigan Avenue (MCL 257.650)
- pedestrians crossing between signals to reach a bus stop or a store

See our [pedestrian accident page](https://atlawgroup.com/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents).

**Washtenaw Avenue.** Between US-23 and Ypsilanti, Washtenaw Avenue (M-17) is a five-lane commercial road with driveways on both sides and shopping centers at Hogback and Carpenter. Hogback at Washtenaw had 41 crashes in 2024, the third most in the county. The 14A-1 District Court sits on the same road. The crashes are:

- left turns into and out of the shopping centers
- lane changes into an occupied lane (MCL 257.642(1)(a))
- rear-end crashes in the center-turn and right-turn lanes
- drivers turning across a sidewalk into a business and striking a pedestrian or cyclist

**Michigan Avenue, State Street, and Ann Arbor-Saline Road.** Michigan Avenue (US-12) runs southwest toward Saline past subdivisions, schools, and commercial strips, and meets Carpenter at the county’s eighth highest-crash intersection. State Street and Ann Arbor-Saline Road carry commuters into Ann Arbor past the I-94 interchanges. These are higher-speed roads with long gaps between signals. The crashes are:

- head-on and crossover crashes on undivided stretches
- left turns at unsignalized intersections and subdivision entrances
- rear-end crashes behind turning vehicles
- deer, especially at dawn and dusk in the fall

**I-94 and US-23.** The two freeways meet at the township’s northeast corner. I-94 has interchanges at State Street and Ann Arbor-Saline Road, and US-23 at Washtenaw Avenue and Michigan Avenue. The township recorded 148 interstate crashes in 2024. The crashes are:

- rear-end and lane-change crashes in the backups at the ramps
- merge crashes where ramps join and leave the freeway
- crashes in work zones
- truck crashes

## Deer crashes

The township’s 96 deer crashes in 2024 raise questions that a two-car crash does not:

- **Your own no-fault policy pays your medical bills and lost wages,** as in any crash. There is no other driver to sue for pain and suffering.
- **Your car is covered only if you carry comprehensive or collision coverage,** depending on the policy. The mini tort claim does not apply, because there is no at-fault driver.
- **Swerving can change the claim.** A driver who swerves to miss a deer and hits another car or a tree may face a fault question the deer itself never raises. If another driver swerved into you, your claim is against that driver.
- **A passenger** in a car that hit a deer claims no-fault benefits from the passenger’s own household policy first.



## Whose road it is

The township owns no roads. Who owns the road at the crash scene decides who you sue for a road defect and how fast you must act:

- **County roads:** Carpenter, Packard, Ellsworth, State Street, Platt, Hogback, and the subdivision streets belong to the Washtenaw County Road Commission. A claim for a road defect requires written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404).
- **State highways:** I-94, US-23, US-12 (Michigan Avenue), and M-17 (Washtenaw Avenue east of US-23) belong to MDOT. A road-defect claim against MDOT requires written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **Sidewalks and pathways along the roads:** the township has the duty, with its own 120-day notice (MCL 691.1401, 691.1402a, 691.1404).

Of the township’s four top-ten intersections, two are on state highways (Hogback at Washtenaw, and Carpenter at Michigan Avenue) and two are county-road intersections (Carpenter at Packard, and Carpenter at Ellsworth). A notice sent to the township about a road, or to the Road Commission about a state highway, is sent to the wrong defendant. We confirm jurisdiction at the exact location before any notice is sent.

## An Ann Arbor address is not an Ann Arbor crash

A crash on a road with an Ann Arbor, Ypsilanti, or Saline mailing address may be in the township. If it is:

- **The report** is written by the Pittsfield Township Department of Public Safety, not the Ann Arbor or Ypsilanti police. A freeway crash may be handled by the State Police.
- **A small claim,** including a mini tort claim, is filed in the 14A-1 District Court at 4133 Washtenaw Avenue, not the 15th District Court in Ann Arbor.
- **The road** belongs to the Road Commission or MDOT, not the City of Ann Arbor.
- **The City of Ann Arbor’s crosswalk ordinance does not apply.** The state rules do: under the Michigan Uniform Traffic Code, which the township has adopted by reference, drivers must yield to a pedestrian in a crosswalk where there is no traffic signal.

The crash report names the jurisdiction. We confirm it in the first conversation.

## When the other vehicle is a bus or a public vehicle

**TheRide** runs routes on Washtenaw Avenue, Carpenter Road, State Street, and Packard Road. A crash with a TheRide bus is a claim against the Ann Arbor Area Transportation Authority under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405), in the circuit court. A bus passenger’s no-fault benefits follow the bus-passenger priority rules (MCL 500.3114(2)). See our [bus accident page](https://atlawgroup.com/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

**Township, Road Commission, and county vehicles** are claims under the same exception, against the agency that owns the vehicle. An individual officer or employee is personally liable only for gross negligence (MCL 691.1407(2)). A crash with a State of Michigan vehicle goes to the Court of Claims, with the six-month notice. Dash camera and body camera footage is overwritten on short schedules, so we request it by FOIA promptly.

## Fault is the case

In a rear-end crash, fault is rarely disputed. At a shopping-center exit on Carpenter, a left turn on Washtenaw, or a freeway merge, it often is. The other driver’s insurer may try to assign you half or more of the fault, and that share matters (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)):

- **More than 50 percent at fault:** you lose your claim for pain and suffering. It is barred, not merely “limited.”
- **50 percent or less:** your recovery is reduced by your share. At exactly 50 percent, you recover half.

The evidence that decides fault is perishable:

- the crash report from the township’s Department of Public Safety or the State Police
- the Road Commission’s or MDOT’s signal timing data
- cameras at the stores, gas stations, and apartment complexes along Carpenter and Washtenaw, many overwritten within days
- TheRide’s bus cameras, if a bus was nearby
- the vehicles’ **event data recorders,** which store speed, braking, and throttle in the seconds before impact
- the other driver’s phone records, for a distracted-driving claim (see our [distracted driving page](https://atlawgroup.com/personal-injury/distracted-driving-accidents))
- witnesses

We move promptly to request the camera footage, signal data, and vehicle data.

## Who pays after a Pittsfield Township crash

**Your no-fault (PIP) claim.** Your own auto insurer pays these benefits regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107):

- your medical expenses, up to the PIP level you chose
- 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years, up to $7,455 a month for crashes on or after October 1, 2026 (the cap DIFS sets each year)
- replacement services at $20 a day
- attendant care

**The PIP level is not the same as your liability limits.** Since July 2, 2020, Michigan drivers choose a PIP medical level for each policy: unlimited, $500,000, $250,000, $50,000 for some Medicaid enrollees, or an opt-out for some Medicare enrollees (MCL 500.3107c). That choice is separate from the liability limits that protect the other driver.

**Pedestrians, cyclists, and bus riders without a car.** A person hit while walking, biking, or riding in someone else’s car claims no-fault benefits in this order (MCL 500.3114, 500.3115, 500.3172):

1. a policy in the person’s household, including a spouse’s or a resident relative’s
2. if none applies and the person was riding in a car, the insurer of that car (MCL 500.3114(4))
3. otherwise, including a person hit while walking or biking, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan, capped at $250,000 for medical benefits (MCL 500.3115(1))

See our [pedestrian accident page](https://atlawgroup.com/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents) and our [bicycle accident page](https://atlawgroup.com/personal-injury/bicycle-accidents).

**Rideshare passengers and drivers.** A passenger in an Uber or Lyft looks first to the passenger’s own household policy, and a driver’s coverage depends on whether the app was on and a ride was accepted. See our [Uber and Lyft accident page](https://atlawgroup.com/personal-injury/rideshare-accidents).

The insurer must receive written notice within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). It must pay each bill within 30 days of reasonable proof, or owe 12 percent interest (MCL 500.3142). See our [no-fault insurance claim page](https://atlawgroup.com/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

**If you had no insurance.** A person who owned a car and was required to insure it, but did not, is generally barred from PIP benefits for their own injuries (MCL 500.3113(b)). A driver who was operating their own uninsured vehicle cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, whoever was at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(c)).

**Your claim against the at-fault driver.** For pain and suffering, you need a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment is an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life. It does not have to be permanent (MCL 500.3135(5); *McCormick v Carrier*, 2010).

You can also recover economic losses that no-fault does not cover (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)):

- lost wages beyond the three years or above the monthly cap
- medical bills above your chosen PIP level

The suit must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The mini tort claim recovers up to $3,000 in vehicle damage not covered by your policy (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)). If the at-fault driver’s insurer will not pay it, the claim is filed in the 14A-1 District Court.

**When the other driver has no insurance, or not enough.** Some drivers carry only the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)), and some carry nothing.

- **Against an uninsured driver:** your pain-and-suffering claim is worth what that driver can personally pay, unless you carry **uninsured motorist coverage**. A hit-and-run is usually a UM claim, with short notice terms in the policy.
- **Against an underinsured driver:** your own **underinsured motorist coverage** pays the difference up to your UIM limit, if you follow the steps in order:
 1. The at-fault driver’s insurer offers its limit.
 2. You notify your own insurer and ask for its written consent to settle.
 3. Only then do you accept the limit and sign the release.
 4. The UIM claim then proceeds.

Accepting without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under many policies. See our [uninsured motorist page](https://atlawgroup.com/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Drunk drivers and the dram shop claim.** A bar, restaurant, or store that serves a visibly intoxicated person, or a minor, is liable for the injuries that person causes (MCL 436.1801). Written notice must go to the business within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, the drunk driver must be named and kept in the suit, and the suit must be filed within two years. See our [drunk driving accident page](https://atlawgroup.com/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

## What to do after a Pittsfield Township crash

1. **Call 911 and get the police report.** Michigan requires a report for any crash with injury or $1,000 in damage (MCL 257.622). Note which agency responded. It tells you whether the crash was in the township.
2. **Photograph the scene:** the vehicles, plates, the other driver’s license and insurance card, the signal and lane markings, and the parking-lot exit or ramp. Note every store or gas station with a camera facing the scene. Footage is kept for days, not weeks.
3. **Get medical care the same day.** Trinity Health Ann Arbor, in neighboring Superior Township, and University of Michigan Health in Ann Arbor are Level I trauma centers.
4. **If a TheRide bus or a public vehicle was involved,** write down the vehicle number and tell us at once.
5. **Report to your own insurer** and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits promptly.
6. **Read the declarations pages** for the PIP medical level, UM/UIM, and collision and comprehensive coverage.
7. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the other driver’s insurer, sign a release, or accept a policy-limits offer before your UIM insurer consents.
8. **Call a lawyer within days** if fault is disputed, the other driver is uninsured, a public body is involved, or a bar served the driver.

## The courts

- **Claims up to $25,000,** including mini tort claims, go to the **14A-1 District Court** at 4133 Washtenaw Avenue, in the township.
- **Injury suits over $25,000** go to the Washtenaw County Trial Court (22nd Circuit) at 101 East Huron Street in Ann Arbor, with a jury drawn from Washtenaw County.
- **Claims against MDOT or the State** go to the Michigan Court of Claims.
- **Cases against out-of-state defendants** may be filed in, or removed to, the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan.
- **A fatal crash** is brought by the estate, opened through the Washtenaw County Probate Court. See our [wrongful death page](https://atlawgroup.com/personal-injury/wrongful-death) and our [Ann Arbor wrongful death page](https://atlawgroup.com/personal-injury/wrongful-death/ann-arbor).

## Frequently asked questions


**My address says Ann Arbor. Was my crash in Pittsfield Township?** Possibly. Most township residents have an Ann Arbor, Ypsilanti, or Saline mailing address. The crash report names the jurisdiction, and the responding agency is a clue: the township’s Department of Public Safety in the township, the Ann Arbor police in the city. The answer decides who owns the road and which district court hears a small claim.

**I was hit at Carpenter and Packard. Who is at fault?** It depends on the signal, the lanes, and the turn. Carpenter and Packard had 39 crashes, 14 injuries, and a death in 2024, the only fatal intersection on the county’s top-ten list. Signal data and nearby store cameras usually decide fault, and both are lost quickly.

**I hit a deer on Michigan Avenue. Do I have a claim?** Your own no-fault policy pays your medical bills and lost wages. Your car is covered only by your own collision or comprehensive coverage. There is no claim for pain and suffering unless another driver caused the crash, for example by swerving into you.

**A pothole on Carpenter Road caused my crash. Do I sue the township?** No. Townships do not own roads. Carpenter is a county road, and written notice must reach the Washtenaw County Road Commission within 120 days. If the road is I-94, US-23, Michigan Avenue, or Washtenaw Avenue east of US-23, the claim is against MDOT, with written notice filed in the Court of Claims within 120 days.

**I was walking to a bus stop on Carpenter and was hit. Who pays my medical bills?** A no-fault policy in your household pays first. If there is none, you claim through the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan. Your claim for pain and suffering is against the driver.

**How many crashes are there in Pittsfield Township?** The State Police recorded 1,143 crashes in the township in 2024. 3 people were killed, 29 were seriously injured, and 215 crashes injured someone.

**Is my PIP medical coverage the same as my liability limit?** Not necessarily. Your liability limits protect the other driver if you cause a crash. Your PIP medical level is a separate choice on the same policy, and it decides how much of your own medical care no-fault pays.

**How long do I have?** - 120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT, and six months for other claims against the State. - 120 days for a notice to the Road Commission or the township. - One year to give your no-fault insurer written notice. - Two years to sue a bar, with a 120-day notice. - Three years to sue the at-fault driver.
