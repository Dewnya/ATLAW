## Where Troy crashes happen, and whose road it is

**I-75 and the interchanges.** I-75 through Troy was rebuilt and widened in the I-75 modernization project, with reconstructed interchanges; the last segment opened in 2023. The Big Beaver and Crooks ramps carry the office commute at volumes that produce merge, weave, and rear-end crashes every weekday; and the Rochester ramps feed the retail corridors. Ramp-terminal signals, where exiting drivers meet arterial traffic, produce red-light and left-turn crashes. Freight moving north toward Auburn Hills and Flint adds tractor-trailers; see our [Troy truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents).

**Big Beaver Road.** The office spine, a wide boulevard from Coolidge past Somerset to Rochester. The crashes are left-turn and red-light crashes at the signals, drivers pulling out of tower and parking-structure driveways across several lanes, rear-ends in the evening backups, and pedestrian crashes at Somerset and the restaurants. Where the road is divided, drivers turning left use median crossovers, and a driver pulling out of a crossover into traffic is a recurring account. See our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents).

**Rochester Road and the retail corridors.** Rochester Road’s retail strip, the Oakland Mall traffic around Fourteen Mile and John R, and the Troy Motor Mall dealerships on Maple Road: left turns into shopping centers and dealerships, rear-ends at the signals, and test drives. A crash in a dealership’s loaner or a car being test-driven raises its own coverage questions about whose policy is first.

**The mile roads, the arterials, and the neighborhoods.** Maple, Long Lake, Square Lake, Crooks, Livernois, Coolidge, John R, and Dequindre carry signal crashes at the major intersections and crashes near the schools and the parks, and the subdivision streets carry backing and driveway crashes. See our [bicycle accident page](/personal-injury/bicycle-accidents).

**Whose road it is.** I-75 is MDOT’s; a road-defect claim against MDOT goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). The Road Commission for Oakland County has jurisdiction over many of Troy’s major roads, and the City maintains its local streets and its own major streets; a claim against the City or the Road Commission takes written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404). The Road Commission operates the traffic signals in Troy, so signal-timing records for a disputed intersection crash come from it. A pothole or other defect in the roadbed can make the agency a defendant alongside the other driver; on State and county roads, signals, signs, and sight obstructions generally fall outside the highway exception. Notice to the wrong agency is not notice.

## Fault is the case

In a rear-end crash, fault is rarely disputed. At a ramp signal, a Big Beaver crossover, a tower driveway, or a left turn into a shopping center, it is disputed often, and the other driver’s insurer may try to assign you half or more of the fault. That number matters: if you are found more than 50 percent at fault, you lose your claim for pain and suffering, and below that your recovery is reduced by your share (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). At exactly 50 percent you recover half. The evidence that decides it is perishable:

- the Troy Police report and the officer’s diagram, or the State Police report for a freeway crash;
- **signal timing data** from the Road Commission for Oakland County, which operates Troy’s signals, and MDOT freeway camera footage;
- office-tower, hotel, retail, and parking-structure cameras along Big Beaver and Rochester, many overwritten within days;
- the vehicles’ **event data recorders**, which store speed, braking, and throttle in the seconds before impact;
- the other driver’s phone records, for a distracted-driving claim (see our [distracted driving page](/personal-injury/distracted-driving-accidents));
- witnesses in the other lanes.

## Who pays after a Troy crash

**Your no-fault (PIP) claim.** Your own auto insurer pays your medical expenses up to the PIP level you chose, 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year, replacement services at $20 a day, and attendant care, regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107). If you have no policy, a resident relative’s policy pays (MCL 500.3114); if none exists, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan does (MCL 500.3172), capped at $250,000 for medical benefits. The insurer must receive written notice within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)) and must pay each bill within 30 days of reasonable proof or owe 12 percent interest (MCL 500.3142). Since the 2019 reform, PIP medical coverage is no longer unlimited unless you chose it; if you opted out of PIP medical, which only a policyholder with Medicare Parts A and B may do (MCL 500.3107d), Medicare pays first and its rules apply. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance) and [Michigan no-fault guide](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

**Your claim against the at-fault driver.** For pain and suffering, you need a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment is an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life; it does not have to be permanent (MCL 500.3135(5); *McCormick v Carrier*, 2010). You can also recover lost wages beyond the three years or above the monthly cap, and medical bills above your chosen PIP level (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)), which matters for the professionals and executives in the Big Beaver offices whose wages exceed the cap. The suit must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The mini tort recovers up to $3,000 in vehicle damage not covered by your policy (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)), filed in the 52-4 District Court if the at-fault driver’s insurer will not pay it.

**Coverage on both sides, and UIM sequencing.** Many Troy drivers carry liability limits at or above the $250,000/$500,000 default, but the freeway and commuter traffic comes from everywhere, and some drivers carry only the $50,000 minimum or nothing. When the at-fault driver’s limit is too small for the injury, your own **underinsured motorist coverage** pays the difference, but most policies require your insurer’s written consent before you accept the at-fault driver’s limit; settling without it can forfeit the UIM claim. We read every policy in the household, including umbrella coverage, before valuing the claim. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Company cars and work drives.** Many Troy crashes involve someone driving for work: an employee in a company car between offices, a sales representative, or a delivery or service driver. If you were working when you were hit, workers’ compensation pays first and no-fault pays the difference (MCL 500.3109(1)), and your claim against the other driver is unaffected. If the at-fault driver was working, the employer and its commercial policy may be liable too.

**Drunk drivers and dram shop.** Oakland County recorded 871 alcohol-involved crashes in 2024. A crash caused by a drunk driver leaving a Big Beaver restaurant, a hotel bar, or a Rochester Road bar adds a claim against the business that served a visibly intoxicated person or a minor. Written notice must go to the retailer within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, the drunk driver must be named and kept in the suit, and the suit must be filed within two years, not three (MCL 436.1801(3), (4)). See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**Government vehicles and rideshare.** Troy Police and Fire, SMART buses in Troy, school buses, and City road trucks fall under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405); see our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents). Uber and Lyft trips to Somerset, the hotels, and the offices carry the TNC insurance tiers (MCL 257.2123); see our [rideshare accident page](/personal-injury/rideshare-accidents).

## What to do after a Troy crash

1. **Call 911 and get the police report.** Michigan requires a report for any crash with injury or $1,000 in damage (MCL 257.622). City streets are worked by the Troy Police Department; freeway crashes may be worked by the Michigan State Police.
2. **Photograph the vehicles, plates, the other driver’s license and insurance card, the signals, and the lane markings.** Note every business, tower, and parking structure with a camera facing the scene; footage is kept for days, not weeks.
3. **Get medical care the same day.** Corewell Health Troy Hospital on Dequindre treats many of the city’s injured; Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak, a Level I trauma center, treats others.
4. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** within the first week.
5. **Read your declarations page** for PIP level, UM/UIM, umbrella, and collision coverage.
6. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the other driver’s insurer, sign a release, or accept a policy-limits offer before your UIM insurer consents.
7. **Call a lawyer within days** if fault is disputed; the camera footage and signal data are the first evidence lost.

## The courts

Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, are filed in the 52nd District Court, Fourth Division, at 520 W. Big Beaver Road, which serves Troy and Clawson. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac. A crash on Dequindre at the Macomb County line, or with a Macomb County defendant, may allow filing in the Sixteenth Circuit in Mount Clemens instead (MCL 600.1629). Defect claims against MDOT go to the Court of Claims. A fatal crash is brought by the estate through the Oakland County Probate Court; see our [Troy wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death).

## Frequently asked questions

**Which Troy intersection has the most crashes?** None of Troy’s intersections was in Oakland County’s 2024 top ten. Troy’s crashes are spread along I-75 and its interchanges, Big Beaver, Rochester Road, and the other arterials. **[CONFIRM: data: Troy’s highest-crash locations from the MTCF query tool]**

**How many car accidents happen in Troy each year?** Troy recorded 2,480 crashes, 686 injuries, and 7 deaths in 2024. Oakland County as a whole recorded 35,951 crashes, 9,139 injuries, and 68 deaths in 2024.

**The crash was caused by a signal problem on Big Beaver. Who do I claim against?** A signal claim is hard to bring: on State and county roads, signals generally fall outside the highway exception, and the Road Commission for Oakland County operates Troy’s signals. A defect in the roadbed itself goes to whichever agency has the road, the City or the Road Commission, with written notice within 120 days; on I-75, MDOT, with notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days.

**The other driver’s insurer offered their $250,000 policy limit. Should I take it?** Not before checking your own underinsured motorist coverage and getting your insurer’s written consent to settle. Accepting the limit without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under most policies.

**I was driving for work when I was hit. Who pays?** Workers’ compensation pays your medical and wage-loss benefits first, your no-fault insurer pays what comp does not, and the at-fault driver is liable for pain and suffering if you have a threshold injury.

**Where would my Troy case be filed?** Mini tort and claims up to $25,000 in the 52-4 District Court on Big Beaver; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court in Pontiac, or in some Dequindre or Macomb County cases the Sixteenth Circuit in Mount Clemens.

**How long do I have?** One year to give your no-fault insurer written notice, three years to sue the at-fault driver, two years to sue a bar, 120 days for a notice to the City or the Road Commission, and 120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT.
