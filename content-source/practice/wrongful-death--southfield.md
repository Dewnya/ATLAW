## Southfield Wrongful Death Lawyer

When someone dies in Southfield because another person or company was careless, Michigan’s Wrongful Death Act (MCL 600.2922) gives the family a claim, but not in the way many families expect. The claim belongs to the estate, not to the spouse or children directly. It must be brought by a personal representative appointed by the Oakland County Probate Court. The recovery is divided among family members by a judge. And the deadline depends on what caused the death: a crash at Telegraph and 12 Mile, a truck in the I-696 work zone, a bar that kept serving, a hospital error, a nursing home. Some of those deadlines are 120 days. This page explains how the process works for a Southfield family and what to do in the first weeks.

## How a Southfield wrongful death claim works

**The estate.** Only the personal representative can bring the claim (MCL 600.2922(2)). Where the person lived in Southfield, the estate is opened in the Oakland County Probate Court, on the county campus at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac. **[CONFIRM: verify address and whether petitions are filed electronically]** If there is a will naming a personal representative, that person petitions for appointment; if not, a family member, usually the spouse or an adult child, petitions under Michigan’s priority rules (MCL 700.3203). The court issues letters of authority, and the personal representative then has standing to hire a lawyer for the estate and file. We file the petition as the first legal step. **[ATTORNEY CHECK: EPIC appointment summary]**

**Notice to the family.** Within 30 days after filing suit, the personal representative must notify in writing every person who may share in the recovery: the spouse, children, descendants, parents, grandparents, brothers and sisters, the children of the deceased’s spouse, and anyone named in the will (MCL 600.2922(2)–(4)).

**The court.** The suit is the injury claim the person would have had, brought by the estate against whoever caused the death. Death claims are filed in circuit court, not the 46th District Court on Evergreen, which hears civil cases only up to $25,000. For a death in Southfield that is usually the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court in Pontiac. Where the defendant is in Wayne County, as with a crash on the Detroit side of Eight Mile, the Third Circuit in Detroit may also be a proper venue (MCL 600.1629). Claims against the State go to the Court of Claims, and claims under federal law to the Eastern District of Michigan. **[ATTORNEY CHECK: venue]**

**The damages** (MCL 600.2922(6)): medical and hospital expenses from the injury to the death; funeral and burial expenses; the person’s **conscious pain and suffering** between the injury and death; **loss of financial support** over a working life; and **loss of society and companionship** for each family member. Michigan places no cap on these except in medical malpractice cases, and does not award punitive damages in wrongful death cases.

**Comparative fault.** If the person who died was partly at fault, the damages are reduced by that share. If their fault was greater than the combined fault of everyone else, the estate cannot recover noneconomic damages (the pain and suffering and loss of companionship), but can still recover reduced economic damages (MCL 600.2959). In an auto case, the no-fault act bars noneconomic damages at more than 50 percent fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)).

**The distribution hearing.** After a settlement or verdict, the personal representative proposes a division among the eligible family members, with notice to each. If they agree, the court approves it; if not, the court decides based on each person’s relationship and loss (MCL 600.2922(6)). Fees and costs are approved at the same hearing. Because a judge divides the money, no family member should sign anything with an insurer; a release signed by one relative does not settle the estate’s claim.

## The causes of Southfield wrongful death claims

**Crashes** at the Telegraph and 12 Mile intersection, on Northwestern Highway, the Lodge, Eight Mile, and I-696. The at-fault driver’s liability insurer pays, subject to policy limits that are often the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5); the default under 3009(1) is $250,000); the deceased’s own underinsured motorist coverage, umbrella policies, and the policy of an employer whose driver was working are checked as well. Separately, **no-fault survivor’s loss benefits** (MCL 500.3108) are paid regardless of fault by the deceased’s own insurer, or the insurer that would have paid their PIP, for up to three years after the death: the economic support the person provided, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each October, $20 a day in replacement services, and funeral expenses up to the policy limit, at least $1,750 and up to $5,000 (MCL 500.3107(1)(a)). Written notice is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)); do not wait for the lawsuit. See our [Southfield car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents/southfield) and, for pedestrian deaths, our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents).

Fatal truck and motorcycle crashes carry their own rules; see our [Southfield truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/southfield) and [Southfield motorcycle accident page](/personal-injury/motorcycle-accidents/southfield).

**Work-zone deaths on I-696** can involve the driver, the contractor that set up the lane shifts, and the road surface: the contractor in circuit court, and MDOT in the Court of Claims for a defect in the roadbed itself; the State is generally immune for the work zone’s design and signs. The contractor’s traffic-control records go in the first preservation letter.

**Drunk driving.** The driver, and under the dram shop act (MCL 436.1801) the bar, restaurant, or store that served a visibly intoxicated person or a minor. The dram shop claim has its own rules: written notice to every defendant within 120 days after the family hires a lawyer, the intoxicated driver must be named and kept in the suit, and the suit must be filed within **two years** of the death, not three. The criminal case proceeds separately; the family’s claim does not wait for it. See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents). **[ATTORNEY CHECK]**

**Workplace deaths** on construction sites, in building maintenance, and on delivery routes. The employer owes workers’ compensation death benefits, up to 500 weeks plus burial (MCL 418.321, 418.345), and cannot otherwise be sued (MCL 418.131); a general contractor, subcontractor, property owner, or manufacturer whose negligence caused the death can be. See our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).

**Hospital and medical negligence** at Henry Ford Providence Southfield Hospital (formerly Ascension Providence), Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak, or the Detroit hospitals: a wrongful death claim with the malpractice rules attached, a 182-day Notice of Intent (MCL 600.2912b), an Affidavit of Merit (MCL 600.2912d), a two-year period (MCL 600.5805(8), 600.5838a), and the cap (MCL 600.1483). See our [Southfield medical malpractice page](/personal-injury/medical-malpractice/southfield).

**Nursing homes and premises.** Southfield has five licensed nursing homes; a death from neglect there may be ordinary negligence or malpractice, which changes the deadline (see our [Southfield nursing home abuse page](/personal-injury/nursing-home-abuse/southfield)). Deaths from falls, fires, and carbon monoxide in apartments and commercial buildings follow premises law and, for tenants, MCL 554.139 (see our [Southfield slip and fall page](/personal-injury/slip-and-fall/southfield)).

**Deaths involving a government agency.** Three agencies own Southfield’s roads, and the notice clock depends on which one. The City owns Evergreen and local streets; the Road Commission for Oakland County owns the mile roads from 9 to 13 and Lahser; MDOT owns Telegraph, Northwestern Highway, the Lodge, Eight Mile, and I-696; a claim against MDOT needs notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). A City or SMART vehicle falls under the motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405). An individual government employee is liable only for gross negligence (MCL 691.1407(2)). **[ATTORNEY CHECK]**

## What the family should do in the first weeks

1. **Get the death certificate and request the Oakland County Medical Examiner’s report**, along with the EMS run sheet and the hospital record. If the family’s customs call for prompt burial, tell the Medical Examiner’s office at once, or call us. **[CONFIRM: verify current Oakland County ME procedure]**
2. **Keep everything**: the vehicle, the phone, the helmet, the medication bottles. Do not authorize salvage of a vehicle until a lawyer has seen it.
3. **Notify the no-fault insurer in writing** if a vehicle was involved; survivor’s loss benefits begin on the claim, not on the lawsuit.
4. **Do not give a recorded statement** to any insurer and do not sign any document, however it is described. An insurer offering to “cover the funeral” in exchange for a signature may be settling the claim for the price of a funeral.
5. **Open the estate.** The probate petition is the first legal step; the wrongful death suit cannot be filed until letters issue.
6. **Call within days** if the death involved a city, county, or state road or vehicle, a bar, or an employer. Those notice periods are short.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who can file a wrongful death lawsuit in Southfield?** Only the personal representative of the estate, appointed by the Oakland County Probate Court for a Southfield resident. A spouse, child, or parent can be appointed, but must be appointed before the claim can be filed.

**Who receives the money?** The spouse, children, descendants, parents, grandparents, siblings, the children of the deceased’s spouse, and anyone named in the will may share. The probate court approves the division after notice to each of them.

**How long do we have?** Three years from the death for crash, fall, and premises claims; two years with a 182-day notice for medical malpractice; two years for a dram shop claim; one year to claim no-fault survivor’s loss benefits; and 120 days to notify the City or the Road Commission of a road defect. The savings provision can extend the deadline for an estate in limited circumstances, but should not be relied on.

**My family member was partly at fault. Is there still a claim?** Often, yes. The damages are reduced by their share of fault. Above a certain point the estate loses the noneconomic damages but can still recover reduced economic losses, such as lost support and funeral costs.

**Is there a lawsuit if the driver was charged with a crime?** Yes. The criminal case and the family’s claim are separate. A conviction can help prove the claim, but the family does not have to wait for it, and the civil deadlines keep running.

**Does the family pay anything up front?** No. The case is handled on contingency, costs are advanced by the firm, and the fee and costs are approved by the probate court at the distribution hearing. **[CONFIRM]**
