## Pontiac, MI Personal Injury Lawyer

If you were hurt in Pontiac because someone else was careless, you have a claim under Michigan law, and its deadlines start the day you are hurt. After a crash, your no-fault insurer must have written notice within one year. A road or sidewalk defect claim against the City of Pontiac or the Road Commission for Oakland County needs written notice within 120 days; a defect on Woodward, the Loop, Telegraph, M-59, or I-75 needs written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days. An injury caused by a defect in a City or county building open to the public needs written notice to that agency within 120 days. A claim against a bar that served a drunk driver must be filed within two years. A medical malpractice suit, including many claims against nursing homes, requires 182 days’ notice first and must be filed within two years. Most other claims allow three years. A personal injury lawyer’s first job is to find out which of those apply to you; the second is to make sure none runs out before your claim is paid.

## What a Pontiac personal injury lawyer does

**The first week.** We calendar every deadline and send preservation letters:

- to the at-fault driver’s insurer;
- to a trucking company for its driver logs and electronic data, after crashes on M-59, Telegraph, or the industrial corridor;
- to MDOT, the City, or the Road Commission for signal timing records;
- to Oakland County for Sheriff’s patrol-car video and dispatch records, and to SMART or the school district for vehicle records;
- to a store, apartment complex, or property manager for footage and incident reports, which many overwrite within days;
- to a nursing home or assisted living community for the resident’s chart, incident reports, and staffing records;
- to a bar or restaurant for its receipts if a drunk driver was involved.

We obtain the police report (Oakland County Sheriff’s Office reports for crashes and incidents in the city; State Police reports on the freeways), the fire department and ambulance run sheets, and the witnesses’ names.

**The first month.** We identify every policy that may pay: your own auto policy for no-fault benefits, household policies, the at-fault party’s liability coverage, your uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage, a property owner’s or management company’s commercial policy, umbrella policies, and the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan where no auto policy exists (MCL 500.3172). In Pontiac the Assigned Claims Plan matters more than in most of the county, because many residents do not own a car or do not have a household auto policy; a pedestrian, cyclist, or bus rider hit by a car is paid first by the insurer of the car’s owner or driver if no household policy applies (MCL 500.3115). For a workplace injury, we identify whether a third party, such as a contractor, a vendor’s driver, or an equipment manufacturer, caused it, because the employer itself usually cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). We file your Application for No-Fault Benefits inside the one-year window (MCL 500.3145(1)) and handle the insurer’s requests for statements, records, and examinations (MCL 500.3151). You stop talking to adjusters; we do that.

**The treatment period.** The claim’s value is set mostly by the medical record, so we make sure it is complete and the bills are paid while you treat. McLaren Oakland on North Perry and Trinity Health Oakland on Woodward are both in the city and both Level II trauma centers on the State’s list; the most serious injuries may go to Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak, the county’s Level I adult trauma center. We collect records from each and from your own physicians and therapists, and document lost wages with your employer, including overtime and shift premiums.

**The demand, the suit, and the resolution.** When your condition has stabilized, we send a demand with the complete record and a valuation. The decision to accept any offer is yours (MRPC 1.2). If the insurer will not pay fair value, suit is filed in the right court:

- **50th District Court** at 70 N. Saginaw Street, for claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims.
- **Sixth Judicial Circuit Court** (Oakland County Circuit Court) at 1200 N. Telegraph Road for injury suits over $25,000, with a jury drawn from all of Oakland County. Most injury cases there go through case evaluation (MCR 2.403) and facilitation before trial.
- **The U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan**, where an out-of-state defendant removes the case.

At settlement we resolve the liens, including health plans, Medicare, Medicaid, and workers’ compensation, before you are paid. Minors’ settlements and wrongful death distributions are approved by the Oakland County Probate Court, on the Telegraph Road campus, or by the trial court (MCR 2.420; MCL 600.2922(6)). Compensation for physical injury is generally not taxable income (26 USC 104(a)(2)).

## Who the defendant is in Pontiac

Pontiac is a city, its police are the County’s, and its fire department is another community’s. Each of those changes who you claim against.

- **The roads are split three ways.** Woodward, the Loop, Telegraph, M-59, and I-75 are MDOT’s, with written notice of a road defect filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). Most city streets are the City of Pontiac’s, and some roads are the Road Commission for Oakland County’s by segment, each with written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404). The notice must go to the agency that has jurisdiction over that segment.
- **The sidewalks are the City’s.** The City must keep the public sidewalks in reasonable repair, including those beside state and county roads (MCL 691.1402a). A fall on a broken public sidewalk is a claim against the City, with the 30-day knowledge requirement, the two-inch presumption, and 120-day notice.
- **The police are Oakland County.** The Oakland County Sheriff’s Office has policed Pontiac under contract since August 2011. A crash with a patrol car in the city is a claim against Oakland County under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405), not against the City.
- Fire protection is the Waterford Regional Fire Department’s, under contract with the City. A crash with a fire engine is a claim against the agency that owns it, usually Waterford Township, under the motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405). Ambulance transport is by a private company, which is an ordinary defendant.
- **The Housing Commission, the City, the County, and the schools are separate agencies.** An injury caused by a dangerous or defective condition in a City building, a county building on the Telegraph Road campus, or a Pontiac School District building proceeds under the public building exception, which requires the agency to have known of the defect and failed to fix it, with written notice to that agency within 120 days (MCL 691.1406). A private landlord, by contrast, is an ordinary defendant with a three-year deadline (MCL 554.139). Housing Commission apartments are different: Michigan courts have held that a public housing unit is not a public building open for use by members of the public, so a tenant’s claim for a defect inside a unit is usually barred (*Griffin v City of Detroit*, Mich App 1989).
- **SMART and City vehicles.** A crash with a SMART bus on Woodward or Telegraph, a City truck, or a school bus is a claim under the motor vehicle exception. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

## When the other driver has no insurance

Some drivers on Pontiac’s roads carry only the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)). Your own no-fault benefits are paid regardless of the other driver’s insurance, by your insurer, a household member’s, or the Assigned Claims Plan. Your claim for pain and suffering against an uninsured driver is worth what that driver can personally pay unless you carry **uninsured motorist coverage**; against a minimum-limits driver, $50,000 is often the ceiling unless you carry **underinsured motorist coverage**. A hit-and-run is usually a UM claim with short notice terms in the policy. Two rules cut the other way: an owner who was required to insure a car and did not is generally barred from PIP benefits for injuries in it (MCL 500.3113(b)), and a driver who was operating their own uninsured vehicle cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, whoever was at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(c)))). We check every policy in the household before we value a Pontiac claim. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

## What a Pontiac claim is worth, and what reduces it

A claim includes economic damages (medical expenses, lost wages, lost earning capacity, replacement services) and noneconomic damages (pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, disfigurement). There is no cap except in medical malpractice. In an auto case, noneconomic damages require a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)); a premises or dog bite case has no such threshold. Your share of fault reduces the recovery, and above 50 percent it bars noneconomic damages (MCL 600.2959; in auto cases MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). At exactly 50 percent you recover half. Where more than one party caused the injury, each defendant generally pays only its own share of fault (MCL 600.2956, 600.6304), so every responsible party has to be identified and named. Michigan does not award punitive damages in ordinary injury cases; exemplary damages for humiliation or outrage are available only in narrow circumstances.

In many Pontiac cases the practical limit is insurance. The at-fault driver’s liability limit defaults to $250,000/$500,000 unless the driver chose lower limits, down to the $50,000/$100,000 minimum (MCL 500.3009(1)), and some drivers carry nothing; your own UM and UIM coverage fills the gap; and in a premises, workplace, or nursing home case, the commercial policy of the property owner, contractor, or nursing home operator pays. Where UIM is involved, settling with the at-fault driver without your own insurer’s consent can forfeit the UIM claim. For a worker with a third-party claim, the workers’ compensation insurer has a lien on the recovery (MCL 418.827), and how that lien is negotiated changes what you take home. Medicare and Medicaid liens are negotiated the same way.

## What it costs

Michigan caps contingency fees in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery (MCR 8.121). Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted from the recovery first; the one-third fee is calculated on the remaining amount. The agreement is in writing; read how costs (filing fees, records, experts, depositions) are handled if the case is lost, because that is where firms differ. A promise that “you pay nothing unless we win” should say whether that includes costs, and whether “win” means the same thing as “settle.”

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in Pontiac?** Three years for most claims. But you have one year to notify your no-fault insurer after a crash, 120 days to notify the City, the Road Commission, the Housing Commission, or another local agency of a defect, 120 days for a defect on a state highway, two years to sue a bar, and a 182-day notice and two-year limit for malpractice.

**I don’t have car insurance and was hit while walking. Who pays my medical bills?** If no one in your household has an auto policy, the insurer of the car that hit you pays your PIP benefits first; if that car was uninsured, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan does. You also have a claim against the driver if your injury meets the threshold.

**A Sheriff’s deputy crashed into me in Pontiac. Do I claim against the City?** No. The Sheriff’s Office polices Pontiac under contract, so the claim is against Oakland County, under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity.

**I was hurt by a broken stair in Housing Commission housing. Do I have a claim?** Usually not for a defect inside your unit: Michigan courts have held that a public housing apartment is not a public building open to the public, so governmental immunity generally bars the claim. A defect in a part of the building open to the public, such as an office, may be different, with written notice within 120 days. A private landlord is an ordinary defendant with a three-year deadline.

**What does a Pontiac personal injury attorney charge?** Michigan caps the fee in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery, with no fee if there is no recovery. Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted only from a recovery.

**Where would my Pontiac case be filed?** In Pontiac: claims up to $25,000 in the 50th District Court on Saginaw Street; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court on Telegraph Road.

**If I was partly at fault, do I still have a claim?** Yes, unless you were more than 50 percent at fault, which bars pain-and-suffering damages. Below that, your recovery is reduced by your share; at exactly 50 percent you recover half. No-fault benefits are paid regardless of fault.

**Do I need a lawyer whose office is in Pontiac?** No. What matters is that the lawyer knows the courts your case will be in, the agencies, insurers, and property owners involved, and will meet you where you are.

**The insurer already offered me money. Should I take it?** Not before you know what the claim is worth and what the release gives up, and not before your UIM insurer has consented if UIM may apply.
