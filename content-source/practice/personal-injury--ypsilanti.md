## Ypsilanti Personal Injury Lawyer

If you were hurt in Ypsilanti because someone else was careless, you have a claim, and its deadlines start the day you are hurt:

- **A claim against Eastern Michigan University or the State,** including a University vehicle, needs a verified notice filed with the Court of Claims within six months; a defective University building needs written notice filed there within 120 days.
- **A road, signal, or sidewalk defect** needs written notice within 120 days to the right public body: the City of Ypsilanti for a city street, the Washtenaw County Road Commission for a township road, or the township for a township sidewalk. A defect on I-94, US-12, or M-17 needs written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days.
- **After a crash,** your no-fault insurer must have written notice within one year.
- **A claim against a bar or store** that served a visibly intoxicated person or a minor needs written notice within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, and suit within two years.
- **A medical malpractice suit** against a Michigan provider requires 182 days’ notice first and must be filed within two years. A claim about care at the VA requires a federal administrative claim within two years.
- **Most other claims,** including falls in rentals and stores, dog bites, and crashes with private drivers, allow three years.

A personal injury lawyer’s first job is to find out which of those deadlines apply to you. In Ypsilanti, that starts with one question: **were you hurt in the City of Ypsilanti or in Ypsilanti Charter Township?** They are two governments with different road owners, police, district courts, and notice rules. The second job is to make sure no deadline runs out before your claim is paid.

## Who the defendant is in Ypsilanti

- **Eastern Michigan University is the State.** EMU is a constitutional state university. A claim against it goes to the Michigan Court of Claims, is decided by a judge, and requires a verified notice, or the claim itself, filed with that court within six months (MCL 600.6431). The Michigan Supreme Court has held that the notice requirement applies to any claim against the State, wherever it is filed (*Christie v Wayne State University*, 2023). That covers: a crash with a University vehicle or an EMU police car, under the motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405); and an injury caused by a dangerous or defective condition in a University building open for use by members of the public, such as a classroom building or the stadium and arena, under the public building exception, which requires that the University knew, or should have known, of the defect and failed to fix it within a reasonable time, and written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1406).
- Injuries on University grounds outside a building, such as a fall on a campus walk, may fall outside every exception. An injury at a private apartment complex next to campus is an ordinary claim against its owner.
- **The City of Ypsilanti.** The City owns its streets and sidewalks, its buildings, and its parks. A road, signal, or sidewalk defect is a claim against the City, with written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1402a, 691.1404). The sidewalk claim requires that the City knew, or should have known, of the defect at least 30 days before the injury, and a defect under two inches is presumed to be in reasonable repair. A dangerous condition in a City building falls under the public building exception (MCL 691.1406).
- **Ypsilanti Charter Township and the Road Commission.** Townships do not own roads. A township road belongs to the Washtenaw County Road Commission, with written notice within 120 days. The township has the duty for sidewalks along those roads, and for its own buildings and vehicles (MCL 691.1401, 691.1402a, 691.1405, 691.1406). A notice sent to the township about a road, or to the Road Commission about a sidewalk, is sent to the wrong defendant.
- **MDOT** for I-94, US-12, M-17, and any state trunkline routes through the city, with written notice of a road defect filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **TheRide.** A crash with a TheRide bus is a claim against the Ann Arbor Area Transportation Authority under the motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405).
- **Landlords and student rentals.** Both the city and the township have many renters, from older houses near campus to apartment complexes in the township. A landlord must keep the premises and common areas fit for their intended use and in reasonable repair, and comply with health and safety laws (MCL 554.139). That covers: porches and balconies; stairs and railings; wiring; smoke and carbon monoxide detectors; and snow and ice the landlord controls.
- A lease clause that waives the landlord’s liability for its own negligence is not enforceable. Earlier complaints, work orders, and any City rental inspection reports are often strong evidence of what the landlord knew. A guest at a party in a rental may have a premises claim too. See our [Ypsilanti slip and fall page](/personal-injury/slip-and-fall) and our [tenant injury page](/personal-injury/tenant-injuries).
- **Stores, restaurants, and parking lots.** A fall in a store, a restaurant, or a parking lot is a premises claim against the owner, the tenant, or both, depending on who controlled the area. Snow and ice are often handled by a contractor, which can be a separate defendant. Since *Kandil-Elsayed v F & E Oil* (2023), an “open and obvious” hazard such as visible ice goes to comparative fault rather than ending the claim.
- **Bars, stores, and service to minors.** A bar, restaurant, or store that serves a visibly intoxicated person, or anyone under 21, is liable for the injuries that person causes (MCL 436.1801). The State Police recorded 13 alcohol-involved crashes in the city and 36 in the township in 2024. The claim needs written notice within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, the intoxicated person named and kept in the suit, and suit within two years. Michigan generally does not hold a private social host liable for serving adults, but furnishing alcohol to minors can create liability. See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).
- **Boat owners on Ford Lake.** The owner of a boat is liable for injuries caused by its negligent operation by anyone the owner allowed to use it, and consent is presumed when the operator is a member of the owner’s family (MCL 324.80157). An injury in a township park, including Ford Lake Park, is limited by governmental immunity unless an exception applies.
- **Dog owners.** An owner is liable for a bite when the person bitten was lawfully present and did not provoke the dog (MCL 287.351). The city and the township each have their own animal ordinances, and a broken local rule, such as a leash requirement, can be evidence in a non-bite injury. See our [dog bite page](/personal-injury/dog-bites).
- **Employers and third parties.** EMU, the hospitals, Willow Run Airport’s cargo operations and the warehouses near it, construction on I-94 and the corridors, and the area’s retail and restaurants are major workplaces. Workers’ compensation is the remedy against the employer (MCL 418.131), but a contractor, property owner, or equipment maker who caused the injury can be sued. See our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).
- **Nursing homes.** A claim against a nursing home may be ordinary negligence, with a three-year deadline, or medical malpractice, with two years and a 182-day notice. See our [nursing home abuse page](/personal-injury/nursing-home-abuse).

- a 182-day Notice of Intent before filing (MCL 600.2912b)

## When the other driver has no insurance, or not enough

Some drivers carry only the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)), and some carry nothing.

- **Against an uninsured driver,** the claim for pain and suffering depends on your own **uninsured motorist coverage**, or a parent’s, if you are a resident relative under their policy. Without it, the claim is worth what the driver can personally pay. A hit-and-run is usually a UM claim, with short notice terms in the policy.
- **Against an underinsured driver,** your own **underinsured motorist coverage** pays the difference up to your UIM limit, if you follow the steps in order:
 1. The at-fault driver’s insurer offers its limit.
 2. You notify your own insurer and ask for its written consent to settle.
 3. Only then do you accept the limit and sign the release.
 4. The UIM claim then proceeds.

Accepting without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under many policies.

If you have no auto insurance of your own and no household policy, your no-fault benefits may come from the Assigned Claims Plan, with its $250,000 medical cap. That is often the case for a pedestrian or cyclist who does not own a car. A bus passenger with no household policy is paid by the bus’s insurer (MCL 500.3114(2)). Two rules cut the other way:

- An owner who was required to insure a car and did not is generally barred from PIP benefits (MCL 500.3113(b)).
- A driver who was operating their own uninsured vehicle cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, whoever was at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(c)).

See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

## What an Ypsilanti claim is worth, and what reduces it

A claim includes two kinds of damages:

- **Economic damages:** medical expenses, lost wages, lost earning capacity, and replacement services.
- **Noneconomic damages:** pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, and disfigurement.

For a student, **lost earning capacity**, meaning the career the injury cut short, can be a large part of the economic loss. It is proved with education records, the field of study, and expert testimony.

There is no cap except in medical malpractice and product liability cases (MCL 600.1483, 600.2946a). In an auto case, noneconomic damages require a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment does not have to be permanent (*McCormick v Carrier*, 2010). Vehicle damage up to $3,000 can be recovered from the at-fault driver through the mini tort claim, without a threshold (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)). A premises, dog bite, or dram shop case has no threshold.

**Comparative fault.**

- **More than 50 percent at fault:** noneconomic damages are barred (MCL 600.2959; in auto cases, MCL 500.3135(2)(b)).
- **50 percent or less:** your recovery is reduced by your share. At exactly 50 percent, you recover half.
- **Economic damages** are reduced by your share but not barred.

So a recovery is not simply reduced by your share of fault. Whether your share is above 50 percent is often the whole dispute.

Where more than one party caused the injury, each defendant generally pays only its own share of fault (MCL 600.2956, 600.6304). That is why the landlord, the property manager, the snow-removal contractor, and the bar all have to be identified and named. Michigan does not award punitive damages in ordinary injury cases.

You cannot sue the at-fault party’s liability insurer directly in Michigan. The claim is against the driver, owner, or business, and its insurer defends and pays.

The practical limit is often insurance:

- **The at-fault driver’s limit** defaults to $250,000/$500,000 unless the driver chose lower limits, down to the $50,000/$100,000 minimum (MCL 500.3009(1)).
- **Your UM and UIM coverage,** or a parent’s, fills the gap.
- **In a premises, workplace, or nursing home case,** the commercial policy of the landlord, owner, contractor, or operator pays, and those policies are often larger than an auto policy.
- **In a dram shop case,** the bar’s liquor liability policy adds to it.

 For a worker with a third-party claim, the workers’ compensation insurer has a lien on the recovery (MCL 418.827). Medicare, Medicaid, and health plan liens are negotiated the same way.

## What it costs

Michigan caps contingency fees in personal injury cases at one-third of the net recovery (MCR 8.121). A fee above one-third of an injury recovery exceeds that cap. You pay nothing up front and nothing if there is no recovery. Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted from the recovery first; the one-third fee is calculated on the remaining amount.

The agreement is in writing. Read how costs are handled if the case is lost, because that is where firms differ: filing fees, records, experts, and depositions. A promise that “you pay nothing unless we win” should say whether that includes costs, and whether “win” means the same thing as “settle.” A “success rate” or a list of verdicts tells you about past cases, not yours, and says nothing unless it says when, where, and what the case was.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in Ypsilanti?** Three years for most claims. Several deadlines are much shorter: - six months for a claim against EMU or the State, and 120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT or a building-defect notice against EMU - 120 days for a notice to the City, the township, or the Road Commission of a road, sidewalk, or building defect - one year to notify your no-fault insurer after a crash - 120 days for a dram shop notice after you hire a lawyer, and two years to sue the bar - a 182-day notice and a two-year limit for malpractice - two years for a federal claim about VA care

**How do I know if I was hurt in the city or the township?** The police report names the jurisdiction, and the responding agency is a clue: the Ypsilanti Police Department in the city, the Sheriff’s Office in the township. A mailing address that says “Ypsilanti” does not settle it.

**I was hurt on the EMU campus. Can I sue?** Often, but against the State, in the Court of Claims, before a judge, with a verified notice within six months, or 120 days for a building defect. A crash with a University vehicle and an injury from a defective University building can be claims. A fall on a campus walk outside a building may be barred by governmental immunity.

**I fell on the porch of my rental house near campus. Is the landlord responsible?** Usually, if the porch was in disrepair. A landlord must keep the premises in reasonable repair and fit for their use, and a lease clause waiving that duty is not enforceable. Photograph the defect, report it in writing, and keep earlier complaints.

**I tripped on a broken sidewalk in Ypsilanti Township. Who is responsible?** Usually the township, which has the duty for sidewalks along county roads, with written notice within 120 days. A defect in the road itself is the Road Commission’s.

**I was hurt by someone else’s boat on Ford Lake. Who pays?** The boat’s operator, and the owner if the owner allowed that person to use it; consent is presumed for a family member. The owner’s boat policy usually pays; many homeowner’s policies exclude larger motorboats.

**Can I sue the other driver’s insurance company?** No. In Michigan, the claim is against the driver, and the driver’s insurer defends and pays. Your own no-fault insurer is a separate claim, for your medical bills and lost wages.

**What does an Ypsilanti personal injury attorney charge?** Michigan caps the fee in personal injury cases at one-third of the net recovery, with no fee if there is no recovery. Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted only from a recovery.

**If I was partly at fault, do I still have a claim?** Yes, unless you were more than 50 percent at fault, which bars pain-and-suffering damages. Below that, your recovery is reduced by your share, and at exactly 50 percent you recover half. No-fault benefits are paid regardless of fault.

**Do I need a lawyer whose office is in Ypsilanti?** No. What matters is that the lawyer knows the courts your case will be in, the agencies, insurers, and property owners involved, and will meet you where you are.
