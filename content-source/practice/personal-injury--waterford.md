## Waterford, MI Personal Injury Lawyer

If you were hurt in Waterford Township because someone else was careless, you have a claim under Michigan law, and its deadlines start the day you are hurt. After a crash, your no-fault insurer must have written notice within one year. A road defect claim against the Road Commission for Oakland County needs written notice within 120 days; a defect on M-59, Dixie Highway, or Telegraph needs written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days. A fall on a broken public sidewalk, or in a township building, needs written notice to Waterford Township within 120 days. A claim against a bar or marina that served a drunk driver or boat operator must be filed within two years. A medical malpractice suit, including many claims against nursing homes, requires 182 days’ notice first and must be filed within two years. Most other claims, including boating injuries, allow three years. A personal injury lawyer’s first job is to find out which of those apply to you; the second is to make sure none runs out before your claim is paid.

## What a Waterford personal injury lawyer does

**The first week.** We calendar every deadline and send preservation letters:

- to the at-fault driver’s insurer;
- to a trucking or gravel-hauling company for its driver logs and electronic data, after crashes on Dixie Highway or M-59;
- to MDOT or the Road Commission for Oakland County for signal timing records;
- to the township, the Waterford School District, or SMART for vehicle records;
- to an M-59 or Dixie store, a marina, or an apartment complex’s management, for footage and incident reports, which many overwrite within days;
- to a nursing home or assisted living community for the resident’s chart, incident reports, and staffing records;
- to a bar, restaurant, or marina for its receipts if a drunk driver or boat operator was involved.

We obtain the police report (Waterford Township Police Department reports; some crashes are State Police or Oakland County Sheriff reports, and some lake incidents are handled by the Sheriff’s Marine Division), the Waterford Regional Fire Department EMS run sheet, and the witnesses’ names.

**The first month.** We identify every policy that may pay: your own auto policy for no-fault benefits, household policies, the at-fault party’s liability coverage, your uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage, a property owner’s or management company’s commercial policy, a boat owner’s or homeowner’s policy after a lake injury, umbrella policies, and the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan where no auto policy exists (MCL 500.3172). For a workplace injury, we identify whether a third party, such as a contractor, a vendor’s driver, or an equipment manufacturer, caused it, because the employer itself usually cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). We file your Application for No-Fault Benefits inside the one-year window (MCL 500.3145(1)) and handle the insurer’s requests for statements, records, and examinations (MCL 500.3151). For an older client on Medicare who opted out of PIP medical coverage, we coordinate Medicare’s payment and its lien from the start. You stop talking to adjusters; we do that.

**The treatment period.** The claim’s value is set mostly by the medical record, so we make sure it is complete and the bills are paid while you treat. We collect records from McLaren Oakland and Trinity Health Oakland in Pontiac, Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak for the most serious injuries, and your own physicians and therapists, and document lost wages with your employer, including overtime and shift premiums.

**The demand, the suit, and the resolution.** When your condition has stabilized, we send a demand with the complete record and a valuation. The decision to accept any offer is yours (MRPC 1.2). If the insurer will not pay fair value, suit is filed in the right court:

- **51st District Court** at 5100 Civic Center Drive in Waterford, which serves Waterford Township, for claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims.
- **Sixth Judicial Circuit Court** (Oakland County Circuit Court) at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac for injury suits over $25,000. Most injury cases there go through case evaluation (MCR 2.403) and facilitation before trial.
- **The U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan**, where an out-of-state defendant removes the case.

At settlement we resolve the liens, including health plans, Medicare, Medicaid, and workers’ compensation, before you are paid. Minors’ settlements and wrongful death distributions are approved by the Oakland County Probate Court, on the same Telegraph Road campus in Pontiac, or by the trial court (MCR 2.420; MCL 600.2922(6)). Compensation for physical injury is generally not taxable income (26 USC 104(a)(2)).

## Who the defendant is in a township

Waterford is a charter township, and that changes who you claim against in a way that catches many people out.

- **The roads are not the township’s.** Highland Road (M-59), Dixie Highway, and Telegraph (US-24) are MDOT’s, with written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). Walton, Sashabaw, Crescent Lake, Williams Lake, Pontiac Lake, Elizabeth Lake, Cass Lake, Airport Road, Scott Lake, Andersonville, and most subdivision streets are the Road Commission for Oakland County’s, with written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404).
- **The sidewalks are.** A township is a “municipal corporation” under the immunity act, and must keep the public sidewalks in reasonable repair, including those beside county and state roads (MCL 691.1401(d), 691.1402a). A fall on a broken public sidewalk is a claim against Waterford Township, with the two-inch rule and 120-day notice.
- **So are the township’s buildings and vehicles.** A fall caused by a defect in a township building proceeds under the public building exception (MCL 691.1406); a crash with a township police car, fire truck, or other vehicle under the motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405).
- **The schools, the county, and the State are separate agencies.** The Waterford School District takes its own notice for a building defect. Oakland County International Airport and the county’s parks are Oakland County’s; the State’s lake access sites and recreation areas are the Department of Natural Resources’. An injury on park or recreation land is often subject to immunity or to the recreational land use act, which allows a claim against a landowner who let you use the land for free only for gross negligence or willful and wanton misconduct (MCL 324.73301).

## Injuries on the lakes

Waterford’s lakes produce injuries that are not car cases. No-fault does not apply to a boat, so a boating, jet-ski, tubing, or dock injury is an ordinary negligence claim with a three-year deadline, paid by the boat owner’s or homeowner’s policy. The Marine Safety Act sets the operating rules (MCL 324.80101 and following), and a violation is evidence of negligence. The owner of a boat is liable for injuries caused by its negligent operation by anyone using it with the owner’s consent, and consent is presumed when a spouse, child, parent, or sibling was driving (MCL 324.80157). If the operator had been drinking, the bar, restaurant, or marina that served them may also be liable, with the 120-day notice and two-year limit of the dram shop act (MCL 436.1801). A dock or seawall injury on someone else’s property is a premises claim, decided under *Kandil-Elsayed* (2023) like any other.

## What a Waterford claim is worth, and what reduces it

A claim includes economic damages (medical expenses, lost wages, lost earning capacity, replacement services) and noneconomic damages (pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, disfigurement). There is no cap except in medical malpractice. In an auto case, noneconomic damages require a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)); a boating or premises case has no such threshold. Your share of fault reduces the recovery, and above 50 percent it bars noneconomic damages (MCL 600.2959; in auto cases MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). At exactly 50 percent you recover half. Where more than one party caused the injury, each defendant generally pays only its own share of fault (MCL 600.2956, 600.6304), so every responsible party has to be identified and named. Michigan does not award punitive damages in ordinary injury cases; exemplary damages for humiliation or outrage are available only in narrow circumstances.

In many Waterford cases the practical limit is insurance. The at-fault driver’s liability limit defaults to $250,000/$500,000 unless the driver chose lower limits, down to the $50,000/$100,000 minimum (MCL 500.3009(1)), and some drivers carry nothing; your own underinsured motorist coverage fills the gap; and in a premises, lake, workplace, or nursing home case, the commercial, boat, or homeowner’s policy pays. Where UIM is involved, settling with the at-fault driver without your own insurer’s consent can forfeit the UIM claim. For a worker with a third-party claim, the workers’ compensation insurer has a lien on the recovery (MCL 418.827), and how that lien is negotiated changes what you take home. For an older client, the Medicare lien is negotiated the same way.

## What it costs

Michigan caps contingency fees in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery (MCR 8.121). Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted from the recovery first; the one-third fee is calculated on the remaining amount. You pay nothing up front and nothing if there is no recovery. The agreement is in writing; read how costs (filing fees, records, experts, depositions) are handled if the case is lost, because that is where firms differ. A promise that “you pay nothing unless we win” should say whether that includes costs.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in Waterford?** Three years for most claims. But you have one year to notify your no-fault insurer after a crash, 120 days to notify the Road Commission of a road defect or the township of a sidewalk or building defect, 120 days for a defect on a state highway, two years to sue a bar, and a 182-day notice and two-year limit for malpractice.

**I fell on a broken sidewalk along Dixie Highway. Do I notify MDOT or the township?** Generally the township. MDOT’s duty stops at the roadway; the township must keep the public sidewalk beside it in reasonable repair. It must have known or should have known of the defect for 30 days, a defect under two inches is presumed to be reasonable repair, and written notice must reach the township within 120 days.

**I was hurt on a friend’s boat on Cass Lake. Can I make a claim without suing my friend personally?** The claim is against the operator and the owner, but it is usually paid by their boat or homeowner’s insurance, if the policy covers the boat. No-fault does not apply, so there is no threshold; you have three years.

**What does a Waterford personal injury attorney charge?** Michigan caps the fee in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery, with no fee if there is no recovery. Ask how costs are handled. At ATLAW, case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted only from a recovery.

**Where would my Waterford case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000 in the 51st District Court on Civic Center Drive; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court in Pontiac.

**If I was partly at fault, do I still have a claim?** Yes, unless you were more than 50 percent at fault, which bars pain-and-suffering damages. Below that, your recovery is reduced by your share; at exactly 50 percent you recover half. No-fault benefits are paid regardless of fault.

**I’m on Medicare and opted out of no-fault medical coverage. Who pays my bills after a crash?** Medicare pays first for the care it covers, and it will expect to be repaid from any recovery against the at-fault driver. The claim has to be built with that lien in view.

**Do I need a lawyer whose office is in Waterford?** No. What matters is that the lawyer knows the courts your case will be in, the road agencies, property owners, and insurers involved, and will meet you where you are.

**I searched for a Waterford injury lawyer and found firms in Connecticut and New York. Does that matter?** Yes. Michigan’s no-fault law, its deadlines, and its courts are different from those states’. An injury in Waterford, Michigan needs a lawyer licensed and practicing in Michigan.

**The insurer already offered me money. Should I take it?** Not before you know what the claim is worth and what the release gives up, and not before your UIM insurer has consented if UIM may apply.
