## Personal Injury Law Firm Serving Macomb Township, MI

Macomb Township is a charter township of about 95,000 people north of Hall Road in central Macomb County, one of the fastest-growing communities in Michigan, built largely since the 1990s on a grid of mile roads from 21 Mile to 26 Mile and north-south arterials from Hayes to North Avenue. Hall Road (M-59) forms its southern edge with the Partridge Creek retail corridor just across it; Romeo Plank, Garfield, Hayes, Card, and North Avenue carry its subdivisions to Hall Road and to M-53 and I-94 beyond; and its growth has left it with construction traffic, new signals, roundabouts, and stretches of road that have not kept pace with the houses. In 2024, Macomb Township recorded 1,422 traffic crashes, with 476 people injured and five killed.

Macomb Township is also a township, and that changes an injury claim in two ways. Michigan townships do not own public roads: Hall Road is MDOT’s, and Romeo Plank, Garfield, Hayes, Card, North Avenue, and the mile roads are the Macomb County Department of Roads’, so a qualifying pavement-defect claim generally involves the responsible state or county road agency, with a 120-day notice requirement. Signal and sight-line claims require separate analysis, and township property or sidewalks may involve different duties. And Macomb Township contracts its police services to the Macomb County Sheriff’s Office, so a crash involving a Sheriff’s patrol car may give rise to a claim against Macomb County under the governmental motor-vehicle exception, depending on the circumstances. The township answers for its own fire department, buildings, and parks.

## What is different about a Macomb Township claim

**The roads are not the township’s.** Under Michigan law, townships generally do not have jurisdiction over public roads; Macomb Township’s public county roads are maintained by the Macomb County Department of Roads, and Hall Road (M-59) is maintained by MDOT. A crash caused by a qualifying defect in the traveled roadway may implicate the highway exception to governmental immunity, MCL 691.1402. Signal malfunctions, roundabout design, signage, and sight obstructions require separate legal analysis. For a qualifying defect on a county-maintained road, notice must be directed to the responsible road agency rather than relying on notice to Macomb Township. Where a road contractor’s negligent work-zone traffic control contributed, the contractor may also be liable if it owed and breached an applicable duty.

**The police are the county.** Macomb Township contracts its police services to the Macomb County Sheriff’s Office. A crash involving a Macomb County Sheriff’s patrol car, township fire apparatus, school bus, or Department of Roads truck may fall under the Governmental Tort Liability Act’s motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405). The proper defendant depends on vehicle ownership, operation, and the circumstances of the crash. The township’s buildings and parks are township facilities. Qualifying defects in public buildings may fall under MCL 691.1406, while park injuries are subject to separate governmental-immunity and recreational-use rules.

**Fault is the case.** Most Macomb Township injury crashes are intersection, roundabout, and turnaround crashes, and in a left-turn, roundabout, or signal dispute the other driver’s insurer will assign you 50 percent or more as a matter of routine, which under MCL 500.3135(2)(b) would end your pain-and-suffering claim. What decides it: the police report and diagram, the signal timing data held by MDOT or the Department of Roads, the store and subdivision-entrance cameras along Hall Road and Romeo Plank (retained for days), the vehicles’ event data recorders, and the witnesses in the other lanes. Camera footage and signal-timing data should be requested promptly.

**Coverage.** Macomb Township’s drivers carry more insurance on average than drivers to the south, and the $250,000/$500,000 default limits under MCL 500.3009(1) are common; the Hall Road traffic, however, comes from everywhere. Your own no-fault insurer pays your medical bills and lost wages regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107), or the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan does if no household policy applies (MCL 500.3172, capped at $250,000); the claim against the at-fault driver requires a threshold injury (MCL 500.3135) and is worth no more than the insurance available unless you carry uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage, with the UIM consent-to-settle handled before any settlement. See our [Macomb Township car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents), [no-fault insurance guide](about:blank), and [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**The 41A District and the Sixteenth Circuit.** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims for vehicle damage (MCL 500.3135(3)(e), up to $3,000), are filed in the 41A District Court, Shelby Township division, at 52420 Van Dyke Avenue, which serves Macomb Township, Shelby Township, and Utica. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court at 40 N. Main Street in Mount Clemens. Wrongful death cases may require estate proceedings in Macomb County Probate Court at 40 N. Main Street in Mount Clemens, while the lawsuit proceeds in the court with jurisdiction. Settlements involving minors require court approval under MCR 2.420.

**The hospitals.** Henry Ford Macomb Hospital in Clinton Township, just south of Hall Road, and McLaren Macomb in Mount Clemens are regional Level II trauma centers that treat seriously injured Macomb Township residents; Corewell Health Troy and Henry Ford and McLaren outpatient facilities also provide medical care in the surrounding area. ## Frequently asked questions

**I was hurt because of a pothole in Macomb Township. Do I sue the township?** Usually not for a defect in a county-maintained road. Macomb Township does not own or maintain its public county-road network. Hall Road is MDOT’s, with a 120-day notice requirement for a qualifying highway-defect claim, filed in the Court of Claims. For a county roadway-defect claim, notice must be directed to the responsible road agency. A contractor may also face liability if negligent work-zone conduct contributed to the injury.

**I was hit by a Sheriff’s patrol car in Macomb Township. Who do I claim against?** Macomb County may be responsible because the Macomb County Sheriff’s Office provides policing under contract. The governmental motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405) may apply, depending on the circumstances. Available vehicle video, dispatch records, and other incident evidence should be requested promptly through applicable records procedures.

**The other driver’s insurer says I was 50 percent at fault for a roundabout or left-turn crash. Is my claim over?** Not because an adjuster says so. Under MCL 500.3135(2)(b) you lose the pain-and-suffering claim only if you are found more than 50 percent at fault; at exactly 50 percent you recover half. The police diagram, signal data, cameras, and witnesses decide the number.

**I was hurt by a construction truck or in a work zone on a mile road. Who is responsible?** Potentially the truck driver or motor carrier, a road contractor responsible for negligent work-zone traffic control, or a government road agency if a qualifying highway defect contributed. For a construction-site injury, the responsible employer or third party depends on the circumstances and applicable workers' compensation rules. Work-zone traffic-control violations may be relevant evidence when determining negligence and fault.

**Where would my Macomb Township injury case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, in the 41A District Court’s Shelby Township division on Van Dyke; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court in Mount Clemens.

**Who pays my medical bills after a crash in Macomb Township?** Your own no-fault insurer, regardless of fault, up to your PIP level, after you file an Application for No-Fault Benefits within one year; a household member’s insurer if you have none; the Assigned Claims Plan if no policy applies.

**How much does it cost to hire you?** Nothing up front, and no fee unless we recover money for you. Michigan caps contingency fees in personal injury cases at one-third of the recovery (MCR 8.121).

**How long do I have?** Three years for most injury suits, one year to notify your no-fault insurer in writing, and as little as 120 days for a claim against Macomb County or the township.
