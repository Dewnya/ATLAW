Macomb County’s crashes happen at intersections. The county is laid out on a mile-road grid crossed by wide, fast north-south arterials, Van Dyke, Gratiot, Mound, Groesbeck, Hayes, Schoenherr, and the signals where they meet 8 Mile through 26 Mile and Hall Road are where the county’s serious injuries occur. In 2024, Michigan’s two highest-crash intersections were both along Van Dyke Avenue in Macomb County: 11 Mile Road/I-696 at Van Dyke in Warren/Center Line, with 185 crashes, and 18½ Mile Road at Van Dyke in Sterling Heights, with 162 crashes. County-wide, the Michigan State Police recorded 21,960 crashes, 6,596 people injured, 333 of them seriously, and 70 killed, two more deaths than Oakland County on 14,000 fewer crashes.

## Who pays after a Macomb County crash

**Your no-fault (PIP) claim, against your own insurer.** Under MCL 500.3107, your own policy pays medical expenses up to the PIP level you selected ($50,000 for Medicaid enrollees, $250,000, $500,000, or unlimited), 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years (capped at $7,201 a month for crashes in the year beginning October 1, 2025), replacement services at $20 a day, and attendant care, regardless of fault. If you have no policy, a spouse’s or resident relative’s pays (MCL 500.3114); if none exists, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3172), capped at $250,000 for medical benefits. Written notice must reach the insurer within **one year** (MCL 500.3145(1)); each bill must be paid within 30 days of reasonable proof or carry 12 percent interest (MCL 500.3142). Our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance) covers the application, the benefits, and the denials.

**Your claim against the at-fault driver.** For pain and suffering and losses beyond no-fault, you need a threshold injury under MCL 500.3135: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement. The deadline is three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). Recovery is reduced by your share of fault and barred above 50 percent (MCL 500.3135(2)(b), 600.2959). For vehicle damage, the **mini tort** under 3135(3)(e) recovers up to $3,000 from the at-fault driver.

## What makes a Macomb County crash claim different

**Fault is the whole case.** Most Macomb County injury crashes are intersection crashes, left turns across traffic on Van Dyke and Gratiot, red-light and late-yellow crashes at the Hall Road signals, and rear-end crashes in the stacked left-turn lanes. In a rear-end crash fault is rarely disputed; in a left-turn or traffic-signal crash, fault may be disputed, and the other driver’s insurer may argue that you share responsibility for the collision, which under MCL 500.3135(2)(b) would end your pain-and-suffering claim. What decides it: the police report and the officer’s diagram, any available signal-timing plans, controller records, or other traffic-signal data maintained by the Macomb County Department of Roads or MDOT, the traffic and business cameras along the corridor (the Hall Road retail strip is heavily covered, for a few days), the vehicles’ event data recorders, and the witnesses who were in the other lanes. All of it is perishable. We assess available camera footage, traffic-signal records, and witness evidence promptly after accepting a case and take steps to preserve relevant evidence when appropriate.

**The road is the county’s or the state’s.** Macomb County’s mile roads and most county arterials are maintained by the Macomb County Department of Roads; Van Dyke (M-53), Gratiot (M-3), Hall Road (M-59), Groesbeck (M-97), and I-94 are MDOT’s; city streets belong to the 27 municipalities. A crash caused by a qualifying defect in the improved portion of a public roadway may support a claim against the responsible governmental agency under the highway exception to governmental immunity, MCL 691.1402. Claims based solely on malfunctioning traffic signals, inadequate signage, or obstructed sight lines generally do not fall within that exception, For qualifying highway-defect claims, written notice is generally required within 120 days under MCL 691.1404, including claims against MDOT. Notice involving the state must be filed with the Court of Claims as required by the statute. Identifying the agency from the location is a one-day task and a 120-day deadline.

**Coverage.** Macomb County’s at-fault drivers carry a mix of policies, from the $50,000 per person minimum (MCL 500.3009(1)) to the $250,000/$500,000 default and above, and the county’s uninsured rate sits between Wayne’s and Oakland’s. The practical rule is the same: your own uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage is often the difference between a recovery that reflects the injury and one that reflects the other driver’s policy. Read your declarations page in the first week, and do not settle with the at-fault driver without your insurer’s written consent if a UIM claim is possible. See our [uninsured and underinsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Government and federal vehicles.** Crashes involving SMART buses, school buses, county sheriff and municipal police vehicles, or Department of Roads trucks may support claims under the Governmental Tort Liability Act’s motor vehicle exception, MCL 691.1405, when injuries result from negligent operation of a government-owned vehicle. Certain tort claims against SMART also require written notice within 60 days under MCL 124.419. Certain accidents involving federal employees acting within the scope of their duties at Selfridge Air National Guard Base or the Detroit Arsenal may give rise to claims against the United States under the Federal Tort Claims Act, subject to applicable exceptions. A claimant generally must present an administrative claim to the appropriate federal agency within two years under 28 USC 2401(b) and 2675. A separate six-month lawsuit deadline generally applies after a final written denial. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

**The case will be in the Sixteenth Circuit.** Most Macomb County injury suits over $25,000 are filed in the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court at 40 N. Main Street in Mount Clemens. Mini tort and other small claims go to the district court for the community: the 37th in Warren, the 41A in Sterling Heights and Shelby Township, the 41B in Clinton Township, the 40th in St. Clair Shores, the 39th in Roseville and Fraser, the 38th in Eastpointe, and the 42nd’s divisions in Romeo and New Baltimore. For crashes near Eight Mile Road or the Oakland County boundary, the proper venue is determined under the statutory order of priority in MCL 600.1629, generally beginning with the county where the original injury occurred and where a defendant resides, conducts business, or maintains a registered office.****

**Where you were treated.** Henry Ford Macomb Hospital in Clinton Township and McLaren Macomb in Mount Clemens are the county’s trauma centers; Henry Ford Warren Hospital and the Corewell Health hospitals across the Oakland line in Troy and Royal Oak also serve injured patients from Macomb County. ## What to do after a Macomb County crash

1. **Stop, call 911 if anyone is hurt, and call police.** A report is required for any crash with injury or $1,000 in damage (MCL 257.622). The UD-10 is purchased for $15 at michigan.gov/tcps.
2. **Photograph the intersection**: the signals and their phase if you can, lane markings, the vehicles’ positions, skid marks, and sight lines. Note every business with a camera facing the intersection.
3. **Get witnesses.** In a left-turn or signal dispute the drivers in the other lanes decide the case; get names and numbers before they leave.
4. **Get medical care the same day** and tell the provider it was a car accident.
5. **Report to your own insurer and request the Application for No-Fault Benefits.** File it in the first week.
6. **Read your declarations page** for PIP level, UM/UIM, and collision.
7. **Do not give a recorded statement to the other driver’s insurer**, sign a medical authorization, or sign a release, and do not accept a fault assignment from the other driver’s adjuster.
8. **Stay off social media** about the crash and your activities.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many car accidents happen in Macomb County each year?** 21,960 in 2024, with 6,596 people injured and 70 killed, according to the Michigan State Police. The two highest-crash intersections in Michigan in 2024 were both on Van Dyke: 18½ Mile in Sterling Heights and 11 Mile in Warren.

**The other driver’s insurer says I was 50 percent at fault for a left-turn crash. Is my claim over?** Not because an adjuster says so. Fault is decided by the evidence and, if necessary, a jury. Under MCL 500.3135(2)(b) you lose the pain-and-suffering claim only if you are found more than 50 percent at fault; at exactly 50 percent you recover half. The police report, signal data, cameras, and witnesses decide the number, which is why they have to be gathered immediately.

**I was hurt because a signal was malfunctioning or a sign was missing. Who do I claim against?** The responsible agency depends on the location and the type of alleged defect. MDOT generally maintains state trunklines such as Van Dyke, Gratiot, Hall Road, Groesbeck, and I-94; the Macomb County Department of Roads maintains county roads; and municipalities maintain certain local streets. Governmental immunity generally protects these agencies against claims based solely on malfunctioning traffic signals or missing signs. A qualifying defect in the roadway itself may support a claim under MCL 691.1402, with written notice generally required within 120 days under MCL 691.1404, including for state highway-defect claims.

**Where would my Macomb County car accident case be filed?** Most injury suits go to the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court in Mount Clemens. Mini tort and other claims under $25,000 go to the district court for the community where the crash occurred or the defendant lives.

**Who pays my medical bills?** Your own no-fault insurer, regardless of fault, up to your PIP level, after you file an Application for No-Fault Benefits within one year; a household member’s insurer if you have none; the Assigned Claims Plan if no policy applies.

**How long do I have?** One year to give your no-fault insurer written notice, three years to sue the at-fault driver, and as little as 120 days for a claim against the county or a city.

**Do I need a lawyer for a minor crash in Macomb County?** Not if no one was hurt and the insurer is paying. Call if anyone was injured, fault is disputed, a signal or road defect or a government vehicle was involved, the other driver was uninsured, the police report is wrong, or an insurer has denied or delayed benefits.
