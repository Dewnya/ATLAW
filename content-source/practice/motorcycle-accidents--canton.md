## Where Canton motorcycle crashes happen

**The left turn on Ford Road.** A driver turning left across oncoming traffic, misjudging the speed of a motorcycle or not seeing it at all, is the recurring account in rider cases. Under Michigan law a driver must yield to oncoming traffic before turning left (MCL 257.650). Ford Road’s signals at Haggerty, Lilley, Morton Taylor, Canton Center, and Sheldon, and the dozens of shopping-center driveways between them, produce that crash every day; drivers pulling out of a driveway across the lanes produce its mirror image. MDOT’s boulevard rebuild between Sheldon and Lotz, scheduled for 2028 and 2029, is designed to remove direct left turns, but its work zones will bring lane shifts and grooved pavement in the meantime.

**I-275 and the interchanges.** At the Ford Road and Michigan Avenue interchanges, drivers merging and changing lanes for an exit often fail to check for a motorcycle in the next lane, and rear-end crashes in stop-and-go traffic throw a rider where a car would absorb them. Tractor-trailers bound for the Michigan Avenue distribution centers add blind spots and wind blast. See our [Canton truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/canton).

**Michigan Avenue (US-12) and the county roads.** Trucks turning into distribution-center driveways on Michigan Avenue, and on the county roads, cars pulling out of subdivision entrances on Canton Center, Sheldon, Beck, and Lilley. Western Canton’s two-lane roads toward Superior and Salem townships can add loose gravel at shoulders and driveways and slow-moving equipment.

**The road itself.** A single-vehicle crash caused by a pothole, a broken concrete joint, a pavement drop-off, or gravel is a claim against the agency with jurisdiction under the highway exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1402). In Canton that means two possible defendants, and neither is the township, which has no road jurisdiction:

- MDOT for Ford Road, Michigan Avenue, and I-275: written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **Wayne County** for the mile roads, Sheldon, Canton Center, Lilley, Beck, Haggerty, Lotz, and the other county roads: written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).

A defect that was readily apparent to an ordinary observer for 30 days or more is conclusively presumed known to the agency (MCL 691.1403). Notice to the wrong agency is not notice.

**Bars and restaurants.** 31 percent of Michigan’s 2024 rider deaths involved alcohol. A bar that over-served the driver who hit a rider can be liable under the dram shop act: written notice within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, and suit within two years (MCL 436.1801(3)). See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

## Who pays a Canton rider’s medical bills

**If a car or truck was involved, you have no-fault benefits, but not from your motorcycle insurer.** Under MCL 500.3114(5), your Personal Injury Protection benefits come, in order, from:

1. the insurer of the owner or registrant of the motor vehicle involved;
2. the insurer of that vehicle’s operator;
3. the motor vehicle insurer of the motorcycle’s operator;
4. the motor vehicle insurer of the motorcycle’s owner or registrant.

“Involved” does not require contact; a car that cut you off is involved even if it never touched you. If no insurer in that chain exists, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan pays (MCL 500.3172), with its $250,000 medical cap, unless you owned the motorcycle and it was uninsured (MCL 500.3113(b)).

The benefits are the ones a driver would get under MCL 500.3107: medical expenses up to the PIP limit on the policy that pays, 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year, $20 a day in replacement services, attendant care, and mileage. Written notice is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

The first medical limit is set by a policy you did not choose. Since the 2019 reform, the PIP medical level on the policy first in priority pays first; if the driver who hit you chose $250,000, that policy pays up to $250,000, and a lower-priority policy with more coverage, such as your own car policy, may then pay the rest.

**If no car or truck was involved, no-fault does not apply.** A single-vehicle crash on a pothole, a broken joint, or gravel on a county road produces no PIP claim. Your bills go to your health insurance and any medical coverage you bought on the motorcycle policy, and your injury claim is against whoever caused the hazard. Some summaries say motorcycles are simply “not included” in no-fault, or that benefits are unavailable “in most cases”; that is true of the definition, but a rider hit by a car usually has a full PIP claim.

**Your motorcycle.** Covered by your own collision coverage, or by the mini tort claim against the at-fault driver for up to $3,000 (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)), filed in the 35th District Court in Plymouth if the insurer will not pay it.

## Your claim against the driver who hit you

For pain and suffering and economic losses beyond no-fault, you sue the at-fault driver if your injury is a serious impairment of body function, permanent serious disfigurement, or death (MCL 500.3135). Motorcycle injuries clear that threshold more often than car injuries: fractures, road rash requiring grafts, torn ligaments, [brain injuries](/personal-injury/brain-injury), and [spinal cord injuries](/personal-injury/spinal-cord-injury). You also recover excess economic damages above the PIP limit that applied to you (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)). You have three years to sue (MCL 600.5805(2)); a fatal crash is brought by the estate (see our [Canton wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death/canton)).

**What the claim is worth depends on the other driver’s policy.** Many drivers carry the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)). The recovery then turns on your own **uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage**, which must be on the motorcycle policy; many car policies exclude the insured while riding an owned motorcycle. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Comparative fault is the fight.** Above 50 percent at fault you recover no noneconomic damages; below that your award is reduced by your share, and at exactly 50 percent you recover half (MCL 500.3135(2)(b), 600.2959). Economic damages beyond no-fault are reduced by your share as well. Insurers argue speed, lane position, lane splitting (illegal in Michigan), and “came out of nowhere.” The police report, witnesses, the other driver’s phone records, store and parking-lot cameras along Ford Road, and the rider’s own camera answer it.

## The helmet question

Since 2012, Michigan has let riders 21 and older ride without a helmet if they carry at least $20,000 in first-party medical coverage and have either held a motorcycle endorsement for two years or passed an approved safety course (MCL 257.658). Riders under 21 must wear a helmet. If you met those conditions, riding helmetless was legal, so it was not a traffic violation. In a head-injury case the insurer will argue a helmet would have reduced the injury, which is a damages argument for the jury, not a bar; in a leg or shoulder case it is irrelevant.

## What to do after a Canton motorcycle crash

1. **Get medical care the same day.** Canton has no hospital of its own; many of the seriously injured go to Trinity Health Ann Arbor in Superior Township, a Level I trauma center, or to Trinity Health Livonia, and University of Michigan Health in Ann Arbor treats others.
2. **Get the police report** from Canton Public Safety, or from the State Police on I-275. If a car caused the crash without contact, make sure the report says so.
3. **Identify every vehicle involved**, including the one that never touched you.
4. **Photograph the bike, the scene, the road surface, your gear, and your injuries.** Do not repair or sell the bike.
5. **Read your motorcycle policy for first-party medical, UM/UIM, and collision coverage**, and your car policy’s PIP level.
6. **Apply for no-fault benefits with the right insurer within the first week**, and do not give a recorded statement to the other driver’s insurer.
7. **If a road defect was involved, call within days.** The 120-day notice is already running.

## Frequently asked questions

**A car turned left in front of me on Ford Road and I went down. Do I have a claim?** Yes. A driver turning left must yield to oncoming traffic, and the car is “involved” under no-fault whether or not it touched you, so its owner’s insurer is first in line for your medical bills. Get the plate and witnesses before the car is gone.

**I hit a pothole on Canton Center Road. Do I notify the township?** No. Canton Center and the other county roads are Wayne County’s: written notice within 120 days stating the exact location, the defect, the injury, and the witnesses. Ford Road, Michigan Avenue, and I-275 are MDOT’s, with notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days. Loose gravel is not always a defect an agency is liable for, so the notice should go out while the facts are checked.

**I read that the deadline is two years and the helmet rule is different. Is that right?** Not in Michigan. Many pages about “Canton” motorcycle claims describe Canton, Ohio or Canton, Georgia, where the deadline to sue is two years and the comparative-fault and helmet rules differ. In Michigan you generally have three years to sue the at-fault driver, one year to notify the no-fault insurer, and riders under 21 must wear a helmet.

**Are motorcycles covered by no-fault?** A motorcycle is not a “motor vehicle” under the no-fault act, so a rider who crashes alone has no PIP claim. A rider hit by a car or truck does: from that vehicle’s insurer first, under the priority rule.

**I wasn’t wearing a helmet. Can I still recover?** Yes, if you were 21 or older with the required $20,000 medical coverage and either two years of endorsement or a safety course. The insurer can argue a helmet would have reduced a head injury, which goes to damages, not to whether you have a claim.

**How long do I have?** One year to notify the responsible no-fault insurer, three years to sue the at-fault driver, two years to sue a bar, 120 days for a notice to Wayne County, and six months for a claim against MDOT.
