A motorcycle crash in Westland is governed by a rule many riders learn only after they are hurt: under Michigan no-fault, a motorcycle is not a “motor vehicle.” That definition decides:

- who pays your medical bills, and how much they pay

- how the insurer will argue the helmet question

## Where Westland motorcycle crashes happen

**Hines Drive.** The two-lane parkway through Hines Park follows the Middle Rouge along Westland’s northern edge, through woods, picnic areas, and trail crossings. It is a popular ride. The crashes are:

- **A car crossing the center line** on a curve, or a rider forced off the road by one.
- **A car pulling out** of a park entrance or picnic-area lot into a motorcycle the driver did not see (MCL 257.652).
- **Cyclists and walkers** at the trail crossings, and a rider who swerves to avoid them.
- **Loose surfaces:** sand, silt, leaves, and branches left on the pavement after the low spots flood.
- **Deer** in the park, most often at dawn and dusk.

Hines Drive and Hines Park belong to Wayne County. A crash caused by a defect in the road is a claim against the county with written notice within 120 days. Whether debris left after a flood is a “defect” in the road, and whether the county knew of it, are the questions in those cases.

**Ford Road.** The recurring account in rider cases is a driver turning left across oncoming traffic who misjudges the speed of a motorcycle or never sees it. Under Michigan law, a driver must yield to oncoming traffic before turning left (MCL 257.650). Ford Road produces that crash at:

- the signals at Venoy, Wayne Road, Newburgh, and Hix
- the dozens of store, restaurant, and apartment-complex driveways between them, where a driver turning in crosses the rider’s lane and a driver pulling out must yield to traffic on the road

Rear-end crashes in the stacked turn lanes, and drivers changing lanes into a motorcycle in the next lane (MCL 257.642(1)(a)), add to it.

**Michigan Avenue.** US-12 along the southern edge carries trucks and through traffic. The crashes are trucks making wide or slow turns into driveways, drivers turning left across the rider’s path, and rear-end crashes at the signals. Trucks add blind spots and wind blast. See our [Westland truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/westland).

**The county-road grid and the subdivisions.** Intersection crashes at the signals and four-way stops on Wayne Road, Merriman, Middlebelt, Newburgh, Cherry Hill, Joy, and Warren, and drivers backing out of driveways or pulling from side streets on residential streets.

**The road itself.** A single-vehicle crash caused by a pothole, a broken pavement seam, or a loose surface left by road work is a claim against the agency with jurisdiction, under the highway exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1402). In Westland there are three possible defendants:

- **MDOT** for Ford Road and Michigan Avenue: written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **Wayne County** for Wayne Road, Hines Drive, and other major roads: written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **The City of Westland** for the subdivision streets: written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).

A defect that was readily apparent to an ordinary observer for 30 days or more is conclusively presumed known to the agency (MCL 691.1403). Notice to the wrong agency is not notice.

**Drinking and riding.** 31 percent of Michigan’s 2024 rider deaths involved alcohol, and the State Police recorded 48 alcohol-involved crashes of all kinds in Westland in 2024. A bar or restaurant that over-serves a driver who then hits a rider can be liable under the dram shop act. The notice is due within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, and suit within two years (MCL 436.1801(3)). See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

## Who pays a Westland rider’s medical bills

**If a car or truck was involved, you have no-fault benefits, but not from your motorcycle insurer.** Under MCL 500.3114(5), your Personal Injury Protection benefits come, in order, from:

1. the insurer of the owner or registrant of the motor vehicle involved
2. the insurer of that vehicle’s operator
3. the motor vehicle insurer of the motorcycle’s operator
4. the motor vehicle insurer of the motorcycle’s owner or registrant

Priority follows the vehicles’ insurers, not who was at fault. “Involved” does not require contact; a car that cut you off is involved even if it never touched you. If no insurer in that chain exists, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan pays (MCL 500.3172), with its $250,000 medical cap, unless you owned the motorcycle and it was uninsured (MCL 500.3113(b)). In a crash with an uninsured driver, and with no car policy of your own in the household, the Assigned Claims Plan is often where the claim goes.

The benefits are the ones a driver would get under MCL 500.3107:

- medical expenses, up to the PIP limit on the policy that pays
- 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- $20 a day in replacement services
- attendant care
- mileage

Written notice is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

The first medical limit is set by a policy you did not choose. Since the 2019 reform, the PIP medical level on the policy first in priority pays first; if the driver who hit you chose $250,000, or a lower level, that policy pays up to its limit, and a lower-priority policy with more coverage, such as your own car policy, may then pay the rest. This is why optional first-party medical coverage on a motorcycle policy matters.

**If no car or truck was involved, no-fault does not apply.** Several common Westland crashes produce no PIP claim:

- a slide on flood sand or leaves on Hines Drive
- a deer strike in the park with no other vehicle involved
- a pothole or broken pavement seam on a county road
- a low-speed drop in a parking lot

Your bills go to your health insurance and to any medical coverage you bought on the motorcycle policy. Any injury claim is against whoever caused the hazard, if anyone did.

**Your motorcycle.** Your own collision or comprehensive coverage pays for the bike. The mini tort claim does not cover a motorcycle, because a motorcycle is not a “motor vehicle” under the no-fault act (*Nerat v Swacker*, 150 Mich App 61, 1986).

## Your claim against the driver who hit you

For pain and suffering and economic losses beyond no-fault, you can sue the at-fault driver if your injury meets the threshold: serious impairment of body function, permanent serious disfigurement, or death (MCL 500.3135). A serious impairment is an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life. It does not have to be permanent (*McCormick v Carrier*, 2010).

Motorcycle injuries often clear that threshold. Common examples include:

- fractures
- road rash requiring grafts
- torn ligaments
- [brain injuries](/personal-injury/brain-injury)
- [spinal cord injuries](/personal-injury/spinal-cord-injury)

You also recover excess economic damages above the PIP limit that applied to you, including lost wages above the monthly cap (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)). You have three years to sue (MCL 600.5805(2)). A fatal crash is brought by the estate through the Wayne County Probate Court; see our [wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death).

**What the claim is worth depends on the policies.** Some drivers on Ford Road and Michigan Avenue carry only the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)), and some carry nothing. The recovery above the other driver’s limit turns on your own **uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage**. That coverage must be on the motorcycle policy, because many car policies exclude the insured while riding an owned motorcycle. Your UIM insurer’s written consent is needed before you accept the at-fault driver’s limit. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Comparative fault is the fight.** Michigan’s rule is modified comparative fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(b), 600.2959):

- **More than 50 percent at fault:** you recover no noneconomic damages.
- **50 percent or less:** your award is reduced by your share.
- **Exactly 50 percent:** you recover half.

Economic damages beyond no-fault are reduced by your share as well. Insurers argue speed, lane position, lane splitting (illegal in Michigan), and “came out of nowhere.” The evidence that answers them:

- the Westland Police report and witnesses
- the other driver’s phone records
- store, gas-station, and apartment-complex cameras along Ford Road
- photographs of the curve on Hines Drive, the debris, or the driveway
- the vehicles’ event data recorders
- the rider’s own camera

## The helmet question

Since 2012, Michigan has let riders 21 and older ride without a helmet if they meet two conditions (MCL 257.658):

- They carry at least $20,000 in first-party medical coverage.
- They have either held a motorcycle endorsement for two years or passed an approved safety course.

Riders under 21 must wear a helmet, and a passenger has rules of their own. If you met those conditions, riding without a helmet was legal, so it was not a traffic violation. In a head-injury case, the insurer will argue a helmet would have reduced the injury. That is a damages and causation argument for the jury, not a bar to the claim. In a leg or shoulder case, it is irrelevant.

## What to do after a Westland motorcycle crash

1. **Get medical care the same day.** Garden City Hospital, on Inkster Road just across the city line, and Corewell Health Wayne Hospital have emergency departments. The most serious injuries may go to a trauma center on the State’s list, such as Trinity Health Livonia or Corewell Health Dearborn (Level II), or a Level I center in Detroit.
2. **Get the police report.** Crashes in the city are worked by the Westland Police Department. A crash on the stretch of Hines Drive outside the city may be worked by another agency. If a car caused the crash without making contact, make sure the report says so.
3. **Identify every vehicle involved,** including the one that never touched you.
4. **Photograph everything:** the bike, the scene, the curve, driveway, or intersection, the road surface and any sand or debris, your gear, and your injuries. Do not repair or sell the bike.
5. **Read your policies.** Check your motorcycle policy for first-party medical, UM/UIM, collision, and comprehensive coverage, and your car policy’s PIP level.
6. **Apply for no-fault benefits with the right insurer promptly.** Do not give a recorded statement to the other driver’s insurer.
7. **If a road defect or a bar was involved, call within days.** The 120-day road-defect notice is already running, and the dram shop notice runs from the day you hire a lawyer.

## Frequently asked questions


**A car crossed the center line on a Hines Drive curve and I went off the road. Do I have a claim?** Usually. The car is “involved” under no-fault whether or not it touched you, so its owner’s insurer is first in line for your medical bills. Your claim for pain and suffering is against the driver. Get the plate and witnesses, because the car may not stop.

**I slid on sand on Hines Drive after a flood. Is anyone responsible?** Possibly Wayne County, which owns Hines Drive, if the debris was a defect in the road that it knew or should have known of and did not address in a reasonable time. Written notice must reach the county within 120 days. There is no no-fault claim if no other vehicle was involved. Photograph the sand and the low spot before it is cleared.

**A driver turned left across Ford Road in front of me. Who is at fault?** Usually the driver turning left, who must yield to oncoming traffic, including a motorcycle. Insurers will argue your speed, so store cameras and witnesses matter, and footage is overwritten within days.

**Does the at-fault driver’s insurance pay my medical bills?** Not because the driver was at fault. Under the rider priority rule, the insurer of the car involved pays first, whoever caused the crash. Fault matters for your separate claim for pain and suffering.

**The driver who hit me had no insurance. Who pays my medical bills?** If the car was uninsured, the next insurer in the priority chain pays: the driver’s own insurer, if the driver insures another vehicle; then your own car insurer; then the car insurer of the motorcycle’s owner. If there is none, the Assigned Claims Plan pays, up to $250,000 in medical benefits. Your pain-and-suffering claim depends on uninsured motorist coverage on your motorcycle policy.

**I have my own car insurance. Doesn’t that cover me on the motorcycle?** Not first. If a car was involved, that car’s insurer is first in line. Your own car insurer is third, after the involved vehicle’s owner’s and operator’s insurers. A motorcycle policy itself does not carry Michigan PIP. If no car was involved, there is usually no PIP claim at all.

**I wasn’t wearing a helmet. Can I still recover?** Yes, if three conditions were met: - you were 21 or older - you carried the required $20,000 in medical coverage - you had either two years of endorsement or a safety course. The insurer can argue a helmet would have reduced a head injury. That goes to damages, not to whether you have a claim.

**How long do I have?** - One year to notify the responsible no-fault insurer. - Three years to sue the at-fault driver. - 120 days for a notice to Wayne County or the City. - 120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT. - 120 days for a dram shop notice, and two years to sue a bar.
