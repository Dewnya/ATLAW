## Taylor, MI Wrongful Death Lawyer

When someone dies in Taylor because another person or company was careless, Michigan’s Wrongful Death Act (MCL 600.2922) gives the family a claim, but not in the way many families expect:

- The claim belongs to the estate, not to the spouse or children directly.
- It must be brought by a personal representative appointed by the probate court.
- A judge divides the recovery among family members.
- The deadline depends on what caused the death.

Deaths in Taylor come from many causes:

- a crash on Telegraph, Eureka, I-75, or I-94
- a pedestrian struck crossing a wide road
- a tractor-trailer on the freeway
- a driver leaving a bar
- a death on a job site or in a warehouse
- a hospital error
- a fire or carbon monoxide in a rental home or manufactured home

Some of those deadlines are as short as 120 days. This page explains how the process works for a Taylor family and what to do in the first weeks.

## How a Taylor wrongful death claim works

**The estate.** Only the personal representative can bring the claim (MCL 600.2922(2)).

- **If the person lived in Taylor,** the estate is opened in the Wayne County Probate Court at the Coleman A. Young Municipal Center in downtown Detroit.
- If the person lived elsewhere in Michigan but died in Taylor, the estate is opened in the county where they lived. The suit can still be filed in Wayne County.

If there is a will naming a personal representative, that person petitions for appointment. If not, a family member, usually the spouse or an adult child, petitions under Michigan’s priority rules (MCL 700.3203). The court issues letters of authority, and the personal representative then has standing to hire a lawyer for the estate and file the claim. We file the petition as the first legal step.

**Notice to the family.** Within 30 days after filing suit, the personal representative must notify in writing every person who may share in the recovery (MCL 600.2922(2)–(4)):

- the spouse
- children and other descendants
- parents and grandparents
- brothers and sisters
- the children of the deceased’s spouse
- anyone named in the will

The list is not limited to dependents. A parent, sibling, or adult child who was not financially dependent can still share for the loss of companionship. The Act does not rank these people in a fixed order; the court divides the recovery according to each person’s loss. A person named in the will may share in the recovery but does not bring the claim. Only the personal representative does.

**The court.** The suit is the injury claim the person would have had, brought by the estate against whoever caused the death. Death claims are filed in circuit court, not in the 23rd District Court on Goddard Road, which hears civil cases only up to $25,000.

- **Most deaths in Taylor:** the Third Judicial Circuit Court, also at the Coleman A. Young Municipal Center in Detroit, with a jury drawn from all of Wayne County.
- **An out-of-state trucking company or driver:** the case may be filed in, or removed to, federal court in Detroit.
- **Claims against the State:** the Court of Claims.



**The damages** (MCL 600.2922(6)) include:

- medical and hospital expenses from the injury to the death
- funeral and burial expenses
- the person’s **conscious pain and suffering** between the injury and death
- **loss of financial support** over a working life
- **loss of society and companionship** for each family member

Michigan places no cap on these except in medical malpractice cases. Michigan does not award punitive damages in wrongful death cases. No-fault survivor’s loss benefits, described below, are a separate insurance claim, not part of these damages.

**Comparative fault.** If the person who died was partly at fault, the damages are reduced by that share. If their fault was greater than the combined fault of everyone else, the estate cannot recover noneconomic damages, but it can still recover reduced economic damages (MCL 600.2959). In an auto case, the no-fault act bars noneconomic damages at more than 50 percent fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)).

**The distribution hearing.** After a settlement or verdict, the personal representative proposes a division among the eligible family members, with notice to each. If they agree, the court approves it. If not, the court decides based on each person’s relationship and loss (MCL 600.2922(6)). Fees and costs are approved at the same hearing. Because a judge divides the money, no family member should sign anything with an insurer. A release signed by one relative does not settle the estate’s claim.

## The causes of Taylor wrongful death claims

**Crashes** on Telegraph, Eureka, I-75, I-94, and the mile roads, including pedestrians struck crossing wide roads between bus stops, stores, and apartment complexes.

**The claim against the driver.** A wrongful death claim after a crash does not step outside no-fault. The claim against the at-fault driver still requires a threshold injury, and a death meets it (MCL 500.3135(1)). No-fault benefits are paid alongside the claim. The at-fault driver’s liability insurer pays, subject to the policy limits. Those default to $250,000/$500,000 unless the driver chose lower limits, down to the $50,000/$100,000 minimum (MCL 500.3009(1)). We also check:

- the deceased’s own uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage
- umbrella policies
- the policy of an employer whose driver was working

When the at-fault driver had no insurance, the family’s recovery for loss of companionship depends on UM coverage in the household.

**No-fault survivor’s loss benefits** (MCL 500.3108) are separate. They are paid regardless of fault, by the deceased’s own insurer or by the insurer that would have paid their PIP, for up to three years after the death. They cover:

- the economic support the person provided, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- $20 a day in replacement services
- funeral expenses up to the policy limit, at least $1,750 and up to $5,000 (MCL 500.3107(1)(a))

Survivor’s loss stops at the cap. Support above the cap is part of the wrongful death claim. If the person who died had no auto policy and no household policy applied, survivor’s loss may be paid by the insurer of the vehicle the person was riding in (MCL 500.3114(4)) or by the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3115, 500.3172). Written notice is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)), so do not wait for the lawsuit. See our [Taylor car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents/taylor) and, for pedestrian deaths, our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents).

**Out-of-state drivers and rental cars.** Taylor is next to Detroit Metropolitan Airport, so some fatal crashes involve a rental car or a driver from another state. The family’s survivor’s loss benefits are paid as usual. The claim against the driver can involve the rental company’s coverage, the driver’s home-state policy, and that insurer’s Michigan certification (MCL 500.3163).

**Truck and motorcycle deaths.** A fatal crash with a tractor-trailer on I-75, I-94, or Telegraph, including an underride crash into the side or rear of a trailer, brings in three things:

- the federal trucking rules
- the carrier’s policy of $750,000 or more
- evidence that can be overwritten within days

See our [Taylor truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/taylor). A fatal motorcycle crash follows the rider priority rules; see our [Taylor motorcycle accident page](/personal-injury/motorcycle-accidents/taylor).

**Drunk driving.** The claim is against the driver. Under the dram shop act (MCL 436.1801), it can also be against the bar, restaurant, or store that served a visibly intoxicated person or a minor. The dram shop claim has its own rules:

- Written notice must go to every defendant within 120 days after the family hires a lawyer.
- The intoxicated driver must be named and kept in the suit.
- The suit must be filed within **two years**, not three.

The criminal case proceeds separately, and the family’s claim does not wait for it. See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**Deaths on the job.** Workplace deaths in Taylor happen at Downriver plants, warehouses, airport and rail cargo operations, retail distribution, and construction on the freeways and corridors. When a worker dies on the job, the employer owes workers’ compensation death benefits to dependents, up to 500 weeks plus burial (MCL 418.321, 418.345). The employer generally cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). There are two important exceptions:

- **A third party** whose negligence caused the death can be sued in a wrongful death action. That may be a contractor or subcontractor on the site, a property owner, a trucking company, or the manufacturer of a machine with a missing guard or a defective safety device ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability)).
- **The employer itself** can be sued only under the narrow intentional tort exception. That requires that the employer had actual knowledge an injury was certain to occur and willfully disregarded it (MCL 418.131(1)).

The MIOSHA investigation file is one of the first records requested. See our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).

**Hospital and medical negligence** at Corewell Health Taylor, the hospitals that receive Taylor’s most seriously injured, or the clinics and medical offices around them. This is a wrongful death claim with the malpractice rules attached:

- a 182-day Notice of Intent (MCL 600.2912b)
- an Affidavit of Merit (MCL 600.2912d)
- a two-year period (MCL 600.5805(8), 600.5838a)
- the damages cap (MCL 600.1483)

See our [Taylor medical malpractice page](/personal-injury/medical-malpractice/taylor).

**Housing, nursing homes, and premises.** Taylor has apartment complexes along Telegraph, Eureka, and Northline, and several manufactured-home communities, and about a third of Taylor households rent. Deaths from fires, carbon monoxide, failed smoke detectors, and falls in rental homes, apartments, and manufactured homes follow premises law. For tenants, they also follow MCL 554.139, and for a manufactured-home community, the community owner’s duties for the common areas and utilities. **[ATTORNEY CHECK: manufactured-home community duties]** See our [tenant injury page](/personal-injury/tenant-injuries).

A death from neglect in a nursing home may be ordinary negligence or malpractice, and that changes the deadline. See our [Taylor nursing home abuse page](/personal-injury/nursing-home-abuse/taylor).

**Deaths involving a government agency.** Taylor is a city, so the road defendant can be the State, Wayne County, or the City:

- MDOT owns Telegraph, I-75, and I-94. A road-defect claim requires written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- Wayne County owns Eureka, Allen, and other mile roads.
- **The City of Taylor** owns the subdivision streets.

A claim against Wayne County or the City requires written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404). The City also answers for its sidewalks and buildings, with the same 120-day notice (MCL 691.1402a, 691.1406). Crashes with Taylor Police and Fire vehicles, SMART buses, and school buses fall under the motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405). An individual government employee is liable only for gross negligence (MCL 691.1407(2)).

## Deadlines for Taylor wrongful death claims

| Claim | Deadline | Statute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crash, fall, fire, product, and premises claims | 3 years, generally from the injury that caused the death | MCL 600.5805(2), 600.5827, 600.2922 |
| Medical malpractice | Notice of Intent, then 2 years, with a 6-month discovery rule and 6-year repose | MCL 600.2912b, 600.5805(8), 600.5838a |
| Savings provision for the estate | Up to 2 years after letters of authority, but no more than 3 years after the ordinary period ran | MCL 600.5852 |
| No-fault survivor’s loss benefits | Written notice within 1 year | MCL 500.3145(1) |
| Dram shop claim | Notice within 120 days of hiring a lawyer; suit within 2 years | MCL 436.1801(3) |
| Road defect: Wayne County or City of Taylor | Written notice within 120 days | MCL 691.1404 |
| Sidewalk or building defect: City of Taylor | Written notice within 120 days | MCL 691.1402a, 691.1404, 691.1406 |
| Claim against MDOT or the State | 120 days for a road or building defect; 6 months for other claims, such as a State vehicle | MCL 691.1404, 691.1406, 600.6431 |
| Workers’ compensation death benefits | MCL 418.381(1) | MCL 418.381 |

When the injury and the death happen on the same day, the difference between “from the injury” and “from the death” does not matter. When a person lingers for weeks or months, the ordinary period may already be running before the estate exists, which is what the savings provision is for. It adds only a limited period, and courts apply it strictly. Open the estate and file inside the ordinary deadline.

## What the family should do in the first weeks

1. **Get the death certificate and request the Wayne County Medical Examiner’s report.** Also request the fire department and ambulance run sheets, the police report (Taylor Police for city streets, State Police for the freeways), and the hospital record. The Medical Examiner’s office is in Detroit. If the family’s customs call for prompt burial, tell the Medical Examiner’s office at once, or call us.
2. **Keep everything:** the vehicle, the phone, the helmet, the work gear, the medication bottles. Do not authorize salvage of a vehicle until a lawyer has seen it.
3. **Notify the no-fault insurer in writing** if a vehicle was involved. Survivor’s loss benefits begin with the claim, not with the lawsuit.
4. **Do not give a recorded statement** to any insurer, and do not sign any document, however it is described. An insurer offering to “cover the funeral” in exchange for a signature may be settling the claim for the price of a funeral.
5. **Open the estate.** The probate petition is the first legal step, and the wrongful death suit cannot be filed until letters of authority issue.
6. **Call within days** if the death involved a road, a sidewalk, a government vehicle, a bar, a trucking company, or an employer. Those notice periods are short, and trucking records expire.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who can file a wrongful death lawsuit in Taylor?** Only the personal representative of the estate, who for a Taylor resident is appointed by the Wayne County Probate Court. A spouse, child, or parent can be appointed, but the appointment has to happen before the claim can be filed.

**Who receives the money?** Any of these may share, whether or not they were financially dependent: the spouse, children, descendants, parents, grandparents, siblings, the children of the deceased’s spouse, and anyone named in the will. The probate court divides the recovery according to each person’s loss, after notice to each of them.

**How long do we have?** - Generally three years from the injury that caused the death for crash, fall, and premises claims, with a limited extension for the estate. - Two years, after a 182-day notice, for medical malpractice. - Two years for a dram shop claim. - One year to claim no-fault survivor’s loss benefits. - 120 days to notify Wayne County or the City of a defect.

**Does the three years run from the date of death?** Not necessarily. For most claims, the period generally runs from the injury that caused the death, not from the death itself. The savings provision gives the estate a limited extension. Pages that say “three years from the date of death” can leave a family short when the person lingered before dying.

**My husband died in a crash. Are survivor’s loss benefits part of the lawsuit?** No. Survivor’s loss is paid by the no-fault insurer regardless of fault, up to a monthly cap, for up to three years. The wrongful death claim against the at-fault driver is separate. It covers what survivor’s loss does not, including support above the cap and the family’s loss of companionship.

**A bad road contributed to my son’s crash. Do we sue the City of Taylor?** It depends on the road. Telegraph, I-75, and I-94 belong to MDOT, with written notice of a road defect filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days. Eureka, Allen, and other mile roads belong to Wayne County, and the subdivision streets to the City, each with written notice within 120 days.

**The driver who killed my mother had no insurance. Is there anything we can recover?** Yes. The no-fault system pays survivor’s loss benefits and funeral expenses regardless, through the family’s insurer, the insurer of the vehicle the person was riding in, or the Assigned Claims Plan. The claim for loss of companionship depends on uninsured motorist coverage in the household. Without it, that claim is worth what the driver can personally pay.

**Can we get punitive damages?** Michigan does not award punitive damages in wrongful death cases. The family recovers the losses the Act lists, including loss of companionship, which has no cap outside medical malpractice.

**Is there a lawsuit if the driver was charged with a crime?** Yes. The criminal case and the family’s claim are separate. The family does not have to wait for the criminal case, and the civil deadlines keep running.
