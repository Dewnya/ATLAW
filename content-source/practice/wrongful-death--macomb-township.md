## Macomb Township, MI Wrongful Death Lawyer

When someone dies in Macomb Township because another person or company was careless, Michigan’s Wrongful Death Act (MCL 600.2922) gives the family a claim, but not in the way many families expect:

- **The claim belongs to the estate,** not to the spouse or children directly.
- **It must be brought by a personal representative** appointed by the probate court.
- **The recovery is divided among family members by a judge.**
- **The deadline depends on what caused the death.** Some of those deadlines are 120 days.

The cause might be a crash on Hall Road or a two-lane mile road, a dump truck at a construction entrance, a driver who had been drinking, a fall on a construction site, a hospital error, or a nursing home. ## How a Macomb Township wrongful death claim works

**The estate.** Only the personal representative can bring the claim (MCL 600.2922(2)). Where the person lived in Macomb Township, the estate is opened in the Macomb County Probate Court, at 40 North Main Street in Mount Clemens. The steps are:

1. If there is a will naming a personal representative, that person petitions for appointment.
2. If not, a family member, usually the spouse or an adult child, petitions under Michigan’s priority rules (MCL 700.3203).
3. The court issues letters of authority.
4. The personal representative then has standing to hire a lawyer for the estate and file.

We file the petition as the first legal step.

**Notice to the family.** Within 30 days after filing suit, the personal representative must notify in writing every person who may share in the recovery (MCL 600.2922(2)–(4)):

- the spouse
- children and descendants
- parents and grandparents
- brothers and sisters
- the children of the deceased’s spouse
- anyone named in the will

The list is not limited to dependents. A parent, sibling, or adult child who was not financially dependent can still share for the loss of companionship. The Act does not rank them in a fixed order; the court divides the recovery according to each person’s loss. A person named in the will may share in the recovery but does not bring the claim. Only the personal representative does.

**The court.** The suit is the injury claim the person would have had, brought by the estate against whoever caused the death. Death claims are filed in circuit court, not the 41A District Court, which hears civil cases only up to $25,000.

- **For a death in Macomb Township,** that is usually the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court (Macomb County Circuit Court) at 40 North Main Street in Mount Clemens.
- **A case against an out-of-state trucking company or driver** may be filed in, or removed to, federal court in Detroit.
- **Claims against the State** go to the Court of Claims.

**The damages** (MCL 600.2922(6)):

- medical and hospital expenses from the injury to the death
- funeral and burial expenses
- the person’s **conscious pain and suffering** between the injury and death
- **loss of financial support** over a working life
- **loss of society and companionship** for each family member

Michigan places no cap on these except in medical malpractice and product liability cases (MCL 600.1483, 600.2946a). Michigan does not award punitive damages in wrongful death cases. No-fault survivor’s loss benefits, described below, are a separate insurance claim, not part of these damages.

**Comparative fault.** If the person who died was partly at fault, the damages are reduced by that share. If their fault was greater than the combined fault of everyone else, the estate cannot recover noneconomic damages, but can still recover reduced economic damages (MCL 600.2959). Fault above 50 percent does not end the whole claim. In an auto case, the no-fault act bars noneconomic damages at more than 50 percent fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)).

**The distribution hearing.** After a settlement or verdict, the personal representative proposes a division among the eligible family members, with notice to each. If they agree, the court approves it. If not, the court decides based on each person’s relationship and loss (MCL 600.2922(6)). Fees and costs are approved at the same hearing. Because a judge divides the money, no family member should sign anything with an insurer; a release signed by one relative does not settle the estate’s claim.

## The causes of Macomb Township wrongful death claims

**Crashes on Hall Road and the mile roads.** Macomb Township’s fatal crashes happen on Hall Road and on county roads where some stretches are two lanes with ditches and no shoulders. A wrongful death claim after a crash does not step outside no-fault. The claim against the at-fault driver requires a threshold injury, and death meets the threshold (MCL 500.3135(1)). No-fault benefits are paid alongside it.

The at-fault driver’s liability insurer pays, subject to policy limits that default to $250,000/$500,000 unless the driver chose lower limits, down to the $50,000/$100,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(1), (5)). We also check the deceased’s own underinsured motorist coverage, umbrella policies, and the policy of an employer whose driver was working.

**No-fault survivor’s loss benefits** (MCL 500.3108) are paid regardless of fault by the deceased’s own insurer, or the insurer that would have paid their PIP, for up to three years after the death:

- the economic support the person provided, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- $20 a day in replacement services
- funeral expenses up to the policy limit, at least $1,750 and up to $5,000 (MCL 500.3107(1)(a))

Survivor’s loss stops at the cap. Support above it is part of the wrongful death claim. Written notice is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)); do not wait for the lawsuit. See our [Macomb Township car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents/macomb-township).

**Truck and motorcycle deaths.** A fatal crash with a tractor-trailer on Hall Road, or with a dump truck or concrete mixer on a mile road, brings the federal trucking rules, the carrier’s commercial policy, and evidence that can be overwritten within days. On a construction job, the hauler, the supplier, and the builder may all be responsible. See our [Macomb Township truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/macomb-township).

A fatal motorcycle crash follows the rider priority rules, and a rider’s death in a crash with no other vehicle, such as a deer strike, may carry no survivor’s loss claim at all. See our [Macomb Township motorcycle accident page](/personal-injury/motorcycle-accidents/macomb-township).

**Drunk driving.** The family may have claims against the driver and, under the dram shop act (MCL 436.1801), the bar, restaurant, or store that served a visibly intoxicated person or a minor. The dram shop claim has its own rules:

- Written notice must go to every defendant within 120 days after the family hires a lawyer.
- The intoxicated driver must be named and kept in the suit.
- The suit must be filed within **two years**, not three.

The criminal case proceeds separately, and the family’s claim does not wait for it. See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**Deaths on the job, including construction sites.** Macomb Township has been under construction for a generation, and construction sites are where many workplace deaths happen. Falls, trench collapses, struck-by and caught-between injuries, and electrocutions are the usual causes. When a worker dies on the job, the employer owes workers’ compensation death benefits to dependents, up to 500 weeks plus burial (MCL 418.321, 418.345), and generally cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). There are two important openings:

- **A third party** whose negligence caused the death can be sued in a wrongful death action. That includes a general contractor responsible for common work areas, another subcontractor, a property owner or developer, a trucking company, or the manufacturer of a machine, ladder, scaffold, or tool with a defect ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability)).
- **The employer itself** can be sued only under the narrow intentional tort exception, where the employer had actual knowledge that an injury was certain to occur and willfully disregarded it (MCL 418.131(1)).

The MIOSHA investigation file is one of the first records requested. See our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).

**Hospital and medical negligence.** A death after care at Henry Ford Macomb Hospital in Clinton Township, McLaren Macomb in Mount Clemens, or the medical offices along Hall Road is a wrongful death claim with the malpractice rules attached:

- a 182-day Notice of Intent (MCL 600.2912b)
- an Affidavit of Merit (MCL 600.2912d)
- a two-year period (MCL 600.5805(8), 600.5838a)
- the cap (MCL 600.1483)

See our [medical malpractice page](/personal-injury/medical-malpractice).

**Nursing homes, homes, and premises.** A death from neglect in a nursing home, assisted living, or memory care community may be ordinary negligence or malpractice, which changes the deadline. See our [nursing home abuse page](/personal-injury/nursing-home-abuse). Deaths in homes and on other property follow premises law:

- falls, fires, and carbon monoxide in apartments, condominiums, and homes, and for tenants, MCL 554.139
- a child’s drowning in a subdivision pond or an unfenced pool, where the association, landowner, or homeowner may be responsible
- a child’s death on a construction site near the family’s home

See our [Macomb Township personal injury page](/personal-injury/personal-injury/macomb-township).

**Deaths involving a government agency.** In a township, the road defendant is never the township:

- **Hall Road** belongs to MDOT, with written notice of a road defect filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **Every other public road** belongs to the Macomb County Department of Roads, with written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404).
- **Sheriff’s patrol cars:** the township’s police services are provided by the Macomb County Sheriff’s Office, so a death in a crash with a patrol car is a claim against the county, under the motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405).
- **The township** answers for its own fire vehicles and buildings, and its public sidewalks and paths along the roads (MCL 691.1402a, 691.1405, 691.1406), with written notice within 120 days for a building or sidewalk defect.

An individual government employee is liable only for gross negligence (MCL 691.1407(2)).

## Deadlines for Macomb Township wrongful death claims

| Claim | Deadline | Statute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crash, fall, fire, construction, product, and premises claims | 3 years, generally from the injury that caused the death | MCL 600.5805(2), 600.5827, 600.2922 |
| Medical malpractice | Notice of Intent, then 2 years, with a 6-month discovery rule and 6-year repose | MCL 600.2912b, 600.5805(8), 600.5838a |
| Savings provision for the estate | Up to 2 years after letters of authority, but no more than 3 years after the ordinary period ran | MCL 600.5852 |
| No-fault survivor’s loss benefits | Written notice within 1 year | MCL 500.3145(1) |
| Dram shop claim | Notice within 120 days of hiring a lawyer; suit within 2 years | MCL 436.1801(3) |
| Road defect: Macomb County Department of Roads | Written notice within 120 days | MCL 691.1404 |
| Building or sidewalk defect: the responsible agency | Written notice within 120 days | MCL 691.1402a, 691.1404, 691.1406 |
| Claim against MDOT (Hall Road) or the State | 120 days for a road defect; 6 months for other claims, such as a State vehicle | MCL 691.1404, 600.6431 |
| Workers’ compensation death benefits | Claim within 2 years of the death | MCL 418.381 |

When the injury and the death happen on the same day, the difference between “from the injury” and “from the death” does not matter. When a person lingers for weeks or months, the ordinary period may already be running before the estate exists, which is what the savings provision is for. It adds only a limited period, and courts apply it strictly. Open the estate and file inside the ordinary deadline.

## What the family should do in the first weeks

1. **Get the death certificate and request the Macomb County Medical Examiner’s report,** along with the Sheriff’s crash or incident report, the Medstar ambulance run sheet, and the hospital record. If the family’s customs call for prompt burial, tell the Medical Examiner’s office at once, or call us.
2. **Keep everything:** the vehicle, the phone, the helmet, the work gear, the medication bottles. Do not authorize salvage of a vehicle until a lawyer has seen it.
3. **Notify the no-fault insurer in writing** if a vehicle was involved. Survivor’s loss benefits begin on the claim, not on the lawsuit.
4. **Do not give a recorded statement** to any insurer, and do not sign any document, however it is described. An insurer offering to “cover the funeral” in exchange for a signature may be settling the claim for the price of a funeral.
5. **Open the estate.** The probate petition is the first legal step; the wrongful death suit cannot be filed until letters issue.
6. **Call within days** if the death involved a road, a government vehicle, a bar, a trucking company, a construction site, or an employer. Those notice periods are short, and the trucking and site records expire.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who can file a wrongful death lawsuit for a Macomb Township resident?** Only the personal representative of the estate, appointed by the Macomb County Probate Court in Mount Clemens. A spouse, child, or parent can be appointed, but must be appointed before the claim can be filed.

**Who receives the money?** The spouse, children, descendants, parents, grandparents, siblings, the children of the deceased’s spouse, and anyone named in the will may share, whether or not they were financially dependent. The probate court divides the recovery according to each person’s loss, after notice to each of them.

**How long do we have?** - Generally three years from the injury that caused the death for crash, fall, construction, and premises claims, with a limited extension for the estate. - Two years, with a 182-day notice, for medical malpractice. - Two years for a dram shop claim. - One year to claim no-fault survivor’s loss benefits. - 120 days to notify the Department of Roads of a road defect.

**My husband died in a crash. Are survivor’s loss benefits part of the lawsuit?** No. Survivor’s loss is paid by the no-fault insurer regardless of fault, up to a monthly cap, for up to three years. The wrongful death claim against the at-fault driver is separate. It covers the losses survivor’s loss does not, including support above the cap and the family’s loss of companionship.

**If my son was partly at fault, do we lose the whole claim?** No. His share of fault reduces the damages. If it was greater than everyone else’s combined, the estate cannot recover noneconomic damages such as loss of companionship, but it can still recover reduced economic damages, such as lost support and funeral costs.

**My father died on a construction site. Can we sue anyone besides his employer?** Often, yes. The employer usually pays workers’ compensation death benefits and cannot be sued. But a general contractor, another subcontractor, the property owner, or the maker of defective equipment can be sued in a wrongful death action, if their negligence caused the death.

**A bad road contributed to my daughter’s crash. Do we sue Macomb Township?** No. Townships do not own roads. A county road claim goes to the Macomb County Department of Roads, with written notice within 120 days. A Hall Road claim goes to MDOT, with written notice of the defect filed in the Court of Claims within 120 days.

**Can we get punitive damages?** No. Michigan does not award punitive damages in wrongful death cases. The family recovers the losses the Act lists, including the deceased’s conscious pain and suffering and the family’s loss of companionship, which have no cap outside medical malpractice and product liability cases.

**Is there a lawsuit if the driver was charged with a crime?** Yes. The criminal case and the family’s claim are separate. The family does not have to wait for the criminal case, and the civil deadlines keep running.

**Our address is “Macomb, MI.” Is this the right page?** Yes. “Macomb, MI” is the mailing address for most of Macomb Township. The estate is opened in the Macomb County Probate Court, and a suit over a death in Macomb County is usually filed in the Macomb County Circuit Court in Mount Clemens.
