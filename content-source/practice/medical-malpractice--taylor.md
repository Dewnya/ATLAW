## Taylor, MI Medical Malpractice Lawyer

A medical malpractice claim in Michigan has rules no other injury claim has:

- a two-year deadline instead of three
- a written Notice of Intent served on each provider at least 182 days before suit
- a sworn Affidavit of Merit from a qualified expert, filed with the complaint
- strict rules on who that expert can be
- a cap on pain-and-suffering damages

A case that misses any one of these can be lost before its merits are heard.

Those centers include:

A federally funded clinic is sued under federal law, on a different clock and in a different court.

## The rules, by the numbers

| Rule | Figure | Statute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deadline to file suit | 2 years from the act or omission | MCL 600.5805(8) |
| Discovery rule | 6 months from when the claim was or should have been discovered; never more than 6 years after the act (exceptions for retained objects, fraud, reproductive injury) | MCL 600.5838a(2) |
| Notice of Intent before suit | 182 days; the provider must respond in writing within 154 days | MCL 600.2912b |
| Affidavit of Merit | Filed with the complaint | MCL 600.2912d |
| Defendant’s Affidavit of Meritorious Defense | Within 91 days after the answer | MCL 600.2912e |
| Expert qualification | Same specialty; same board certification if the defendant is certified; majority of professional time in that specialty in the year before | MCL 600.2169 |
| 2026 cap on noneconomic damages | $596,400 (standard) / $1,065,000 (catastrophic) | MCL 600.1483 |
| Child injured before age 8 | Deadline runs to the 10th birthday | MCL 600.5851(7) |
| Federally funded provider | Administrative claim (SF-95) within 2 years; suit after denial or 6 months | 28 USC 2401(b), 2675 |

Economic damages are not capped. These include medical bills, lost earnings, and the cost of future care. MCL 600.2912d requires the Affidavit of Merit, and MCL 600.2169 decides who is qualified to sign it.

## What malpractice is, and is not

A bad outcome is not malpractice. Malpractice is a bad outcome caused by a provider who failed to do what a reasonably competent provider in the same specialty would have done in the same circumstances (MCL 600.2912a). You must prove four things:

- the standard of care
- a breach of it
- that the breach more likely than not caused the injury
- damages

Michigan requires proof that the patient would have had a better outcome. A lost chance of a better outcome is compensable only where that chance was greater than 50 percent (MCL 600.2912a(2)). Before anything is filed, a qualified physician’s written review of the records decides whether a case exists. Many do not, and we say so.

## Taylor’s providers, and who answers for each

**Corewell Health Taylor Hospital.** This is the city’s hospital, with its employed physicians, nurses, and staff. Health-system names and ownership have changed several times here. The correct legal entity for the Notice of Intent has to be identified two ways:

- from current corporate filings
- from the name the hospital used at the time of care, not from the sign or the letterhead

Care given under the Oakwood or Beaumont name may involve an entity name that no longer appears on the building. Claims against the hospital and its employed providers are filed in the Third Judicial Circuit Court in Detroit.

**Emergency physicians and other contracted groups.** Many hospitals staff their emergency departments, anesthesia, and radiology through separate physician groups. A 2024 recruitment listing for Corewell Health Taylor’s emergency department was posted by a national emergency-medicine staffing company. Where a patient came to the hospital for care and reasonably believed the doctor worked for it, Michigan law can hold the hospital responsible under ostensible agency (*Grewe v Mt Clemens General Hospital*, 1978).

**Transfers to a higher-level center.** Strokes, cardiac emergencies, major trauma, and complex surgical cases may be transferred from Corewell Health Taylor to Corewell Health Dearborn, Corewell Health Trenton, or one of the Detroit Level I centers. A transfer case can involve two hospitals, two sets of physicians, and the transport between them. The question is which delay or decision caused the harm:

- the first hospital’s diagnosis
- the decision and timing of the transfer
- the transport
- the care after arrival

All of these hospitals are in Wayne County, so the same circuit court hears the claims.

**Medical offices, urgent care, surgery centers, and imaging centers.** These include the primary-care and specialty practices along Telegraph and around the hospital, urgent care clinics, and outpatient surgery, dialysis, and imaging centers in and around the city. **[VERIFY: outpatient centers in the city]** Frequent claims include:

- a delayed diagnosis in an office practice
- a missed fracture or infection at urgent care
- a surgical complication at an outpatient center

The employer of each provider who touched the chart must be identified. A health-system-employed physician and an independent physician in the same building are different defendants with different insurers. Michigan rules apply to all of them.

**Federally funded community health centers.** Some Downriver residents get their primary, prenatal, and dental care at community health centers that receive federal Health Center Program funding. Providers at a funded center can be deemed federal employees for malpractice purposes. **[VERIFY: deemed status of centers serving Taylor, by name, at intake; do not name on the page]** A claim against one follows the Federal Tort Claims Act, not Michigan procedure:

- an administrative claim on Standard Form 95 to the Department of Health and Human Services within two years (28 USC 2401(b))
- no Notice of Intent
- suit in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan, tried to a judge without a jury

Identifying a deemed clinic early is the difference between a timely claim and none.

**Veterans.** Veterans treated at the John D. Dingell VA Medical Center in Detroit or a VA outpatient clinic have an FTCA claim on the same path. They may also qualify for disability compensation under 38 USC 1151 where the injury was caused by VA carelessness, negligence, or error, or by an event that was not reasonably foreseeable. See our [VA benefit claims page](about:blank).

**EMS.** Claims against EMS personnel, and against the agency that employs them, public or private, including a private ambulance company handling a transfer between hospitals, are limited by the Emergency Medical Services Act to gross negligence or willful misconduct (MCL 333.20965(1)).

**Nursing homes and assisted living.** Taylor has three Medicare-certified nursing homes, as well as assisted living and adult foster care homes. Which one it is decides whether the Notice of Intent, the two-year period, and the cap apply. A nurse’s wound-care decision is malpractice. Leaving a resident unsupervised against the care plan is usually negligence. See our [Taylor nursing home abuse page](/personal-injury/nursing-home-abuse/taylor).

**Workplace clinics.** Occupational clinics and employer medical departments that treat injured workers at the Downriver plants and warehouses are a separate case. A claim against a provider employed by your own employer may be barred by workers’ compensation exclusivity, while an outside clinic or physician can be sued for malpractice. See our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).

## How a Taylor malpractice case proceeds

1. **The records,** including the electronic audit trail, are requested from every provider promptly, under whatever name the provider used at the time of care.
2. **Expert review** is done in writing by a physician in the defendant’s specialty, before anything is filed.
3. **Identify the defendants and the forum:** a hospital entity, physician group, surgery center, urgent care operator, or independent practice goes to the Third Circuit for Wayne County care; a deemed clinic or the VA goes through the SF-95 and the federal court; and EMS is subject to the EMS Act’s gross-negligence standard. Serving a Notice of Intent on a federal provider while the federal two-year clock runs can lose the case.
4. **The Notice of Intent** (MCL 600.2912b) is served at least 182 days before suit. A timely notice pauses the two-year period during the notice period (MCL 600.5856(c)). If the provider does not respond within 154 days, suit may be filed then.
5. **The complaint and Affidavit of Merit** are filed together (MCL 600.2912d). The defense then files its own affidavit (MCL 600.2912e).
6. **Discovery, case evaluation, and facilitation** follow. Cases that do not settle go to trial before a jury in the Third Circuit at the Coleman A. Young Municipal Center in Detroit. Two to four years is typical.
7. **Resolution:** minors’ and estates’ settlements are approved by the court or the Wayne County Probate Court, and Medicare, Medicaid, and health plan liens are resolved before distribution.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to sue a doctor or hospital in Taylor?** Two years from the malpractice, with a six-month discovery rule capped at six years. The Notice of Intent must be served 182 days before suit, and the expert review comes first, so call well inside the first year.

**Is the Notice of Intent “six months” or 182 days?** 182 days, which is not the same as six calendar months. A notice served on the last day of a six-month count can be late, so count the days.

**My care was at “Beaumont Taylor.” Does the name change matter?** It can. The Notice of Intent and the complaint must name the legal entity responsible for your care at the time it was given. After a merger or rebranding, that may not be the name on the building today. We identify it from the records and the corporate filings.

**I was seen in the Corewell Health Taylor emergency room and transferred. Which hospital is responsible?** It depends on where the decision or delay that caused the harm happened. That could be the first hospital’s diagnosis and decision to transfer, the transport, or the care after arrival. Each provider is a separate defendant with its own Notice of Intent.

**The emergency doctor didn’t work for the hospital. Can I still claim against the hospital?** Possibly. Where a patient came to the hospital for care and reasonably believed the doctor was the hospital’s, Michigan law can hold the hospital responsible under ostensible agency. The physician group is named too.

**I’m a veteran and was treated at the VA. Is that different?** Yes. A claim against VA care follows the Federal Tort Claims Act: an administrative claim within two years, no Notice of Intent, and suit in federal court without a jury. A veteran may also qualify for disability compensation for an injury caused by VA error or an unforeseeable event.

**Is there a cap on what I can recover?** Only on pain-and-suffering damages. In 2026, the cap is $596,400 in the standard case and $1,065,000 for the catastrophic permanent injuries the statute lists, adjusted each year. Medical bills, lost earnings, and future care are not capped.

**My child was injured at birth. How long do we have?** A child injured before age eight has until the tenth birthday to file. The parents’ own claims are subject to the ordinary two-year period, so the family should not wait.
