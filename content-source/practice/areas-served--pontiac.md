## Personal Injury Law Firm Serving Pontiac, MI

Pontiac is Oakland County’s seat, with an estimated 62,528 residents in 2024, according to the U.S. Census Bureau. The city is home to the 50th District Court, while the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court, Oakland County Probate Court, Sheriff’s Office, and other county government facilities are located in Pontiac. These institutions play important roles in the county’s legal system, although the proper court for any injury lawsuit depends on the type of claim, jurisdiction, and venue requirements. Its roads are the state’s and the county’s: the Woodward Loop and Woodward, Telegraph and Dixie Highway, M-59, and the arterials between them, Perry, Baldwin, Joslyn, Opdyke, Walton, Square Lake, and Orchard Lake. According to Michigan Traffic Crash Facts, Pontiac recorded 1,967 traffic crashes in 2024, resulting in 660 people injured and three fatalities. The total included 474 injury crashes and three fatal crashes.

## Where Pontiac injuries happen

**The Woodward Loop and Woodward (M-1).** The one-way loop around downtown, Wide Track Drive, built in the 1960s to move traffic around the city center at speed, and the Woodward corridor south toward Bloomfield. The loop’s merges, its wide one-way lanes, and its pedestrian crossings between downtown and the neighborhoods produce rear-end, sideswipe, and pedestrian crashes; MDOT’s plan to convert the loop to two-way traffic is meant to address exactly that. Woodward and the loop are MDOT’s.

**Telegraph Road (US-24) and Dixie Highway.** Telegraph Road connects Pontiac with surrounding Oakland County communities and provides access to government facilities, businesses, and residential areas. Its intersections with Orchard Lake Road, Square Lake Road, Elizabeth Lake Road, and M-59 may be relevant in collisions involving turning vehicles, commercial traffic, or pedestrians. Dixie Highway continues northwest toward Waterford. See our [Pontiac truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents).

**Perry, Baldwin, Joslyn, Opdyke, Walton, Square Lake, and Orchard Lake.** The city’s arterials, carrying neighborhood traffic to M-59, Woodward, and Telegraph and to I-75 at the city’s north edge. Intersection crashes at the signals, and the pedestrian and bicycle crashes of a city where many residents walk or ride.

**The employers.** United Wholesale Mortgage’s headquarters campus on South Boulevard, one of the county’s largest private employers; the Amazon fulfillment center on the Silverdome site; GM’s Pontiac operations; the M1 Concourse; the hospitals and the county government. Commuter crashes at the campus and warehouse driveways, parking-lot crashes, and workplace injuries inside, where workers’ compensation is generally the exclusive remedy against the employer (MCL 418.131) but a third-party claim survives. See our [workers’ compensation](/personal-injury/workers-compensation) and [product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability) pages.

**The neighborhoods.** Pontiac’s housing is older than its neighbors’, with a renter share above half, large apartment complexes, Pontiac Housing Commission properties, and scattered vacant structures. Falls on deteriorated stairs and porches, rental-housing injuries under MCL 554.139, fires and carbon monoxide, lead exposure from pre-1978 paint, dog bites under MCL 287.351, and sidewalk injuries under the two-inch rule (MCL 691.1402a). Where the landlord is the Pontiac Housing Commission, governmental immunity and special notice rules must be considered. MCL 125.663 may require written notice within 60 days, depending on the housing commission’s governing arrangements. See our [tenant injury](/personal-injury/tenant-injuries), [premises liability](/personal-injury/premises-liability), and [dog bite](/personal-injury/dog-bites) pages. **[CONFIRM: renter share, ACS]**

## What is different about a Pontiac claim

**The other driver is more likely to have no insurance.** For your claim: eligible no-fault benefits may cover medical expenses and lost wages, subject to applicable coverage, exclusions, and priority rules (MCL 500.3107), or the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan may provide benefits when no other applicable insurer is available, generally subject to a $250,000 PIP medical limit; your pain-and-suffering claim against an uninsured driver is worth what the driver can personally pay unless you carry **uninsured motorist coverage**; and a driver’s policy with the selected minimum $50,000 bodily-injury limit (MCL 500.3009(1)) is often the ceiling unless you carry underinsured motorist coverage. We check every policy in the household before we value a Pontiac claim. See our [Pontiac car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents), [no-fault insurance guide](/resources/michigan-no-fault-guide), and [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**The police are the county.** Pontiac has contracted its police services to the Oakland County Sheriff’s Office since 2011, and its fire protection to the Waterford Regional Fire Department. A crash involving a Sheriff’s patrol car or government fire apparatus may fall under the Governmental Tort Liability Act’s motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405). The proper defendant depends on the vehicle’s ownership, operation, and the circumstances of the crash. Claims involving City of Pontiac vehicles, SMART buses, and Pontiac School District buses may also implicate the governmental motor vehicle exception. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

**The roads are the state’s and the county’s.** Woodward, the Loop, Telegraph, Dixie, and M-59 are MDOT’s; Perry, Baldwin, Joslyn, Opdyke, Walton, Square Lake, and Orchard Lake are the Road Commission for Oakland County’s or the City of Pontiac’s by segment. A crash caused by a defect in the traveled roadway may implicate the highway exception to governmental immunity, MCL 691.1402. Signal malfunctions, sight obstructions, and design-related allegations require separate analysis.

**The courts are in Pontiac.** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims for vehicle damage (MCL 500.3135(3)(e), up to $3,000), are filed in the 50th District Court at 70 N. Saginaw Street. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac. Wrongful death cases may require an estate opened in Oakland County Probate Court, while the lawsuit proceeds in the court with jurisdiction. Settlements involving minors require court approval under MCR 2.420. A Pontiac injury case may be filed in Pontiac or another court with proper jurisdiction and venue; jury selection depends on the court and type of case.

**The hospitals.** McLaren Oakland on North Perry and Trinity Health Oakland (formerly St. Joseph Mercy Oakland) on Woodward are both in the city and treat most of Pontiac’s injured; Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak is the county’s Level I trauma center. We obtain the full record from each, including the Waterford Regional Fire Department EMS run sheet.

## Frequently asked questions

**The driver who hit me in Pontiac had no insurance. What now?** Your own no-fault insurer, a household member’s, or the Assigned Claims Plan pays your medical bills and lost wages regardless. Your pain-and-suffering claim depends on whether you carry uninsured motorist coverage; without it, the claim is worth what the uninsured driver can personally pay. Check your declarations page today.

**I was hit by a Sheriff’s patrol car or a fire truck in Pontiac. Who do I claim against?** Pontiac receives police services from the Oakland County Sheriff’s Office and fire services from the Waterford Regional Fire Department. A collision involving one of these government vehicles may fall under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity, but the proper defendant depends on the facts.

**I live in Pontiac Housing Commission housing and was hurt by a dangerous condition. Do I have a claim?** Possibly, under the public building exception to governmental immunity, which requires written notice to the Commission within 120 days. A private landlord is an ordinary defendant under MCL 554.139 with a three-year deadline.

**Where would my Pontiac injury case be filed?** In Pontiac. Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, in the 50th District Court on Saginaw Street; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court on Telegraph; wrongful death estates in the Oakland County Probate Court on the same campus.

**I was hurt because of a signal problem or road defect on Woodward or Telegraph. Who is responsible?** MDOT, in the Court of Claims with a six-month notice. On Perry, Baldwin, Joslyn, or the other arterials, the Road Commission or the city, with written notice within 120 days. A lawyer can confirm the agency from the location in a day.

**Who pays my medical bills after a crash in Pontiac?** Your own no-fault insurer, regardless of fault, up to your PIP level, after you file an Application for No-Fault Benefits within one year; a household member’s insurer if you have none; the Assigned Claims Plan if no policy applies.

**How much does it cost to hire you?** Nothing up front, and no fee unless we recover money for you. Michigan caps contingency fees in personal injury cases at one-third of the recovery (MCR 8.121).

**How long do I have?** Three years for most injury suits, one year to notify your no-fault insurer in writing, and as little as 120 days for a claim against the city, the county, or the Housing Commission.
