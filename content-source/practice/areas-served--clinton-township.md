## Personal Injury Law Firm Serving Clinton Township, MI

Clinton Township is Michigan’s largest township, about 100,000 people between Fourteen Mile and Hall Road, Groesbeck and the Harrison Township line, wrapped around the county seat at Mount Clemens. It holds Henry Ford Macomb Hospital, Macomb Community College’s Center Campus, the Partridge Creek mall, and the eastern half of the Hall Road retail corridor. Major roads such as Gratiot Avenue, Hall Road, and Groesbeck Highway carry significant commuter and commercial traffic, creating opportunities for intersection, turning, pedestrian, and rear-end collisions. Macomb County recorded 21,960 crashes, 6,596 injuries, and 70 deaths in 2024, according to the Michigan State Police, more deaths than Oakland County on 14,000 fewer crashes. Clinton Township itself recorded 1,907 crashes, 749 people injured, and eight fatalities in 2024.

Clinton Township is also a township, and that changes an injury claim in a way no competing page mentions: Michigan townships do not own public roads. Gratiot, Hall Road, Groesbeck, and I-94 are MDOT’s; Garfield, Romeo Plank, Harper, Moravian, Cass, and the mile roads are the Macomb County Department of Roads’. A claim for a signal, sight-line, or pavement defect goes to the state or the county, with a six-month or 120-day notice, never to the township. The township’s own police, fire, buildings, and parks are a different matter.

## What is different about a Clinton Township claim

**The roads are not the township’s.** Under Michigan law, townships have no road jurisdiction; Clinton Township’s roads belong to the Macomb County Department of Roads or to MDOT. A qualifying road-defect claim generally proceeds against the government agency responsible for the roadway, subject to Michigan’s governmental immunity exceptions. Under MCL 691.1404, written notice of a qualifying highway defect generally must be provided within 120 days, including for state highway claims. Traffic-signal malfunctions and sight-line conditions do not automatically fall within the highway exception. The responsible agency must be identified from the location and nature of the alleged defect.

**The township’s own agencies.** Claims involving Clinton Township vehicles, public buildings, or other government property are evaluated under Michigan’s governmental immunity laws. MCL 691.1405 addresses injuries caused by negligent operation of government-owned motor vehicles, while MCL 691.1406 provides a limited exception for qualifying dangerous conditions in public buildings. Accidents involving SMART buses or public school buses may raise additional governmental liability questions. Claims involving public parks depend on the location, the alleged hazard, and whether a recognized exception applies.

**Fault is the case.** Most Clinton Township injury crashes are intersection crashes on Gratiot, Hall Road, and the arterials, and in a left-turn or signal dispute the other driver’s insurer will assign you 50 percent or more as a matter of routine, which under MCL 500.3135(2)(b) would end your pain-and-suffering claim. What decides it: the police report and diagram, the signal timing data held by MDOT or the Department of Roads, the corridor and store cameras (dense along Hall Road and Gratiot, retained for days), the vehicles’ event data recorders, and the witnesses in the other lanes.

**Coverage.** Your own no-fault insurer pays your medical bills and lost wages regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107), or the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan does if no household policy applies (MCL 500.3172, capped at $250,000); the claim against the at-fault driver requires a threshold injury (MCL 500.3135) and is worth no more than the insurance available, which on Gratiot ranges from the $50,000 minimum (MCL 500.3009(1)) to full coverage, unless you carry uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage. See our [Clinton Township car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents), [no-fault insurance guide](about:blank), and [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**The 41B District Court, Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court, and Macomb County Probate Court.** Civil cases involving $25,000 or less generally fall within district court jurisdiction under MCL 600.8301. The 41B District Court is located at 22380 Starks Drive, Clinton Township, MI 48036. Civil injury actions exceeding the district court’s jurisdictional limit are generally handled in circuit court, subject to applicable venue rules. The Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court is located at 40 N. Main Street, Mount Clemens, MI 48043. The Macomb County Probate Court is also located at 40 N. Main Street, on the fifth floor. Probate proceedings associated with wrongful death claims and protective arrangements for certain settlements may require additional court filings and approvals.

**The hospitals.** Henry Ford Macomb Hospital, located at 15855 19 Mile Road in Clinton Township, provides emergency services and is identified by Henry Ford Health as a Level II Trauma Center. McLaren Macomb in nearby Mount Clemens also serves patients in the region. Medical records, emergency department documentation, diagnostic imaging, and ambulance reports can be important evidence when evaluating accident-related injuries. Medical and EMS records may help establish the injuries, treatment received, and relationship between an accident and the resulting medical care.

## Frequently asked questions

**I was hurt because of a bad signal or pothole in Clinton Township. Do I sue the township?** No. Clinton Township is a township and does not own its roads. Garfield, Romeo Plank, Harper, Moravian, Cass, and the mile roads are Macomb County’s, with written notice due within 120 days; Gratiot, Hall Road, Groesbeck, and I-94 are MDOT’s, with a six-month notice to the Court of Claims. A notice sent to the township is sent to the wrong defendant.

**The other driver’s insurer says I was 50 percent at fault for a left-turn crash on Gratiot. Is my claim over?** Not because an adjuster says so. Under MCL 500.3135(2)(b) you lose the pain-and-suffering claim only if you are found more than 50 percent at fault; at exactly 50 percent you recover half. The police diagram, signal data, cameras, and witnesses decide the number.

**I fell at Partridge Creek or a Hall Road store. Who is responsible?** Responsibility depends on who possessed or controlled the property, what caused the fall, and whether reasonable care was taken to address the hazard. Depending on the circumstances, a property owner, business operator, or another responsible party may be involved. In Kandil-Elsayed v F & E Oil, Inc. (2023), the Michigan Supreme Court clarified that an open and obvious hazard does not automatically eliminate a land possessor’s duty to an invitee. The hazard’s obviousness may still affect whether reasonable care was exercised and whether the injured person shares fault. Photographs, incident reports, witness information, and available security footage can be important.

**My parent was injured in a Clinton Township nursing home. Is that malpractice or negligence?** It depends on the circumstances. Some nursing home injuries may involve ordinary negligence, such as unsafe property conditions, while others may involve medical malpractice when the claim concerns professional healthcare judgment, treatment, or clinical care. Falls, unsafe transfers, pressure injuries, and medication errors require careful evaluation because the legal classification can affect the applicable deadline and filing requirements. Michigan generally provides a three-year period for ordinary negligence and a two-year period for medical malpractice, subject to applicable exceptions. These claims should be reviewed promptly.

**Where would my Clinton Township injury case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, in the 41B District Court on Starks Drive; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court in Mount Clemens; wrongful death estates in the Macomb County Probate Court on Dunham Road.

**Who pays my medical bills after a crash in Clinton Township?** Your own no-fault insurer, regardless of fault, up to your PIP level, after you file an Application for No-Fault Benefits within one year; a household member’s insurer if you have none; the Assigned Claims Plan if no policy applies.

**How much does it cost to hire you?** Nothing up front, and no fee unless we recover money for you. Michigan caps contingency fees in personal injury cases at one-third of the recovery (MCR 8.121).

**How long do I have?** Three years for most injury suits, one year to notify your no-fault insurer in writing, and as little as 120 days for a claim against Macomb County or the township.
