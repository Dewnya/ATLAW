## Pontiac, MI Car Accident Lawyer

A car crash in Pontiac is a Michigan no-fault claim shaped by a road system built to move traffic around the city rather than through it. The Woodward Loop, Wide Track Drive, circles downtown one way, several lanes wide, with merges, weaves, and pedestrian crossings between downtown and the neighborhoods. Woodward runs south from the Loop toward Bloomfield. Telegraph (US-24) runs along the city’s west side past the county government complex and the circuit court. M-59 crosses the city as Huron Street, past McLaren Oakland and downtown, and continues east as a freeway toward Auburn Hills and I-75. Between them, Perry, Baldwin, Joslyn, Opdyke, Walton, Square Lake, Orchard Lake, and the city streets carry neighborhood traffic, and many residents walk, bike, or ride the bus. A Pontiac crash claim usually turns on who had the right of way, how much insurance is on the other side, and, when the road played a part, whether it was the City’s, the county’s, or the State’s. ## Where Pontiac crashes happen

**The Woodward Loop.** Traffic on the Loop moves one way at speed, and drivers enter and leave it across several lanes to reach downtown streets, Woodward south, Huron Street, and the neighborhoods. The crashes are lane-change and sideswipe crashes at the weaves, rear-ends at the signals and merges, crashes with drivers who enter the Loop going the wrong way or miss their exit and cut across lanes, and pedestrian crashes at the crossings. The Loop is MDOT’s. MDOT plans to convert it to a two-way avenue, with construction scheduled to begin in 2028, and to convert Pike Street and Cass Avenue to two-way traffic in fall 2026. **[VERIFY against MDOT]** Changes in traffic patterns and work zones produce their own crashes: drivers following old patterns, confusing lane shifts, and crashes with work vehicles. A work-zone crash can involve the contractor as well as a driver.

**Telegraph Road (US-24).** A multi-lane highway along the city’s west side past the county complex, the courts, and the retail centers, to its end at Dixie Highway. Left-turn and red-light crashes at Orchard Lake, Elizabeth Lake, Square Lake, and Huron, rear-ends in the turn lanes, and driveway crashes at the shopping centers. Telegraph is MDOT’s.

**M-59 (Huron Street) and the freeway.** Huron Street carries M-59 through the city past McLaren Oakland and downtown; east of the city M-59 becomes a freeway to Auburn Hills and I-75. Intersection crashes at Telegraph, Perry, Opdyke, and Baldwin; freeway merge and rear-end crashes; and trucks serving the industrial and warehouse sites on the city’s east side. MDOT plans to close Huron Street between the two sides of the Loop from spring to fall 2027 for the conversion work. **[VERIFY]** See our [Pontiac truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/pontiac).

**The arterials and city streets.** Perry, Baldwin, Joslyn, Opdyke, Walton, Square Lake, Orchard Lake, Saginaw, and the neighborhood streets: signal crashes, crashes at stop signs where a driver rolls through, and crashes with pedestrians and cyclists where sidewalks, crosswalks, and lighting are uneven. See our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents) and [bicycle accident page](/personal-injury/bicycle-accidents).

**Whose road it is: the State, the county, or the City.** Pontiac is a city, and unlike a township, it owns most of its streets. Woodward, the Loop, Telegraph, M-59, and I-75 are MDOT’s; a road-defect claim against MDOT goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). City streets, which include many of the arterials and the neighborhood streets, are the City of Pontiac’s, and some roads are the Road Commission for Oakland County’s by segment; a claim against the City or the Road Commission takes written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404). A pothole or other defect in the road can make the agency a defendant alongside the other driver; on State and county roads, signals, signs, and sight obstructions generally fall outside the highway exception (*Nawrocki v Macomb County Road Commission*, Mich 2000); the notice has to go to the agency that actually has jurisdiction over that segment, or it is no notice. The City also has the duty to keep public sidewalks in repair, which matters for a pedestrian who falls (MCL 691.1402a); see our [sidewalk injury page](/personal-injury/sidewalk-injuries).

## Fault is the case

In a rear-end crash, fault is rarely disputed. At a Loop weave, a Telegraph left turn, or a neighborhood stop sign, it is disputed often, and the other driver’s insurer may try to assign you half or more of the fault. That number matters: if you are found more than 50 percent at fault, you lose your claim for pain and suffering, and below that your recovery is reduced by your share (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). At exactly 50 percent you recover half. The evidence that decides it is perishable:

- the Oakland County Sheriff’s Office crash report and the deputy’s diagram, or the State Police report on the freeway;
- **signal timing data** from MDOT, the City, or the Road Commission;
- store, gas-station, business, and residential cameras, many overwritten within days;
- the vehicles’ **event data recorders**, which store speed, braking, and throttle in the seconds before impact;
- the other driver’s phone records, for a distracted-driving claim (see our [distracted driving page](/personal-injury/distracted-driving-accidents));
- witnesses, including bus riders and pedestrians who saw the crash.

We move promptly to request the camera footage, signal data, and vehicle data.

## Who pays after a Pontiac crash

**Your no-fault (PIP) claim.** Your own auto insurer pays your medical expenses up to the PIP level you chose, 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year, replacement services at $20 a day, and attendant care, regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107). If you have no policy, a resident relative’s policy pays (MCL 500.3114); if none exists, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan does (MCL 500.3172), capped at $250,000 for medical benefits. A pedestrian or cyclist hit by a car with no household policy claims from the insurer of the car’s owner or driver first (MCL 500.3115). The insurer must receive written notice within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)) and must pay each bill within 30 days of reasonable proof or owe 12 percent interest (MCL 500.3142). Since the 2019 reform, PIP medical coverage is no longer unlimited unless you chose it, so the level on your declarations page matters. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance) and [Michigan no-fault guide](about:blank).

**If you had no insurance.** A person who owned a car and was required to insure it but did not is generally barred from PIP benefits for their own injuries (MCL 500.3113(b)), and a driver who was operating their own uninsured vehicle cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, whoever was at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(c)). A passenger or pedestrian with no policy of their own is not barred; the Assigned Claims Plan may be the payer.

**Your claim against the at-fault driver.** For pain and suffering, you need a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment is an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life; it does not have to be permanent (MCL 500.3135(5); *McCormick v Carrier*, 2010). You can also recover lost wages beyond the three years or above the monthly cap, and medical bills above your chosen PIP level (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)). The suit must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The mini tort recovers up to $3,000 in vehicle damage not covered by your policy (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)), filed in the 50th District Court if the at-fault driver’s insurer will not pay it.

**When the other driver has no insurance, or the minimum.** Some drivers on Pontiac’s roads carry the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)). Your pain-and-suffering claim against an uninsured driver is worth what that driver can personally pay unless you carry **uninsured motorist coverage**; against a minimum-limits driver, $50,000 is often the ceiling unless you carry **underinsured motorist coverage**. Most UIM policies require your insurer’s written consent before you accept the at-fault driver’s limit; settling without it can forfeit the UIM claim. A hit-and-run is usually a UM claim, with notice terms in the policy that are often short. We read every policy in the household, including umbrella coverage, before valuing the claim. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Drunk drivers and dram shop.** Oakland County recorded 871 alcohol-involved crashes in 2024. A crash caused by a drunk driver leaving a downtown Pontiac bar or restaurant adds a claim against the business that served a visibly intoxicated person or a minor. Written notice must go to the retailer within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, the drunk driver must be named and kept in the suit, and the suit must be filed within two years, not three (MCL 436.1801(3), (4)). See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**Sheriff’s cars, fire trucks, and buses.** Pontiac’s police are the Oakland County Sheriff’s Office, which has policed the city under contract since 2011, so a crash with a patrol car in Pontiac is a claim against Oakland County, not the City. Fire protection in Pontiac is provided by the Waterford Regional Fire Department under contract with the City. The motor vehicle exception runs against the agency that owns the vehicle (MCL 691.1405), so a crash with a fire engine is usually a claim against Waterford Township rather than the City. Dash camera and dispatch records are overwritten on short schedules; we request them by FOIA in the first week. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

## What to do after a Pontiac crash

1. **Call 911 and get the police report.** Michigan requires a report for any crash with injury or $1,000 in damage (MCL 257.622). Crashes in the city are worked by the Oakland County Sheriff’s Office; freeway crashes may be worked by the Michigan State Police. **[VERIFY: report request process]**
2. **Photograph the vehicles, plates, the other driver’s license and insurance card, the signals, and the lane markings.** If the other driver cannot show proof of insurance, photograph the plate and the vehicle and tell the deputy. Note every business with a camera facing the scene; footage is kept for days, not weeks.
3. **Get medical care the same day.** McLaren Oakland and Trinity Health Oakland, both in Pontiac, are Level II trauma centers on the State’s list; the most serious injuries may go to Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak, the county’s Level I adult trauma center.
4. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** within the first week. If you have no policy, a lawyer can identify the insurer next in priority or the Assigned Claims Plan.
5. **Read your declarations page** for PIP level, UM/UIM, umbrella, and collision coverage.
6. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the other driver’s insurer, sign a release, or accept a policy-limits offer before your UIM insurer consents.
7. **Call a lawyer within days** if fault is disputed, the other driver is uninsured, or the road played a part; the camera footage and signal data are the first evidence lost, and the 120-day notice to the City or the Road Commission is already running.

## The courts

The state courts that hear most Pontiac crash cases sit in the city. Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, are filed in the 50th District Court at 70 N. Saginaw Street. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court at 1200 N. Telegraph Road, with a jury drawn from all of Oakland County. A case against an out-of-state driver or carrier may be filed in or removed to the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan. Defect claims against MDOT go to the Court of Claims. A fatal crash is brought by the estate through the Oakland County Probate Court on the Telegraph Road campus; see our [Pontiac wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death).

## Frequently asked questions

**The driver who hit me in Pontiac had no insurance. What now?** Your own no-fault insurer, a household member’s, or the Assigned Claims Plan pays your medical bills and lost wages regardless of fault. Your pain-and-suffering claim depends on whether you carry uninsured motorist coverage; without it, the claim is worth what the uninsured driver can personally pay. Check your declarations page today.

**I was hit by a Sheriff’s patrol car in Pontiac. Who do I claim against?** Oakland County, because the Sheriff’s Office polices Pontiac under contract. The claim proceeds under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity, and the patrol car’s video and dispatch records should be requested right away.

**A pothole or a bad signal caused my crash. Do I file against the City of Pontiac?** It depends on the road. A defect in the roadbed on Woodward, the Loop, Telegraph, M-59, or I-75 is MDOT’s, with notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days. A signal problem on a State or county road generally falls outside the highway exception. Most other streets are the City’s, and some are the Road Commission’s, each with written notice within 120 days.

**Which Pontiac intersections have the most crashes?** No Pontiac intersection was among Oakland County’s ten highest-crash locations in the State Police data for 2024. **[CONFIRM: data: Pontiac’s highest-crash locations from the MTCF query tool]**

**The Loop is being rebuilt. Who is responsible for a crash in a work zone?** The driver who caused it, and possibly the contractor if signs, barriers, or lane shifts were inadequate. MDOT itself is liable only for a defect in the roadbed, with notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days.

**I was walking and was hit by a car. Who pays my medical bills?** Your own household’s auto policy if you have one; if not, the insurer of the car’s owner or driver; if none, the Assigned Claims Plan. A pedestrian has the same pain-and-suffering claim as a driver, subject to the threshold.

**Where would my Pontiac case be filed?** In Pontiac: mini tort and claims up to $25,000 in the 50th District Court on Saginaw Street; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court on Telegraph.

**How long do I have?** One year to give your no-fault insurer written notice, three years to sue the at-fault driver, two years to sue a bar, 120 days for a notice to the City or the Road Commission, and 120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT..
