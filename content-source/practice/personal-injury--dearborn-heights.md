## Dearborn Heights, MI Personal Injury Lawyer

If you were hurt in Dearborn Heights because someone else was careless, you have a claim under Michigan law, and its deadlines start the day you are hurt:

- **After a crash,** your no-fault insurer must have written notice within one year.
- A road defect needs written notice within 120 days to Wayne County or the City of Dearborn Heights. A defect on Telegraph, Ford Road, or Michigan Avenue needs written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days.
- **A sidewalk defect, or an injury in a City or school building,** needs written notice to that agency within 120 days.
- **A sewer backup** that injured you or damaged your home needs written notice to the City within 45 days of when you discovered it.
- **A claim against a bar or restaurant** that served a drunk driver needs written notice within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, and suit within two years.
- **A medical malpractice suit,** including many claims against nursing homes, requires 182 days’ notice first and must be filed within two years.
- **Most other claims,** including falls in stores and apartment complexes, electrical injuries, dog bites, and crashes, allow three years.

A personal injury lawyer’s first job is to find out which of those deadlines apply to you. The second is to make sure none runs out before your claim is paid.

## Who the defendant is in Dearborn Heights

Dearborn Heights is a city with its own police and fire departments, sidewalks, sewers, and buildings, but many of its major roads, and Hines Park, belong to Wayne County. Its housing includes apartment complexes along Telegraph, Ford Road, and Van Born, its retail runs in strips along Ford Road and Telegraph, and its north end has restaurants, markets, and places of worship along Warren Avenue. Each of those changes who you claim against.

- **Landlords and apartment complexes.** A landlord must keep the premises and common areas fit for their intended use and in reasonable repair, and comply with health and safety laws (MCL 554.139). That covers stairs, railings, walkways, parking lots, lighting, wiring, and snow and ice the landlord controls. Prior complaints, work orders, and incident reports are the evidence. A tenant’s guest may have a premises claim against the landlord as well. See our [tenant injury page](/personal-injury/tenant-injuries).
- **Stores, restaurants, and parking lots.** A fall in a store, a restaurant, a market, or a strip-center parking lot on Ford Road, Telegraph, or Warren is a premises claim against the owner, the tenant, or both, depending on who controlled the area. Snow and ice are often handled by a contractor, which can be a separate defendant. Since *Kandil-Elsayed v F & E Oil* (2023), an “open and obvious” hazard such as visible ice goes to comparative fault rather than ending the claim. The deadline is three years. See our [slip and fall](/personal-injury/slip-and-fall) and [premises liability](/personal-injury/premises-liability) pages.
- **Places of worship and community halls.** A fall or other injury at a mosque, church, or hall is a premises claim against the organization that owns or controls the building. Michigan has no general charitable immunity, and the organization’s liability policy usually pays. A person attending services is generally a licensee, so the organization must warn of dangers it knows about but need not inspect for unknown ones (Stitt v Holland Abundant Life Fellowship, Mich 2000).
- **Negligent security.** A tenant or customer assaulted at a complex, a store, or a parking lot may have a claim against the owner if it knew of a specific risk and failed to respond reasonably.
- **The bar that served the drunk driver.** A bar, restaurant, or store that served a visibly intoxicated person, or a minor, is liable for the injuries that person causes (MCL 436.1801). The claim needs written notice within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, the drunk driver named and kept in the suit, and suit within two years. The State Police recorded 34 alcohol-involved crashes in Dearborn Heights in 2024. See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).
- **The roads are split three ways.** MDOT: Telegraph, Ford Road, and Michigan Avenue, with written notice of a road defect filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). Wayne County: Warren, Hines Drive, and other major roads. The City of Dearborn Heights: the local streets.
- A claim against the county or the City requires written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404), and the notice must go to the agency that has jurisdiction.
- **Sidewalks: the City, and possibly the adjacent owner.** The City must keep public sidewalks in reasonable repair (MCL 691.1402a). A fall on a broken public sidewalk is a claim against the City, with three limits: the City must have known, or should have known, of the defect at least 30 days before the injury; a defect of less than two inches is presumed not to be actionable; and written notice is due within 120 days.
- Even where an ordinance puts sidewalk repair on the adjacent owner, that duty runs to the City and does not by itself make the owner liable to a pedestrian (Levendoski v Geisenhaver, Mich 1965). See our [sidewalk injury page](/personal-injury/sidewalk-injuries).
- **Sewer backups and flooded basements.** Heavy storms have flooded basements in parts of Dearborn Heights. When a sewer system the City owns or operates backs up because of a defect the City knew or should have known of and failed to fix in a reasonable time, the City can be liable for the property damage and any physical injury, such as a fall or illness, that the backup caused (MCL 691.1416–691.1417). The claimant must give the City written notice within 45 days after discovering the damage (MCL 691.1419). That deadline is far shorter than any other on this page, and missing it ends the claim.
- **Hines Park.** Hines Park and Hines Drive belong to Wayne County. A crash caused by a defect in Hines Drive is a highway claim against the county, with 120-day notice. An injury in the park itself, on a trail or at a picnic area, generally falls outside every exception to governmental immunity unless it happened in a public building.
- **City buildings and schools.** An injury caused by a dangerous or defective condition in a public building falls under the public building exception, which requires that the agency knew, or should have known, of the defect and failed to fix it within a reasonable time, and written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1406). That covers City buildings and the buildings of the school districts that serve parts of the city, including Crestwood, Dearborn Heights School District 7, Westwood, and Dearborn.
- **Police and fire are the City’s.** A crash with a Dearborn Heights Police car or fire truck is a claim against the City, under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405). EMS treatment claims are limited to gross negligence or willful misconduct (MCL 333.20965).
- **Buses.** A crash with a SMART bus on Telegraph, Ford Road, or Michigan Avenue, or with a school bus, is a claim under the motor vehicle exception. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).
- **Dog owners.** An owner is liable for a bite when the person bitten was lawfully present and did not provoke the dog (MCL 287.351). See our [dog bite page](/personal-injury/dog-bites).
- **Nursing homes.** A claim against a nursing home may be ordinary negligence, with a three-year deadline, or medical malpractice, with two years and a 182-day notice, depending on whether the conduct involved professional judgment. See our [Dearborn Heights nursing home abuse page](/personal-injury/nursing-home-abuse/dearborn-heights).

## Electrical injuries

An electrical injury can be a shock, a burn, a fall from a ladder after a shock, or a fire. The burns are often deeper than they look, and nerve, heart, and brain effects can show up later, so the medical record from the first visit onward matters. Who you claim against depends on where the current came from:

- **A downed or low-hanging power line after a storm, or a line too close to a building, tree, or ladder.** The utility that owns the line has a duty of reasonable care in how it builds, maintains, and repairs it, and its outage and repair records are the evidence. Stay away from any downed line and call 911 and the utility.
- **Faulty wiring, outlets, or panels in a rental.** The landlord’s duty to keep the premises in reasonable repair and to comply with health and safety laws covers the wiring (MCL 554.139). Prior complaints and work orders are the evidence.
- **Work by an electrician or contractor.** A contractor who installed or repaired the wiring is responsible for its own negligent work, and its liability policy pays.
- **A defective appliance, tool, or cord.** A product liability claim against the manufacturer or seller (MCL 600.2945–600.2949a). Keep the product. See our [product liability page](/personal-injury/product-liability).
- **On the job.** An electrician, lineworker, or other worker hurt by electricity is covered by workers’ compensation, and may have a separate suit against a third party who is not the employer, such as a general contractor, another trade, the property owner, or a manufacturer (MCL 418.131, 418.827). See our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).

Most electrical injury claims allow three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). A claim against a government agency for a defective public building needs 120 days’ notice.

## When the other driver has no insurance, or not enough

Some drivers on Telegraph, Ford Road, and Michigan Avenue carry only the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)), and some carry nothing.

- **Against an uninsured driver,** the claim for pain and suffering depends on your own **uninsured motorist coverage**. Without it, the claim is worth what the driver can personally pay. A hit-and-run is usually a UM claim, with short notice terms in the policy.
- **Against an underinsured driver,** your own **underinsured motorist coverage** pays the difference up to your UIM limit, if you follow the steps in order:
 1. The at-fault driver’s insurer offers its limit.
 2. You notify your own insurer and ask for its written consent to settle.
 3. Only then do you accept the limit and sign the release.
 4. The UIM claim then proceeds.

Accepting without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under most policies.

If you have no auto insurance of your own and no household policy, your no-fault benefits may come from the Assigned Claims Plan, with its $250,000 medical cap. Two rules cut the other way:

- An owner who was required to insure a car and did not is generally barred from PIP benefits (MCL 500.3113(b)).
- A driver who was operating their own uninsured vehicle cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, whoever was at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(c)).

See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

## What a Dearborn Heights claim is worth, and what reduces it

A claim includes two kinds of damages:

- **Economic damages:** medical expenses, lost wages, lost earning capacity, and replacement services.
- **Noneconomic damages:** pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, and disfigurement.

There is no cap except in medical malpractice. In an auto case, noneconomic damages require a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment does not have to be permanent (*McCormick v Carrier*, 2010). Vehicle damage up to $3,000 can be recovered from the at-fault driver through the mini tort claim, without a threshold (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)). A premises, electrical, dog bite, or dram shop case has no threshold.

Your share of fault reduces the recovery, and above 50 percent it bars noneconomic damages (MCL 600.2959; in auto cases, MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). At exactly 50 percent, you recover half. Where more than one party caused the injury, each defendant generally pays only its own share of fault (MCL 600.2956, 600.6304). That is why the landlord, the management company, the snow-removal contractor, and the electrician all have to be identified and named. Michigan does not award punitive damages in ordinary injury cases. Exemplary damages for humiliation or outrage are available only in narrow circumstances.

You cannot sue the at-fault party’s liability insurer directly in Michigan. The claim is against the driver, owner, or business, and its insurer defends and pays.

The practical limit is often insurance:

- **The at-fault driver’s limit** defaults to $250,000/$500,000 unless the driver chose lower limits, down to the $50,000/$100,000 minimum (MCL 500.3009(1)).
- **Your UM and UIM coverage** fill the gap.
- **In a premises, electrical, workplace, or nursing home case,** the commercial policy of the landlord, owner, contractor, utility, or operator pays, and those policies are often larger than an auto policy.
- **In a dram shop case,** the bar’s liquor liability policy adds to it.

For a worker with a third-party claim, the workers’ compensation insurer has a lien on the recovery (MCL 418.827). Medicare, Medicaid, and health plan liens are negotiated the same way.

## What it costs

Michigan caps contingency fees in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery (MCR 8.121). Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted from the recovery first; the one-third fee is calculated on the remaining amount. You pay nothing up front and nothing if there is no recovery.

The agreement is in writing. Read how costs are handled if the case is lost, because that is where firms differ: filing fees, records, experts, and depositions. A promise that “you pay nothing unless we win” should say whether that includes costs, and whether “win” means the same thing as “settle.” A recovery total or a list of verdicts tells you about past cases, not yours, and says nothing unless it says when and what the case was.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in Dearborn Heights?** Three years for most claims. Several deadlines are much shorter: - 45 days to notify the City of a sewer backup - one year to notify your no-fault insurer after a crash - 120 days to notify Wayne County or the City of a road or sidewalk defect, or a public agency of a building defect - 120 days for a road-defect notice on Telegraph, Ford Road, or Michigan Avenue - 120 days for a dram shop notice after you hire a lawyer, and two years to sue the bar - a 182-day notice and a two-year limit for malpractice

**I fell on the stairs in my apartment complex. Who is responsible?** Usually the landlord and its management or maintenance company, which must keep common areas in reasonable repair. Report the fall in writing, photograph the stairs and lighting, and keep any earlier complaints you made. Since 2023, the fact that a hazard was visible does not by itself end the claim.

**I slipped on ice in a parking lot on Ford Road or Telegraph. Who is responsible?** The owner or tenant that controlled the lot, and often the snow-removal contractor. Photograph the ice and the lot, report the fall, and ask for the incident report.

**I was shocked or burned by electricity. Who do I claim against?** It depends on the source. A downed or low line is a claim against the utility that owns it. Faulty wiring in a rental is a claim against the landlord. Bad electrical work is a claim against the contractor. A defective appliance or tool is a product liability claim against the maker or seller. If it happened at work, you have workers’ compensation and possibly a suit against a third party. Get medical care right away, even if the burn looks small, and keep the product.

**My basement flooded with sewage after a storm. Do I have a claim against the City?** Possibly, if the City’s sewer system backed up because of a defect it knew or should have known of and failed to fix. The written notice to the City is due within 45 days after you discovered the damage, so act quickly. Photograph everything and keep receipts for cleanup and repairs.

**I was hurt on Hines Drive or in Hines Park. Who do I claim against?** For a crash with another driver, the driver, through no-fault and the threshold. For a defect in Hines Drive itself, Wayne County, with written notice within 120 days. For an injury in the park outside a building, governmental immunity usually bars the claim.

**The driver who hit me had no insurance. What now?** Your own no-fault insurer, a household member’s insurer, or the Assigned Claims Plan pays your medical bills and lost wages regardless of fault. Your pain-and-suffering claim depends on whether you carry uninsured motorist coverage.

**Can I sue the other driver’s insurance company?** No. In Michigan, the claim is against the driver, and the driver’s insurer defends and pays. Your own no-fault insurer is a separate claim, for your medical bills and lost wages.

**What does a Dearborn Heights personal injury attorney charge?** Michigan caps the fee in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery, with no fee if there is no recovery. Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted only from a recovery.

**Where would my Dearborn Heights case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000 go to the 20th District Court on Michigan Avenue. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Third Judicial Circuit Court in Detroit. Claims against MDOT go to the Court of Claims.

**If I was partly at fault, do I still have a claim?** Yes, unless you were more than 50 percent at fault, which bars pain-and-suffering damages. Below that, your recovery is reduced by your share, and at exactly 50 percent you recover half. No-fault benefits are paid regardless of fault.

**Do I need a lawyer whose office is in Dearborn Heights?** No. What matters is that the lawyer knows the courts your case will be in, the agencies, insurers, and property owners involved, and will meet you where you are.
