## Troy, MI Slip and Fall Lawyer

For twenty years a fall on someone else’s property in Michigan was close to unwinnable, because a hazard that was “open and obvious” ended the case before it began. That rule is gone. Since the Michigan Supreme Court’s July 2023 decision in *Kandil-Elsayed v F & E Oil*, a Troy fall case turns on who had the duty to keep the place safe, what they knew about the hazard and when, what they did about it, and how much of the fall was your fault. In Troy those questions have local answers: Somerset Collection and Oakland Mall, with their parking decks, escalators, and elevators; the office towers and parking structures along Big Beaver; the stores, restaurants, and dealerships on Rochester Road, Maple, and the Troy Motor Mall; the hotels; apartment, condominium, and senior communities throughout the city; schools and city buildings, each with a 120-day notice rule; and sidewalks, which the City must keep in repair.

## Where Troy falls happen, and who is responsible

**Somerset Collection and Oakland Mall.** Wet floors at the entrances and in the food courts, spills in the corridors, uneven transitions, parking-deck stairs and ramps, icy walks from the decks, and escalator and elevator incidents. A shopper is an invitee: a property owner or possessor must exercise reasonable care under the circumstances, which may include inspecting for hazards, correcting dangerous conditions, or providing appropriate warnings. The case is decided on **notice**, actual (an employee saw or caused it) or constructive (it existed long enough that a reasonable inspection would have found it). A mall usually involves several parties: the mall owner and its management company for the common areas, the tenant store for its own floor, and the janitorial, security, snow-removal, and escalator or elevator maintenance contractors. The leases and service contracts decide who had the duty where you fell, so we request them with the footage.

**Escalators and elevators.** A sudden stop, a misleveled elevator, a step that collapses, or clothing caught in an escalator can cause serious injuries, particularly to older shoppers and children. Michigan regulates elevators and escalators and requires periodic inspection (MCL 408.801 et seq.), and the maintenance contractor’s service records and the State inspection history are evidence. A defective component can add a product liability claim against the manufacturer ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability)).

**Office towers and parking structures on Big Beaver.** Parking-structure ramps, stairwells, and icy top decks; plazas and walks between towers; wet lobby floors; and loading areas. A visitor or client is an invitee. The building owner, the property manager, the tenant, and the snow-removal or janitorial contractor may each have had a role. An employee who falls at their own employer’s building generally has a workers’ compensation claim against the employer (MCL 418.131), but may also have a third-party claim against a building owner, manager, or contractor that is not their employer, with the comp insurer repaid from that recovery (MCL 418.827). See our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).

**Stores, restaurants, hotels, and dealerships.** Grocery stores, big-box retailers, restaurants, and the strips along Rochester Road, Maple, Big Beaver, and Long Lake; the hotels near the I-75 interchanges; and the Troy Motor Mall’s showrooms and service drives: spills, freshly mopped floors without signs, merchandise in the aisle, oil and fluids in service areas, ice at entrances, and potholes and broken curbs in shared lots. The lease decides whether the business or the shopping-center owner maintained the lot and the walk out front.

**Apartments, condominiums, and senior communities.** Troy has apartment and condominium complexes along Big Beaver, Rochester, Maple, and Long Lake, and several senior apartment, independent living, and assisted living communities. In a condominium, the association, not the unit owner, usually controls the common walks, stairs, and lots, and its snow-removal contract and maintenance records are the evidence. Landlords must keep the premises and common areas fit for their intended use and in reasonable repair (MCL 554.139): broken stairs, loose handrails, dark stairwells and parking lots, and failed lighting are the usual claims. A lot with an inch or two of snow is still “fit” for parking under *Allison v AEW Capital* (2008), so tenant snow cases depend on whether the accumulation actually prevented use; the general negligence claim after *Kandil-Elsayed* is a separate route. A lease clause waiving the landlord’s liability for its own negligence is void. A fall in an assisted living community may be a care claim as well as a premises claim. See our [tenant injury page](/personal-injury/tenant-injuries) and [nursing home abuse page](/personal-injury/nursing-home-abuse).

**Sidewalks: the City.** The Road Commission for Oakland County has many of Troy’s major roads, but the City must keep the public sidewalks within the city in reasonable repair, including those beside county and state roads (MCL 691.1402a(1)); the State’s and the county’s duties under the highway exception stop at the roadway (MCL 691.1402(1)). So a fall on a broken public sidewalk along Big Beaver, Rochester, or Maple is generally a claim against the City of Troy. The City must have known, or should have known, of the defect at least 30 days before the fall; a defect under two inches is presumed to be reasonable repair unless the evidence shows otherwise, and the judge decides whether the presumption is rebutted (MCL 691.1402a(2)–(4)). Measure and photograph the defect with a ruler before it is patched, and serve written notice on the City stating the exact location, the defect, the injury, and the witnesses within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). A sidewalk inside a mall, office campus, apartment complex, or shopping center belongs to its owner, and the claim is an ordinary premises claim.

**Schools, city buildings, and parks.** Troy is served mainly by the Troy School District, with Avondale, Birmingham, and other districts serving parts of the city. Each is a governmental agency: a fall caused by a dangerous or defective condition of one of their buildings proceeds under the public building exception, which requires the agency to have known of the defect and failed to fix it, with written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1406). The same rule applies to City Hall and the city’s community center, library, and other public buildings. A fall on a city park path or field, outside a building, has no comparable exception and is difficult to bring.

**Hospitals and nursing homes.** A visitor’s fall at Corewell Health Troy Hospital or a medical office is a premises claim. A patient’s fall, or a resident’s fall in one of the city’s two Medicare-certified nursing homes, may be premises, negligence, or medical malpractice, and which one decides the deadline. See our [Troy medical malpractice page](/personal-injury/medical-malpractice/troy).

## A parking-lot fall can be a no-fault claim

If you fell while getting into or out of your own car, or while loading it, Michigan’s no-fault law may cover your medical bills and lost wages even though no crash occurred. An injury from a parked vehicle is covered when the person was “occupying, entering into, or alighting from” it, or was injured by property being lifted onto or lowered from it during loading or unloading (MCL 500.3106(1)). That claim runs on the no-fault notice rules: written notice to the auto insurer within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). It sits alongside any premises claim against the lot’s or parking deck’s owner. A worker hurt on the job while loading, unloading, or getting into or out of a parked vehicle is generally limited to workers’ compensation for that injury, with narrow exceptions (MCL 500.3106(2)).

## The law after *Kandil-Elsayed*

Under the old rule (*Lugo v Ameritech*, 2001), an owner owed no duty to protect visitors from a danger an average person would have noticed, and insurers denied fall claims on that basis. In *Kandil-Elsayed v F & E Oil* (Michigan Supreme Court, July 28, 2023), the Court held that whether a hazard was open and obvious no longer decides whether a duty existed. It is now a factor in whether the owner breached the duty of reasonable care, and in how much of the fault belongs to the person who fell; both are questions for a jury. That does not make every fall a case. A visible hazard still matters: if the jury assigns you some fault, your damages are reduced by that share, and above half you recover no pain-and-suffering damages, only reduced economic losses (MCL 600.2959).

Duty also depends on why you were there. A shopper, client, hotel guest, or business visitor is an invitee, owed inspection and repair; a social guest at a home is a licensee, owed a warning of hidden dangers the owner knows about; a trespasser is owed protection from willful and wanton misconduct.

## Snow and ice

An owner must take reasonable steps, within a reasonable time after snow or ice accumulates, to make walkways, entrances, parking decks, and lots safe. What is reasonable depends on the property: a mall opening at 10 a.m. or an office tower opening at 7 a.m. after an overnight storm is expected to have treated its walks and the exposed top level of its parking deck, and a contractor with a salting contract that missed the morning run may share the fault. The law does not require an owner to keep every surface bare at every moment of a storm. Black ice from refreezing meltwater, a leaking gutter or a dripping parking-deck expansion joint, or snow piled where it melts across the walk can be a condition the owner created, and those cases are stronger than natural accumulation.

Some Michigan communities require adjacent owners to clear snow and ice from public sidewalks by ordinance. Photograph the ice, the path, the alternatives you had, and the weather the same day.

## What to do after a fall in Troy

1. **Get medical care the same day** and tell the provider where and how you fell.
2. **Photograph the hazard immediately**, from several angles, with something for scale, before it is mopped, salted, or patched. Photograph your shoes.
3. **Report the fall** to the store or mall security, the building manager, the landlord, or the City, and get the incident report number. Do not give a recorded statement to any insurer.
4. **Get witness names**, including employees, and note the time; it matters for constructive notice.
5. **Measure a sidewalk defect** with a ruler in the photograph.
6. **Keep the shoes** unworn and unwashed.
7. **Call a lawyer within days.** Mall and tower footage may be overwritten in days; if the City or a school district may be involved, a short notice period is already running.

## Frequently asked questions

**I slipped in a mall corridor at Somerset. Who is responsible?** Usually the mall owner and its management company for the common areas, and possibly a janitorial contractor. If the fall happened inside a store, the store itself. The leases and service contracts decide it, and the mall’s footage and incident report should be requested right away.

**I was hurt on an escalator. Is that a slip and fall case?** It may be a premises claim against the owner, a negligence claim against the maintenance contractor, and a product claim against the manufacturer of a failed part. The maintenance records and the State inspection history are evidence.

**I fell on ice on the top deck of a Big Beaver parking structure. Do I have a case?** Possibly. Since 2023, an obvious hazard no longer ends the case. The questions are whether the owner or its contractor should have salted or cleared it by the time you arrived, whether you had a realistic alternative, and how much of the fault is yours. If you work in the building, workers’ compensation may also apply.

**I fell on a broken sidewalk along Rochester Road. Who do I notify?** Generally the City of Troy, which must keep public sidewalks in reasonable repair. It must have known or should have known of the defect at least 30 days before, defects under two inches are presumed reasonable repair, and written notice must reach the City within 120 days.

**I fell getting out of my car in a parking lot. Is that a car accident claim?** It may be. No-fault can cover injuries from entering or leaving a parked vehicle, separate from any claim against the lot’s owner.

**How long do I have?** Three years to sue a private owner; 120 days for written notice to the City or a school district; and one year to notify a no-fault insurer. The footage disappears far sooner.
