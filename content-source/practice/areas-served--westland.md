## Personal Injury Law Firm Serving Westland, MI

Westland is a city of about 83,000 in western Wayne County, laid out on the grid between Joy Road and Michigan Avenue, Inkster and Hix, with Ford Road running through its center, Wayne Road and Warren Road crossing it at the Westland Shopping Center, and I-275 just to the west. It is a city of apartment complexes and mid-century subdivisions, with about 39 percent of occupied housing units rented and five Medicare- or Medicaid-certified nursing homes, and its injuries follow that profile: intersection crashes on Ford and Wayne, pedestrian crashes between the complexes and the retail, falls in the stores and the complexes, and neglect in the senior facilities. Hines Drive, the county parkway along the Rouge at the city’s northern edge, adds its own crashes and park injuries. In 2024, Westland recorded 1,604 traffic crashes, with 545 people injured and three killed.

ATLAW is a personal injury firm in Dearborn, twenty minutes east on Ford Road. In 13 years the firm has handled more than 10,000 cases and recovered more than $100 million for clients across southeast Michigan, Westland residents among them. This page explains what is particular about a Westland injury claim: where the injuries happen, who the defendants usually are, which courts hear the cases, and the deadlines that end them. ## Where Westland injuries happen

**Ford Road (M-153).** The city’s spine, five to seven lanes from Inkster to I-275, lined with retail, restaurants, and apartment complexes, with signals at Inkster, Middlebelt, Merriman, Wayne Road, Newburgh, and Hix and dozens of driveways between them. Left-turn and red-light crashes at the signals, driveway crashes at the strips, rear-end crashes in the stacked turn lanes, and pedestrian crashes between the complexes and the stores. Ford Road is MDOT’s.

**Wayne Road and Warren Road.** Wayne Road north and south through the center of the city, past the Westland Shopping Center, City Hall, and the 18th District Court on Ford; Warren Road east and west past the mall and the Westland Sports Arena. The Wayne and Warren intersection at the mall, and the Wayne and Ford intersection, carry substantial retail traffic, creating opportunities for intersection and parking-lot crashes; the mall’s lots carry the pedestrian strikes and the falls. See our [premises liability page](/personal-injury/premises-liability).

**Cherry Hill, Joy, Palmer, Michigan Avenue, and the north-south arterials.** Cherry Hill, Joy, and Palmer across; Michigan Avenue (US-12) along the southern edge; Inkster, Middlebelt, Merriman, Newburgh, Hix, and Venoy north and south. Intersection crashes at the signals, school-zone crashes near the Wayne-Westland and Livonia schools, and pedestrian and bicycle crashes near the complexes. Michigan Avenue is MDOT’s; responsibility for the other roads varies by segment between Wayne County and the City of Westland.

**I-275.** Just west of the city, with the Ford Road and Cherry Hill/Michigan Avenue interchanges and the I-96/M-14 stack to the north in Livonia. Merge and weave crashes at the Ford Road interchange and rear-end crashes in the stop-and-go, and the heavy trucks that use I-275 to reach the Plymouth Road and Michigan Avenue industrial corridors. See our [Westland truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents).

**Hines Drive.** The Wayne County parkway along the Middle Rouge at the city’s northern edge, a winding two-lane road through Hines Park with its picnic areas, trails, and flood-prone low spots. Run-off-road and head-on crashes on the curves, bicycle and pedestrian crashes where the trail meets the drive, and park injuries. Hines Drive and Hines Park are Wayne County’s, under the Wayne County Parks division and the Department of Public Services; a road-defect claim is against the county with 120-day notice, and park injuries face the recreational-use and immunity defenses that make them difficult. See our [bicycle accident page](/personal-injury/bicycle-accidents).

**The apartment complexes.** About 39 percent of Westland’s occupied housing units are rented, concentrated in large complexes along Ford Road, Warren, Joy, Cherry Hill, and Newburgh, alongside several manufactured-home communities. Falls on stairs and ice, inadequate lighting and security, snow and ice in the lots, rental-housing injuries under MCL 554.139, manufactured-home-park injuries, dog bites under MCL 287.351, and sidewalk injuries under the two-inch rule (MCL 691.1402a). See our [tenant injury](/personal-injury/tenant-injuries) and [dog bite](/personal-injury/dog-bites) pages.

**The senior-care facilities.** Five Medicare- or Medicaid-certified nursing homes, plus assisted-living and senior apartment communities along Ford Road, Warren, and Newburgh. Falls during transfers, pressure injuries, medication errors, dehydration, and neglect, with the distinction between ordinary negligence (three-year deadline) and medical malpractice (two years, with the 182-day notice of intent under MCL 600.2912b) requiring careful analysis. See our [nursing home abuse page](/personal-injury/nursing-home-abuse).

## What is different about a Westland claim

**Coverage.** Westland’s drivers carry a mix of policies, and uninsured and minimum-limits drivers are more common on Ford Road and Michigan Avenue than in the northern suburbs. Your own no-fault insurer pays your medical bills and lost wages regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107), or the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan does if no household policy applies (MCL 500.3172, capped at $250,000); the claim against the at-fault driver requires a threshold injury (MCL 500.3135) and recovery may depend on the at-fault driver’s insurance, available assets, and any uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage. Michigan’s default bodily-injury liability limits are $250,000 per person and $500,000 per accident, although drivers may select limits as low as $50,000 per person. We review every policy in the household before we value a Westland claim. See our [Westland car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents), [no-fault insurance guide](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance), and [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Three road agencies.** Wayne Road, Warren, Cherry Hill, Joy, Palmer, Inkster, Middlebelt, Merriman, Newburgh, Hix, and Hines Drive may fall under Wayne County or City of Westland jurisdiction depending on the segment, while Hines Drive is maintained by Wayne County. A crash caused by a defect in the traveled roadway may implicate the highway exception to governmental immunity, MCL 691.1402. Signal malfunctions and sight obstructions require separate legal analysis.

**Government vehicles.** Crashes involving Westland Police and Fire vehicles, SMART buses, Wayne-Westland and Livonia school buses, and county road and parks vehicles may fall under the Governmental Tort Liability Act’s motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405), depending on ownership, operation, and the circumstances of the crash. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

**The 18th District and the Third Circuit.** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims for vehicle damage (MCL 500.3135(3)(e), up to $3,000), are filed in the 18th District Court at 36675 Ford Road, which serves Westland. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Third Judicial Circuit Court at the Coleman A. Young Municipal Center in Detroit. Wrongful death cases may require estate proceedings in Wayne County Probate Court, while the lawsuit itself proceeds in the court with jurisdiction. Settlements involving minors require court approval under MCR 2.420.

**The hospitals.** Garden City Hospital on Inkster Road, just across the city line, and Corewell Health Wayne Hospital in Wayne treat injured Westland residents; Trinity Health Livonia, Corewell Health Dearborn, and Detroit’s Level I trauma centers also treat patients with serious injuries.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where are the crashes in Westland?** At the Ford Road signals from Inkster to Newburgh, at Wayne and Warren by the mall, at Wayne and Ford, on Michigan Avenue, and at the I-275 interchange. In 2024, Westland recorded 1,604 traffic crashes, with 545 people injured and three killed. Wayne County as a whole recorded 50,355 crashes, 16,458 injuries, and 177 deaths in 2024.

**I was hurt in my apartment complex in Westland. Who is responsible?** Potentially the landlord, management company, or maintenance contractor, under Michigan’s rental-housing statute (MCL 554.139) and premises liability law; since Kandil-Elsayed (2023), the obviousness of the hazard is considered when evaluating breach of duty and comparative fault rather than automatically ending the claim. Prior complaints, work orders, and incident reports are the evidence; request them early. **[ATTORNEY CHECK]**

**My parent was injured in a Westland nursing home. Is that malpractice or negligence?** It depends on who did what. A fall during a transfer or a pressure injury from inadequate turning may involve ordinary negligence or medical malpractice, depending on whether the alleged failure requires professional medical judgment. Ordinary negligence generally has a three-year deadline; medical malpractice generally has a two-year deadline and requires advance notice of intent. Treat the shorter deadline as the real one.

**I was hurt on Hines Drive or in Hines Park. Who do I claim against?** For a crash with another driver, the driver, through no-fault and a threshold claim. For a road defect on Hines Drive, Wayne County, with written notice within 120 days. For an injury in the park itself, the county, subject to the recreational-use and governmental-immunity defenses that make park claims difficult. **[ATTORNEY CHECK]**

**Where would my Westland injury case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, in the 18th District Court on Ford Road; injury suits over $25,000 in the Third Judicial Circuit Court in Detroit.

**The driver who hit me on Ford Road had no insurance. What now?** Your own no-fault insurer, a household member’s, or the Assigned Claims Plan pays your medical bills and lost wages regardless. Your pain-and-suffering claim depends on whether you carry uninsured motorist coverage; without it, the claim is worth what the uninsured driver can personally pay.

**How much does it cost to hire you?** Nothing up front, and no fee unless we recover money for you. Michigan caps contingency fees in personal injury cases at one-third of the recovery (MCR 8.121).

**How long do I have?** Three years for most injury suits, one year to notify your no-fault insurer in writing, two years for nursing home clinical negligence, and as little as 120 days for a claim against the City of Westland or Wayne County.
