## West Bloomfield, MI Car Accident Lawyer

A car crash in West Bloomfield Township is a Michigan no-fault claim on these roads:

- **Orchard Lake Road** runs north and south through the township’s center, past the retail at Fourteen Mile and Maple and on between the lakes toward Pontiac Trail.
- **Maple Road** runs east and west past Henry Ford West Bloomfield Hospital, the schools, and the retail at Orchard Lake, Drake, Farmington, and Middlebelt.
- **Fourteen Mile, Haggerty, Farmington, Drake, Middlebelt, Inkster, and Halsted** carry commuters at the edges and between the subdivisions.
- **The lake roads,** including Lone Pine, Long Lake, Walnut Lake, Commerce, Cass Lake, and Pontiac Trail, are two-lane roads with curves, hills, and driveways between the lakes and the woods.

In 2024, the Michigan State Police recorded 1,223 crashes in West Bloomfield Township. No one was killed. 11 people were seriously injured and 317 injured in all, and 91 of the crashes involved a deer. Farmington Road at West Maple Road was one of Oakland County’s ten highest-crash intersections.

A West Bloomfield crash claim usually turns on four questions:

- Who had the right of way at a signal, a roundabout, or a driveway?
- Was a deer, a curve, or the road itself part of the cause?
- How do the other driver’s liability limit, any umbrella policy, and your own underinsured motorist coverage fit together?
- If the road played a part, which agency owns it? In a township, the answer is almost never the township.

## Where West Bloomfield crashes happen

**Farmington Road at Maple, and the other retail intersections.** The signals where the township’s arterials cross carry the commuter and shopping traffic of the whole area. The crashes are:

- left-turn crashes across oncoming traffic (MCL 257.650)
- red-light and late-yellow crashes
- rear-end crashes in the turn lanes and at the signals
- crashes at the shopping center and restaurant driveways next to the intersections, where a driver leaving a lot must yield to traffic on the road (MCL 257.652)

Farmington Road at Maple recorded 64 crashes in 2024. It is a two-lane roundabout, where the crashes are mostly drivers entering without yielding to traffic already in the circle and lane-change crashes inside it. Orchard Lake Road at Maple and at Fourteen Mile, and Haggerty at Fourteen Mile and Maple, carry the same patterns.

**Orchard Lake Road.** In its southern miles, Orchard Lake Road is a multi-lane road, with a roundabout at Fourteen Mile and dual left-turn lanes at Maple. The crashes come from drivers turning left across traffic, drivers changing lanes near the intersections, and drivers entering the roundabout without yielding. North of Maple, the road narrows and winds between the lakes.

**The lake roads.** Lone Pine, Long Lake, Walnut Lake, Commerce, Cass Lake, Pontiac Trail, and the roads around Pine, Orchard, Cass, and Walnut lakes have curves, hills, and short sight lines. They carry commuter traffic at speed past driveways, lake-access roads, and subdivision entrances. The crashes are:

- head-on and run-off-road crashes on the curves
- crashes at driveways and side roads hidden by a hill or a curve
- rear-end crashes behind a driver turning into a driveway
- winter crashes on shaded curves where ice stays longest

See our [motorcycle accident page](/personal-injury/motorcycle-accidents/west-bloomfield), because riders crowd these roads in summer.

**Deer.** The State Police recorded 91 deer-involved crashes in West Bloomfield in 2024. The township’s woods, wetlands, parks, and lakes put deer on the roads, most often at dawn and dusk and in the fall. A deer crash with no other vehicle is a claim against your own insurer:

- your PIP coverage pays your medical bills and lost wages, as in any crash
- your comprehensive coverage, not collision, usually pays for the car
- there is no at-fault driver to sue

If you swerved to miss a deer and hit another car, or another driver hit you after you stopped, the other driver’s fault becomes the question.

**Schools, the hospital, and the subdivisions.** School traffic on Maple, Orchard Lake, and the roads to the township’s schools, hospital and medical-office traffic on Maple, and drivers leaving subdivision entrances all add left-turn and pull-out crashes. Pedestrians and cyclists use the township’s pathways and cross at the signals. See our [pedestrian](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents) and [bicycle](/personal-injury/bicycle-accidents) accident pages.

**Whose road it is: almost never the township’s.** Michigan townships do not own or maintain public roads. In West Bloomfield:

- **The Road Commission for Oakland County** owns Orchard Lake Road, Maple, the mile roads, the lake roads, and the subdivision streets that are public. A claim against it requires written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404).
- **MDOT** owns M-10 (Northwestern Highway), which ends at Orchard Lake Road in the township. A road-defect claim against MDOT goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **Private roads** in some subdivisions and lake associations belong to the association or the owners, and a claim about one is an ordinary premises claim.

A pothole or other defect in the road can make the agency a defendant alongside the other driver; on county and State roads, signals, signs, and sight obstructions generally fall outside the highway exception (Nawrocki v Macomb County Road Commission, Mich 2000). A notice sent to West Bloomfield Township is a notice to the wrong defendant, and it does not stop the 120 days.

## Fault is the case

In a rear-end crash, fault is rarely disputed. At a roundabout, a retail driveway, or a curve between the lakes, it often is, and the other driver’s insurer may try to assign you half or more of the fault. That share matters (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)):

- **More than 50 percent at fault:** you lose your claim for pain and suffering.
- **50 percent or less:** your recovery is reduced by your share. At exactly 50 percent, you recover half.

The evidence that decides fault is perishable:

- the West Bloomfield Police Department crash report and diagram
- **signal timing data** from the Road Commission for the intersection
- cameras at the shopping centers, gas stations, and homes near the scene, many overwritten within days
- the vehicles’ **event data recorders**, which store speed, braking, and throttle in the seconds before impact
- the other driver’s phone records, for a distracted-driving claim (see our [distracted driving page](/personal-injury/distracted-driving-accidents))
- photographs of the curve, the hill, or the vegetation that blocked the view
- witnesses, including other drivers stopped at the signal

We move promptly to request the camera footage, signal data, and vehicle data.

## Who pays after a West Bloomfield crash

**Your no-fault (PIP) claim.** Your own auto insurer pays these benefits regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107):

- your medical expenses, up to the PIP level you chose
- 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- replacement services at $20 a day
- attendant care

If you have no policy, a resident relative’s policy pays (MCL 500.3114). If none exists, an occupant claims from the insurer of the vehicle they were in (MCL 500.3114(4)), and then the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3172), capped at $250,000 for medical benefits. A pedestrian or cyclist hit by a car, with no household policy, claims through the Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3115).

The insurer must receive written notice within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). It must pay each bill within 30 days of reasonable proof, or owe 12 percent interest (MCL 500.3142). Since the 2019 reform, PIP medical coverage is no longer unlimited unless you chose it, so the level on your declarations page matters. Providers’ charges are also subject to the fee schedule in MCL 500.3157. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

**Your claim against the at-fault driver.** For pain and suffering, you need a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment is an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life. It does not have to be permanent (MCL 500.3135(5); *McCormick v Carrier*, 2010).

You can also recover economic losses that no-fault does not cover (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)):

- lost wages beyond the three years or above the monthly cap, which matters for anyone who earns more than the cap
- medical bills above your chosen PIP level

The suit must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The mini tort claim recovers up to $3,000 in vehicle damage not covered by your policy (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)). If the at-fault driver’s insurer will not pay it, the claim is filed in the 48th District Court.

**Three layers of coverage, in the right order.** In a serious crash, the at-fault driver’s liability limit may be less than the claim is worth. There may be two more layers: an umbrella policy over the at-fault driver’s auto policy, and your own **underinsured motorist coverage**. Follow these steps in order:

1. Find out whether the at-fault driver has an umbrella policy, and give notice to that carrier.
2. The at-fault driver’s insurer offers its limit.
3. You notify your own insurer and ask for its written consent to settle.
4. Only then do you accept the limit and sign the release.
5. The umbrella and UIM claims then proceed.

Accepting without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under most policies. Your own umbrella policy may also provide underinsured coverage, but only if its terms say so. Against an uninsured driver, or in a hit-and-run, the claim for pain and suffering depends on **uninsured motorist coverage**, whose notice terms are often short. Two rules cut the other way:

- An owner who was required to insure a car and did not is generally barred from PIP benefits (MCL 500.3113(b)).
- A driver who was operating their own uninsured vehicle cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, whoever was at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(c)).

See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Drunk drivers and the dram shop claim.** A restaurant, bar, club, or store that serves a visibly intoxicated person, or a minor, is liable for the injuries that person causes under Michigan’s dram shop act (MCL 436.1801). The State Police recorded 26 alcohol-involved crashes in West Bloomfield in 2024. The claim has its own rules:

- Written notice must go to the business within 120 days after you hire a lawyer.
- The drunk driver must be named and kept in the suit.
- The suit must be filed within two years, not three.

See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**Police cars, fire trucks, and buses.** All of these fall under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405):

- West Bloomfield Police and Fire vehicles, which are the township’s
- school buses that the districts serving the township run themselves
- Road Commission trucks, including snowplows

Dash camera and dispatch records are overwritten on short schedules, so we request them by FOIA in the first week. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

## What to do after a West Bloomfield crash

1. **Call 911 and get the police report.** Michigan requires a report for any crash with injury or $1,000 in damage (MCL 257.622). Crashes in the township are worked by the West Bloomfield Police Department. A crash in Orchard Lake Village, Keego Harbor, or Sylvan Lake is worked by that city’s police.
2. **Photograph the scene.** Get the vehicles, plates, the other driver’s license and insurance card, the intersection, roundabout, or curve, the signals, and anything that blocked the view. Note every store and home with a camera facing the scene. Footage is kept for days, not weeks.
3. **After a deer strike,** photograph the deer and the damage, and report the crash. Your insurer will ask for the report.
4. **Get medical care the same day.** Henry Ford West Bloomfield Hospital, on Maple Road, is a Level III trauma center. The most severe injuries may go to a Level I center such as Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak.
5. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** promptly.
6. **Read your declarations page** for PIP level, UM/UIM, umbrella, comprehensive, and collision coverage.
7. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the other driver’s insurer, sign a release, or accept a policy-limits offer before your UIM insurer consents.
8. **Call a lawyer within days** if fault is disputed, the other driver’s limit looks too low, or the road played a part. The camera footage is the first evidence lost, and the 120-day Road Commission notice is already running.

## The courts

- **Claims up to $25,000,** including mini tort claims, go to the 48th District Court at 4280 Telegraph Road in Bloomfield Township. It serves West Bloomfield and several neighboring communities.
- **Injury suits over $25,000** go to the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac, with a jury drawn from all of Oakland County.
- **Defect claims against MDOT** go to the Court of Claims.
- **A fatal crash** is brought by the estate through the Oakland County Probate Court. See our [wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death).

## Frequently asked questions

**A driver turned left in front of me at Orchard Lake and Maple. Who is at fault?** Usually the driver turning left, who must yield to oncoming traffic. Left-turn crashes are often disputed, though: the signal phase, your speed, and whether the view was blocked all matter. The Road Commission’s signal timing records and nearby store cameras can settle it, but footage is kept only days.

**A driver entered the Farmington and Maple roundabout and hit me. Who is at fault?** Usually the driver entering, who must yield to traffic already in the roundabout. These crashes are often disputed. The yield signs, the lane markings, the sight lines, and your speed all matter. Photograph the roundabout and get the witnesses.

**I hit a deer on a West Bloomfield road. Who pays?** Your own insurer. PIP pays your medical bills and lost wages, and comprehensive coverage usually pays for the car. There is no one to sue unless another driver was involved.

**A pothole caused my crash. Do I file against West Bloomfield Township?** No. Michigan townships do not own public roads. Orchard Lake Road, Maple, the mile roads, and the lake roads belong to the Road Commission for Oakland County, with written notice due within 120 days. M-10 (Northwestern Highway) belongs to MDOT, with notice filed in the Court of Claims within 120 days. A private subdivision road belongs to its association or owners.

**The other driver’s insurer offered its policy limit. Should I take it?** Not before checking for an umbrella policy above it and your own underinsured motorist coverage, and getting your insurer’s written consent to settle. Accepting the limit without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under most policies.

**How many crashes are there in West Bloomfield?** The State Police recorded 1,223 crashes in West Bloomfield Township in 2024. No one was killed, 11 people were seriously injured, and 317 were injured in all. 91 crashes involved a deer, and Farmington Road at West Maple Road recorded 64 crashes, eighth in Oakland County.

**Where would my West Bloomfield case be filed?** Mini tort claims and claims up to $25,000 go to the 48th District Court on Telegraph Road in Bloomfield Township. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court in Pontiac.

**How long do I have?** - One year to give your no-fault insurer written notice. - Three years to sue the at-fault driver. - 120 days for a notice to the Road Commission. - 120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT. - Two years to sue a bar or restaurant, with a 120-day notice.
