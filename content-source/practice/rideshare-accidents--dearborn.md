## The three periods, and how to prove which one applied

Michigan regulates Uber, Lyft, and similar companies as transportation network companies under 2016 PA 345, MCL 257.2101 and following. The Act requires insurance in three periods (MCL 257.2123):

| Period | What the driver was doing | Coverage | Who provides it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| App off | Personal driving | Driver’s personal policy; Michigan minimum $50,000 / $100,000 | Driver’s insurer, which may exclude rideshare use |
| App on, no ride accepted | Logged in, waiting for a request | $50,000 per person / $100,000 per crash / $25,000 property damage, plus PIP and PPI | Uber or Lyft, or the driver’s rideshare endorsement |
| Ride accepted or passenger on board | En route to pick up, or carrying a passenger | $1,000,000 combined, plus PIP and PPI | Uber or Lyft |

The moment the driver accepts a ride, the $1 million policy attaches and stays attached until the passenger is dropped off. The gap period, app on with no ride, has coverage only at the Act’s minimums, which is why a serious injury in that period usually turns on the injured person’s own uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage.

**Proving the period.** Uber and Lyft keep trip logs, GPS traces, and timestamps for every driver session. A **preservation letter** to the company and its insurer in the first days, followed by a subpoena if necessary, obtains them. Screenshot the trip screen, the driver’s name and photo, the plate, the route, and the time before you leave the scene; it is the one piece of evidence the companies will not preserve for you. **[CONFIRM: firm’s practice]**

## Who pays your medical bills

**If you were a passenger.** Michigan no-fault pays your medical expenses and wage loss regardless of fault, but being a paying customer does not put Uber’s insurer first in line. Your own auto policy pays first, or a policy held by your spouse or a relative you live with; if there is none, you claim through the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3114, as amended by 2016 PA 345; MCL 500.3172). **[ATTORNEY CHECK: TNC passenger PIP priority, carried from the rideshare hub]** Written notice to the responsible insurer is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance) and [passenger injury page](/personal-injury/passenger-injury).

**If you were the rideshare driver.** Your own policy pays PIP if it does not exclude rideshare use; if it does, the company’s policy covers PIP while the app is on. Drivers are generally independent contractors under the Act, so there is usually no workers' compensation claim, which makes the PIP claim and the claim against the at-fault driver everything. A personal policy can be rescinded if rideshare use was misrepresented when the policy was bought. **[ATTORNEY CHECK: rescission language]** Many Dearborn drivers work the airport run; a crash on I-94 with a passenger on board is a $1 million-policy case for the passenger and a PIP-plus-UM case for the driver.

**If you were in another car, on a bike, or on foot.** Your own no-fault insurer pays your benefits, as in any crash (or the priority chain under MCL 500.3114 and 3115 for pedestrians and cyclists). The rideshare period decides which liability policy you claim against. See our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents).

## Your claim for pain and suffering

For pain and suffering and economic losses beyond no-fault, you need a threshold injury under MCL 500.3135 (death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement) and three years to file (MCL 600.5805(2)); a fatal crash is brought by the estate (see our [Dearborn wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death/dearborn)). The defendants:

- **The rideshare driver**, covered by whichever policy applied in that period.
- **The other driver**, if someone else hit the rideshare vehicle, covered by their own policy, which on Michigan Avenue and Warren is often the $50,000 opt-down minimum or nothing. If that driver was uninsured or fled, the rideshare policy’s UM/UIM coverage (if any) and your own UM/UIM coverage apply; the Act does not require Uber or Lyft to carry UM, so your own policy is what matters. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims). **[ATTORNEY CHECK]**
- **Uber or Lyft directly**, in limited circumstances: because drivers are generally independent contractors (MCL 257.2137), the companies are usually liable only for their own negligence, such as approving a driver who failed, or should have failed, the criminal and driving-record checks the Act requires (MCL 257.2107), keeping a driver on the platform after impairment complaints (MCL 257.2139), or allowing a vehicle that failed inspection (MCL 257.2109). The arbitration clause in the app’s terms generally sends claims against the companies themselves to arbitration; it does not bind your claim against the driver’s insurer or the other driver, which proceed in court.
- **A bar that over-served the driver who hit the Uber**, under the dram shop act (MCL 436.1801), with a 120-day notice. See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

## What to do after a Dearborn rideshare crash

1. **Screenshot the app**: trip screen, driver, plate, route, time. If you are the driver, screenshot your status and the trip log.
2. **Call 911 and get the Dearborn Police report.** Give the officer the rideshare information so the UD-10 reflects it.
3. **Get medical care the same day.**
4. **Report the crash in the app** (both companies have in-app crash reporting), which creates a company record, and to your own insurer.
5. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the rideshare company’s insurer or the other driver’s, in any language.
6. **Read your own declarations page** for PIP level and UM/UIM coverage.
7. **Call a lawyer within the week**, so the preservation letter for app and GPS data goes out before retention windows close.

## Frequently asked questions

**I was a passenger in an Uber that crashed on I-94 in Dearborn. Who pays my medical bills?** Your own auto insurer first, or a policy held by your spouse or a relative you live with; if there is none, the Assigned Claims Plan. Being a paying passenger does not put Uber’s insurer first for your medical bills; it does make the $1 million policy available for your injury claim.

**I drive for Uber from Dearborn and was hit while waiting for a ride on Michigan Avenue. What applies?** In the waiting period the rideshare coverage is only Michigan’s minimum limits, and the Act does not require UM coverage, so your no-fault benefits come from your own policy (or the company’s if yours excludes rideshare use) and your injury claim depends on the other driver’s policy and your own UM coverage.

**Does Uber or Lyft have to carry insurance in Michigan?** Yes: $1 million while a ride is accepted or a passenger is on board; $50,000 / $100,000 / $25,000 while the app is on with no ride; nothing while the app is off, when the driver’s personal policy applies.

**Can I sue Uber or Lyft directly?** Only for the company’s own negligence, such as approving a driver who should have failed the background check. The arbitration clause may send that claim to arbitration; your claim against the driver’s insurer is not affected.

**How do I prove the driver was on a trip?** Uber and Lyft keep trip and GPS logs. A preservation letter, and a subpoena if needed, obtains them. Your own screenshots from the scene are the backup.

**How long do I have?** One year to notify the responsible no-fault insurer, three years to sue, and as little as 120 days for a dram shop notice. The app data should be requested within days.
