## Ann Arbor Social Security Disability Lawyer

**Looking for the Social Security office in Ann Arbor?** Social Security’s Ann Arbor field office is at 3971 Research Park Drive, Suite A, Ann Arbor, MI 48108, south of the city near State Street and I-94. At the field office you can:

- apply for disability, retirement, or survivor benefits
- report changes
- apply for or replace a Social Security card, including international students and scholars applying for a number for the first time

You do not have to go in person for a disability claim. You can apply or appeal a denial online at ssa.gov or by calling Social Security’s national number, 1-800-772-1213. If you visit, bring your work history and the names of every doctor and clinic that treated you. International students should follow their school’s guidance on when to apply for a number.

About three in five first applications for Social Security Disability are denied. Many of the people denied are in fact disabled. They are denied because the application did not answer the question Social Security actually asks. It is not “are you sick?” It is:

Can you do any job that exists in significant numbers in the national economy, given your age, education, and work history?

In Ann Arbor, that question often meets a particular work history: a nurse, technician, researcher, engineer, teacher, or administrator whose work was skilled. Skilled work can make a claim harder at the last step, because Social Security asks whether those skills transfer to easier work. A claimant whose record shows only a diagnosis, rather than the specific limits that end skilled work too, is the claimant who is denied.

## The two programs

**Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI)** is for people who worked and paid Social Security taxes:

- generally 40 work credits, 20 of them in the ten years before disability, with fewer for younger workers
- a benefit based on your earnings record
- Medicare after 24 months of entitlement

Because the benefit tracks earnings, a worker with a long skilled or professional career often has a substantial SSDI benefit, and the back pay matters accordingly. See our [SSDI page](/disability/social-security-disability-insurance).

**Supplemental Security Income (SSI)** is for people with little income and few assets, whether or not they worked:

- $994 a month for an individual in 2026 and $1,029 in 2027, reduced by other income
- a $2,000 asset limit, with the home and one vehicle excluded
- Michigan Medicaid

Some claimants qualify for both. See our [SSI page](/disability/supplemental-security-income).

**Disabled adult children.** A person disabled before age 22 may receive benefits on a parent’s earnings record, once the parent is receiving retirement or disability benefits or has died. This matters for young adults in Ann Arbor whose own work history is short.

## The five-step test, as it applies to an Ann Arbor claim

Social Security uses a five-step sequential evaluation (20 CFR 404.1520), deciding the claim at the first step where the answer is clear.

1. **Are you working above the substantial gainful activity level?** Earning more than $1,690 a month in 2026 ($1,770 in 2027) generally ends the claim at step one. Part-time or accommodated work below that level does not. For a claimant still working reduced hours at the University or a hospital, the pay stubs and the accommodations are evidence.
2. **Is your impairment severe and expected to last 12 months or result in death?** A diagnosis is not enough. The record must show functional limits.
3. **Does your condition meet or equal a Listing?** Meeting one wins the claim here. See our [qualifying impairments page](/disability/qualifying-impairments). Some conditions, including many cancers, are fast-tracked under [Compassionate Allowances](/disability/compassionate-allowances).
4. **Can you do your past relevant work?** Social Security assesses your residual functional capacity (RFC), what you can still do despite your limitations, and compares it to the jobs you held in the past five years. A nurse or patient-care technician who lifts and transfers patients does medium or heavy work. A researcher, engineer, or administrator usually does sedentary or light work that is skilled. For skilled sedentary work, the claim at step four often turns on mental and cognitive limits: concentration, pace, memory, and the ability to keep a schedule.
5. **Can you do any other work?** Social Security uses your RFC, age, education, and work experience under the Medical-Vocational Guidelines (the “grids”): At 50 and above, a claimant limited to sedentary work whose past work was unskilled or semi-skilled, with no transferable skills, is generally found disabled under the grids; At 55 and above, the same is true for many claimants limited to light work; and **For skilled past work, the question is transferable skills.** A claimant whose skills transfer to other work within the RFC, with little adjustment, is generally not disabled under the grids, even at 55. Whether a nurse’s, technician’s, or professional’s skills really transfer, and to what, is often the whole hearing.

## Where the evidence comes from in Ann Arbor

The claim is the record. We obtain and organize it from every source:

- **University of Michigan Health,** including its specialty clinics. Records are requested from its health information office, and patient-portal notes are not the complete record.
- **Trinity Health Ann Arbor** and its affiliated physician groups.
- **Independent physician, specialty, and pain-management practices** across the city.
- **Public mental health:** Washtenaw County’s community mental health agency, and private therapists and psychiatrists. These records are often the core of a claim and the hardest to obtain.
- **University Health Service and counseling services** for students and recent students.
- **The VA Ann Arbor Healthcare System** for veterans.
- **An employer’s occupational health file,** any workers’ compensation file, and any long-term disability file.
- **The consultative examination** ordered by Michigan’s Disability Determination Services, which a claimant should attend but should never rely on.

A treating provider’s opinion on your specific functional limits, in a form the regulations recognize (20 CFR 404.1520c), is often the difference at the hearing. For a skilled worker, the provider should address the mental demands of the work as well as the physical ones. Your employer’s records also count. Restricted-duty assignments, medical leave, reduced appointments, and accommodation requests can show how the condition affected your work before you stopped.

Michigan has seven Social Security hearing offices: Detroit, Oak Park, Livonia, Flint, Lansing, Grand Rapids, and Mount Pleasant. There is no hearing office in Ann Arbor. Hearings may be held in person, by video, or by phone, and you may ask to appear in person. In the year to July 2026, the Livonia office approved about 57 percent of the cases its judges decided and the Lansing office about 52.5 percent. The judge assigned to the case, and the record in front of that judge, matter more than the office average.

Missing a 60-day deadline usually means starting over with a new application and can cost back pay. Appeal; do not reapply.

## Back pay and the fee

- SSDI benefits begin five full months after the established onset date.
- SSI is not paid for months before the application.
- SSDI back pay reaches to the onset date, up to 12 months before the application, so the onset date your lawyer proves sets the amount.

The fee is set by federal law: 25 percent of back benefits, capped at the current limit, and nothing if the claim is not approved (42 USC 406(a)). Social Security pays it directly from back pay. Ongoing monthly benefits are not subject to it. Record costs are advanced by the firm and deducted only from a recovery.

## How an Ann Arbor disability claim interacts with other benefits

**University of Michigan and other public-employer disability plans.** Many Ann Arbor workers have long-term disability coverage through a public employer: the University, the City, Ann Arbor Public Schools, Washtenaw County, or Washtenaw Community College. Two points:

- **Governmental plans are generally not governed by ERISA** (29 USC 1003(b)(1)). The 180-day ERISA appeal rule and federal ERISA litigation may not apply. The plan document, Michigan law, and, for the University, the State’s rules decide how a denial is challenged.
- **Many plans still require you to apply for SSDI,** reduce their benefit by the SSDI award, and may claim repayment from your back pay. The plan file and the Social Security file should tell the same story.

**Private-employer LTD plans.** Hospital, technology, and research employers often provide group long-term disability insurance. Those plans are usually governed by ERISA, with its 180-day appeal deadline (29 CFR 2560.503-1). See our [long-term disability page](/disability/short-long-term-disability).

**Federal employees.** VA and other federal employees covered by FERS who apply for FERS disability retirement are generally required to apply for SSDI as well, and the two benefits are coordinated. Federal employees under the older CSRS system generally did not pay Social Security tax on that work and may not be insured for SSDI.

**Workers’ compensation** offsets SSDI (42 USC 424a), and a comp redemption should be written to limit the offset. See our [Ann Arbor workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation/ann-arbor).

**A personal injury settlement** does not reduce SSDI but can affect SSI. See our [Ann Arbor personal injury lawyer page](/personal-injury/personal-injury/ann-arbor).

**VA disability** is evidence Social Security must consider, but it does not bind Social Security.

## Young adults and students

Ann Arbor has many young adults, including students, whose disability began before they built a work record:

- **SSI** may be available if income and assets are low. A full-time student under 22 may be able to exclude some earned income under SSI’s student earned income exclusion.
- **Disabled adult child benefits** may be available on a parent’s record if the disability began before 22.
- **Scholarships, fellowships, and stipends** can count as income for SSI in some circumstances, and a graduate stipend can bear on whether work is substantial gainful activity.
- **Records from University Health Service and counseling services,** and from disability-services accommodations, can document how a condition limited school and work.

## What to do if you are applying or were denied

1. **Apply as soon as you stop working** or drop below the SGA level. SSDI back pay reaches back no more than 12 months before the application.
2. **If denied, appeal within 60 days.** Do not file a new application.
3. **Keep treating,** and tell every provider how your condition limits you day to day, including concentration, memory, and pace. Gaps in treatment are a common reason claims are denied.
4. **Keep your employer’s records:** the last job description, accommodation and leave records, occupational health visits, and any LTD file.
5. **Attend the consultative examination** and tell us what happened in it.
6. **Call a lawyer before the hearing is scheduled,** not after the notice arrives. See our [how to win your claim page](/disability/winning-your-disability-claim).

## Frequently asked questions

**Where is the Social Security office in Ann Arbor?** At 3971 Research Park Drive, Suite A, Ann Arbor, MI 48108. You can also apply or appeal online at ssa.gov or by phone at 1-800-772-1213, without visiting.

**Where will my hearing be?** Ann Arbor has no hearing office of its own. Your appeal is assigned to one of Michigan’s seven hearing offices, and the hearing may be in person, by video, or by phone. You can ask to appear in person.

**I was a nurse (or engineer, or researcher) for 20 years. Does skilled work hurt my claim?** It can make the last step harder. If your skills transfer to easier work within your limits, Social Security may find you can do that work, even at 55. The answer is a record that shows the limits that rule out the transferable work too, often concentration, pace, or reliability, and a lawyer who cross-examines the vocational expert on it.

**My University of Michigan long-term disability plan told me to apply for Social Security. Why?** Because many disability plans reduce their benefit by the SSDI award and may ask you to repay overlapping months from your back pay. A University or other public-employer plan is generally not governed by ERISA, so its appeal rules differ from a private plan’s. Read the plan before you sign a repayment agreement.

**I work for the VA. Do I apply for Social Security too?** If you are under FERS and apply for disability retirement, you generally must apply for SSDI as well. The two benefits are coordinated.

**My adult son has been disabled since he was a teenager. Can he get benefits?** Possibly, on your earnings record as a disabled adult child, once you are receiving retirement or disability benefits or have died, if his disability began before 22. He may also qualify for SSI on his own.

**How long do I have to appeal?** Sixty days from the date on the denial notice, plus five days for mailing, at every stage.

**What changes in 2027?** From January 2027, the SGA limit rises to $1,770 a month, the trial work month to $1,270, and the SSI federal benefit to $1,029 for an individual, with a 3.6 percent increase in benefits.

**What does a disability lawyer cost?** Twenty-five percent of your back benefits, capped by federal law, paid by Social Security from the back pay. Nothing if you do not win.
