## Who pays after an Oakland County crash

**Your no-fault (PIP) claim, against your own insurer.** Under MCL 500.3107, your own policy pays medical expenses up to the PIP level you selected ($250,000, $500,000, or unlimited for most Oakland County policyholders; $50,000 is available only to Medicaid enrollees), 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years (capped at $7,201 a month for crashes in the year beginning October 1, 2025), replacement services at $20 a day, and attendant care, regardless of fault. If you have no policy, a spouse’s or resident relative’s policy pays (MCL 500.3114), and if none exists, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3172), capped at $250,000 for medical benefits. Written notice must reach the insurer within **one year** (MCL 500.3145(1)); each bill must be paid within 30 days of reasonable proof or carry 12 percent interest (MCL 500.3142). Our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance) covers the application, the benefits, and the denials.

**Your claim against the at-fault driver.** For pain and suffering and economic losses beyond no-fault, you need a threshold injury under MCL 500.3135: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement. The deadline is three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). Recovery is reduced by your share of fault and barred above 50 percent (MCL 500.3135(2)(b), 600.2959). For vehicle damage, the **mini tort** under 3135(3)(e) recovers up to $3,000 from the at-fault driver for your deductible or unpaid repair cost.

## What to do after an Oakland County crash

1. **Stop, call 911 if anyone is hurt, and call police.** A report is required for any crash with injury or $1,000 in damage (MCL 257.622). The UD-10 is purchased for $15 at michigan.gov/tcps.
2. **Photograph everything and get witnesses** before the scene clears.
3. **Get medical care the same day** and tell the provider it was a car accident.
4. **Report to your own insurer and request the Application for No-Fault Benefits.** File it in the first week.
5. **Read your declarations page.** Note your PIP level, your UM/UIM limits, and your collision deductible; in an Oakland County claim the UIM limit is often the number that matters.
6. **Do not give a recorded statement to the other driver’s insurer**, sign a medical authorization, or sign a release, and do not settle with the at-fault driver without your own insurer’s written consent if you may have a UIM claim.
7. **Stay off social media** about the crash and your activities.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many car accidents happen in Oakland County each year?** 35,951 in 2024, with 9,139 people injured and 68 killed, according to the Michigan State Police, about 12 percent of Michigan’s crashes.

**The other driver’s insurance offered their policy limit. Should I take it?** Not before checking your own underinsured motorist coverage and getting your insurer’s written consent to settle. Accepting the liability limit without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under most policies. A lawyer can sequence the two claims so you recover both.

**I was hurt because of a bad signal or pothole on a mile road in Oakland County. Who is responsible?** A qualifying defect in the roadway may support a claim against the Road Commission for Oakland County, with written notice generally required within 120 days under MCL 691.1404. A malfunctioning traffic signal is different because governmental immunity generally protects the agency from claims based solely on signal maintenance. If the road was a state trunkline such as Telegraph, Woodward, or M-59, a qualifying highway-defect claim against MDOT generally requires notice within 120 days, filed with the Court of Claims.

**Where would my Oakland County car accident case be filed?** Most injury suits go to the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court in Pontiac. Mini tort and other claims under $25,000 go to the district court for the community where the crash occurred or the defendant lives.

**Who pays my medical bills?** Your own no-fault insurer, regardless of fault, up to your PIP level, after you file an Application for No-Fault Benefits within one year; a household member’s insurer if you have none; the Assigned Claims Plan if no policy applies.

**How long do I have?** One year to give your no-fault insurer written notice, three years to sue the at-fault driver, and as little as 120 days for a claim against the RCOC or a city.

**Do I need a lawyer for a minor crash in Oakland County?** Not if no one was hurt and the insurer is paying. Call if anyone was injured, the other driver’s limits may be too low for the injury, a government vehicle or road defect was involved, the police report is wrong, or an insurer has denied or delayed benefits.
