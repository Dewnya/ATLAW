## Taylor, MI Personal Injury Lawyer

If you were hurt in Taylor because someone else was careless, you have a claim under Michigan law, and its deadlines start the day you are hurt:

- **After a crash,** your no-fault insurer must have written notice within one year.
- A road or sidewalk defect on a Wayne County road or a city street needs written notice to the county or the City of Taylor within 120 days. A defect on Telegraph, I-75, or I-94 needs written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days.
- **An injury in a City, county, or school building** needs written notice to that agency within 120 days.
- **A claim against a bar** that served a drunk driver must be filed within two years.
- **A medical malpractice suit,** including many claims against nursing homes, requires 182 days’ notice first and must be filed within two years.
- **Most other claims** allow three years.

A personal injury lawyer’s first job is to find out which of those deadlines apply to you. The second is to make sure none runs out before your claim is paid.

## What a Taylor personal injury lawyer does

**The first week.** We calendar every deadline and send preservation letters:

- to the at-fault driver’s insurer
- to a trucking company for its driver logs and electronic data, after a crash on I-75, I-94, or Telegraph
- to MDOT, Wayne County, or the City for signal timing records
- to the City for Taylor Police patrol-car video and dispatch records, and to SMART or the school district for vehicle records
- to a store, mall, hotel, apartment complex, or manufactured-home community for footage and incident reports, which many overwrite within days
- to a nursing home or assisted living community for the resident’s chart, incident reports, and staffing records
- to a bar or restaurant for its receipts, if a drunk driver was involved

We obtain the police report and the witnesses’ names. Crashes and incidents in the city are reported by the Taylor Police Department, and freeway crashes by the State Police. We also obtain the fire department and ambulance run sheets.

**The first month.** We identify every policy that may pay:

- your own auto policy, for no-fault benefits
- other household policies
- the at-fault party’s liability coverage
- your uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage
- a property owner’s or management company’s commercial policy
- umbrella policies
- the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan, where no auto policy exists (MCL 500.3172)

A pedestrian or cyclist hit by a car, with no household policy, is paid through the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3115). A Michigan resident hit by an out-of-state driver or a rental car near the airport has their own benefits paid as usual. The claim against that driver can involve the rental company, the driver’s home-state policy, and that insurer’s Michigan certification (MCL 500.3163).

For a workplace injury at a plant, warehouse, or store, we look for a third party who caused it, such as a contractor, a vendor’s driver, or an equipment manufacturer, because the employer itself usually cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). We file your Application for No-Fault Benefits inside the one-year window (MCL 500.3145(1)). We also handle the insurer’s requests for statements, records, and examinations (MCL 500.3151). You stop talking to adjusters, and we do that instead.

**The treatment period.** The claim’s value is set mostly by the medical record, so we make sure it is complete and the bills are paid while you treat. Corewell Health Taylor, on Telegraph, has an emergency department. The most serious injuries often go to Corewell Health Dearborn or Corewell Health Trenton (Level II), or the Level I trauma centers in Detroit, Detroit Receiving Hospital and Henry Ford Hospital. We document lost wages with your employer, including overtime and shift premiums.

**The demand, the suit, and the resolution.** When your condition has stabilized, we send a demand with the complete record and a valuation. The decision to accept any offer is yours (MRPC 1.2). If the insurer will not pay fair value, suit is filed in the right court:

- **23rd District Court,** 23365 Goddard Road in Taylor, for claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims. It serves Taylor only.
- **Third Judicial Circuit Court,** at the Coleman A. Young Municipal Center in Detroit, for injury suits over $25,000, with a jury drawn from all of Wayne County. Most injury cases there go through case evaluation (MCR 2.403) and facilitation before trial.
- **U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan,** if an out-of-state defendant removes the case there.

At settlement, we resolve the liens before you are paid, including health plans, Medicare, Medicaid, and workers’ compensation. Minors’ settlements and wrongful death distributions are approved by the Wayne County Probate Court or by the trial court (MCR 2.420; MCL 600.2922(6)). Compensation for physical injury is generally not taxable income (26 USC 104(a)(2)).

## Who the defendant is in Taylor

Taylor is a city with its own police and fire departments, but most of its main roads belong to someone else. Each of those facts changes who you claim against.

- **The roads are split three ways.** MDOT owns Telegraph, I-75, and I-94. A road-defect claim against MDOT requires written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). Wayne County owns Eureka, Allen, and other mile roads, and the City of Taylor owns the subdivision streets. A claim against the county or the City requires written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404). The notice must go to the agency that has jurisdiction over that stretch of road.
- **The sidewalks are the City’s.** The City must keep public sidewalks in reasonable repair, including those beside state and county roads (MCL 691.1402a). A fall on a broken public sidewalk is a claim against the City, subject to three conditions: the City must have known, or should have known, of the defect at least 30 days before the injury; a defect of less than two inches is presumed not to be actionable; and written notice is due within 120 days.
- **Police and fire vehicles are the City’s.** A crash with a Taylor Police car or fire truck is a claim against the City of Taylor, under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405). A crash with an ambulance is a claim against whoever operates it, public or private.
- **Public buildings belong to separate agencies.** These include City buildings, the Taylor School District, Wayne County Community College District’s Downriver campus, and county buildings. An injury caused by a dangerous or defective condition in one of them falls under the public building exception. That exception requires the agency to have known of the defect and failed to fix it, and it requires written notice to that agency within 120 days (MCL 691.1406). A private landlord, by contrast, is an ordinary defendant with a three-year deadline (MCL 554.139).
- **Buses and shuttles.** A crash with a SMART bus on Telegraph or Eureka, a school bus, or a Wayne County road truck is a claim under the motor vehicle exception. Airport shuttles may be public or private, depending on the operator. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).
- **Private businesses** are ordinary defendants. These include stores, the Southland corridor’s property owners and management companies, snow-removal contractors, hotels, and apartment and manufactured-home community owners. Since *Kandil-Elsayed v F & E Oil* (2023), an “open and obvious” hazard goes to comparative fault rather than ending the claim.

## When the other driver has no insurance

Some drivers on Taylor’s roads carry only the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)) Your own no-fault benefits are paid regardless of the other driver’s insurance, by your insurer, a household member’s insurer, or the Assigned Claims Plan.

- **Against an uninsured driver:** your pain-and-suffering claim is worth what that driver can personally pay, unless you carry **uninsured motorist coverage**.
- **Against a minimum-limits driver:** $50,000 is often the ceiling, unless you carry **underinsured motorist coverage**.
- **A hit-and-run:** usually a UM claim, with short notice terms in the policy.

Two rules cut the other way:

- An owner who was required to insure a car and did not is generally barred from PIP benefits for injuries in it (MCL 500.3113(b)).
- A driver who was operating their own uninsured vehicle cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, whoever was at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(c)).

We check every policy in the household before we value a Taylor claim. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

## What a Taylor claim is worth, and what reduces it

A claim includes two kinds of damages:

- **Economic damages:** medical expenses, lost wages, lost earning capacity, and replacement services.
- **Noneconomic damages:** pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, and disfigurement.

There is no cap except in medical malpractice. In an auto case, noneconomic damages require a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A premises or dog bite case has no such threshold.

Your share of fault reduces the recovery, and above 50 percent it bars noneconomic damages (MCL 600.2959; in auto cases, MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). At exactly 50 percent, you recover half. Where more than one party caused the injury, each defendant generally pays only its own share of fault (MCL 600.2956, 600.6304), so every responsible party has to be identified and named. Michigan does not award punitive damages in ordinary injury cases. Exemplary damages, for humiliation or outrage, are available only in narrow circumstances.

In many Taylor cases, the practical limit is insurance:

- The at-fault driver’s liability limit defaults to $250,000/$500,000 unless the driver chose lower limits, down to the $50,000/$100,000 minimum (MCL 500.3009(1)). Some drivers carry nothing.
- Your own UM and UIM coverage fills the gap.
- In a premises, workplace, or nursing home case, the commercial policy of the property owner, contractor, or nursing home operator pays.

Where UIM is involved, settling with the at-fault driver without your own insurer’s consent can forfeit the UIM claim. For a worker with a third-party claim, the workers’ compensation insurer has a lien on the recovery (MCL 418.827), and how that lien is negotiated changes what you take home. Medicare and Medicaid liens are negotiated the same way.

## What it costs

Michigan caps contingency fees in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery (MCR 8.121). Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted from the recovery first; the one-third fee is calculated on the remaining amount.

The agreement is in writing. Read how costs are handled if the case is lost, such as filing fees, records, experts, and depositions, because that is where firms differ. A promise that “you pay nothing unless we win” should say whether that includes costs, and whether “win” means the same thing as “settle.”

1. **Who will handle my case day to day, and how do I reach them?**
2. How many cases like mine have you handled in the 23rd District and the Wayne County Circuit Court?

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in Taylor?** Three years for most claims. But some deadlines are much shorter: - one year to notify your no-fault insurer after a crash - 120 days to notify Wayne County, the City, or another local agency of a defect - 120 days for a defect on Telegraph or the freeways. - two years to sue a bar - a 182-day notice and a two-year limit for malpractice

**I was hit by a Taylor Police car. Who do I claim against?** The City of Taylor, under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity. The patrol car’s video and the dispatch records should be requested right away.

**I fell on a broken sidewalk on a county road. Is that Wayne County’s claim?** Usually not. The City of Taylor has the duty to keep public sidewalks in repair, including those beside county and state roads. Written notice is due to the City within 120 days. **[ATTORNEY CHECK]**

**I don’t have car insurance and was hit while walking. Who pays my medical bills?** If no one in your household has an auto policy, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan pays your PIP benefits (MCL 500.3115). You also have a claim against the driver if your injury meets the threshold.

**What does a Taylor personal injury attorney charge?** Michigan caps the fee in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery, with no fee if there is no recovery. Ask how costs are handled.

**Where would my Taylor case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000 go to the 23rd District Court on Goddard Road. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Third Judicial Circuit Court in Detroit.

**If I was partly at fault, do I still have a claim?** Yes, unless you were more than 50 percent at fault, which bars pain-and-suffering damages. Below that, your recovery is reduced by your share, and at exactly 50 percent you recover half. No-fault benefits are paid regardless of fault.

**Do I need a lawyer whose office is in Taylor?** No. What matters is that the lawyer knows the courts your case will be in and the agencies, insurers, and property owners involved, and will meet you where you are.

**The insurer already offered me money. Should I take it?** Not before you know what the claim is worth and what the release gives up. If UIM may apply, not before your UIM insurer has consented.
