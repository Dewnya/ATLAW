## Where Taylor crashes happen

**Telegraph Road (US-24).** Telegraph is a multi-lane road through the city, from Van Born at the north end to the Brownstown line at the south. Its signals at Eureka, Goddard, Northline, Ecorse, and Van Born carry heavy left-turn traffic into the shopping centers and apartment complexes. The crashes are:

- left turns across several lanes of oncoming traffic
- red-light crashes
- rear-end crashes in the stacked turn lanes
- drivers pulling out of driveways between signals
- pedestrians crossing between bus stops and complexes

Fault at a Telegraph left turn is governed by the duty to yield to oncoming traffic (MCL 257.650). Telegraph belongs to MDOT.

**Eureka Road and the Southland corridor.** Eureka runs east from Telegraph past Southland Center, the big-box stores, and the restaurants, to Pardee, Allen, and the I-75 interchange. The typical crashes involve:

- driveways and parking-lot exits
- rear-end crashes in stop-and-go traffic
- lane changes into an occupied lane (MCL 257.642(1)(a))
- pedestrians in the lots and crossing Eureka between stores

A crash in a store’s parking lot can also involve the property owner if lighting, signs, or the lot’s layout contributed. See our [premises liability page](/personal-injury/premises-liability).

**I-75 and I-94.** I-75 crosses the south side of the city, with interchanges at Telegraph, Eureka, and Allen/Northline. I-94 crosses the north side, with interchanges at Ecorse, Telegraph, and Pelham, and continues to the airport. The crashes are:

- merging and lane changes at the interchanges
- rear-end crashes in backups
- truck crashes
- crashes in work zones

Freeway crashes are often worked by the Michigan State Police. See our [Taylor truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/taylor) and our [Taylor motorcycle accident page](/personal-injury/motorcycle-accidents/taylor).

**The mile roads and neighborhoods.** Goddard, Northline, Van Born, Ecorse, Pelham, Beech Daly, Allen, Pardee, and Inkster pass schools, parks, and the Wayne County Community College District’s Downriver campus. The crashes are signal and stop-sign crashes, crashes at uncontrolled subdivision intersections, and pedestrian and bicycle crashes near the schools. See our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents) and our [bicycle accident page](/personal-injury/bicycle-accidents).

**Whose road it is: the State, the county, or the City.** Taylor is a city, and it owns its subdivision streets. The main roads belong to two other agencies:

- MDOT: Telegraph, I-75, and I-94. A road-defect claim against MDOT goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). (Champine, Mich 2022.)
- Wayne County: Eureka, Allen, and other mile roads, through its Department of Public Services. A claim against the county takes written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404).
- **The City of Taylor:** the subdivision streets, with the same 120-day notice.

A pothole or other defect in the road can make the agency a defendant alongside the other driver; on State and county roads, signals, signs, and sight obstructions generally fall outside the highway exception (*Nawrocki v Macomb County Road Commission*, Mich 2000). The notice must go to the agency that actually has jurisdiction over that stretch of road, or it is no notice. **[ATTORNEY CHECK: road allocation and signal maintenance at Eureka and Telegraph]** The City also has the duty to keep public sidewalks in repair (MCL 691.1402a); see our [sidewalk injury page](/personal-injury/sidewalk-injuries).

## Fault is the case

In a rear-end crash, fault is rarely disputed. At a Telegraph left turn, an Eureka driveway, or a subdivision intersection, it often is, and the other driver’s insurer may try to assign you half or more of the fault. That share matters:

- **More than 50 percent at fault:** you lose your claim for pain and suffering.
- **50 percent or less:** you can recover, reduced by your share. At exactly 50 percent, you recover half.

The rule is in MCL 500.3135(2)(b). The evidence that decides fault is perishable:

- the Taylor Police Department crash report and diagram, or the State Police report for a freeway crash
- **signal timing data** from MDOT, Wayne County, or the City
- cameras at stores, gas stations, apartment complexes, and businesses, many overwritten within days
- the vehicles’ **event data recorders**, which store speed, braking, and throttle in the seconds before impact
- the other driver’s phone records, for a distracted-driving claim (see our [distracted driving page](/personal-injury/distracted-driving-accidents))
- witnesses, including bus riders and shoppers who saw the crash

We move promptly to request the camera footage, signal data, and vehicle data.

## Who pays after a Taylor crash

**Your no-fault (PIP) claim.** Your own auto insurer pays these benefits regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107):

- your medical expenses, up to the PIP level you chose
- 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- replacement services at $20 a day
- attendant care

If you have no policy, a resident relative’s policy pays (MCL 500.3114). If none exists, an occupant claims from the insurer of the vehicle they were in (MCL 500.3114(4)), and then the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3172), capped at $250,000 for medical benefits. A pedestrian or cyclist hit by a car, with no household policy, claims through the Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3115).

The insurer must receive written notice within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). It must pay each bill within 30 days of reasonable proof, or owe 12 percent interest (MCL 500.3142). Since the 2019 reform, PIP medical coverage is no longer unlimited unless you chose it, so the level on your declarations page matters. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance) and our [Michigan no-fault guide](/resources/michigan-no-fault-guide).

**If you had no insurance.** A person who owned a car and was required to insure it, but did not, is generally barred from PIP benefits for their own injuries (MCL 500.3113(b)). A driver who was operating their own uninsured vehicle cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, whoever was at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(c)). A passenger or pedestrian with no policy of their own is not barred, and the Assigned Claims Plan may be the payer.

**Out-of-state drivers and rental cars.** Taylor sits next to the airport, so rental cars and out-of-state drivers are part of the traffic on Telegraph and I-94. If one of them hits you, two separate questions arise:

- **Your benefits:** your own Michigan no-fault benefits are paid as usual.
- **Your claim against the driver:** this can involve the rental company’s coverage, the driver’s home-state policy, and the out-of-state insurer’s certification to provide Michigan coverage (MCL 500.3163).

A non-resident generally has no Michigan PIP claim of their own unless they own a vehicle registered and insured in Michigan (MCL 500.3113(c)).

**Your claim against the at-fault driver.** For pain and suffering, you need a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment is an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life. It does not have to be permanent (MCL 500.3135(5); *McCormick v Carrier*, 2010).

You can also recover lost wages beyond the three years or above the monthly cap, and medical bills above your chosen PIP level (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)). The suit must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The mini tort claim recovers up to $3,000 in vehicle damage not covered by your policy (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)). If the at-fault driver’s insurer will not pay it, the claim is filed in the 23rd District Court.

**When the other driver has no insurance, or the minimum.** Some drivers on Taylor’s roads carry the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5))

- **Against an uninsured driver:** your pain-and-suffering claim is worth what that driver can personally pay, unless you carry **uninsured motorist coverage**.
- **Against a minimum-limits driver:** $50,000 is often the ceiling, unless you carry **underinsured motorist coverage**. Most UIM policies require your insurer’s written consent before you accept the at-fault driver’s limit, and settling without it can forfeit the UIM claim.
- **A hit-and-run:** usually a UM claim, and the policy’s notice terms are often short.

We read every policy in the household, including umbrella coverage, before valuing the claim. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Drunk drivers and dram shop.** Wayne County recorded 1,331 alcohol-involved crashes in 2024. A crash caused by a drunk driver leaving a bar or restaurant on Telegraph or Eureka can add a claim against the business, if it served a visibly intoxicated person or a minor. That claim has its own rules (MCL 436.1801(3), (4)):

- Written notice must go to the business within 120 days after you hire a lawyer.
- The drunk driver must be named and kept in the suit.
- The suit must be filed within two years, not three.

See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**Police cars, fire trucks, and buses.** Crashes with Taylor Police and Fire vehicles, SMART buses on Telegraph and Eureka, school buses, and Wayne County road trucks are claims under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405). Dash camera and dispatch records are overwritten on short schedules, so we request them by FOIA in the first week. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

## What to do after a Taylor crash

1. **Call 911 and get the police report.** Michigan requires a report for any crash with injury or $1,000 in damage (MCL 257.622). Crashes in the city are worked by the Taylor Police Department, and freeway crashes may be worked by the Michigan State Police. **[VERIFY: report request process]**
2. **Photograph the scene.** Get the vehicles, the plates, the other driver’s license and insurance card, the signals, and the lane markings. If the other driver shows an out-of-state or rental-car document, photograph that too. Note every business with a camera facing the scene, because footage is kept for days, not weeks.
3. **Get medical care the same day.** Corewell Health Taylor, on Telegraph, has an emergency department. The most serious injuries often go to a trauma center on the State’s list, such as Corewell Health Dearborn or Corewell Health Trenton (Level II), or Detroit Receiving Hospital or Henry Ford Hospital (Level I) in Detroit.
4. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** within the first week. If you have no policy, a lawyer can identify the insurer next in priority or the Assigned Claims Plan.
5. **Read your declarations page** for your PIP level, UM/UIM, umbrella, and collision coverage.
6. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the other driver’s insurer, sign a release, or accept a policy-limits offer before your UIM insurer consents.
7. **Call a lawyer within days** if fault is disputed, the other driver is uninsured or from out of state, or the road played a part. Camera footage and signal data are the first evidence lost, and the 120-day notice to Wayne County or the City is already running.

## The courts

- **Claims up to $25,000,** including mini tort claims, are filed in the 23rd District Court at 23365 Goddard Road in Taylor.
- **Injury suits over $25,000** go to the Third Judicial Circuit Court at the Coleman A. Young Municipal Center in Detroit, with a jury drawn from all of Wayne County.
- **Cases against an out-of-state driver or carrier** may be filed in, or removed to, the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan.
- **Defect claims against MDOT** go to the Court of Claims.
- **A fatal crash** is brought by the estate, opened through the Wayne County Probate Court. See our [Taylor wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death).

## Frequently asked questions

**How many crashes are there in Taylor?** The State Police recorded 1,916 crashes in Taylor in 2024. They killed 12 people and injured 547, 26 of them seriously. Eureka at Telegraph had 68 crashes and 36 injuries, the fifth-highest crash count of any intersection in Wayne County.

**The driver who hit me on Telegraph had no insurance. What now?** Your own no-fault insurer, a household member’s insurer, or the Assigned Claims Plan pays your medical bills and lost wages regardless of fault. Your pain-and-suffering claim depends on whether you carry uninsured motorist coverage. Without it, the claim is worth what the uninsured driver can personally pay. Check your declarations page today.

**I was hit by an out-of-state driver in a rental car. Who pays?** Your own no-fault insurer pays your benefits as usual. Your claim against the driver can involve the rental company’s coverage, the driver’s home policy, and that insurer’s Michigan certification. The order depends on the rental agreement and the policies.

**A pothole caused my crash.** **Do I file against the City of Taylor?** It depends on the road: - **Telegraph, I-75, and I-94** belong to MDOT. Notice is filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days. - Eureka, Allen, and other mile roads belong to Wayne County. Written notice is due within 120 days. - **Subdivision streets** belong to the City, with the same 120 days.

**I was hit while walking on Eureka or Telegraph. Who pays my medical bills?** Your own household’s auto policy pays if you have one. If not, the Assigned Claims Plan pays. (MCL 500.3115.) A pedestrian has the same pain-and-suffering claim as a driver, subject to the threshold.

**Where would my Taylor case be filed?** Mini tort claims and claims up to $25,000 go to the 23rd District Court on Goddard Road. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Third Judicial Circuit Court in Detroit.

**How long do I have?** - One year to give your no-fault insurer written notice. - Three years to sue the at-fault driver. - Two years to sue a bar. - 120 days for a notice to Wayne County or the City. - 120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT.
