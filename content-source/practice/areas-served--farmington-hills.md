## Personal Injury Law Firm Serving Farmington Hills, MI

Farmington Hills is Oakland County’s second-largest city, about 83,000 people between Eight Mile and Fourteen Mile, Inkster and Haggerty, and it is where the western suburbs’ freeways converge: I-696 ends, I-96 and I-275 pass, and M-5 begins, all at one interchange on the city’s southwest corner. The Twelve Mile and Halsted office corridor brings tens of thousands of engineers and office workers into the city daily, Orchard Lake Road and Grand River carry its retail traffic, and its subdivisions and senior communities serve a population in which approximately 21 percent of residents are age 65 or older. In 2024, Farmington Hills recorded 2,672 traffic crashes, with 540 people injured and three killed, according to Michigan Traffic Crash Facts.

## Where Farmington Hills injuries happen

**The interchange.** I-696, I-96, I-275, and M-5 converge at a major freeway interchange near the city’s southwest corner. MDOT completed reconstruction of the adjoining I-96 corridor in 2024 and the western I-696 corridor in 2025, improving pavement, bridges, and related infrastructure. See our [Farmington Hills truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/farmington-hills).

**Orchard Lake Road.** The city’s main north-south arterial, a boulevard with Michigan-left turnarounds between Twelve and Fourteen Mile, lined with retail, the hospital, and the civic center, and carrying commuter traffic to and from the I-696 interchange. Turnaround and crossover crashes at the Michigan lefts, left-turn and red-light crashes at Twelve Mile, Thirteen Mile, and Fourteen Mile, and pedestrian crashes at the retail strips. Orchard Lake Road is the Road Commission for Oakland County’s.

**Twelve Mile and the office corridor.** From Orchard Lake west to Haggerty, Twelve Mile and Halsted carry the engineering and headquarters campuses that make Farmington Hills a regional employment center: Bosch, Nissan’s technical center, and the office parks around them. Commuter crashes at the campus driveways and the Halsted and Haggerty signals, parking-structure crashes, and falls on campus walkways and in lobbies; workplace injuries inside, where workers’ compensation is the exclusive remedy against the employer (MCL 418.131) but a third-party claim survives. See our [premises liability](/personal-injury/premises-liability) and [workers’ compensation](/personal-injury/workers-compensation) pages.

**Grand River (M-5), Middlebelt, Inkster, Farmington Road, and the mile roads.** Grand River’s diagonal through the south of the city, past the Farmington downtown and the retail strips; Middlebelt and Inkster along the east; Farmington Road through the center; and Eight through Fourteen Mile. Intersection crashes at the signals and pedestrian crashes at the strips and near the schools. MDOT, the Road Commission for Oakland County, and the City of Farmington Hills maintain different portions of these roadways, depending on the location and applicable jurisdiction.

**The neighborhoods and the senior communities.** Farmington Hills’ housing runs from mid-century subdivisions to the lake-area estates, with condominiums and apartment complexes along Grand River, Twelve Mile, and Orchard Lake and a range of senior apartments, assisted-living communities, and nursing-care facilities. ## What is different about a Farmington Hills claim

**Coverage.** Farmington Hills drivers carry more insurance on average than drivers in most of the region, and the typical at-fault driver here has a policy worth pursuing; the freeway traffic, however, comes from everywhere. Your own no-fault insurer pays your medical bills and lost wages regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107), or the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan does if no household policy applies (MCL 500.3172, capped at $250,000); the claim against the at-fault driver requires a threshold injury (MCL 500.3135) and is worth no more than the insurance available unless you carry uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage. If the at-fault driver’s liability coverage is insufficient, your own underinsured motorist coverage may provide additional compensation, depending on the policy’s terms, limits, offsets, and exclusions. Before accepting a settlement with the at-fault driver, review any notice, consent-to-settle, or other requirements in your UIM policy to avoid jeopardizing available benefits. See our [Farmington Hills car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents/farmington-hills), [no-fault insurance guide](about:blank), and [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Three road agencies.** MDOT has jurisdiction over state trunklines such as I-696, I-96, I-275, M-5, and designated portions of Grand River Avenue and Eight Mile Road. The Road Commission for Oakland County maintains certain major roads within Farmington Hills, including portions of the mile-road network and other designated arterials. The City maintains local streets and other roads under municipal jurisdiction. Responsibility for individual segments of Orchard Lake, Middlebelt, Inkster, Farmington Road, Halsted, and Haggerty should be checked against the City’s roadway-jurisdiction map. A qualifying defect in the improved portion of a public roadway may support a claim against the responsible governmental agency under the highway exception to governmental immunity, MCL 691.1402. Claims based solely on malfunctioning traffic signals, inadequate signage, or obstructed sight lines generally do not fall within that exception. Qualifying highway-defect claims generally require written notice within 120 days under MCL 691.1404, including claims against MDOT, for which notice must be filed with the Court of Claims as required by statute.

**Government vehicles.** Crashes involving Farmington Hills Police or Fire Department vehicles, SMART buses, Farmington Public Schools buses, or RCOC trucks may support claims under the Governmental Tort Liability Act’s motor vehicle exception, MCL 691.1405, when injuries result from negligent operation of government-owned vehicles. Certain tort claims against SMART also require written notice within 60 days under MCL 124.419. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

**The 47th District and the Sixth Circuit.** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims for vehicle damage (MCL 500.3135(3)(e), up to $3,000), are filed in the 47th District Court at 31605 W. Eleven Mile Road, which serves Farmington Hills and Farmington. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac; wrongful death estates (MCL 600.2922) and minors’ settlements (MCR 2.420) to the Oakland County Probate Court on the same campus. For crashes near Eight Mile Road or Haggerty Road, or involving defendants from another county, venue is determined under the statutory order of priority in MCL 600.1629, generally beginning with the county where the original injury occurred and where a defendant resides, conducts business, or maintains a registered office.

**The hospitals.** Corewell Health Farmington Hills Hospital (formerly Botsford), on Grand River Avenue, is a Level II trauma center serving the city. Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak, a Level I trauma center, Henry Ford Providence hospitals in Novi and Southfield, and Trinity Health Livonia Hospital also serve injured patients from Farmington Hills and surrounding communities.

## Frequently asked questions

**I was hurt in a crash at the I-696/I-96/I-275/M-5 interchange. Who is responsible for the road?** MDOT. A claim that the interchange’s design, signage, or maintenance contributed goes to the Court of Claims with a six-month notice. The claim against the other driver is unaffected.

**I was hurt in a Michigan-left turnaround crash on Orchard Lake Road. Who is at fault?** Usually the driver who failed to yield at the turnaround or ran the signal, but these crashes are disputed often, and the signal phase and the turnaround’s sight lines matter. Photograph the turnaround and the signals, get the witnesses, and note the Road Commission’s cameras at the signalized turnarounds.

**The other driver’s insurer offered their $250,000 policy limit. Should I take it?** Review your own underinsured motorist coverage before accepting the settlement. Some UIM policies require advance notice or the insurer’s consent before you release the at-fault driver. Settling without following those requirements may jeopardize your UIM benefits. An attorney can review the policy, coordinate any required notice or consent, and assess whether additional compensation may be available. Recovery under both policies is not guaranteed.

**I fell at an office campus on Twelve Mile. Who is responsible?** Responsibility depends on who possessed or controlled the property, who was responsible for maintaining the area, and whether negligence contributed to the injury. Since Kandil-Elsayed v. F & E Oil, Inc. (2023), an open-and-obvious hazard does not automatically eliminate a property possessor’s duty; its obviousness may be considered in determining breach of duty and comparative fault. Incident reports and available surveillance footage may be important evidence, so preservation should be requested promptly. If the injury occurred in the course of employment, a workers' compensation claim may also be available, and a separate claim against a negligent third party may be possible.

**Where would my Farmington Hills injury case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, in the 47th District Court on Eleven Mile; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court in Pontiac.

**My parent was injured in a Farmington Hills nursing home. Is that malpractice or negligence?** It depends on the circumstances and the type of care involved. Under Michigan law, ordinary negligence generally concerns conduct that can be evaluated using common knowledge, while medical malpractice involves professional medical judgment or specialized standards of care. A fall during a transfer or a pressure injury may involve either type of claim, depending on the allegations and evidence. Ordinary negligence claims generally have a three-year limitations period, while medical malpractice claims generally have a two-year period and additional procedural requirements, including a notice of intent. Because the applicable deadline depends on the claim, prompt legal review is important.

**How much does it cost to hire you?** Nothing up front, and no fee unless we recover money for you. Michigan caps contingency fees in personal injury cases at one-third of the recovery (MCR 8.121).

**How long do I have?** Three years for most injury suits, one year to notify your no-fault insurer in writing, and as little as 120 days for a claim against the City of Farmington Hills or the RCOC.
