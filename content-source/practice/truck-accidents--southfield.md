## Where Southfield truck crashes happen

**I-696 and the work zone.** Merges at five interchanges in four miles, lane shifts and narrowed shoulders through the reconstruction, and stopped traffic at the end of each closure. Rear-end and underride crashes at the back of a queue, sideswipes in narrowed lanes, and crashes with construction trucks entering and leaving the work zone. A crash in a work zone carries enhanced penalties for the at-fault driver (MCL 257.601b), and a contractor whose traffic control did not meet the Michigan Manual of Uniform Traffic Control Devices can be a defendant alongside the carrier. **[ATTORNEY CHECK: 257.601b; contractor liability and MDOT immunity]**

**Telegraph, the Lodge, and Northwestern.** Through-trucks on Telegraph turning across four lanes at 10, 11, and 12 Mile; the Lodge’s stop-and-go where it meets Northwestern; and delivery trucks entering and leaving the office-tower driveways and loading docks along Northwestern and at the Town Center.

**Eight Mile and the mile roads.** Freight along the Detroit line and the mile roads from Nine to Thirteen, with box trucks and tractor-trailers serving the Telegraph and Greenfield retail and industrial parcels; turning and backing crashes that injure drivers of smaller vehicles, cyclists, and pedestrians (see our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents)).

**Whose road it is.** Telegraph, Northwestern, the Lodge, Eight Mile, and I-696 are MDOT’s; a road-defect claim against the State goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404); the design of a work zone and its signs generally fall outside the highway exception. The mile roads are the Road Commission for Oakland County’s and the local streets the City of Southfield’s, with 120 days’ notice (MCL 691.1404).

## Who is responsible, and who pays

**The carrier, not just the driver.** The motor carrier is responsible for its drivers’ compliance with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations and is liable for a driver’s negligence in the course of employment; Michigan applies the federal rules to intrastate carriers (MCL 480.11a), so a local hauler is held to largely the same standards as an interstate carrier. The rules that decide Southfield cases most often:

| Rule | 49 CFR | What it means for the case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hours of service | Part 395 | 11 hours driving in a 14-hour window after 10 off; 60/70-hour weekly limits; recorded on an electronic logging device. Time stopped in work-zone queues counts as on-duty time. |
| Driver qualification | Part 391 | CDL, medical certificate, driving record, background check; negligent hiring and retention are claims against the carrier. |
| Post-crash testing | Part 382 | Alcohol within 8 hours and drugs within 32 after a fatal crash, or an injury or tow-away crash with a citation. |
| Inspection and maintenance | Part 396 | Pre-trip inspections, defect reports, maintenance records; brake and tire cases are maintenance cases. |
| Cargo securement | Part 393 | Securement standards; shifted loads cause rollovers on ramps. |
| Handheld phone ban; hazardous conditions | 392.82, 392.14 | No handheld phone use; reduce speed or stop in snow, ice, and fog. |
| Minimum insurance | Part 387 | $750,000 general freight; $1 million oil; $5 million hazardous materials. |

**Michigan no-fault still applies to you.** Your own auto insurer pays your medical bills, 85 percent of lost wages up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each October, replacement services, and attendant care regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107), with written notice due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)); the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan pays if no household policy applies (MCL 500.3172). Your claim against the driver and the carrier for pain and suffering and losses beyond no-fault requires a threshold injury (MCL 500.3135) and must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The difference from a car case is the money available: the carrier’s $750,000-plus policy, often with excess coverage above it. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

**Other defendants.** The shipper or broker that loaded the trailer or hired an unsafe carrier; the maintenance contractor; the manufacturer of a failed component ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability)); the trailer’s owner; the construction contractor in a work-zone crash; and, for a government truck (City of Southfield, RCOC, MDOT, SMART), the agency under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405). **If you were the truck driver**, workers’ compensation pays your benefits (MCL 418.131) and you can sue a third party whose negligence caused the crash; see our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).

## The evidence, and how fast it disappears

Carriers must keep hours-of-service records for six months (49 CFR 395.8) and driver inspection reports for three months (49 CFR 396.11); after that they may lawfully be destroyed. MDOT freeway cameras, office-tower and retail cameras, and dash cameras overwrite in days to weeks. The truck’s engine control module and event data recorder can be cleared when it is repaired and returned to service. The first act in a Southfield truck case is a **preservation letter** to the carrier, its insurer, the trailer owner, the shipper, and, in a work-zone crash, the construction contractor, demanding the ELD and ECM data, dash camera footage, the driver’s qualification file and post-crash test results, dispatch and bill-of-lading records, maintenance files, the contractor’s traffic-control plan and daily logs, and the truck itself for inspection. A party that destroys evidence after receiving the letter faces spoliation sanctions.

## What to do after a Southfield truck crash

1. **Call 911 and get medical care the same day.** Henry Ford Providence Southfield Hospital (formerly Ascension Providence) on Nine Mile and Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak treat many of the area’s seriously injured; Henry Ford Providence Southfield is a Level II trauma center and Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital is Level I.
2. **Photograph the truck**: the cab, the trailer, the USDOT number and carrier name on the door, both plates, the load, and, in a work zone, the signs, barrels, and lane markings.
3. **Get the police report.** Southfield Police or the Michigan State Police respond on I-696; the UD-10 is purchased for $15 at michigan.gov/tcps. A fatal or serious crash may produce a separate MSP Commercial Vehicle Enforcement post-crash inspection report.
4. **Do not speak to the carrier’s insurer or its investigator.**
5. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** within the first week.
6. **Call a lawyer within days**, so the preservation letter goes out before the records expire.

## The courts and deadlines

Most Southfield truck cases are filed in the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court (Oakland County Circuit Court) in Pontiac; cases against out-of-state carriers may be removed to or filed in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan; a crash on Eight Mile, or with a Wayne County defendant, may allow filing in the Third Circuit in Detroit (MCL 600.1629); claims against the State go to the Court of Claims; smaller claims go to the 46th District Court on Evergreen Road. ## Frequently asked questions

**How many truck accidents happen in Southfield?**  Countywide, 2,081 heavy trucks and buses were involved in Oakland County crashes in 2024.

**I was hit by a truck in the I-696 construction zone. Who is responsible?** The driver and carrier, if the driver was speeding, following too closely, or over hours; and possibly the construction contractor, if the lane shifts, signs, or barrels did not meet the required standards. A claim against the State is limited to a defect in the roadbed itself; the State is generally immune for the work zone’s design and signs. Photograph the work zone that day.

**Who pays my medical bills after a truck crash in Southfield?** Your own no-fault insurer, regardless of fault, after you file an Application for No-Fault Benefits; the Assigned Claims Plan if no household policy applies. The carrier’s insurer pays for pain and suffering and losses beyond no-fault once fault and a threshold injury are established.

**Why does the lawyer need to act within days?** Because the records expire: six months for hours-of-service logs, three months for inspection reports, days or weeks for camera footage, and the truck’s electronic data can be cleared on repair.

**Is a truck case worth more than a car case?** The injuries are usually worse and the insurance is usually larger: $750,000 minimum for a general freight carrier against $50,000 for a minimally insured car.
