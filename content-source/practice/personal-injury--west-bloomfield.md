## West Bloomfield, MI Personal Injury Lawyer

If you were hurt in West Bloomfield because someone else was careless, you have a claim under Michigan law, and its deadlines start the day you are hurt:

- **After a crash,** your no-fault insurer must have written notice within one year.
- A road defect needs written notice within 120 days to the Road Commission for Oakland County, not to the township. A defect on a state trunkline needs written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days.
- **A defect in a public pathway or sidewalk, or in a township building,** needs written notice to West Bloomfield Township within 120 days.
- **A claim against a bar, restaurant, club, or marina** that served a drunk driver or boater needs written notice within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, and suit within two years.
- **A medical malpractice suit,** including claims about hospital care and many claims against nursing homes, requires 182 days’ notice first and must be filed within two years.
- **Most other claims,** including boating and dock injuries, falls, dog bites, and crashes, allow three years.

A personal injury lawyer’s first job is to find out which of those deadlines apply to you. The second is to make sure none runs out before your claim is paid.

## Who the defendant is in West Bloomfield

West Bloomfield is a charter township. It runs its own police and fire departments, parks, and buildings, but it does not own the public roads. Its lakes, clubs, and associations add defendants most cities do not have.

- **The roads belong to the Road Commission, not the township.** The Road Commission for Oakland County owns Orchard Lake Road, Maple, the mile roads, the lake roads, and the public subdivision streets, with written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404). MDOT owns M-10 (Northwestern Highway), which ends at Orchard Lake Road in the township, with written notice of a road defect filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). Private roads in some subdivisions and lake associations belong to the association or the owners, as an ordinary premises claim. A notice sent to the township about a road defect is a notice to the wrong defendant.
- **Pathways and sidewalks: the township.** West Bloomfield has many miles of pathways along its roads. The sidewalk statute puts the repair duty on the municipal corporation in which the sidewalk is installed, which includes a township (MCL 691.1402a, 691.1401). A fall on a broken public pathway is a claim against the township, with three limits: the township must have known, or should have known, of the defect at least 30 days before the injury; a defect of less than two inches is presumed not to be actionable; and written notice is due within 120 days. Whether a given path counts as a “sidewalk” under the statute, and whether a township ordinance shifts any duty to the adjacent owner, has to be checked. Trails inside a park are treated differently. See our [sidewalk injury page](/personal-injury/sidewalk-injuries).
- **Township buildings, parks, and schools.** An injury caused by a dangerous or defective condition in a public building falls under the public building exception, which requires that the agency knew of the defect and failed to fix it, and written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1406). That covers township buildings and the buildings of the school districts that serve the township. An injury on park grounds or a public beach outside a building generally falls outside every exception.
- **Police and fire are the township’s.** A crash with a West Bloomfield Police car or fire truck is a claim against the township, under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405). EMS treatment claims are limited to gross negligence or willful misconduct (MCL 333.20965).
- **Boat operators and owners.** No-fault does not apply to a boat. A person hurt in a boating, jet-ski, or tubing incident on Pine, Orchard, Cass, Walnut, Union, or another lake has an ordinary negligence claim against the operator. The Marine Safety Act sets the operating rules, and a violation is evidence of negligence (MCL 324.80101 and following). The boat’s owner is liable for injuries caused by its negligent operation by someone using it with the owner’s consent (MCL 324.80157). The coverage is usually a watercraft or homeowner’s policy.
- **Docks, seawalls, beaches, and lake associations.** A fall from a rotted dock, a dive into shallow water, or an injury at a lake association’s beach is a premises claim against the owner or the association. Michigan’s Recreational Land Use Act limits the liability of a landowner who lets people use land for recreation without charge, unless there is gross negligence or willful and wanton misconduct (MCL 324.73301). Whether it applies to an association’s members, or to guests at a private home, is a question in each case.
- **Clubs, bars, and marinas.** A club, restaurant, bar, or marina that serves a visibly intoxicated person, or a minor, is liable for the injuries that person causes, on the road or on the water (MCL 436.1801). The claim needs written notice within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, the intoxicated person named and kept in the suit, and suit within two years. See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).
- **Stores, restaurants, and shopping centers.** A fall in a store, restaurant, or parking lot along Orchard Lake Road, Maple, or Haggerty is a premises claim against the owner, the tenant, or both, depending on who controlled the area. Snow and ice in the lots are often handled by a contractor, which can be a separate defendant. Since *Kandil-Elsayed v F & E Oil* (2023), an “open and obvious” hazard such as visible ice goes to comparative fault rather than ending the claim. The deadline is three years. See our [slip and fall](/personal-injury/slip-and-fall) and [premises liability](/personal-injury/premises-liability) pages.
- **Condominiums and apartments.** A condominium association is responsible for the common elements under its bylaws and the Condominium Act, including walks, steps, parking areas, and snow removal it controls. A landlord must keep rental premises and common areas fit for their intended use and in reasonable repair (MCL 554.139). See our [tenant injury page](/personal-injury/tenant-injuries).
- **Dog owners.** An owner is liable for a bite when the person bitten was lawfully present and did not provoke the dog (MCL 287.351). Report the bite to the West Bloomfield Police Department. See our [dog bite page](/personal-injury/dog-bites).
- **Buses.** A crash with a school bus a district runs itself is a claim under the motor vehicle exception. Walled Lake’s buses are run by a private contractor, so a claim over one of them is an ordinary negligence claim. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

## When the other driver has no insurance, or not enough

In a serious crash, the at-fault driver’s liability limit may be less than the claim is worth. There may be two more layers: an umbrella policy over the at-fault driver’s auto policy, and your own **underinsured motorist coverage**. The sequence matters:

1. Find out whether the at-fault driver has an umbrella policy, and give notice to that carrier.
2. The at-fault driver’s insurer offers its limit.
3. You notify your own insurer and ask for its written consent to settle.
4. Only then do you accept the limit and sign the release.
5. The umbrella and UIM claims then proceed.

Accepting without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under most policies. Your own umbrella policy may also provide underinsured coverage, but only if its terms say so.

Against an uninsured driver, the claim for pain and suffering depends on **uninsured motorist coverage**. A hit-and-run is usually a UM claim, with short notice terms in the policy. Two rules cut the other way:

- An owner who was required to insure a car and did not is generally barred from PIP benefits (MCL 500.3113(b)).
- A driver who was operating their own uninsured vehicle cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, whoever was at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(c)).

You do not have to use up your no-fault benefits before suing the at-fault driver. The claim for pain and suffering can be brought once the injury meets the threshold, within three years, while no-fault benefits are still being paid. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

## What a West Bloomfield claim is worth, and what reduces it

A claim includes two kinds of damages:

- **Economic damages:** medical expenses, lost wages, lost earning capacity, and replacement services.
- **Noneconomic damages:** pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, and disfigurement.

There is no cap except in medical malpractice. In an auto case, noneconomic damages require a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment does not have to be permanent (*McCormick v Carrier*, 2010). A boating, premises, dog bite, or dram shop case has no such threshold.

Your share of fault reduces the recovery, and above 50 percent it bars noneconomic damages (MCL 600.2959; in auto cases, MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). At exactly 50 percent, you recover half. Where more than one party caused the injury, each defendant generally pays only its own share of fault (MCL 600.2956, 600.6304). That is why the boat operator, the boat’s owner, and the club that served the operator all have to be identified and named. Michigan does not award punitive damages in ordinary injury cases. Exemplary damages for humiliation or outrage are available only in narrow circumstances.

You cannot sue the at-fault party’s liability insurer directly in Michigan. The claim is against the driver, owner, or business, and its insurer defends and pays.

The practical limit is often insurance:

- **The at-fault driver’s limit** defaults to $250,000/$500,000 unless the driver chose lower limits, down to the $50,000/$100,000 minimum (MCL 500.3009(1)).
- **Umbrella policies** often sit above it, on both sides.
- **Your UIM coverage** fills the gap.
- **In a boating case,** the watercraft or homeowner’s policy pays, and an umbrella may apply.
- **In a premises or nursing home case,** the commercial policy of the owner, association, or operator pays.

For a worker with a third-party claim, the workers’ compensation insurer has a lien on the recovery (MCL 418.827). Medicare, Medicaid, and health plan liens are negotiated the same way.

## What it costs

Michigan caps contingency fees in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery (MCR 8.121). Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted from the recovery first; the one-third fee is calculated on the remaining amount. You pay nothing up front and nothing if there is no recovery.

The agreement is in writing. Read how costs are handled if the case is lost, because that is where firms differ: filing fees, records, experts, and depositions. A promise that “you pay nothing unless we win” should say whether that includes costs, and whether “win” means the same thing as “settle.” A success rate quoted without saying which cases it counts tells you little.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in West Bloomfield?** Three years for most claims. Several deadlines are much shorter: - one year to notify your no-fault insurer after a crash - 120 days to notify the Road Commission of a road defect, or the township of a pathway or building defect - 120 days for a road-defect notice on a state trunkline - 120 days for a dram shop notice after you hire a lawyer, and two years to sue the bar, club, or marina - a 182-day notice and a two-year limit for malpractice

**A pothole caused my crash. Do I sue West Bloomfield Township?** No. The township does not own the public roads. The Road Commission for Oakland County does, and the written notice must reach it within 120 days. A notice sent to the township does not stop that clock.

**I tripped on a broken pathway along a West Bloomfield road. Who is responsible?** Usually the township, under the sidewalk statute, with written notice within 120 days and the two-inch rule. Whether the path counts as a sidewalk under the statute has to be checked. Photograph the defect with something that shows its height.

**I was hurt in a boating accident on one of the lakes. Is that a personal injury case?** Yes. No-fault does not apply to boats, so it is an ordinary negligence claim against the operator, with a three-year deadline. The boat’s owner can be liable if the operator was using it with permission. If the operator had been drinking at a club or marina, that business may be liable too.

**I was hurt on a dock or at a lake association beach. Do I have a claim?** Possibly. A dock or beach owner owes a duty to keep it reasonably safe. If you were allowed to use the land for recreation without paying, Michigan’s Recreational Land Use Act may limit the claim to gross negligence or willful misconduct. Who owned the dock, and on what terms you were there, decide which rule applies.

**Do I have to use up my no-fault benefits before I can sue the other driver?** No. You can bring the claim against the at-fault driver once your injury meets the threshold, within three years, while your no-fault benefits are still being paid.

**Can I sue the other driver’s insurance company?** No. In Michigan, the claim is against the driver, and the driver’s insurer defends and pays. Your own no-fault insurer is a separate claim, for your medical bills and lost wages.

**What does a West Bloomfield personal injury attorney charge?** Michigan caps the fee in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery, with no fee if there is no recovery. Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted only from a recovery.

**Where would my West Bloomfield case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000 go to the 48th District Court on Telegraph Road in Bloomfield Township. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court in Pontiac. Claims against MDOT go to the Court of Claims.

**If I was partly at fault, do I still have a claim?** Yes, unless you were more than 50 percent at fault, which bars pain-and-suffering damages. Below that, your recovery is reduced by your share, and at exactly 50 percent you recover half. No-fault benefits are paid regardless of fault.

**Do I need a lawyer whose office is in West Bloomfield?** No. What matters is that the lawyer knows the courts your case will be in, the agencies, insurers, and property owners involved, and will meet you where you are.
