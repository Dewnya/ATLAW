## Where Dearborn crashes happen, and whose road it is

**Michigan Avenue (US-12).** Seven to nine lanes through the east-side business district between Wyoming and Schaefer, with the city’s heaviest pedestrian traffic, then through downtown West Dearborn to Telegraph. Left-turn crashes at Schaefer, Greenfield, and the Southfield Freeway ramps, rear-end crashes in the stacked signals, and pedestrian crashes along the east side.

**Ford Road (M-153).** The northern arterial past Fairlane Town Center, Ford’s headquarters campus, Henry Ford College, and UM-Dearborn; the signals at Greenfield, the Southfield Freeway, Evergreen, and Telegraph carry the high-volume crashes.

**The freeways.** I-94 across the south of the city with the Rotunda, Oakwood, and Greenfield interchanges serving the Rouge and Ford; M-39, the Southfield Freeway, with its short ramps; and the I-94/M-39 interchange. Heavy trucks to the Rouge complex are on all of them; see our [Dearborn truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/dearborn). Riders on the same roads are covered on our [Dearborn motorcycle accident page](/personal-injury/motorcycle-accidents/dearborn).

**Telegraph, Greenfield, Schaefer, Wyoming, Outer Drive, and Warren.** The north-south arterials and Warren Avenue on the east side, where residential streets meet six-lane roads and where many of the city’s pedestrians and cyclists are hit; see our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents).

**Whose road it is matters when the road caused the crash.** Michigan Avenue, Ford Road, Telegraph, the Southfield Freeway, and I-94 are MDOT trunklines; a defect claim goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). Greenfield, Outer Drive, and Warren are Wayne County roads; Schaefer and the neighborhood streets are the City’s; both take written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404). ## Who pays after a Dearborn crash

**Your no-fault (PIP) claim.** Your own auto insurer pays your medical expenses up to the PIP level you chose, 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each October, replacement services at $20 a day, and attendant care, regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107). If you have no policy, a resident relative’s policy pays (MCL 500.3114), and if none exists, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3172), capped at $250,000 for medical benefits. The insurer must receive written notice within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)) and must pay each bill within 30 days of reasonable proof or owe 12 percent interest (MCL 500.3142). See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance) and [Michigan no-fault guide](about:blank); passengers are covered on our [passenger injury page](/personal-injury/passenger-injury).

**Your claim against the at-fault driver.** For pain and suffering and losses beyond no-fault, you need a threshold injury under MCL 500.3135: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement. Three years to sue (MCL 600.5805(2)), or by the estate after a fatal crash (see our [Dearborn wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death/dearborn)); recovery reduced by your share of fault and barred if you were more than 50 percent at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). The mini tort recovers up to $3,000 in vehicle damage from the at-fault driver (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)), filed in the 19th District Court if the insurer will not pay it.

**The Dearborn insurance picture.** The city sits between Detroit and the western suburbs in coverage: more drivers carry real coverage than in Detroit, but uninsured and $50,000-minimum drivers are common on Michigan Avenue and Warren, and many Dearborn drivers chose reduced PIP levels to afford a policy after the reform. Two consequences. Your own **uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage** is often the real recovery; see our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims). And medical expenses above your chosen PIP level become part of your claim against the at-fault driver (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)), which makes that driver’s limits, and your UIM, decisive. We read every policy in the household before we value a Dearborn claim. **[ATTORNEY CHECK: excess allowable expenses under 3135(3)(c)]**

## What to do after a Dearborn crash

1. **Call 911 and get the Dearborn Police report.** Michigan requires a report for any crash with injury or $1,000 in damage (MCL 257.622). Dearborn Police crash reports are purchased online through CLEMIS, linked from the Dearborn Police records page.
2. **Photograph the vehicles, the plates, the other driver’s license and insurance card, the signals, and the road**, and get witness names before the scene clears.
3. **Get medical care the same day.** Corewell Health Dearborn Hospital on Oakwood Boulevard is where many of the city’s seriously injured are treated; Henry Ford Medical Center Fairlane and the Detroit Level I centers treat the rest. **[CONFIRM: hospital list and Corewell Dearborn’s trauma designation]** Tell the provider it was a car crash.
4. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** within the first week.
5. **Read your declarations page** for PIP level, UM/UIM, and collision coverage.
6. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the other driver’s insurer, in any language, and do not sign a medical authorization or release. If an adjuster calls and you would rather speak Arabic, call us first.
7. **Stay off social media** about the crash.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many car accidents happen in Dearborn each year?**  Dearborn recorded 3,326 crashes, 1,113 injuries, and 4 deaths in 2024. Wayne County as a whole recorded 50,355 crashes, 16,458 injuries, and 177 deaths in 2024, according to the Michigan State Police.

**The driver who hit me on Michigan Avenue had no insurance. What now?** Your no-fault benefits are unaffected: your own insurer, a household member’s, or the Assigned Claims Plan pays your medical bills and lost wages. Your pain-and-suffering claim depends on whether you carry uninsured motorist coverage; without it, the claim is worth only what the uninsured driver can personally pay.

**I hit a pothole on Ford Road. Who do I claim against?** Ford Road is a state trunkline, so the claim is against MDOT in the Court of Claims, with notice filed there within 120 days, not against the City of Dearborn. On a city street the notice goes to the City within 120 days; on Greenfield, Warren, or Outer Drive, to Wayne County.

**Where would my Dearborn case be filed?** Mini tort and other claims up to $25,000 in the 19th District Court on Michigan Avenue; injury suits over $25,000 in the Third Judicial Circuit Court in Detroit.

**Can I handle my claim in Arabic?** Yes, your consultation can be in Arabic. Consultations can also be accommodated in English, French, Mandarin, Urdu, and Spanish.

**How long do I have?** One year to give your no-fault insurer written notice, three years to sue the at-fault driver, and 120 days for a notice to the City, County, or State.

**Do I need a lawyer for a Dearborn fender-bender?** Not if no one was hurt and the insurer is paying. Call if anyone was injured, the other driver was uninsured or fled, a government vehicle was involved, the police report is wrong, or an insurer has denied or delayed benefits.
