## Personal Injury Law Firm Serving Sterling Heights, MI

Sterling Heights is Michigan’s fourth-largest city, about 134,000 people between Fourteen Mile and M-59, Dequindre and Hayes, built around Van Dyke, Mound, and the Hall Road retail corridor. Stellantis’s Sterling Heights Assembly and Sterling Stamping plants on Van Dyke and Mound, the supplier shops around them, and the trucks that serve them share the roads with the shoppers bound for Hall Road and the families in the subdivisions between. In 2024, the 18½ Mile Road roundabout at Van Dyke in Sterling Heights recorded 162 crashes, the second-highest intersection count in Michigan. The 11 Mile Road/I-696 intersection at Van Dyke in Warren/Center Line ranked first, with 185 crashes.

## Where Sterling Heights injuries happen

**Van Dyke (M-53).** Eight lanes from Fourteen Mile north past the Sterling Heights Assembly plant to 18½ Mile, where it meets the Van Dyke freeway and the plant and retail traffic in the intersection that leads the state. North of there, the M-53 freeway carries traffic to Utica, Shelby Township, and Romeo at freeway speed, with the M-59 interchange a mile on. Left-turn crashes across four lanes, red-light crashes, rear-end crashes in the stacked turn lanes, and the merge and weave crashes where the surface road becomes a freeway. Van Dyke is MDOT’s road.

**Hall Road (M-59).** The county’s retail spine across the north of the city, from Mound to Hayes, lined with the Lakeside corridor, big-box strips, restaurants, and the medical offices along Schoenherr. The signals at Van Dyke, Schoenherr, Hayes, Dequindre, and Mound carry left-turn and red-light crashes at commercial volumes; the parking lots carry the low-speed crashes and the pedestrian strikes; and the stores carry the falls. The planned Lakeside City Center redevelopment could bring significant changes to traffic and development along the Hall Road corridor as the former mall property is transformed into a mixed-use destination.

**Mound Road.** The plant corridor, rebuilt from I-696 to M-59 in the Innovate Mound project, carrying tractor-trailers into and out of Sterling Stamping and Sterling Heights Assembly at every shift change, with turning-truck crashes at the gates and rear-end crashes into slowing trucks. See our [Sterling Heights truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/sterling-heights).

**Metro Parkway (16 Mile), 15 Mile, 17 Mile, 18 Mile, 19 Mile, and the north-south arterials.** Dequindre, Ryan, Schoenherr, and Hayes. Metro Parkway is a divided boulevard with Michigan-left turnarounds, and the U-turn and crossover crashes at the turnarounds are a Sterling Heights case type; the mile roads are the Macomb County Department of Roads’. Pedestrian and bicycle crashes near the schools and along the Clinton River trail.

**The plants and the shops.** Sterling Heights Assembly, Sterling Stamping, and the supplier and tool shops along Van Dyke, Mound, and 18 Mile. Workers’ compensation is the exclusive remedy against the employer (MCL 418.131), but a worker injured by a contractor, a vendor’s driver, or a defective machine has a lawsuit as well. ## What is different about a Sterling Heights claim

**Fault is the case.** Many Sterling Heights crash claims involve intersections on Van Dyke, Hall Road, Mound, and the mile roads. In a left-turn or traffic-signal crash, fault may be disputed, and the other driver’s insurer may argue that you share responsibility for the collision. Under MCL 500.3135(2)(b), a person found more than 50 percent at fault generally cannot recover noneconomic damages. What decides it: the police report and diagram, any available signal-timing plans, controller records, or other traffic-signal information maintained by MDOT or the Macomb County Department of Roads, the corridor and store cameras (dense along Hall Road and Van Dyke, retained for days), the vehicles’ event data recorders, and the witnesses in the other lanes. We promptly assess available camera footage, traffic-signal records, and witness evidence and take appropriate steps to preserve relevant information after accepting a case.

**Coverage.** Sterling Heights drivers carry more insurance on average than drivers to the south, but the $50,000 minimum (MCL 500.3009(1)) is common and the Hall Road traffic comes from everywhere. Your own no-fault insurer pays your medical bills and lost wages regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107), or the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan does if no household policy applies (MCL 500.3172, capped at $250,000); the claim against the at-fault driver requires a threshold injury (MCL 500.3135) and is worth no more than the insurance available unless you carry uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage. When the at-fault driver’s limit is too small for the injury, the client’s own UIM coverage pays the difference, with the insurer’s consent required before any settlement. See our [Sterling Heights car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents/sterling-heights), [no-fault insurance guide](about:blank), and [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Three road agencies.** MDOT has jurisdiction over state trunklines such as Van Dyke (M-53), Hall Road (M-59), and the M-53 freeway. The Macomb County Department of Roads maintains Mound Road, Metropolitan Parkway, and other designated county roads, including portions of Schoenherr, Hayes, and Dequindre. The City of Sterling Heights maintains subdivision streets and certain local arterials and mile roads, including Ryan Road. Responsibility for a particular road segment should be verified against the applicable road-jurisdiction map. A qualifying defect in the improved portion of a public roadway may support a claim against the responsible governmental agency under the highway exception to governmental immunity, MCL 691.1402. Claims based solely on malfunctioning traffic signals, inadequate signs, or obstructed sight lines generally do not fall within that exception. Qualifying highway-defect claims generally require written notice within 120 days under MCL 691.1404, including claims involving MDOT-maintained roads, for which notice must be filed with the Court of Claims as prescribed by statute.

**Government vehicles.** Crashes involving Sterling Heights Police or Fire Department vehicles, SMART buses, school district buses, or Department of Roads trucks may support claims under the Governmental Tort Liability Act’s motor vehicle exception, MCL 691.1405, when injuries result from negligent operation of government-owned vehicles. Certain tort claims against SMART also require written notice within 60 days under MCL 124.419. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

**The 41A District and the Sixteenth Circuit.** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims for vehicle damage (MCL 500.3135(3)(e), up to $3,000), are filed in the 41A District Court, Sterling Heights division, at 40111 Dodge Park Road. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court at 40 N. Main Street in Mount Clemens; wrongful death estates (MCL 600.2922) and minors’ settlements (MCR 2.420) to the Macomb County Probate Court in Mount Clemens. For crashes near Dequindre Road or involving defendants from Oakland County, venue is determined under the statutory order of priority in MCL 600.1629, generally beginning with the county where the original injury occurred and where a defendant resides, conducts business, or maintains a registered office.

**The hospitals.** Henry Ford Macomb Hospital in Clinton Township and McLaren Macomb in Mount Clemens are Level II trauma centers serving the region. Corewell Health Troy and Henry Ford Warren Hospital also serve injured patients from Sterling Heights and surrounding communities.

**Language.** Sterling Heights is home to a large and well-established Chaldean and Iraqi community, with residents who may prefer to communicate in Arabic, Sureth, or another language.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is 18½ Mile and Van Dyke one of Michigan’s highest-crash intersections?** Yes. The roundabout recorded 162 crashes in 2024, the second-highest intersection total statewide that year, based on Michigan State Police crash data. Crash frequency is not the same as injury severity, and the location’s traffic conditions should be considered when evaluating individual collisions.

**The other driver’s insurer says I was 50 percent at fault for a left-turn crash on Van Dyke or Hall Road. Is my claim over?** Not because an adjuster says so. Under MCL 500.3135(2)(b) you lose the pain-and-suffering claim only if you are found more than 50 percent at fault; at exactly 50 percent you recover half. The police diagram, signal data, cameras, and witnesses decide the number.

**I was hurt in a Michigan-left turnaround crash on Metro Parkway. Who is at fault?** Usually the driver who failed to yield at the turnaround or who ran the signal, but these crashes are disputed often, and the signal phase and the turnaround’s sight lines matter. Photograph the turnaround and the signals, and get the witnesses.

**I was hit by a truck turning into Sterling Heights Assembly or Sterling Stamping. Who is responsible?** The driver and the carrier, and possibly the plant or its logistics provider if the gate layout, traffic control, or delivery schedule contributed. Plant-gate cameras are overwritten quickly; the preservation letter goes to the plant as well as the carrier.

**I fell in a Hall Road store or parking lot. Who is responsible?** Responsibility depends on who possessed or controlled the property, who was responsible for maintaining the area, and whether negligence contributed to the injury. Since Kandil-Elsayed v. F & E Oil, Inc. (2023), an open-and-obvious hazard does not automatically eliminate the property possessor’s duty; its obviousness may be considered in determining breach of duty and comparative fault. Incident reports and available security-camera footage may be important evidence, so preservation should be requested promptly.

**Where would my Sterling Heights injury case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, in the 41A District Court on Dodge Park Road; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court in Mount Clemens.

**Do you speak Arabic or Sureth?** ATLAW serves Arabic-speaking clients and also offers services in English, French, Mandarin, Urdu, and Spanish. If you prefer Sureth, please contact our team to discuss what language assistance may be available.

**How much does it cost to hire you?** Nothing up front, and no fee unless we recover money for you. Michigan caps contingency fees in personal injury cases at one-third of the recovery (MCR 8.121).

**How long do I have?** Three years for most injury suits, one year to notify your no-fault insurer in writing, and as little as 120 days for a claim against the City of Sterling Heights or Macomb County.
