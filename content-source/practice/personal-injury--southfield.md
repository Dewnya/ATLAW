## Southfield Personal Injury Lawyer

If you were hurt in Southfield because someone else was careless, you have a claim under Michigan law, and it has deadlines that start the day you are hurt: one year to notify your no-fault insurer after a crash, 120 days to notify the City of Southfield or the Road Commission for Oakland County of a road or sidewalk defect, 120 days to notify the State for a defect on Telegraph, Northwestern, or I-696, 120 days from hiring a lawyer to notify a bar that served a drunk driver, and two years to sue it, 182 days’ notice before a malpractice suit, and three years for most everything else. ## What a Southfield personal injury lawyer does

**The first week.** We calendar every deadline and send preservation letters: to the at-fault driver’s insurer; to the trucking company for logs and electronic data; to the City, the RCOC, or SMART for vehicle or signal records; to the office tower, apartment complex, or store for footage and incident reports, which the Northwestern Highway buildings and Telegraph retail centers overwrite in days; to the bar for its receipts if a drunk driver was involved. We obtain the Southfield Police report (the UD-10, sold through Oakland County’s CLEMIS system), the Southfield Fire Department EMS run sheet, and the witnesses’ names.

**The first month.** We identify every policy that may pay: your own auto policy for no-fault benefits, household policies, the at-fault party’s liability coverage, your uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage, a property owner’s commercial policy, umbrella policies, and the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan where no auto policy exists (MCL 500.3172). We file your Application for No-Fault Benefits inside the one-year window (MCL 500.3145(1)) and handle the insurer’s requests for statements, records, and examinations (MCL 500.3151). You stop talking to adjusters; we do that.

**The treatment period.** The claim’s value is set mostly by the medical record, so we make sure it is complete and the bills are paid while you treat, collecting records from Henry Ford Providence Southfield Hospital (formerly Ascension Providence), Corewell Health’s Royal Oak and Farmington Hills hospitals, and documenting lost wages with your employer, including the office-tower employers along Northwestern.

**The demand, the suit, and the resolution.** When your condition has stabilized, we send a demand with the complete record and a valuation; the decision to accept any offer is yours (MRPC 1.2). If the insurer will not pay fair value, suit is filed: claims up to $25,000 in the 46th District Court at 26000 Evergreen Road; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court (Oakland County Circuit Court) at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac, which may send injury cases to case evaluation (MCR 2.403) or facilitation before trial; and where a defendant lives or does business in Wayne County, or the crash was on Eight Mile, possibly the Third Circuit in Detroit (MCL 600.1629). At settlement we resolve the liens before you are paid; minors’ settlements and wrongful death distributions are approved by the Oakland County Probate Court or the trial court (MCR 2.420; MCL 600.2922(6)). Compensation for physical injury is not taxable income (26 USC 104(a)(2)). **[ATTORNEY CHECK]**

## What a Southfield claim is worth, and what reduces it

Economic damages (medical expenses, lost wages, lost earning capacity, replacement services) and noneconomic damages (pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, disfigurement), with no cap except in medical malpractice. Your share of fault reduces the recovery and, above 50 percent, bars noneconomic damages (MCL 600.2959). In most Southfield cases the practical limit is insurance: the at-fault driver’s policy, which ranges from an office commuter’s high limits and umbrella to an Eight Mile driver’s $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)); your own underinsured motorist coverage; and, in a premises case, the building owner’s and management company’s commercial policies. Where UIM is involved, settling with the at-fault driver without your own insurer’s consent can forfeit the UIM claim. **[ATTORNEY CHECK]**

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in Southfield?** Three years for most claims, but one year to notify your no-fault insurer after a crash, 120 days to notify the City, the RCOC, or the State of a road defect, 120 days from hiring a lawyer to notify a bar, and a 182-day notice before any malpractice suit.

**What does a Southfield personal injury lawyer charge?** One-third of the recovery, the Michigan cap for injury cases, with no fee if there is no recovery. Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted only from a recovery.

**Where would my Southfield case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000 in the 46th District Court on Evergreen Road; injury suits over $25,000 in the Oakland County Circuit Court in Pontiac, or in some cases the Third Circuit in Detroit.

**Do I need a lawyer whose office is in Southfield?** No. What matters is that the lawyer knows the courts your case will be in, the road agencies and insurers involved, and will meet you where you are.

**Do I have to go to court?** Most Southfield injury cases settle, many after suit is filed and before trial. Filing is usually part of getting a fair offer.

**How long will my case take?** A no-fault benefits claim can resolve in months; a disputed injury claim in the Oakland County Circuit Court typically takes a year to two from filing.

**The insurer already offered me money. Should I take it?** Not before you know what the claim is worth and what the release gives up, and not before your UIM insurer has consented if UIM may apply.
