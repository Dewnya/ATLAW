## Where Clinton Township crashes happen

**Hall Road (M-59) and the Michigan lefts.** The county’s retail corridor runs along the township’s northern edge, from Hayes past Partridge Creek and the big-box centers to Romeo Plank and Garfield. Left turns are made through median crossovers past the intersection, so the crashes are drivers leaving a crossover into the near lanes without a gap, rear-ends at the signals and in the turnaround queues, red-light crashes at Hayes, Garfield, and Romeo Plank, and driveway crashes at the shopping centers. Hall at Hayes and Hall at Garfield both ranked in Macomb County’s 2024 top ten.

**Gratiot Avenue (M-3).** From the Roseville line through Fifteen Mile, Metro Parkway, and Cass toward Mount Clemens and Chesterfield: left-turn and red-light crashes at the signals, rear-ends in the stacked turn lanes, crashes at dealership and strip-center driveways, and pedestrian crashes between the apartment complexes and the stores (see our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents)).

**Groesbeck (M-97) and I-94.** Groesbeck’s diagonal meets the mile roads at skewed angles, where sight lines are poor and drivers misjudge the gap; its industrial frontage adds trucks (see our [Clinton Township truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/clinton-township)). I-94 crosses the township’s southeastern corner, with an interchange at Harper Avenue.

**The county roads.** Garfield, Romeo Plank, Hayes, Harper, Moravian, Cass, Utica Road, and the mile roads from Fourteen to Nineteen carry signal crashes at the major intersections, crashes at unsignalized crossings, and crashes near the schools, Macomb Community College’s Center Campus, and Henry Ford Macomb Hospital.

**Whose road it is: never the township’s.** Michigan townships do not own public roads. Hall Road, Gratiot, Groesbeck, and I-94 are MDOT’s; a road-defect claim against MDOT goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). Garfield, Romeo Plank, Hayes, Harper, Moravian, Cass, Utica Road, Metro Parkway, and the mile roads are generally the Macomb County Department of Roads’, and so are most subdivision streets; a claim against the county takes written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404). A pothole or other defect in the roadbed can make the agency a defendant alongside the other driver; on State and county roads, signals, signs, and sight obstructions generally fall outside the highway exception. A notice sent to Clinton Township for a road defect is sent to the wrong agency. See our [sidewalk injury page](/personal-injury/sidewalk-injuries).

## Fault is the case

In a rear-end crash, fault is rarely disputed. At a Hall Road crossover, a Gratiot left turn, a skewed Groesbeck intersection, or a shopping-center driveway, it is disputed often, and the other driver’s insurer may try to assign you half or more of the fault. That number matters: if you are found more than 50 percent at fault, you lose your claim for pain and suffering, and below that your recovery is reduced by your share (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). At exactly 50 percent you recover half. The evidence that decides it is perishable:

- the Clinton Township Police report and the officer’s diagram, or the State Police or Macomb County Sheriff’s report where they worked the crash;
- **signal timing data** from MDOT or the Macomb County Department of Roads, which show how each light was timed and, where the controller logs events, what phase it was in;
- store, mall, dealership, and gas-station cameras along Hall Road and Gratiot, many overwritten within days;
- the vehicles’ **event data recorders**, which store speed, braking, and throttle in the seconds before impact;
- the other driver’s phone records, for a distracted-driving claim (see our [distracted driving page](/personal-injury/distracted-driving-accidents));
- witnesses in the other lanes.

## Who pays after a Clinton Township crash

**Your no-fault (PIP) claim.** Your own auto insurer pays your medical expenses up to the PIP level you chose, 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year, replacement services at $20 a day, and attendant care, regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107). If you have no policy, a resident relative’s policy pays (MCL 500.3114); if none exists, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan does (MCL 500.3172), capped at $250,000 for medical benefits. The insurer must receive written notice within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)) and must pay each bill within 30 days of reasonable proof or owe 12 percent interest (MCL 500.3142). Since the 2019 reform, PIP medical coverage is no longer unlimited unless you chose it, so the level on your declarations page matters. An older driver on Medicare may have opted out of PIP medical entirely; in that case Medicare pays first and its rules apply. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance) and [Michigan no-fault guide](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

**Your claim against the at-fault driver.** For pain and suffering, you need a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment is an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life; it does not have to be permanent (MCL 500.3135(5); *McCormick v Carrier*, 2010). You can also recover lost wages beyond the three years or above the monthly cap, and medical bills above your chosen PIP level (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)). The suit must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The mini tort recovers up to $3,000 in vehicle damage not covered by your policy (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)), filed in the 41B District Court if the at-fault driver’s insurer will not pay it.

**Coverage on the other side, and UIM sequencing.** Liability limits on Hall Road and Gratiot range from the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)) to large policies, and some drivers carry nothing. When the at-fault driver’s limit is too small for the injury, your own **underinsured motorist coverage** pays the difference, but most policies require your insurer’s written consent before you accept the at-fault driver’s limit; settling without it can forfeit the UIM claim. We read every policy in the household, including umbrella coverage, before valuing the claim. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Drunk drivers and dram shop.** Macomb County recorded 583 alcohol-involved crashes in 2024. A crash caused by a drunk driver leaving a Hall Road or Gratiot bar or restaurant adds a claim against the business that served a visibly intoxicated person or a minor. Written notice must go to the retailer within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, the drunk driver must be named and kept in the suit, and the suit must be filed within two years, not three (MCL 436.1801(3), (4)). See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**Government vehicles.** Clinton Township Police and Fire, Macomb County Sheriff and Department of Roads vehicles, SMART buses on Gratiot, Hall Road, and Groesbeck, and school buses from the districts that serve the township fall under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405). See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

## What to do after a Clinton Township crash

1. **Call 911 and get the police report.** Michigan requires a report for any crash with injury or $1,000 in damage (MCL 257.622). Most township crashes are worked by the Clinton Township Police Department; some freeway and highway crashes by the Michigan State Police or the Macomb County Sheriff’s Office. **[VERIFY: report request process]**
2. **Photograph the vehicles, plates, the other driver’s license and insurance card, the signals, the crossover, and the lane markings.** Note every business with a camera facing the scene; footage is kept for days, not weeks.
3. **Get medical care the same day.** Henry Ford Macomb Hospital in the township and McLaren Macomb in Mount Clemens treat most of the township’s injured.
4. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** within the first week.
5. **Read your declarations page** for PIP level, UM/UIM, umbrella, and collision coverage.
6. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the other driver’s insurer, sign a release, or accept a policy-limits offer before your UIM insurer consents.
7. **Call a lawyer within days** if fault is disputed or the road played a part; the camera footage and signal data are the first evidence lost, and the 120-day county notice is already running.

## The courts

Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, are filed in the 41B District Court at 22380 Starks Drive in Clinton Township, which serves Clinton Township, Harrison Township, and Mount Clemens. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court at 40 N. Main Street in Mount Clemens. A case against an out-of-state driver or carrier may be filed in or removed to the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan. Defect claims against MDOT go to the Court of Claims. A fatal crash is brought by the estate through the Macomb County Probate Court; see our [Clinton Township wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death).

## Frequently asked questions

**Which Clinton Township intersections have the most crashes?** In the State Police data for 2024, two Hall Road intersections on the township’s northern edge ranked in Macomb County’s top ten: Hall at Hayes, with 76 crashes and 14 injuries, and Hall at Garfield, with 67 crashes and 19 injuries. The list credits both to Macomb Township. **[CONFIRM: data: Clinton Township’s highest-crash locations from the MTCF query tool]**

**How many car accidents happen in Clinton Township each year?** Clinton Township recorded 1,907 crashes, 749 injuries, and 8 deaths in 2024. Macomb County as a whole recorded 21,960 crashes, 6,596 injuries, and 70 deaths in 2024.

**A pothole or a bad signal caused my crash. Do I file against Clinton Township?** No. Townships do not own roads. A defect in the roadbed on Hall Road, Gratiot, Groesbeck, or I-94 is MDOT’s, with notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days; on the county roads it is the Macomb County Department of Roads', with written notice within 120 days. A signal problem on a State or county road generally falls outside the highway exception.

**A driver pulled out of a Hall Road crossover and hit me. Who is at fault?** Usually the driver leaving the crossover, who must yield to traffic in the lanes being entered. The police diagram, cameras at the nearby businesses, and witnesses decide it.

**The other driver’s insurer offered their policy limit. Should I take it?** Not before checking your own underinsured motorist coverage and getting your insurer’s written consent to settle. Accepting the limit without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under most policies.

**Where would my Clinton Township case be filed?** Mini tort and claims up to $25,000 in the 41B District Court on Starks Drive; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court in Mount Clemens.

**How long do I have?** One year to give your no-fault insurer written notice, three years to sue the at-fault driver, two years to sue a bar, 120 days for a notice to the Department of Roads, and 120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT.
