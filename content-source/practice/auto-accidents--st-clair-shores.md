## St. Clair Shores, MI Car Accident Lawyer

A car crash in St. Clair Shores is a Michigan no-fault claim on these roads:

- **I-94** runs along the city’s west side, with interchanges and service drives at the mile roads.
- **Jefferson Avenue** runs the length of the city along Lake St. Clair, past the marinas, restaurants, and bars of the Nautical Mile and the lakefront parks.
- **Harper Avenue,** the city’s inland commercial strip, and **Greater Mack** run parallel between I-94 and the lake.
- **Little Mack** runs along the freeway, and **Nine through Thirteen Mile** cross the city from I-94 to the water.

In 2024, the Michigan State Police recorded 1,356 crashes in St. Clair Shores. 3 people were killed, 20 were seriously injured, and 365 were injured in all. A third of the crashes, 454, were on I-94. 55 involved alcohol, about one in twenty-five.

A St. Clair Shores crash claim usually turns on four questions:

- What happened at the interchange, the service drive, or the signal?
- Was the other driver drunk, and who served them?
- Did the other driver have insurance, and how much, and how does your own uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage fit with it?
- If the road played a part, does it belong to the State, Macomb County, or the City?

## Where St. Clair Shores crashes happen

**The I-94 interchanges and service drives.** I-94 carries commuter traffic to Detroit and through traffic to Port Huron, with interchanges and service drives at the mile roads only a mile apart. The crashes are:

- rear-end crashes in the stop-and-go, and where traffic backs up from the exit ramps onto the freeway
- merge and weave crashes between closely spaced entrance and exit ramps
- crashes at the ramp terminals and service-drive signals, where drivers turn across the service drive onto the mile road
- left-turn crashes at the mile-road signals just east of the freeway

In the State Police’s 2025 intersection data, 12 Mile at I-94 had 86 crashes, third in Macomb County, and 10 Mile at I-94 had 66, ninth. The freeway and its ramps belong to MDOT. See our [St. Clair Shores truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/st-clair-shores).

**Jefferson Avenue and the Nautical Mile.** Jefferson runs along the lake from the Grosse Pointe line to the Harrison Township line. Between about Nine and Ten Mile, the Nautical Mile adds marinas, boat dealers, waterfront restaurants, and bars, and the city’s lakefront parks draw summer crowds. The crashes are:

- drunk-driving crashes leaving the restaurants and bars, often late at night
- pedestrians struck crossing Jefferson between the parking lots and the restaurants
- cars and trucks towing boat trailers, turning into and out of the marinas and the launches
- left turns into restaurant and marina driveways across oncoming traffic (MCL 257.650)
- cyclists on Jefferson, struck by passing or turning drivers

See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents) and our [bicycle accident page](/personal-injury/bicycle-accidents).

**Harper Avenue.** The city’s inland commercial strip, with stores, restaurants, and dozens of driveways between the mile roads. The crashes are:

- left-turn and red-light crashes at the mile-road signals
- driveway crashes, where a driver leaving a lot must yield to traffic on the road (MCL 257.652)
- rear-end crashes at the signals and behind turning cars
- pedestrians struck near the stores and bus stops

**Greater Mack, Little Mack, and the mile roads.** Greater Mack runs past homes and schools between Harper and Jefferson, and Little Mack runs along the freeway. The mile roads cross the city from I-94 to the lake. The crashes are intersection crashes at the signals and four-way stops, school-zone crashes, and drivers pulling out from side streets.

**Whose road it is: the State, the county, or the City.** St. Clair Shores is a city, and road ownership is split:

- **MDOT** owns I-94 and its ramps. A road-defect claim against MDOT goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **Macomb County** (the Department of Roads) or the City of St. Clair Shores owns Jefferson, Harper, Greater Mack, Little Mack, and the mile roads, depending on the segment. The I-94 service drives may belong to MDOT or to the local agency, depending on the stretch.
- **The City** owns the local streets.

The State Police recorded no crashes on U.S. or state routes in the city in 2024. Apart from I-94, the major roads are county or City roads.

A claim against the county or the City requires written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404). A pothole or other defect in the road can make the agency a defendant alongside the other driver; on State and county roads, signals, signs, and sight obstructions generally fall outside the highway exception (Nawrocki v Macomb County Road Commission, Mich 2000). The notice must go to the agency that actually has jurisdiction over that stretch of road, or it is no notice.

## Fault is the case

In a rear-end crash, fault is rarely disputed. At an I-94 merge, a service-drive signal, a Harper driveway, or a Jefferson crossing, it often is, and the other driver’s insurer may try to assign you half or more of the fault. That share matters (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)):

- **More than 50 percent at fault:** you lose your claim for pain and suffering.
- **50 percent or less:** your recovery is reduced by your share. At exactly 50 percent, you recover half.

The evidence that decides fault is perishable:

- the St. Clair Shores Police Department crash report and diagram, or the State Police report for a crash on the freeway
- signal timing data from MDOT, the county, or the City
- cameras at the restaurants, marinas, stores, and gas stations along Jefferson and Harper, many overwritten within days
- the vehicles’ **event data recorders**, which store speed, braking, and throttle in the seconds before impact
- the other driver’s phone records, for a distracted-driving claim (see our [distracted driving page](/personal-injury/distracted-driving-accidents))
- witnesses, including other drivers and people on the sidewalk

We move promptly to request the camera footage, signal data, and vehicle data.

## When the other driver was drunk: the claim against the bar

A bar, restaurant, or store that serves alcohol to a visibly intoxicated person, or to a minor, is liable for the injuries that person causes under Michigan’s dram shop act (MCL 436.1801). After a crash involving a driver leaving the Nautical Mile or a Harper bar, that claim can matter more than the claim against the driver:

- **The drunk driver’s own policy is often small,** or the driver has none. The bar’s liquor liability policy is a separate source of payment.
- **The evidence is the bar’s,** and it does not last: point-of-sale receipts, tabs, cameras, staff schedules, and the witnesses who saw the driver served.
- **The claim has its own rules.** Written notice must go to the business within 120 days after you hire a lawyer. The drunk driver must be named and kept in the suit. The suit must be filed within two years, not three.

The police report, the driver’s blood alcohol result, and the timeline from receipts usually show where the driver was drinking. A driver may have been served at more than one place.

A rideshare ride home is often how people leave the Nautical Mile. A passenger or another driver hurt in a crash with a rideshare vehicle may have a claim against the rideshare company’s commercial policy, which applies during a trip and often has higher limits than a personal policy. See our [rideshare accident page](/personal-injury/rideshare-accidents).

## Who pays after a St. Clair Shores crash

**Your no-fault (PIP) claim.** Your own auto insurer pays these benefits regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107):

- your medical expenses, up to the PIP level you chose
- 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- replacement services at $20 a day
- attendant care

**The PIP level is not the same as your liability limits.** Since July 2, 2020, Michigan drivers choose a PIP medical level for each policy: unlimited, $500,000, $250,000, $50,000 for some Medicaid enrollees, or an opt-out for some Medicare enrollees (MCL 500.3107c). That choice is separate from the $250,000/$500,000 liability limits that protect the other driver. The level on your declarations page decides how much of your medical care no-fault pays.

If you have no policy, a resident relative’s policy pays (MCL 500.3114). If none exists, an occupant claims from the insurer of the vehicle they were in (MCL 500.3114(4)), and then the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3172), capped at $250,000 for medical benefits. A pedestrian or cyclist hit by a car, with no household policy, claims through the Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3115).

The insurer must receive written notice within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). It must pay each bill within 30 days of reasonable proof, or owe 12 percent interest (MCL 500.3142). Providers’ charges are subject to the fee schedule in MCL 500.3157. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance) and our [Michigan no-fault guide](/resources/michigan-no-fault-guide).

**If you had no insurance.** A person who owned a car and was required to insure it, but did not, is generally barred from PIP benefits for their own injuries (MCL 500.3113(b)). A driver who was operating their own uninsured vehicle cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, whoever was at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(c)). A passenger or pedestrian who did not own an uninsured car may still be covered through a household policy or the Assigned Claims Plan.

**Your claim against the at-fault driver.** For pain and suffering, you need a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment is an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life. It does not have to be permanent (MCL 500.3135(5); *McCormick v Carrier*, 2010).

You can also recover economic losses that no-fault does not cover (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)):

- lost wages beyond the three years or above the monthly cap
- medical bills above your chosen PIP level

The suit must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The mini tort claim recovers up to $3,000 in vehicle damage not covered by your policy (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)). If the at-fault driver’s insurer will not pay it, the claim is filed in the 40th District Court.

**When the other driver has no insurance, or not enough.** Some drivers carry only the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)), and some carry nothing.

- **Against an uninsured driver:** your pain-and-suffering claim is worth what that driver can personally pay, unless you carry **uninsured motorist coverage**. A hit-and-run is usually a UM claim, with short notice terms in the policy.
- **Against an underinsured driver:** your own **underinsured motorist coverage** pays the difference up to your UIM limit, if you follow the steps in order:
 1. The at-fault driver’s insurer offers its limit.
 2. You notify your own insurer and ask for its written consent to settle.
 3. Only then do you accept the limit and sign the release.
 4. The UIM claim then proceeds.

Accepting without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under most policies. In a drunk-driving case, the dram shop claim against the bar is a separate source, and the UIM and dram shop claims have to be coordinated. We read every policy in the household before valuing the claim. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Police cars, fire trucks, and buses.** All of these fall under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405):

- St. Clair Shores Police and Fire vehicles
- SMART buses on Jefferson, Harper, and Nine Mile
- school buses for the districts that serve the city, including Lakeview, Lake Shore, and South Lake
- Macomb County road vehicles

Dash camera and dispatch records are overwritten on short schedules, so we request them by FOIA promptly. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

## What to do after a St. Clair Shores crash

1. **Call 911 and get the police report.** Michigan requires a report for any crash with injury or $1,000 in damage (MCL 257.622). Crashes on city streets are worked by the St. Clair Shores Police Department.
2. **Photograph the scene.** Get the vehicles, plates, the other driver’s license and insurance card, the intersection, ramp, or driveway, the signals, and the lane markings. Note every restaurant, marina, and store with a camera facing the scene. Footage is kept for days, not weeks.
3. **If you think the other driver had been drinking,** tell the officer, and write down anything the driver said about where they had been. Keep any receipts or names of places you saw them.
4. **Get medical care the same day.** Henry Ford St. John Hospital, just south on Moross in Detroit, is a Level I trauma center, and Henry Ford Macomb Hospital in Clinton Township is a Level II. Corewell Health Grosse Pointe is also nearby.
5. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** promptly. If you have no policy, a lawyer can identify the insurer next in priority or the Assigned Claims Plan.
6. **Read your declarations page** for your PIP medical level, UM/UIM, and collision coverage.
7. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the other driver’s insurer, sign a release, or accept a policy-limits offer before your UIM insurer consents.
8. **Call a lawyer within days** if the other driver was drunk, fault is disputed, the other driver is uninsured, or the road played a part. The bar’s cameras and the restaurant footage are the first evidence lost, and the 120-day dram shop notice and road notices are already running.

## The courts

- **Claims up to $25,000,** including mini tort claims, go to the 40th District Court at 27701 Jefferson Avenue in St. Clair Shores.
- **Injury suits over $25,000** go to the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court (Macomb County Circuit Court) at 40 North Main Street in Mount Clemens, with a jury drawn from all of Macomb County.
- **Defect claims against MDOT** go to the Court of Claims.
- **A fatal crash** is brought by the estate, opened through the Macomb County Probate Court. See our [wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death).

## Frequently asked questions


**A drunk driver leaving a Jefferson Avenue bar hit me. Can I sue the bar?** Possibly. A bar or restaurant that served a visibly intoxicated person can be liable for the injuries that person causes. Written notice must reach the bar within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, the drunk driver must be part of the suit, and the suit must be filed within two years. The bar’s receipts and cameras are the evidence, and they disappear quickly.

**I was rear-ended at the I-94 exit at 12 Mile. Who pays?** Your own no-fault insurer pays your medical bills and lost wages regardless of fault. For pain and suffering, your claim is against the driver who hit you if your injury meets the threshold. A rear-end crash is rarely disputed on fault, but the other driver’s insurer may still argue about how badly you were hurt, so get medical care the same day.

**The driver who hit me had no insurance. What now?** Your own no-fault insurer, a household member’s insurer, or the Assigned Claims Plan pays your medical bills and lost wages regardless of fault. Your pain-and-suffering claim depends on whether you carry uninsured motorist coverage. If the driver had been drinking, a claim against the bar that served them may also be available.

**Is my PIP medical coverage the same as my $250,000 or $500,000 limit?** Not necessarily. Your liability limits protect the other driver if you cause a crash. Your PIP medical level is a separate choice on the same policy, and it decides how much of your own medical care no-fault pays. Check your declarations page for both.

**How many crashes are there in St. Clair Shores?** The State Police recorded 1,356 crashes in St. Clair Shores in 2024. 3 people were killed, 20 were seriously injured, and 365 were injured in all. 454 of the crashes were on I-94, and 55 involved alcohol.

**I was hit on my bike on Jefferson. Who pays my medical bills?** Your own household’s auto policy pays if you have one. If not, the Assigned Claims Plan pays. Your pain-and-suffering claim is against the driver. The State Police recorded more bicyclist crashes than pedestrian crashes in the city in 2024.

**A pothole caused my crash. Do I file against the City of St. Clair Shores?** It depends on the road: - **I-94 and its ramps** belong to MDOT, with notice filed in the Court of Claims within 120 days. - Jefferson, Harper, Greater Mack, Little Mack, and the mile roads belong to Macomb County or the City, depending on the segment, with written notice within 120 days. - **Local streets** belong to the City, with the same 120 days.

**Where would my St. Clair Shores case be filed?** Mini tort claims and claims up to $25,000 go to the 40th District Court on Jefferson Avenue. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court in Mount Clemens.

**How long do I have?** - One year to give your no-fault insurer written notice. - Three years to sue the at-fault driver. - 120 days for a notice to Macomb County or the City. - 120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT. - Two years to sue a bar, with a 120-day notice.
