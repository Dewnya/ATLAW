## Royal Oak, MI Truck Accident Lawyer

Royal Oak’s heavy trucks travel two kinds of roads.

**The freeways at the city’s edges.**

- **I-696** (the Walter P. Reuther Freeway) runs along the southern edge, with interchanges at Woodward, Campbell, and I-75.
- **I-75** runs along the city’s east side and meets I-696 at its southeast corner.
- **Tractor-trailers** use both to reach the industrial areas along Stephenson Highway in Madison Heights and Troy.


**The city’s own streets.** Woodward, the mile roads, and downtown’s narrow streets carry:

- box trucks and beverage trucks supplying dozens of restaurants and bars
- garbage and recycling trucks
- construction trucks serving new apartment buildings
- delivery and supply trucks serving the Corewell Health hospital campus at Thirteen Mile

In 2024, according to the Michigan State Police, 2,081 heavy trucks and buses were involved in crashes in Oakland County. Statewide, 110 people were killed and 3,613 injured in heavy truck and bus crashes. The State’s county profile does not break out truck crashes by city.

A truck crash is not a car crash with a larger vehicle:

- **The rules are federal.** The driver and the carrier are governed by federal safety regulations, and a violation is evidence of negligence.
- **The insurance is larger.** The carrier must carry $750,000 or more in insurance.
- **The records expire.** The driver’s electronic logs and the truck’s engine data, the records that prove the case, can be lawfully discarded within months.
- **The other side moves fast.** The carrier’s insurer will have an investigator on the case within days.

## Where Royal Oak truck crashes happen

**I-696 and the I-75 interchange.** The freeway crashes are:

- **Backups:** rear-end and underride crashes in the stop-and-go at the I-75 interchange and the Woodward exit.
- **Lane changes:** trucks moving into a car in the blind spot on the right side of the cab, where the exits and entrances come close together.
- **Merges:** crashes on the ramps.
- **Work zones:** crashes during the long-running rebuilds of the I-75 and I-696 corridors.

A car that strikes the side or rear of a trailer can go under it. That is an **underride** crash, among the deadliest in trucking. Trailers must carry rear impact guards that meet federal standards (49 CFR 393.86). A missing, damaged, or substandard guard is evidence against the carrier and the trailer’s owner. In a work zone, the contractor can share responsibility.

**Woodward and the Michigan lefts.** A tractor-trailer or long box truck making a U-turn at a Woodward turnaround may be too long to clear the median crossover in one move. When that happens, it blocks the lanes. Other truck crashes on Woodward:

- trucks unable to stop for a changing signal at the mile roads
- wide right turns onto Eleven, Twelve, Thirteen, and Fourteen Mile that catch a car on the inside of the turn

On designated Michigan routes, heavy trucks can weigh up to 164,000 pounds, about twice the federal Interstate limit. They need far more distance to stop.

**Downtown deliveries.** Downtown’s restaurants, bars, and stores are supplied by box trucks and beverage trucks. Those trucks:

- double-park in travel lanes
- stop in bike lanes and crosswalks
- back into alleys and loading zones
- make tight right turns at Main, Washington, Fourth, and Eleven Mile

The crashes involve cars, cyclists, and pedestrians. Many happen in the morning delivery hours, and some at night. Where a business’s delivery arrangements routinely put trucks in the street, the business can share responsibility with the carrier. See our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents) and our [bicycle accident page](/personal-injury/bicycle-accidents).

**The hospital campus.** Corewell Health’s Thirteen Mile campus receives a steady stream of supply, linen, food-service, and medical-gas trucks. Those trucks turn into and out of the campus.

**Construction trucks.** New apartment and mixed-use buildings downtown and along Woodward bring dump trucks, cement mixers, and equipment haulers onto narrow streets. Their crashes involve:

- trucks pulling out of sites
- trucks backing without a spotter
- spilled loads



**Garbage trucks and delivery vans in the neighborhoods.** Garbage and recycling trucks, package vans, and furniture trucks work Royal Oak’s grid of residential streets. The crashes are:

- backing crashes
- crashes with vehicles stopped in the lane
- children and pedestrians struck by a truck pulling away from the curb

Delivery vans are often operated by contracted delivery companies rather than by the retailer named on the package. The van’s operator, its insurer, and the contract between them have to be identified.

**Lost and spilled loads.** Cargo, equipment, construction debris, or gravel that falls from a truck is a violation of the federal cargo securement rules (49 CFR 393.100 and following) and of Michigan law (MCL 257.720). The carrier and the company that loaded the truck can both be responsible.

**Whose road it is.** Royal Oak is a city, and the road owner can be any of three agencies:

- MDOT for I-696, I-75, and Woodward. A road-defect claim against the State goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- The Road Commission for Oakland County for Ten, Twelve, and Fourteen Mile. Written notice is due within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404).
- The City of Royal Oak for the other streets, including Eleven and Thirteen Mile, downtown, and the residential streets. Written notice is due within 120 days.

**The split follows the City’s road overview.**

## Who is responsible, and who pays

**The carrier, not just the driver.** The motor carrier is responsible for its drivers’ compliance with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations. It is liable for a driver’s negligence in the course of employment. Michigan applies the federal rules to intrastate carriers (MCL 480.11a). A local hauler, a delivery carrier, or a construction trucker that never leaves Oakland County is held to the same standards as an interstate carrier.

These are the rules that decide truck cases most often:

| Rule | 49 CFR | What it means for the case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hours of service | Part 395 | 11 hours driving in a 14-hour window after 10 hours off; 60/70-hour weekly limits; recorded on an electronic logging device. Some short-haul drivers are exempt from logs but not from the limits. |
| Driver qualification | Part 391 | CDL, medical certificate, driving record, background check. Negligent hiring and retention are claims against the carrier. |
| Post-crash testing | Part 382 | Alcohol within 8 hours and drugs within 32 hours after a fatal crash, or after an injury or tow-away crash with a citation. |
| Inspection and maintenance | Part 396 | Pre-trip inspections, defect reports, maintenance records. Brake, tire, lift-axle, lighting, and reflective-tape cases are maintenance cases. |
| Cargo securement | 393.100–393.136 | Loads must be contained or secured so they cannot shift or fall. A spilled load is evidence of a violation. |
| Equipment: rear impact guards, lighting, reflectors | Part 393 | Underride guards (393.86) and conspicuity marking. A trailer that cannot be seen at night or lacks a working guard is a defective trailer. |
| Handheld phone ban; hazardous conditions | 392.82, 392.14 | No handheld phone use. Reduce speed or stop in snow, ice, and fog. |
| Minimum insurance | Part 387 | $750,000 general freight; $1 million oil; $5 million hazardous materials. |



**The owner of the truck or trailer.** In Michigan, a vehicle’s owner is liable for its negligent operation by someone driving with the owner’s consent (MCL 257.401). When the tractor, the trailer, and the driver belong to different companies, each may be a defendant, although a company in the business of renting or leasing vehicles is generally shielded from owner liability by federal law (49 USC 30106).

**Michigan no-fault still applies to you.** Your own auto insurer pays these benefits regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107):

- your medical bills
- 85 percent of lost wages, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- replacement services
- attendant care

Written notice is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)).

Your claim against the driver and the carrier is for pain and suffering and for losses beyond no-fault. It requires a threshold injury (MCL 500.3135) and must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). Michigan’s comparative fault rule is modified, not pure:

- **More than 50 percent at fault:** you cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages.
- **50 percent or less:** your share reduces them (MCL 500.3135(2)(b); MCL 600.2959).

The noneconomic damages caps in MCL 600.1483 apply to medical malpractice, not to truck crashes.

The difference from a car case is the money available: the carrier’s $750,000-plus policy, often with excess coverage above it. If the carrier’s coverage still falls short, your own underinsured motorist coverage may add to it. Your insurer’s written consent is needed before you accept the carrier’s limit. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

**Other defendants.**

- the shipper or broker that loaded the truck or hired an unsafe carrier
- the store, restaurant, or hospital whose loading dock or delivery schedule contributed
- the general contractor on a downtown construction site whose trucks were involved
- the maintenance contractor
- the manufacturer of a failed component or a defective guard ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability))
- for a government truck (MDOT, the Road Commission, the City of Royal Oak, or SMART), the agency, under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405)

**If you were the truck driver.** Workers’ compensation pays your benefits, and your employer usually cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). If you were injured in your employer’s truck, no-fault benefits generally come from the insurer of that truck (MCL 500.3114(3)). They are coordinated with comp, which is paid first (MCL 500.3109(1)). You can also sue a third party, such as another driver or a business whose dock or yard caused the crash. See our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).

## The evidence, and how fast it disappears

Carriers must keep two kinds of records for a limited time:

- hours-of-service records, for six months (49 CFR 395.8)
- driver inspection reports, for three months (49 CFR 396.11)

After that, the regulations no longer require them. Other evidence goes faster:

- loading-dock cameras, downtown business and parking-structure cameras, MDOT freeway cameras on I-696 and I-75, and dash cameras overwrite in days to weeks
- the truck’s engine control module and event data recorder can be cleared when the truck is repaired and returned to service
- a spilled load is swept up within hours
- a damaged rear guard can be replaced before anyone measures it

The first act in a Royal Oak truck case is a **preservation letter**. It goes to the carrier, its insurer, the tractor and trailer owners, and the shipper or broker. Where the crash happened at a loading dock, in an alley, or at a construction site, it also goes to the business, the property owner, or the contractor. It demands:

- the ELD and engine data
- dash camera footage
- the driver’s qualification file and post-crash test results
- dispatch records, bills of lading, and dock appointment logs
- maintenance files
- the truck and trailer themselves, for inspection of the brakes, lift axles, load securement, and rear guard

Once a party knows a claim is coming, destroying that evidence exposes it to spoliation sanctions.

## What to do after a Royal Oak truck crash

1. **Call 911 and get medical care the same day.** Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital, on Thirteen Mile at Woodward, is Oakland County’s Level I adult trauma center.
2. **Photograph the truck:** the cab and trailer, the USDOT number and carrier name on the door, both plates, the load and anything that fell from it, the rear guard and reflective tape, and the loading zone, alley, or construction entrance if the truck was entering or leaving one.
3. **Get the police report.** Crashes on city streets are worked by the Royal Oak Police Department. Freeway crashes may be worked by the Michigan State Police. A serious crash may produce a separate commercial vehicle inspection report from the State Police. **[VERIFY: commercial vehicle enforcement; report request process]**

## Frequently asked questions

**How many truck accidents happen in Royal Oak?** The State does not publish truck crash figures by city. Countywide, 2,081 heavy trucks and buses were involved in Oakland County crashes in 2024. Statewide, 15,888 crashes involved a heavy truck or bus, and 110 people were killed.

**A delivery truck was double-parked downtown and I hit it, or swerved and hit something else. Whose fault is that?** It depends on the facts. A truck stopped in a travel lane without warning, or in a place where stopping is prohibited, can carry fault. So can the business whose deliveries routinely put it there. Your own speed and attention matter too.

**A box truck turned right and hit me on my bike. Who is responsible?** Usually the truck driver, who must yield to a cyclist traveling straight. A truck’s right-side blind spot is no excuse for turning across a cyclist. The carrier and, if the driver was making a delivery, sometimes the business are part of the claim.

**A semi was making a U-turn at a Woodward turnaround and blocked the road. Whose fault is the crash?** It depends. A truck driver who makes a turnaround the truck cannot safely clear, or who blocks lanes without warning, can be at fault. So can a driver who was following too closely or speeding.

**My car went under the back of a trailer on I-696. Is that the truck’s fault?** It may be partly or wholly. Trailers must carry rear impact guards built to federal standards. A guard that is missing, bent, or too high to stop a car is evidence against the carrier and the trailer’s owner. The trailer has to be inspected before it is repaired.

**Is there a cap on what I can recover from a trucking company?** Not on pain and suffering in a truck case. Michigan’s damages caps apply to medical malpractice. In practice, the limit is the insurance available: the carrier’s policy, any excess policy, and your own underinsured coverage.

**Why does the lawyer need to act within days?** Because the records expire. Hours-of-service logs must be kept for six months and inspection reports for three. Camera footage lasts days or weeks. The truck’s electronic data and the trailer’s damage can disappear when the truck is repaired.
