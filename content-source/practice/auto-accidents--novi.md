## Novi, MI Car Accident Lawyer

A car crash in Novi is a Michigan no-fault claim on these roads:

- **I-96** crosses the city east to west, with interchanges at Novi Road and Beck Road, and at Wixom Road on the Wixom side.
- **I-275 and M-5** run along the east side to the interchange with I-96 and I-696 at the Farmington Hills line. M-5, the Haggerty Connector, continues north as a freeway past Twelve Mile and then as a divided boulevard.
- **Novi Road and Grand River** run past Twelve Oaks Mall, the Novi Town Center, the Suburban Collection Showplace, and the hotels near the interchange.
- **The mile roads,** Eight through Fourteen Mile, cross the city, with Beck, Taft, Meadowbrook, Haggerty, and Wixom Road running north and south.
- **The residential roads** run past the subdivisions, the lakes, and the wooded parks on the west and north sides.

In 2024, the Michigan State Police recorded 1,984 crashes in Novi. They killed 3 people, seriously injured 27, and injured 579 in all. More than a quarter of them, 549, were on the interstates.

A Novi crash claim usually turns on four questions:

- Who had the right of way at a ramp, a signal, or a retail driveway?
- Was a deer, a work zone, or the road itself part of the cause?
- How do the other driver’s liability limit and your own underinsured motorist coverage fit together?
- If the road played a part, does it belong to the State, the county, or the City?

## Where Novi crashes happen

**I-96, I-275, and the interchanges.** I-96 carries commuters and freight across the city, and I-275 and M-5 meet it at the city’s eastern edge. The crashes are:

- merge and lane-change crashes at the ramps, especially where traffic enters and exits within a short distance (MCL 257.642(1)(a))
- rear-end crashes in rush-hour backups and in work zones
- crashes with tractor-trailers headed to the warehouses and distribution centers west of Beck Road
- weather crashes, when snow and ice reach the ramps and overpasses first

The freeways and M-5 belong to MDOT. A work-zone crash can add the contractor as a defendant. See our [Novi truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents).

**M-5 and its crossovers.** North of I-96, M-5 is a freeway past the Twelve Mile interchange, then becomes a divided boulevard at Thirteen and Fourteen Mile. Drivers who turn left use a median crossover or turnaround. The crashes come from drivers pulling out of a crossover without yielding, red-light crashes at the signals, and rear-end crashes at the stops.

**Twelve Oaks, Novi Road, and Grand River.** The retail corridor around the I-96 and Novi Road interchange draws shoppers, hotel guests, and visitors to events at the Showplace from across the region. The crashes are:

- left-turn crashes at the signals on Novi Road and Grand River (MCL 257.650)
- crashes at retail and restaurant driveways, where a driver leaving a lot must yield to traffic on the road (MCL 257.652)
- crashes in mall and store parking lots, including backing crashes and pedestrians struck between rows
- rideshare pickups and drop-offs at the mall, the hotels, and the Showplace

A crash in a parking lot is still a no-fault claim for your medical bills. A pain-and-suffering claim against the other driver needs the same threshold injury as any other crash. If the lot’s design, lighting, or snow removal played a part, the property owner and its contractors may be defendants under premises law. See our [rideshare accident page](/personal-injury/rideshare-accidents) and [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents).

**The mile roads and the subdivisions.** Ten, Eleven, Twelve, Thirteen, and Fourteen Mile, and Beck, Taft, Meadowbrook, Haggerty, and Wixom Road connect the subdivisions to the freeways. The crashes are:

- left-turn and red-light crashes at the signals
- crashes at subdivision entrances, where a driver pulling out misjudges oncoming speed
- cyclist and pedestrian crashes near schools and on the pathways (see our [bicycle accident page](/personal-injury/bicycle-accidents))

**Deer.** The State Police recorded 99 deer-involved crashes in Novi in 2024, about one in every twenty of the city’s crashes. They happen most often at dawn and dusk, on the roads past the parks, lakes, and wooded lots on the west and north sides. A deer crash with no other vehicle is a claim against your own insurer:

- your PIP coverage pays your medical bills and lost wages, as in any crash
- your comprehensive coverage, not collision, usually pays for the car
- there is no at-fault driver to sue

If you swerved to miss a deer and hit another car, or another driver hit you after you stopped, the other driver’s fault becomes the question.

**Whose road it is: the State, the county, or the City.** Novi is a city, and road ownership is split three ways:

- **MDOT** owns I-96, I-275, and M-5. A road-defect claim against MDOT goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **The Road Commission for Oakland County** owns Haggerty, Eight Mile, Napier, Pontiac Trail, and Fourteen Mile.
- **The City of Novi** owns the other public roads, including Novi Road, Grand River, Ten through Thirteen Mile, Beck, Taft, Meadowbrook, and Wixom Road, and the subdivision streets.

A claim against the Road Commission or the City requires written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404). A pothole or other defect in the road can make the agency a defendant alongside the other driver; on State and county roads, signals, signs, and sight obstructions generally fall outside the highway exception (*Nawrocki v Macomb County Road Commission*, Mich 2000). The notice must go to the agency that actually has jurisdiction over that stretch of road, or it is no notice. Eight Mile, along the Wayne County line, needs its own check.

## Fault is the case

In a rear-end crash, fault is rarely disputed. At a ramp merge, a retail driveway, or a mile-road left turn, it often is, and the other driver’s insurer may try to assign you half or more of the fault. That share matters (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)):

- **More than 50 percent at fault:** you lose your claim for pain and suffering.
- **50 percent or less:** your recovery is reduced by your share. At exactly 50 percent, you recover half.

The evidence that decides fault is perishable:

- the Novi Police Department crash report and diagram, or the State Police report on the freeway
- **signal timing data** from MDOT, the Road Commission, or the City
- cameras at the mall, stores, hotels, gas stations, and homes, many overwritten within days
- MDOT’s freeway traffic cameras, which generally do not record **[VERIFY]**
- the vehicles’ **event data recorders**, which store speed, braking, and throttle in the seconds before impact
- the other driver’s phone records, for a distracted-driving claim (see our [distracted driving page](/personal-injury/distracted-driving-accidents))
- witnesses, including other drivers stopped at the signal

## Who pays after a Novi crash

**Your no-fault (PIP) claim.** Your own auto insurer pays these benefits regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107):

- your medical expenses, up to the PIP level you chose
- 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- replacement services at $20 a day
- attendant care

If you have no policy, a resident relative’s policy pays (MCL 500.3114). If none exists, an occupant claims from the insurer of the vehicle they were in (MCL 500.3114(4)), and then the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3172), capped at $250,000 for medical benefits. A pedestrian or cyclist hit by a car, with no household policy, claims through the Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3115).

The insurer must receive written notice within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). It must pay each bill within 30 days of reasonable proof, or owe 12 percent interest (MCL 500.3142). Since the 2019 reform, PIP medical coverage is no longer unlimited unless you chose it, so the level on your declarations page matters. Providers’ charges are also subject to the fee schedule in MCL 500.3157, which can affect where you can get care. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance) and our [Michigan no-fault guide](about:blank).

**Your claim against the at-fault driver.** For pain and suffering, you need a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment is an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life. It does not have to be permanent (MCL 500.3135(5); *McCormick v Carrier*, 2010).

You can also recover economic losses that no-fault does not cover (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)):

- lost wages beyond the three years or above the monthly cap, which matters for anyone who earns more than the cap
- medical bills above your chosen PIP level

The suit must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The mini tort claim recovers up to $3,000 in vehicle damage not covered by your policy (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)). If the at-fault driver’s insurer will not pay it, the claim is filed in the 52-1 District Court in Novi.

**Two policies, in the right order.** In a serious crash, the at-fault driver’s liability limit may be less than the claim is worth. If you carry **underinsured motorist coverage**, your own insurer pays the difference up to your UIM limit. Follow these steps in order:

1. The at-fault driver’s insurer offers its limit.
2. You notify your own insurer and ask for its written consent to settle.
3. Only then do you accept the limit and sign the release.
4. The UIM claim then proceeds.

Accepting without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under most policies. We also check umbrella policies, which often carry their own notice terms. Against an uninsured driver, or in a hit-and-run, the claim for pain and suffering depends on **uninsured motorist coverage**, whose notice terms are often short. Two rules cut the other way:

- An owner who was required to insure a car and did not is generally barred from PIP benefits (MCL 500.3113(b)).
- A driver who was operating their own uninsured vehicle cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, whoever was at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(c)).

See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Out-of-state drivers and work vehicles.** The freeways and the Showplace bring drivers from other states. An out-of-state driver generally has no Michigan PIP claim of their own unless they own a vehicle registered and insured in Michigan (MCL 500.3113(c)). Your own no-fault benefits are unaffected. A crash with someone driving for work, such as a delivery van, a contractor’s truck, or a sales rep’s company car, can bring in the employer’s commercial policy.

**Drunk drivers and the dram shop claim.** A restaurant, bar, or store that serves a visibly intoxicated person, or a minor, is liable for the injuries that person causes under Michigan’s dram shop act (MCL 436.1801). The State Police recorded 31 alcohol-involved crashes in Novi in 2024. The claim has its own rules:

- Written notice must go to the business within 120 days after you hire a lawyer.
- The drunk driver must be named and kept in the suit.
- The suit must be filed within two years, not three.

See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**Police cars, fire trucks, and buses.** All of these fall under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405):

- Novi Police and Fire vehicles
- school buses that the Novi, Northville, and South Lyon districts run themselves
- public transit buses
- Road Commission and City trucks, including snowplows

Walled Lake’s buses are run by a private contractor, so a claim over one of them is an ordinary negligence claim. Dash camera and dispatch records are overwritten on short schedules, so we request them by FOIA in the first week. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

## What to do after a Novi crash

1. **Call 911 and get the police report.** Michigan requires a report for any crash with injury or $1,000 in damage (MCL 257.622). Crashes in the city are worked by the Novi Police Department. Freeway crashes may be worked by the Michigan State Police. **[VERIFY: report request process]**
2. **Photograph the scene.** Get the vehicles, plates, the other driver’s license and insurance card, the ramp or driveway, the signals, and the lane markings. Note every store, hotel, and home with a camera facing the scene. Footage is kept for days, not weeks.
3. **After a deer strike,** photograph the deer and the damage, and report the crash. Your insurer will ask for the report.
4. **Get medical care the same day.** Henry Ford Providence Novi Hospital, on Grand River near I-96 and Beck Road, is a Level II adult trauma center. The most severe injuries may go to a Level I center such as Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak.
5. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** within the first week.
6. **Read your declarations page** for PIP level, UM/UIM, umbrella, comprehensive, and collision coverage.
7. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the other driver’s insurer, sign a release, or accept a policy-limits offer before your UIM insurer consents.
8. **Call a lawyer within days** if fault is disputed, the other driver’s limit looks too low, a truck or work vehicle was involved, or the road played a part. The camera footage is the first evidence lost, and the 120-day road-defect notice is already running.

## The courts

- **Claims up to $25,000,** including mini tort claims, go to the 52nd District Court, First Division, at 48150 Grand River Avenue in Novi. It serves Novi and several neighboring communities in western Oakland County.
- **Injury suits over $25,000** go to the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac, with a jury drawn from all of Oakland County.
- A crash on Eight Mile, the county line, may have happened in Wayne County, which would put the case in the Third Circuit in Detroit (MCL 600.1629).
- **Defect claims against MDOT** go to the Court of Claims.
- **A fatal crash** is brought by the estate through the Oakland County Probate Court. See our [wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death).

The 52-1 District Court is at 48150 Grand River Avenue. It serves Novi, South Lyon, Wixom, and Walled Lake; Milford and Wolverine Lake; and seven townships. The Sixth Circuit is at 1200 N. Telegraph Road.

## Frequently asked questions

**I was hit merging onto I-96 at Novi Road. Who is at fault?** Usually the driver who failed to yield while changing lanes, but merge crashes are often disputed. Speed, lane position, and when each driver signaled all matter. Photograph the ramp and get the witnesses. I-96 belongs to MDOT.

**A car pulled out of a store driveway on Grand River and hit me. Who is at fault?** Usually the driver leaving the driveway, who must yield to traffic on the road. Your own speed and any obstruction to the sight line can still be argued. Store cameras often show the crash, but they are overwritten within days.

**I was in a crash in the Twelve Oaks parking lot. Does no-fault apply?** Yes. Your own no-fault insurer pays your medical bills and lost wages. A pain-and-suffering claim against the other driver needs a threshold injury, as on any road. If the lot’s design, lighting, or snow removal contributed, the owner may also be responsible.

**I hit a deer on a Novi road. Who pays?** Your own insurer. PIP pays your medical bills and lost wages, and comprehensive coverage usually pays for the car. There is no one to sue unless another driver was involved.

**How many crashes are there in Novi?** The State Police recorded 1,984 crashes in Novi in 2024. They killed 3 people, seriously injured 27, and injured 579 in all. 549 of them were on the interstates, and 99 involved a deer.

**My medical bills are covered, but I earn more than the PIP wage cap. Can I recover the rest?** Yes, if you have a claim against the at-fault driver. Lost wages above the monthly cap, or beyond three years, can be recovered from the driver as excess economic loss, without a threshold injury.

**A pothole caused my crash. Do I file against the City of Novi?** It depends on the road: - **I-96, I-275, and M-5** belong to MDOT, with notice filed in the Court of Claims within 120 days. - **Haggerty, Eight Mile, Napier, Pontiac Trail, and Fourteen Mile** belong to the Road Commission for Oakland County, with written notice within 120 days. - **The other public roads**, including Novi Road and Grand River, belong to the City, with the same 120 days.

**Where would my Novi case be filed?** Mini tort claims and claims up to $25,000 go to the 52-1 District Court on Grand River in Novi. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court in Pontiac.

**How long do I have?** - One year to give your no-fault insurer written notice. - Three years to sue the at-fault driver. - 120 days for a notice to the City or the Road Commission. - 120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT. - Two years to sue a bar or restaurant, with a 120-day notice.
