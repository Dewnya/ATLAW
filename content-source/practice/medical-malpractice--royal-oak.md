## Royal Oak, MI Medical Malpractice Lawyer

A medical malpractice claim in Michigan has rules no other injury claim has:

- a two-year deadline instead of three
- a written Notice of Intent, served on each provider at least 182 days before suit
- a sworn Affidavit of Merit from a qualified expert, filed with the complaint
- strict rules on who that expert can be
- a cap on pain-and-suffering damages

A case that misses any one of them can be lost before its merits are heard.

It is also a teaching hospital: residents and fellows treat patients under the supervision of attending physicians.

Because it is a Level I center, the hospital receives patients from across southeast Michigan, many of them transferred from another hospital. A Royal Oak malpractice case can begin in another county and end here, or the reverse.

Health-system-employed physicians practice alongside independent practices and separate physician groups. Each has its own legal entity, insurer, and notice address. A federally funded clinic is sued under federal law, on a different clock and in a different court.

## The rules, by the numbers

| Rule | Figure | Statute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deadline to file suit | 2 years from the act or omission | MCL 600.5805(8) |
| Discovery rule | 6 months from when the claim was or should have been discovered; never more than 6 years after the act (exceptions for retained objects, fraud, reproductive injury) | MCL 600.5838a(2) |
| Notice of Intent before suit | 182 days; the provider must respond in writing within 154 days | MCL 600.2912b |
| Affidavit of Merit | Filed with the complaint | MCL 600.2912d |
| Defendant’s Affidavit of Meritorious Defense | Within 91 days after the answer | MCL 600.2912e |
| Expert qualification | Same specialty; same board certification if the defendant is certified; majority of professional time in that specialty in the year before | MCL 600.2169 |
| 2026 cap on noneconomic damages | $596,400 (standard) / $1,065,000 (catastrophic) | MCL 600.1483 |
| Child injured before age 8 | Deadline runs to the 10th birthday | MCL 600.5851(7) |
| Federally funded provider | Administrative claim (SF-95) within 2 years; suit after denial or 6 months | 28 USC 2401(b), 2675 |

Economic damages are not capped. They include medical bills, lost earnings, and the cost of future care. Michigan does not award punitive damages in malpractice cases. The Affidavit of Merit is required by MCL 600.2912d, and MCL 600.2169 decides who is qualified to sign it.

## What malpractice is, and is not

A bad outcome is not malpractice. Malpractice is a bad outcome caused by a provider who failed to do what a reasonably competent provider in the same specialty would have done in the same circumstances (MCL 600.2912a). You must prove four things:

- the standard of care
- a breach of it
- that the breach more likely than not caused the injury
- damages

Michigan requires proof that the patient would have had a better outcome. A lost chance of a better outcome is compensable only where that chance was greater than 50 percent (MCL 600.2912a(2)). Before anything is filed, a qualified physician’s written review of the records decides whether a case exists. Many do not, and we say so.

## Royal Oak providers, and who answers for each

**Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital.** This is the city’s hospital, with its employed physicians, residents, nurses, and staff. Its name and ownership have changed. Beaumont Health and Spectrum Health combined on February 1, 2022, the system took the name Corewell Health in October 2022, and the hospital’s current name followed that rebrand. The correct legal entity for the Notice of Intent must be identified two ways:

- from current corporate filings
- from the name the hospital used at the time of care, not from today’s sign or letterhead

Care given under the Beaumont name may involve an entity name that differs from today’s. Claims against the hospital and its employed providers for care in Royal Oak are filed in the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court (Oakland County Circuit Court) in Pontiac.

**Residents, fellows, and supervision.** At a teaching hospital, the person at the bedside may be a resident or fellow in training. A resident is held to the standard of care for the specialty. Two questions come up in these cases:

- whether the resident acted within the scope of training and was properly supervised
- whether the attending physician was available, was told, and responded

The Notice of Intent may need to name the resident, the attending physician, and the employer of each. A medical student’s involvement, and whether a state university is implicated by it, is a separate question.

**Emergency physicians and other contracted groups.** Many hospitals staff their emergency departments, anesthesia, radiology, and hospitalist services through separate physician groups. The Notice of Intent must name the group as well as the hospital. Where a patient came to the hospital for care and reasonably believed the doctor worked for it, Michigan law can hold the hospital responsible under ostensible agency (*Grewe v Mt Clemens General Hospital*, 1978).

**Transfers into Royal Oak.** As a Level I adult and Level II pediatric center, the hospital receives major trauma, stroke, cardiac, neonatal, and complex surgical patients from other hospitals. A transfer case can involve two hospitals, two sets of physicians, and the transport between them. The question is which delay or decision caused the harm:

- the first hospital’s diagnosis
- the decision and timing of the transfer
- the transport
- the care after arrival in Royal Oak

When the first hospital is in Wayne or Macomb County, the case may involve two counties’ circuit courts, and where to file becomes its own question (MCL 600.1629).

**Children’s care.** Corewell Health Children’s operates within the Royal Oak hospital, including neonatal and pediatric intensive care. A child injured before age eight has until the tenth birthday to file, but the parents’ own claims, including for the child’s medical expenses before age 18, follow the ordinary two-year period.

**Medical offices, urgent care, surgery centers, and imaging centers.** This includes the primary-care and specialty practices on the hospital campus, along Woodward, and downtown; urgent care clinics; and outpatient surgery, dialysis, and imaging centers. **[VERIFY: outpatient centers in the city]** Frequent claims are:

The employer of each provider who touched the chart must be identified. A health-system-employed physician and an independent physician in the same building are different defendants with different insurers. Michigan rules apply to all of them.

**Federally funded community health centers.** Some Oakland County residents get primary, prenatal, and dental care at community health centers that receive federal Health Center Program funding. Identifying a deemed clinic early is the difference between a timely claim and none.

- **Medication errors** in hospitals, pharmacies, and nursing homes.
- **Cancer misdiagnosis and delayed diagnosis** in office practices and imaging centers, where the “better outcome” causation standard is argued hardest.
- **Falls, pressure injuries, and missed decline** in older patients, in the hospital and in nursing and assisted living settings.

**Injured in a crash, then harmed by the care.** Some malpractice cases here begin as crash cases. That creates two claims with different rules: the crash claim against the driver, with a three-year deadline and no-fault benefits, and the malpractice claim against the provider, with a two-year deadline and the Notice of Intent. ## How a Royal Oak malpractice case proceeds

1. **The records,** including the electronic audit trail, requested from every provider promptly, under whatever name the provider used at the time of care. In a transfer case, that means both hospitals and the ambulance service.
2. **Expert review** by a physician in the defendant’s specialty, in writing, before anything is filed.
3. **Identify the defendants and the forum.** A hospital entity, physician group, surgery center, urgent care operator, or independent practice goes to the Sixth Circuit for Oakland County care. A first hospital in another county may raise a venue question. A deemed clinic or the VA follows the SF-95 route to federal court. EMS is subject to the EMS Act’s gross-negligence standard. Serving a Notice of Intent on a federal provider while the federal two-year clock runs can lose the case.
4. **The Notice of Intent** (MCL 600.2912b), served at least 182 days before suit. A timely notice pauses the two-year period during the notice period (MCL 600.5856(c)). If the provider does not respond within 154 days, suit may be filed then.
5. **The complaint and Affidavit of Merit** (MCL 600.2912d). The defense then files its own affidavit (MCL 600.2912e).
6. **Discovery, case evaluation, and facilitation.** Cases that do not settle go to trial before a jury in the Sixth Circuit at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac. Two to four years is typical.
7. **Resolution.** Minors’ and estates’ settlements are approved by the court or the Oakland County Probate Court. Medicare, Medicaid, ERISA plan, health plan, and no-fault insurer liens are resolved before distribution. A large verdict is reduced to the cap on noneconomic damages unless the injury falls in a catastrophic category.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to sue a doctor or hospital in Royal Oak?** Two years from the malpractice, with a six-month discovery rule capped at six years. The Notice of Intent must be served 182 days before suit, and the expert review comes first, so call well inside the first year.

**Is the Notice of Intent “six months” or 182 days?** 182 days, which is not the same as six calendar months. A notice served on the last day of a six-month count can be late. Count the days.

**My care was at “Beaumont Hospital, Royal Oak.” Does the name change matter?** It can. The Notice of Intent and the complaint must name the legal entity responsible for your care at the time it was given. After the 2022 combination and the renaming, that may not be the name on the building today. We identify it from the records and the corporate filings.

**A resident, not my doctor, made the decision. Who is responsible?** A resident is held to the standard of care for the specialty. The resident’s employer, the supervising attending physician, and the hospital may all be responsible, depending on who knew what and when. Each is named in the Notice of Intent.

**I was transferred to Royal Oak from another hospital. Which hospital is responsible?** It depends on where the decision or delay that caused the harm happened: the first hospital’s diagnosis and decision to transfer, the transport, or the care after arrival in Royal Oak. Each provider is a separate defendant with its own Notice of Intent.

**I was hurt in a crash and then something went wrong at the hospital. Is that one case or two?** Usually two: a crash claim against the driver and a malpractice claim against the provider, with different deadlines and rules. Your no-fault insurer keeps paying the medical bills while both are pending.

**I’m a veteran and was treated at the VA. Is that different?** Yes. A claim against VA care follows the Federal Tort Claims Act: an administrative claim within two years, no Notice of Intent, and suit in federal court without a jury. A veteran may also qualify for disability compensation for an injury caused by VA error or an unforeseeable event.

**Is there a cap on what I can recover?** On pain-and-suffering damages only. In 2026, the cap is $596,400 in the standard case and $1,065,000 for the catastrophic permanent injuries the statute lists, adjusted each year. Medical bills, lost earnings, and future care are not capped.

**My child was injured at birth or in the NICU. How long do we have?** A child injured before age eight has until the tenth birthday to file. The parents’ own claims are subject to the ordinary two-year period, so the family should not wait.
