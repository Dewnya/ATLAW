## Troy, MI Personal Injury Lawyer

If you were hurt in Troy, Michigan, because someone else was careless, you have a claim under Michigan law, and its deadlines start the day you are hurt. After a crash, your no-fault insurer must have written notice within one year. A road or sidewalk defect claim against the City of Troy or the Road Commission for Oakland County needs written notice within 120 days; a defect on I-75 needs written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days. A claim against a bar or restaurant that served a drunk driver must be filed within two years. A medical malpractice suit, including many claims against nursing homes, requires 182 days’ notice first and must be filed within two years. Most other claims allow three years. A personal injury lawyer’s first job is to find out which of those apply to you; the second is to make sure none runs out before your claim is paid.

## What a Troy personal injury lawyer does

**The first week.** We calendar every deadline and send preservation letters:

- to the at-fault driver’s insurer;
- to a trucking company for its driver logs and electronic data, after crashes on I-75;
- to the Road Commission for Oakland County, which operates Troy’s signals, or to MDOT for signal timing and freeway camera records.
- to SMART or the City for vehicle records;
- to Somerset Collection, Oakland Mall, an office-tower or parking-structure manager on Big Beaver, a store on Rochester Road, or an apartment or condominium association, for footage and incident reports, which many overwrite within days;
- to a nursing home or assisted living community for the resident’s chart, incident reports, and staffing records;
- to a bar, restaurant, or hotel for its receipts if a drunk driver was involved.

We obtain the police report (Troy Police Department reports; freeway crashes may be State Police reports), the ambulance run sheet, and the witnesses’ names.

**The first month.** We identify every policy that may pay: your own auto policy for no-fault benefits, household policies, the at-fault party’s liability coverage, your uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage, a property owner’s or management company’s commercial policy, umbrella policies, and the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan where no auto policy exists (MCL 500.3172). For a workplace injury in one of Troy’s offices, dealerships, or industrial parks, we identify whether a third party, such as a building owner, a contractor, a vendor’s driver, or an equipment manufacturer, caused it, because the employer itself usually cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). We file your Application for No-Fault Benefits inside the one-year window (MCL 500.3145(1)) and handle the insurer’s requests for statements, records, and examinations (MCL 500.3151). You stop talking to adjusters; we do that.

**The treatment period.** The claim’s value is set mostly by the medical record, so we make sure it is complete and the bills are paid while you treat. We collect records from Corewell Health Troy Hospital on Dequindre, Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak, and your own physicians and therapists, and document lost wages with your employer. For professionals and executives in the Big Beaver offices, that includes salary above the no-fault wage cap, bonuses, and benefits, which are recovered from the at-fault party rather than from no-fault (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)).

**The demand, the suit, and the resolution.** When your condition has stabilized, we send a demand with the complete record and a valuation. The decision to accept any offer is yours (MRPC 1.2). If the insurer will not pay fair value, suit is filed in the right court:

- **52nd District Court, Fourth Division,** at 520 W. Big Beaver Road, which serves Troy and Clawson, for claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims.
- **Sixth Judicial Circuit Court** (Oakland County Circuit Court) at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac for injury suits over $25,000. Most injury cases there go through case evaluation (MCR 2.403) and facilitation before trial.
- **Sixteenth Circuit Court** in Mount Clemens where a defendant lives or does business in Macomb County, or the injury happened on Dequindre at the Sterling Heights line (MCL 600.1629).

At settlement we resolve the liens, including health plans, Medicare, Medicaid, and workers’ compensation, before you are paid. Minors’ settlements and wrongful death distributions are approved by the Oakland County Probate Court, on the Telegraph Road campus, or by the trial court (MCR 2.420; MCL 600.2922(6)). Compensation for physical injury is generally not taxable income (26 USC 104(a)(2)).

## What a Troy claim is worth, and what reduces it

A claim includes economic damages (medical expenses, lost wages, lost earning capacity, replacement services) and noneconomic damages (pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, disfigurement). There is no cap except in medical malpractice. In an auto case, noneconomic damages require a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). Your share of fault reduces the recovery, and above 50 percent it bars noneconomic damages (MCL 600.2959; in auto cases MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). At exactly 50 percent you recover half. Where more than one party caused the injury, each defendant generally pays only its own share of fault (MCL 600.2956, 600.6304), so every responsible party, the driver, the property owner, the management company, the contractor, has to be identified and named. Michigan does not award punitive damages in ordinary injury cases; exemplary damages for humiliation or outrage are available only in narrow circumstances.

In many Troy cases the practical limit is insurance. The at-fault driver’s liability limit defaults to $250,000/$500,000 unless the driver chose lower limits, down to the $50,000/$100,000 minimum (MCL 500.3009(1)); your own underinsured motorist coverage fills the gap; and in a premises, workplace, or nursing home case, the commercial policy of the property owner, management company, contractor, or facility pays. Where UIM is involved, settling with the at-fault driver without your own insurer’s consent can forfeit the UIM claim. For a worker with a third-party claim, the workers’ compensation insurer has a lien on the recovery (MCL 418.827), and how that lien is negotiated changes what you take home. A higher-earning client’s lost-wage claim is often the largest part of the economic damages, so the employer’s payroll records, bonus history, and benefits are gathered early.

## What it costs

Michigan caps contingency fees in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery (MCR 8.121). Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted from the recovery first; the one-third fee is calculated on the remaining amount. The agreement is in writing; read how costs (filing fees, records, experts, depositions) are handled if the case is lost, because that is where firms differ. A promise that “you pay nothing unless we win” should say whether that includes costs.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in Troy?** Three years for most claims. But you have one year to notify your no-fault insurer after a crash, 120 days to notify the City or the Road Commission of a defect, 120 days for a defect on a state road, two years to sue a bar, and a 182-day notice and two-year limit for malpractice.

**What does a Troy personal injury attorney charge?** Michigan caps the fee in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery, with no fee if there is no recovery. Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted only from a recovery.

**Where would my Troy case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000 in the 52-4 District Court on Big Beaver; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court in Pontiac, or in some Dequindre or Macomb County cases the Sixteenth Circuit in Mount Clemens.

**I fell at Somerset or in an office tower on Big Beaver. Who is responsible?** The property owner and, often, the management company and the snow-removal, janitorial, or security contractor. Since 2023, an obvious hazard no longer ends the claim; it goes to comparative fault. If you were there for work, you may have a workers’ compensation claim too, and a claim against an owner or contractor that is not your employer.

**If I was partly at fault, do I still have a claim?** Yes, unless you were more than 50 percent at fault, which bars pain-and-suffering damages. Below that, your recovery is reduced by your share; at exactly 50 percent you recover half. No-fault benefits are paid regardless of fault.

**My salary is above the no-fault wage cap. Is the rest lost?** No. No-fault pays 85 percent of wages up to the monthly cap for up to three years. Wage loss above the cap, or beyond three years, can be recovered from the at-fault party (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)).

**Do I need a lawyer whose office is in Troy?** No. What matters is that the lawyer knows the courts your case will be in, the road agencies, property owners, employers, facilities, and insurers involved, and will meet you where you are.

**I searched for a Troy injury lawyer and found firms in Troy, New York and Ohio. Does that matter?** Yes. Michigan’s no-fault law, its deadlines, and its courts are different from New York’s or Ohio’s. A Troy, Michigan claim needs a lawyer licensed and practicing in Michigan.

**The insurer already offered me money. Should I take it?** Not before you know what the claim is worth and what the release gives up, and not before your UIM insurer has consented if UIM may apply.
