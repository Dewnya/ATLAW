## Southfield Medical Malpractice Lawyer

A medical malpractice claim in Michigan has rules no other injury claim has: a two-year deadline instead of three; a written Notice of Intent served on each provider at least 182 days before suit; a sworn Affidavit of Merit from a qualified expert filed with the complaint; strict rules on who that expert can be; and a cap on pain-and-suffering damages. A case that misses any one of them can be lost before its merits are heard. In Southfield there is a question to answer first: who the defendant is. The hospital on Nine Mile changed hands in 2024. The emergency physician who saw you may work for the hospital or for a separate group. The specialist in an office building on Northwestern Highway may be independent, hospital-employed, or part of a health system’s medical group. And a clinic funded by the federal government is sued under federal law, on a different clock, in a different court.

## The rules, by the numbers

| Rule | Figure | Statute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deadline to file suit | 2 years from the act or omission | MCL 600.5805(8) |
| Discovery rule | 6 months from when the claim was or should have been discovered; never more than 6 years after the act (exceptions for retained objects, fraud, reproductive injury) | MCL 600.5838a(2) |
| Notice of Intent before suit | 182 days; the provider must respond in writing within 154 days | MCL 600.2912b |
| Affidavit of Merit | Filed with the complaint | MCL 600.2912d |
| Defendant’s Affidavit of Meritorious Defense | Within 91 days after the answer | MCL 600.2912e |
| Expert qualification | Same specialty; same board certification if the defendant is certified; majority of professional time in that specialty in the year before | MCL 600.2169 |
| 2026 cap on noneconomic damages | $596,400 (standard) / $1,065,000 (catastrophic) | MCL 600.1483 **[VERIFY Treasury figures]** |

Economic damages, medical bills, lost earnings, and the cost of future care, are not capped.

## What malpractice is, and is not

A bad outcome is not malpractice. Malpractice is a bad outcome caused by a provider who failed to do what a reasonably competent provider in the same specialty would have done in the same circumstances (MCL 600.2912a). You must prove the standard of care, a breach, that the breach more likely than not caused the injury, and damages. Michigan requires proof that the patient would have had a better outcome; a lost chance of a better outcome is compensable only where that chance was greater than 50 percent (MCL 600.2912a(2)). A qualified physician’s written review of the records decides, before anything is filed, whether a case exists. Many do not, and we say so.

For a claim, the change matters in two ways. **Emergency physicians and other contracted groups.** Many hospitals staff their emergency departments, anesthesia, and radiology through separate physician groups. The Notice of Intent must name the group as well as the hospital. Where a patient came to the hospital for care and reasonably believed the doctor worked for it, Michigan law can hold the hospital responsible under ostensible agency (*Grewe v Mt Clemens General Hospital*, 1978).

**Medical offices along Northwestern Highway, Telegraph, Twelve Mile, and Evergreen.** Southfield has a large number of physician practices, outpatient surgery centers, imaging centers, and urgent care clinics, some independent and some owned by a health system. A delayed diagnosis in a primary-care or specialty office is a frequent claim, and the employer of each provider who touched the chart must be identified. Michigan rules apply.

**Other hospitals Southfield patients use.** Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak (claims in the Sixth Circuit) and DMC Sinai-Grace Hospital on the Detroit side of Eight Mile (claims in the Third Circuit in Detroit). The venue follows where the malpractice occurred and where the defendants are (MCL 600.1629).

**Federally funded community health centers.** Health centers that receive federal Health Center Program funding can have their providers deemed federal employees for malpractice purposes. A claim against one follows the Federal Tort Claims Act, not Michigan procedure: an administrative claim on Standard Form 95 to the Department of Health and Human Services within two years (28 USC 2401(b)), no Notice of Intent, and suit in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan, tried to a judge without a jury. Identifying a deemed clinic early is the difference between a timely claim and none. **[ATTORNEY CHECK: identify deemed centers serving Southfield at intake; application of Michigan caps under the FTCA]**

**Southfield Fire Department EMS.** A City agency. Claims against EMS personnel are limited by the Emergency Medical Services Act to gross negligence or willful misconduct (MCL 333.20965), a limit that also covers the City as the life support agency. Private ambulance companies have the same gross-negligence protection.

## How a Southfield malpractice case proceeds

1. **The records**, including the electronic audit trail, from every provider, requested promptly. Where care spanned the October 2024 hospital transition, from both custodians.
2. **Expert review** by a physician in the defendant’s specialty, in writing, before anything is filed.
3. **Identify the defendants and the forum**: hospital entity, physician group, or independent practice (Sixth or Third Circuit); deemed clinic or VA (SF-95 and the federal court); City EMS (EMS Act). Serving a Notice of Intent on a federal provider while the federal two-year clock runs can lose the case.
4. **The Notice of Intent** (MCL 600.2912b), served at least 182 days before suit. A timely notice pauses the two-year period during the notice period (MCL 600.5856(c)). If the provider does not respond within 154 days, suit may be filed then.
5. **The complaint and Affidavit of Merit** (MCL 600.2912d); the defense then files its own affidavit (MCL 600.2912e).
6. **Discovery, case evaluation, facilitation**, and, for cases that do not settle, trial to an Oakland County jury in Pontiac. Two to four years is typical.
7. **Resolution**, with minors’ and estates’ settlements approved by the court and Medicare, Medicaid, and health plan liens resolved before distribution.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to sue a Southfield doctor or hospital?** Two years from the malpractice, with a six-month discovery rule capped at six years. Because the Notice of Intent must be served 182 days before suit and the expert review comes first, call well inside the first year.

**The hospital was Ascension Providence when I was treated. Does the name change affect my claim?** Not the claim itself, but it affects who is named. Care before and after October 1, 2024 may involve different legal entities and records custodians, and the Notice of Intent must go to the right one.

**The emergency doctor did not work for the hospital. Can I still bring a claim against the hospital?** Sometimes. Michigan recognizes ostensible agency: if you went to the hospital for care and reasonably believed the doctor was the hospital’s, the hospital can be responsible. The physician’s group is named as well.

**My care was at a community health center. Is that different?** It may be. If the center is federally funded and its providers are deemed federal employees, the claim is against the United States under the Federal Tort Claims Act: an administrative claim within two years, no Notice of Intent, and suit in federal court without a jury. We check the center’s status at the first call.

**Is there a cap on what I can recover?** On pain-and-suffering damages only: $596,400 in the standard case and $1,065,000 for the catastrophic permanent injuries the statute lists, in 2026, adjusted each year. Medical bills, lost earnings, and future care are not capped.

**My child was injured at birth. How long do we have?** A child injured before age eight has until the tenth birthday to file. The parents’ own claims are subject to the ordinary two-year period, so the family should not wait.
