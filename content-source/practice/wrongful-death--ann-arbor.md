## Ann Arbor, MI Wrongful Death Lawyer

When someone dies in Ann Arbor because another person, company, or institution was careless, Michigan’s Wrongful Death Act (MCL 600.2922) gives the family a claim, but not in the way many families expect:

- **The claim belongs to the estate,** not to the spouse or children directly.
- **It must be brought by a personal representative** appointed by the probate court.
- **The recovery is divided among family members by a judge.**
- **The deadline depends on what caused the death and who caused it.** Some deadlines are 120 days or six months.

In Ann Arbor, who caused the death changes more than usual. A death involving the University of Michigan, including care at University of Michigan Health, is a claim against the State in the Court of Claims. A death after care at the VA Ann Arbor Healthcare System is a federal claim with its own form and deadline. A crash on a city street, a county road, or a freeway each has a different road owner. This page explains how the process works for an Ann Arbor family and what to do in the first weeks.

## How an Ann Arbor wrongful death claim works

**The estate.** Only the personal representative can bring the claim (MCL 600.2922(2)). Where the person lived in Washtenaw County, the estate is opened in the Washtenaw County Probate Court in Ann Arbor. The steps are:

1. If there is a will naming a personal representative, that person petitions for appointment.
2. If not, a family member, usually the spouse or an adult child, petitions under Michigan’s priority rules (MCL 700.3203).
3. The court issues letters of authority.
4. The personal representative then has standing to hire a lawyer for the estate and file.

We file the petition as the first legal step.

**When the person who died lived somewhere else.** Ann Arbor has many residents whose legal home is elsewhere: students whose parents live in another state, patients who came to University of Michigan Health for care, and visitors on football Saturdays. The estate is generally opened where the person was domiciled, and a Michigan claim may then need an appointment or filing here as well. We work out where the estate should be opened before anything is filed.

**Notice to the family.** Within 30 days after filing suit, the personal representative must notify in writing every person who may share in the recovery (MCL 600.2922(2)–(4)):

- the spouse
- children and descendants
- parents and grandparents
- brothers and sisters
- the children of the deceased’s spouse
- anyone named in the will

The list is not limited to dependents. A parent, sibling, or adult child who was not financially dependent can still share for the loss of companionship. The Act does not rank them in a fixed order; the court divides the recovery according to each person’s loss. A person named in the will may share in the recovery but does not bring the claim. Only the personal representative does.

**The damages** (MCL 600.2922(6)):

- medical and hospital expenses from the injury to the death
- funeral and burial expenses
- the person’s **conscious pain and suffering** between the injury and death
- **loss of financial support** the family would have received
- **loss of society and companionship** for each family member

**What the family cannot recover: the decedent’s lost future earnings.** In *Daher v Prime Healthcare Services-Garden City* (July 2024), the Michigan Supreme Court held unanimously that the Wrongful Death Act does not allow recovery of the lost future earnings the person who died would have made. What the family can recover is the financial support it would actually have received. For a young person, a student, or someone who was not yet supporting anyone, that difference can be large, and it is why the evidence of support (household budgets, tax returns, what the person paid for and for whom) matters from the start.

Michigan places no cap on these damages except in medical malpractice and product liability cases (MCL 600.1483, 600.2946a), and does not award punitive damages in wrongful death cases. No-fault survivor’s loss benefits, described below, are a separate insurance claim, not part of these damages.

**Comparative fault.** If the person who died was partly at fault, the damages are reduced by that share. If their fault was greater than the combined fault of everyone else, the estate cannot recover noneconomic damages, but can still recover reduced economic damages (MCL 600.2959). Fault above 50 percent does not end the whole claim. In an auto case, the no-fault act bars noneconomic damages at more than 50 percent fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)).

**The distribution hearing.** After a settlement or verdict, the personal representative proposes a division among the eligible family members, with notice to each. If they agree, the court approves it. If not, the court decides based on each person’s relationship and loss (MCL 600.2922(6)). Fees and costs are approved at the same hearing. Because a judge divides the money, no family member should sign anything with an insurer; a release signed by one relative does not settle the estate’s claim.

## Deaths involving the University, the VA, and the hospitals

**University of Michigan Health and other University deaths.** The University of Michigan is a constitutional state institution. A death involving the University is a claim against the State, heard in the Michigan Court of Claims by a judge, not a jury:

- a death after care at University of Michigan Health or another University clinic
- a crash with a University truck, bus, or Division of Public Safety and Security vehicle
- a fall or other injury on University property

Michigan’s governmental immunity law does not shield a public hospital from a medical malpractice claim (MCL 691.1407(4)), so the malpractice rules below apply. The Court of Claims also requires written notice, or the claim itself, within six months for personal injury (MCL 600.6431). How that notice works alongside the malpractice Notice of Intent is a question for the first week, not the second year.

**The VA Ann Arbor Healthcare System.** A death after care at the VA medical center or a VA clinic is a claim against the United States under the Federal Tort Claims Act, not a Michigan lawsuit in the first instance:

- The family must first file a written administrative claim with the Department of Veterans Affairs, on Standard Form 95 or its equivalent, stating a specific dollar amount, **within two years** after the claim accrues.
- Suit can be filed in federal court only after the VA denies the claim, or six months pass without a decision, and within six months of a denial.
- The case is decided by a federal judge, without a jury, applying Michigan’s law of damages.

(28 USC 2401(b), 2675.) A death involving a contractor or a non-VA doctor may also bring a separate Michigan claim. VA benefits for survivors are a separate matter.

**Trinity Health Ann Arbor and private practices.** A death after care at Trinity Health Ann Arbor, a private practice, or an outpatient surgery center is an ordinary malpractice claim in the Washtenaw County Trial Court, with the malpractice rules attached:

- a 182-day Notice of Intent (MCL 600.2912b)
- an Affidavit of Merit (MCL 600.2912d)
- a two-year period, with a six-month discovery rule and six-year repose (MCL 600.5805(8), 600.5838a)
- the noneconomic damages cap (MCL 600.1483)

## Other causes of Ann Arbor wrongful death claims

**Crashes.** Ann Arbor’s fatal crashes happen on I-94, US-23, and M-14, at the interchanges, on the corridors, and on the campus streets where more people walk and bike than anywhere else in the county. A wrongful death claim after a crash does not step outside no-fault. The claim against the at-fault driver requires a threshold injury, and death meets the threshold (MCL 500.3135(1)). No-fault benefits are paid alongside it.

The at-fault driver’s liability insurer pays, subject to policy limits that default to $250,000/$500,000 unless the driver chose lower limits, down to the $50,000/$100,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(1), (5)). We also check the deceased’s own underinsured motorist coverage, umbrella policies, and the policy of an employer whose driver was working.

**No-fault survivor’s loss benefits** (MCL 500.3108) are paid regardless of fault by the insurer that would have paid the person’s PIP, for up to three years after the death:

- the economic support the person provided, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- $20 a day in replacement services
- funeral expenses up to the policy limit, at least $1,750 and up to $5,000 (MCL 500.3107(1)(a))

For a pedestrian or cyclist, the insurer may be the family’s own household policy or a parent’s policy for a student, and with no household policy, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3115(1)). Survivor’s loss stops at the cap. Support above it is part of the wrongful death claim. Written notice is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)); do not wait for the lawsuit. See our [Ann Arbor car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents/ann-arbor).

**Truck and motorcycle deaths.** A fatal crash with a tractor-trailer on I-94 or US-23, or with a delivery or construction truck downtown, brings the federal trucking rules, the carrier’s commercial policy, and evidence that can be overwritten within days. See our [Ann Arbor truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/ann-arbor). A fatal motorcycle crash follows the rider priority rules, and a rider’s death in a crash with no other vehicle may carry no survivor’s loss claim at all. See our [Ann Arbor motorcycle accident page](/personal-injury/motorcycle-accidents/ann-arbor).

**Drunk driving.** The family may have claims against the driver and, under the dram shop act (MCL 436.1801), the bar, restaurant, or store that served a visibly intoxicated person or a minor. The dram shop claim has its own rules:

- Written notice must go to every defendant within 120 days after the family hires a lawyer.
- The intoxicated driver must be named and kept in the suit.
- The suit must be filed within **two years**, not three.
- The family of the person who was served, including a minor, generally has no dram shop claim for that person’s own death. The claim belongs to the people that person hurt.

The criminal case proceeds separately, and the family’s claim does not wait for it. See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**Deaths on the job.** Ann Arbor’s workplaces include the University and its hospitals, research labs, construction sites for campus and downtown projects, and the delivery and service work that supports them. When a worker dies on the job, the employer owes workers’ compensation death benefits to dependents, up to 500 weeks plus burial (MCL 418.321, 418.345), and generally cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). There are two important openings:

- **A third party** whose negligence caused the death can be sued in a wrongful death action. That includes a general contractor responsible for common work areas, another subcontractor, a property owner, a trucking company, or the manufacturer of defective equipment ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability)).
- **The employer itself** can be sued only under the narrow intentional tort exception, where the employer had actual knowledge that an injury was certain to occur and willfully disregarded it (MCL 418.131(1)).

The MIOSHA investigation file is one of the first records requested. See our [Ann Arbor workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation/ann-arbor).

**Nursing homes, rental housing, and premises.** A death from neglect in a nursing home, assisted living, or memory care community may be ordinary negligence or malpractice, which changes the deadline. See our [nursing home abuse page](/personal-injury/nursing-home-abuse). Deaths in homes and on other property follow premises law:

- fires, falls, and carbon monoxide in rental houses and apartments, including student rentals, where the landlord must keep the premises fit and in reasonable repair (MCL 554.139) and meet the city’s housing code
- falls from balconies, porches, and stairs
- drowning in a pool or on the Huron River, where an owner, operator, or livery may be responsible

See our [Ann Arbor personal injury page](/personal-injury/personal-injury/ann-arbor).

**Deaths involving a government agency.**

- **City streets** belong to the City of Ann Arbor, with written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404).
- **County roads** outside the city belong to the Washtenaw County Road Commission, with written notice within 120 days.
- **I-94, US-23, M-14, and state trunklines** belong to MDOT: written notice of a road defect filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **City and TheRide vehicles,** including police cars and buses, are claims under the motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405).
- **The University** is a claim in the Court of Claims, as above.

An individual government employee is liable only for gross negligence (MCL 691.1407(2)).

## Deadlines for Ann Arbor wrongful death claims

| Claim | Deadline | Statute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crash, fall, fire, construction, product, and premises claims | 3 years, generally from the injury that caused the death | MCL 600.5805(2), 600.5827, 600.2922 |
| Medical malpractice (Trinity Health Ann Arbor, private practices) | Notice of Intent, then 2 years, with a 6-month discovery rule and 6-year repose | MCL 600.2912b, 600.5805(8), 600.5838a |
| Claim against the University, including University of Michigan Health, or MDOT | Court of Claims notice within 6 months, plus the malpractice rules for a medical death; 120 days for a road defect | MCL 600.6431, 691.1404 |
| VA Ann Arbor Healthcare System | Administrative claim (SF-95) within 2 years; suit within 6 months of denial | 28 USC 2401(b), 2675 |
| Savings provision for the estate | Up to 2 years after letters of authority, but no more than 3 years after the ordinary period ran | MCL 600.5852 |
| No-fault survivor’s loss benefits | Written notice within 1 year | MCL 500.3145(1) |
| Dram shop claim | Notice within 120 days of hiring a lawyer; suit within 2 years | MCL 436.1801(3) |
| Road defect: City of Ann Arbor or Washtenaw County Road Commission | Written notice within 120 days | MCL 691.1404 |
| Workers’ compensation death benefits | Claim within 2 years of the death | MCL 418.381 |

When the injury and the death happen on the same day, the difference between “from the injury” and “from the death” does not matter. When a person lingers for weeks or months, as often happens after a hospital transfer, the ordinary period may already be running before the estate exists, which is what the savings provision is for. It adds only a limited period, and courts apply it strictly. Open the estate and file inside the ordinary deadline. The savings provision is a Michigan rule; it does not extend the federal VA deadline.

## What the family should do in the first weeks

1. **Get the death certificate and request the Washtenaw County Medical Examiner’s report,** along with the police crash or incident report (Ann Arbor Police, the University’s Division of Public Safety and Security, the State Police, or the Sheriff, depending on where it happened), the EMS run sheet, and the hospital record. If the family’s customs call for prompt burial, tell the Medical Examiner’s office at once, or call us.
2. **Keep everything:** the vehicle, the bike, the phone, the helmet, the work gear, the medication bottles, the discharge papers. Do not authorize salvage of a vehicle until a lawyer has seen it.
3. **Notify the no-fault insurer in writing** if a vehicle was involved. Survivor’s loss benefits begin on the claim, not on the lawsuit.
4. **Do not give a recorded statement** to any insurer, hospital risk manager, or claims office, and do not sign any document, however it is described. An insurer offering to “cover the funeral” in exchange for a signature may be settling the claim for the price of a funeral.
5. **Open the estate.** The probate petition is the first legal step; the wrongful death suit cannot be filed until letters issue. If the person lived in another state, ask us before filing anywhere.
6. **Call within days** if the death involved the University, the VA, a road, a government vehicle, a bar, a trucking company, a construction site, or an employer. Those notice periods are short, and the trucking and site records expire.

## The courts

- **Death claims against private defendants** are filed in the Washtenaw County Trial Court (22nd Circuit) at 101 East Huron Street, not the 15th District Court, which hears civil cases only up to $25,000.
- **A case against an out-of-state trucking company or driver** may be filed in, or removed to, the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan, which sits in Ann Arbor as well as Detroit.
- **Claims against the University or the State** go to the Court of Claims.
- **VA claims,** after the administrative claim, go to federal court.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who can file a wrongful death lawsuit for an Ann Arbor resident?** Only the personal representative of the estate, appointed by the Washtenaw County Probate Court. A spouse, child, or parent can be appointed, but must be appointed before the claim can be filed.

**Who receives the money?** The spouse, children, descendants, parents, grandparents, siblings, the children of the deceased’s spouse, and anyone named in the will may share, whether or not they were financially dependent. The probate court divides the recovery according to each person’s loss, after notice to each of them.

**How long do we have?** - Generally three years from the injury that caused the death for crash, fall, construction, and premises claims, with a limited extension for the estate. - Two years, with a 182-day notice, for medical malpractice. - Six months for a notice to the Court of Claims in a claim against the University, and 120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT. - Two years to file the administrative claim for a VA death. - Two years for a dram shop claim. - One year to claim no-fault survivor’s loss benefits. - 120 days to notify the City or the Road Commission of a road defect.

**Our family member died after treatment at University of Michigan Health. Is that different from another hospital?** Yes. The University is part of the State, so the claim is heard in the Court of Claims by a judge, and the six-month Court of Claims notice applies in addition to the malpractice rules. A public hospital is not immune from a malpractice claim.

**My father died after care at the VA in Ann Arbor. What do we do?** File a written administrative claim with the VA within two years, stating a specific dollar amount. The claim must be denied, or left undecided for six months, before suit can be filed in federal court. Michigan’s estate savings provision does not extend that deadline.

**Can we recover the income our son would have earned over his career?** Not as such. In 2024 the Michigan Supreme Court held that the Wrongful Death Act does not allow the decedent’s lost future earnings. The family can recover the financial support it would have received, along with medical and funeral costs, his conscious pain and suffering, and each family member’s loss of companionship.

**Our daughter was a student here, but our home is in another state. Where is the estate opened?** Usually where she was domiciled, which may be your home state. A Michigan claim may then need a Michigan appointment or filing as well. We sort that out before anything is filed.

**My husband died in a crash. Are survivor’s loss benefits part of the lawsuit?** No. Survivor’s loss is paid by the no-fault insurer regardless of fault, up to a monthly cap, for up to three years. The wrongful death claim against the at-fault driver is separate. It covers the losses survivor’s loss does not, including support above the cap and the family’s loss of companionship.

**If our son was partly at fault, do we lose the whole claim?** No. His share of fault reduces the damages. If it was greater than everyone else’s combined, the estate cannot recover noneconomic damages such as loss of companionship, but it can still recover reduced economic damages, such as lost support and funeral costs.

**Can we get punitive damages?** No. Michigan does not award punitive damages in wrongful death cases. The family recovers the losses the Act lists, which have no cap outside medical malpractice and product liability cases.

**Is there a lawsuit if the driver was charged with a crime?** Yes. The criminal case and the family’s claim are separate. The family does not have to wait for the criminal case, and the civil deadlines keep running.
