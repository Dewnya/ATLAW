## Livonia Medical Malpractice Lawyer

A medical malpractice claim in Michigan has rules no other injury claim has: a two-year deadline instead of three; a written Notice of Intent served on each provider at least 182 days before suit; a sworn Affidavit of Merit from a qualified expert filed with the complaint; strict rules on who that expert can be; and a cap on pain-and-suffering damages. A case that misses any one of them can be lost before its merits are heard. In Livonia there are two questions to answer first. Who is the defendant: the hospital on Five Mile, which has been renamed since 2022; a physician group that staffs its emergency department; an independent practice around Laurel Park; or a federally funded clinic, which is sued under federal law on a different clock in a different court? ## The rules, by the numbers

| Rule | Figure | Statute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deadline to file suit | 2 years from the act or omission | MCL 600.5805(8) |
| Discovery rule | 6 months from when the claim was or should have been discovered; never more than 6 years after the act (exceptions for retained objects, fraud, reproductive injury) | MCL 600.5838a(2) |
| Notice of Intent before suit | 182 days; the provider must respond in writing within 154 days | MCL 600.2912b |
| Affidavit of Merit | Filed with the complaint | MCL 600.2912d |
| Defendant’s Affidavit of Meritorious Defense | Within 91 days after the answer | MCL 600.2912e |
| Expert qualification | Same specialty; same board certification if the defendant is certified; majority of professional time in that specialty in the year before | MCL 600.2169 |
| 2026 cap on noneconomic damages | $596,400 (standard) / $1,065,000 (catastrophic) | MCL 600.1483 |
| Child injured before age 8 | Deadline runs to the 10th birthday | MCL 600.5851(7) |
| Federally funded provider | Administrative claim (SF-95) within 2 years; suit after denial or 6 months | 28 USC 2401(b), 2675 |

Economic damages, medical bills, lost earnings, and the cost of future care, are not capped.

## What malpractice is, and is not

A bad outcome is not malpractice. Malpractice is a bad outcome caused by a provider who failed to do what a reasonably competent provider in the same specialty would have done in the same circumstances (MCL 600.2912a). You must prove the standard of care, a breach, that the breach more likely than not caused the injury, and damages. Michigan requires proof that the patient would have had a better outcome; a lost chance of a better outcome is compensable only where that chance was greater than 50 percent (MCL 600.2912a(2)). A qualified physician’s written review of the records decides, before anything is filed, whether a case exists. Many do not, and we say so.

## Livonia’s providers, and who answers for each

**Trinity Health Livonia Hospital.** The hospital on Five Mile Road was St. Mary Mercy Livonia for decades. Trinity Health Michigan announced in 2022 that it would rebrand its hospitals in phases during 2022 and 2023, and the Livonia hospital became Trinity Health St. Mary Mercy Livonia and is now known as Trinity Health Livonia Hospital. **[VERIFY current name and dates]** Some firm pages and many patients’ records still use the old name. For a claim, the name matters in one way: the correct legal entity for the Notice of Intent must be identified from current corporate filings, not from the sign or the letterhead on an older record. Claims against the hospital and its employed providers are filed in the Third Judicial Circuit Court in Detroit.

**Emergency physicians and other contracted groups.** Many hospitals staff their emergency departments, anesthesia, and radiology through separate physician groups. The Notice of Intent must name the group as well as the hospital. Where a patient came to the hospital for care and reasonably believed the doctor worked for it, Michigan law can hold the hospital responsible under ostensible agency, the rule established in *Grewe v Mt Clemens General Hospital* (1978).

**The hospitals across Eight Mile, and the others Livonia patients use.** Corewell Health Farmington Hills Hospital (formerly Beaumont Farmington Hills) and Henry Ford Providence Novi Hospital (formerly Ascension Providence Novi) are in Oakland County, so claims against them are generally filed in the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court in Pontiac. Garden City Hospital and the Detroit hospitals are in Wayne County, in the Third Circuit. Venue follows where the malpractice occurred and where the defendants are (MCL 600.1629); a case against providers in both counties can raise a choice of courts.

**Surgery centers, imaging centers, and physician offices.** Livonia has outpatient surgery and imaging centers, urgent care clinics, and many primary-care and specialty practices around Laurel Park, along Five and Six Mile, and near the hospital, some physician-owned, some independent, and some owned by a health system. A delayed diagnosis in an office practice and a surgical complication at an outpatient center are frequent claims, and the employer of each provider who touched the chart must be identified. Michigan rules apply.

**Federally funded community health centers.** Health centers that receive federal Health Center Program funding can have their providers deemed federal employees for malpractice purposes. A claim against one follows the Federal Tort Claims Act, not Michigan procedure: an administrative claim on Standard Form 95 to the Department of Health and Human Services within two years (28 USC 2401(b)), no Notice of Intent, and suit in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan, tried to a judge without a jury. Identifying a deemed clinic early is the difference between a timely claim and none.

**Veterans.** Livonia veterans treated at the John D. Dingell VA Medical Center in Detroit, or at a VA outpatient clinic, have an FTCA claim on the same path, and may also qualify for disability compensation under 38 USC 1151 where the injury was caused by VA carelessness, negligence, or error, or by an event that was not reasonably foreseeable; see our [VA benefit claims page](about:blank).

**Livonia Fire & Rescue EMS.** A City agency. Claims against EMS personnel are limited by the Emergency Medical Services Act to gross negligence or willful misconduct (MCL 333.20965), a limit that also covers the City as the life support agency. Private ambulance companies have the same gross-negligence protection.

**Nursing homes.** Livonia has five Medicare-certified nursing homes, and their claims are sometimes malpractice and sometimes ordinary negligence; which one it is decides whether the Notice of Intent, the two-year period, and the cap apply. A nurse’s wound-care decision is malpractice; leaving a resident unsupervised against the care plan is usually negligence. See our [Livonia nursing home abuse page](/personal-injury/nursing-home-abuse/livonia).

**Workplace clinics.** Occupational clinics and employer medical departments that treat injured workers are a separate case: a claim against a provider employed by your own employer may be barred by workers’ compensation exclusivity, while an outside clinic or physician can be sued for malpractice. See our [Livonia workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation/livonia).

## How a Livonia malpractice case proceeds

1. **The records**, including the electronic audit trail, from every provider, requested in the first week, under whatever name the provider used at the time of care.
2. **Expert review** by a physician in the defendant’s specialty, in writing, before anything is filed.
3. **Identify the defendants and the forum**: hospital entity, physician group, surgery center, or independent practice (Third Circuit for Wayne County care, Sixth Circuit for Oakland County care); deemed clinic or VA (SF-95 and the federal court); City EMS (EMS Act). Serving a Notice of Intent on a federal provider while the federal two-year clock runs can lose the case.
4. **The Notice of Intent** (MCL 600.2912b), served at least 182 days before suit. A timely notice pauses the two-year period during the notice period (MCL 600.5856(c)). If the provider does not respond within 154 days, suit may be filed then.
5. **The complaint and Affidavit of Merit** (MCL 600.2912d); the defense then files its own affidavit (MCL 600.2912e).
6. **Discovery, case evaluation, facilitation**, and, for cases that do not settle, trial to a jury in Detroit or Pontiac. Two to four years is typical.
7. **Resolution**, with minors’ and estates’ settlements approved by the court and Medicare, Medicaid, and health plan liens resolved before distribution.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to sue a Livonia doctor or hospital?** Two years from the malpractice, with a six-month discovery rule capped at six years. Because the Notice of Intent must be served 182 days before suit and the expert review comes first, call well inside the first year.

**My records say St. Mary Mercy. Does the name change affect my claim?** Not the claim itself, but it affects who is named. The Notice of Intent must go to the legal entity responsible for the care, identified from current filings rather than the name on the record.

**I was treated in Farmington Hills or Novi. Where would the case be filed?** Generally in the Oakland County Circuit Court in Pontiac, because that is where the care happened. A case that also involves Livonia providers may allow a choice between Oakland and Wayne County.

**The emergency doctor did not work for the hospital. Can I still bring a claim against the hospital?** Sometimes. Michigan recognizes ostensible agency: if you went to the hospital for care and reasonably believed the doctor was the hospital’s, the hospital can be responsible. The physician’s group is named as well.

**My care was at a community health center. Is that different?** It may be. If the center is federally funded and its providers are deemed federal employees, the claim is against the United States under the Federal Tort Claims Act: an administrative claim within two years, no Notice of Intent, and suit in federal court without a jury. We check the center’s status at the first call.

**Is there a cap on what I can recover?** On pain-and-suffering damages only: $596,400 in the standard case and $1,065,000 for the catastrophic permanent injuries the statute lists, in 2026, adjusted each year. Medical bills, lost earnings, and future care are not capped.

**My child was injured at birth. How long do we have?** A child injured before age eight has until the tenth birthday to file. The parents’ own claims are subject to the ordinary two-year period, so the family should not wait.
