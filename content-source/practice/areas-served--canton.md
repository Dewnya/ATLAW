## Personal Injury Law Firm Serving Canton, MI

Canton is a charter township of approximately 100,000 people in western Wayne County, the county’s third-largest community after Detroit and Dearborn, built in a generation along Ford Road and I-275. Its injuries follow its geography: the heavily traveled Ford Road retail corridor between I-275 and Canton Center; the I-275 interchanges at Ford Road, Michigan Avenue, and Ann Arbor Road; the Michigan Avenue industrial and distribution corridor; and the subdivisions, schools, and parks serving a community where approximately 23 percent of residents are under age 18. In 2024, Canton Township recorded 1,961 traffic crashes, with 550 people injured and four killed, according to Michigan Traffic Crash Facts.

Canton is a charter township rather than a city, which affects who is responsible for maintaining its roads. Public roads generally fall under Wayne County or MDOT jurisdiction, while some privately owned roads are maintained by property owners or homeowners associations. A qualifying defect in a county- or state-maintained roadway may support a claim against the responsible road agency, generally requiring written notice within 120 days. However, traffic-signal claims and injuries involving sidewalks, township vehicles, or public buildings require separate legal analysis. Canton’s police and fire are its own, under a combined Public Safety department, and its buildings and parks are its own, so those claims do go to the township.

## What is different about a Canton claim

**The roads are not the township’s.** In Canton Township, public roads generally fall under the jurisdiction of Wayne County or the Michigan Department of Transportation rather than the township itself. Wayne County maintains most township public roads, including Sheldon, Canton Center, Lilley, Haggerty, and many local streets, while MDOT has jurisdiction over Ford Road (M-153), Michigan Avenue (US-12), and I-275. Certain private roads are maintained by property owners or homeowners associations. A qualifying defect in the improved portion of a public roadway may support a claim against Wayne County or MDOT under the highway exception to governmental immunity, MCL 691.1402, depending on which agency has jurisdiction. Claims based solely on traffic-signal malfunctions, inadequate signs, or obstructed sight lines generally do not fall within that exception. Qualifying highway-defect claims generally require written notice within 120 days under MCL 691.1404, including claims against MDOT, for which notice must be filed with the Court of Claims as required by statute. Canton Township may have separate legal responsibilities for sidewalks, public buildings, and its own vehicles, so the proper governmental defendant depends on the circumstances.

**Canton’s own agencies.** Canton Public Safety (police and fire), the township’s buildings, parks, and sports complexes, and its vehicles. Crashes involving Canton Township vehicles may support claims under the governmental motor vehicle exception, MCL 691.1405, when injuries result from negligent operation of a government-owned vehicle. Injuries caused by qualifying dangerous conditions in township-owned public buildings may fall within MCL 691.1406, subject to its requirements, including written notice within 120 days. Claims arising in township parks or recreational areas require separate analysis of governmental immunity, the nature of the property, and any applicable recreational-use protections under MCL 324.73301.

**Coverage.** Canton’s drivers carry more insurance on average than drivers to the east, and the typical Canton at-fault driver has a policy worth pursuing; the Ford Road and I-275 traffic, however, comes from everywhere. Your own no-fault insurer pays your medical bills and lost wages regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107), or the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan does if no household policy applies (MCL 500.3172, capped at $250,000); the claim against the at-fault driver requires a threshold injury (MCL 500.3135) and is worth no more than the insurance available unless you carry uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage. When the at-fault driver’s limit is too small for the injury, your own UIM coverage pays the difference, with the insurer’s consent required before any settlement. See our [Canton car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents/canton), [no-fault insurance guide](about:blank), and [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**The 35th District and the Third Circuit.** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims for vehicle damage (MCL 500.3135(3)(e), up to $3,000), are filed in the 35th District Court at 660 Plymouth Road in Plymouth, which serves Canton, Plymouth, Northville, and their townships. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Third Judicial Circuit Court at the Coleman A. Young Municipal Center in Detroit; wrongful death estates (MCL 600.2922) and minors’ settlements (MCR 2.420) to the Wayne County Probate Court in the same building. For crashes near the Washtenaw County boundary or involving defendants from another county, venue must be determined under the statutory order of priority in MCL 600.1629, generally beginning with the county where the original injury occurred and where a defendant resides, conducts business, or maintains a registered office.

**The hospitals.** Canton residents may receive emergency and trauma care at nearby hospitals, including Trinity Health Ann Arbor Hospital in Superior Township and University of Michigan Health in Ann Arbor, both Level I adult trauma centers, and Trinity Health Livonia Hospital, a Level II trauma center. Outpatient facilities such as Corewell Health Canton and local urgent-care centers provide additional medical services depending on the patient’s needs.

## Frequently asked questions

**I was hurt because of a bad signal or pothole in Canton. Do I sue the township?** It depends on the location and the alleged defect. Wayne County generally maintains Canton’s county roads, including Sheldon, Canton Center, Lilley, and Haggerty, while MDOT has jurisdiction over Ford Road, Michigan Avenue, and I-275. A qualifying defect in the improved roadway may support a claim against the responsible county or state agency, generally requiring written notice within 120 days under MCL 691.1404. Claims based solely on defective traffic signals generally do not fall within the highway exception to governmental immunity. Canton Township may have separate responsibility for qualifying sidewalk defects and certain other township-related injuries, so the correct defendant must be determined from the facts.

**Why is Ford Road so dangerous?** Volume and driveways. The corridor between I-275 and Canton Center carries retail traffic from across western Wayne County on a five-lane road with dozens of unsignalized driveways, so left turns across traffic and rear-end crashes at the driveways are constant. MDOT’s boulevard plan is meant to address it.

**I fell at a store on Ford Road. Who is responsible?** Responsibility depends on who possessed or controlled the property, who was responsible for maintaining the area, and whether negligence contributed to the injury. Since Kandil-Elsayed v. F & E Oil, Inc. (2023), an open-and-obvious hazard does not automatically eliminate a property possessor’s duty; its obviousness may be considered when determining breach of duty and comparative fault. Incident reports and available security-camera footage may be important evidence, so preservation should be requested promptly.

**Where would my Canton injury case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, in the 35th District Court on Plymouth Road in Plymouth; injury suits over $25,000 in the Third Judicial Circuit Court in Detroit.

**Which hospital will I be taken to from a Canton crash?** The destination depends on the severity of the injuries, the care needed, emergency medical service protocols, and hospital availability. Nearby options include Trinity Health Ann Arbor Hospital and University of Michigan Health in Ann Arbor, both Level I adult trauma centers, and Trinity Health Livonia Hospital, a Level II trauma center. Emergency responders determine the appropriate destination based on the circumstances.

**Who pays my medical bills after a crash in Canton?** Your own no-fault insurer, regardless of fault, up to your PIP level, after you file an Application for No-Fault Benefits within one year; a household member’s insurer if you have none; the Assigned Claims Plan if no policy applies.

**How much does it cost to hire you?** Nothing up front, and no fee unless we recover money for you. Michigan caps contingency fees in personal injury cases at one-third of the recovery (MCR 8.121).

**How long do I have?** Three years for most injury suits, one year to notify your no-fault insurer in writing, and as little as 120 days for a claim against Wayne County or the township.
