## Wayne County Truck Accident Lawyer

Wayne County is the freight gateway between the United States and Canada. The Ambassador Bridge in Southwest Detroit and the Gordie Howe International Bridge in Delray carry thousands of commercial trucks a day between Ontario and the Midwest; I-94, I-75, I-96, and I-275 move that freight through the county; and the Ford Rouge complex in Dearborn, the Stellantis and General Motors plants in Detroit, the Cleveland-Cliffs steel works, the Marathon refinery, the intermodal rail yards, the airport cargo terminals in Romulus, and the Downriver industrial corridor generate truck trips of their own. A truck crash is not a car crash with a bigger vehicle. ## Where Wayne County truck crashes happen

**The border approaches.** Trucks bound for the Ambassador Bridge leave I-75 and I-96 through the Southwest Detroit neighborhoods around Fort Street, Livernois, Vernor, and the bridge plaza; trucks bound for the Gordie Howe bridge use the new I-75 interchange in Delray. Both corridors mix international freight with local streets, school routes, and pedestrians. **The freeways.** I-94 across the county from the Washtenaw line through Romulus, the airport, Dearborn, Detroit, and the Grosse Pointes; I-75 from Downriver through Southwest Detroit and the Rouge River bridge to the Oakland line; I-96 from Livonia and Redford into Detroit; I-275 along the western edge; and the interchanges where they meet, I-94/I-275 in Romulus, I-94/I-75 and I-75/I-96 in Detroit, and I-96/I-275 in Livonia. Lane-change and merge crashes at the interchanges, jackknifes and rollovers on the curves, and underride crashes in stopped traffic are the freeway cases.

**The industrial corridors.** Michigan Avenue and Rotunda Drive around the Rouge complex in Dearborn; Fort Street, Jefferson, and the Downriver industrial strip through Ecorse, River Rouge, Wyandotte, and Trenton; Mound and Van Dyke to the plants on the Macomb line; and the airport’s cargo roads in Romulus. These are the crashes with turning trucks, backing trucks, and trucks entering and leaving plant gates and truck stops.

**The city streets.** Detroit’s designated truck routes, and the trucks that leave them. Under Detroit Code of Ordinances § 55-4-81, trucks are restricted from operating on certain properly posted streets, subject to limited exceptions for property access and crossings. A truck traveling on a restricted residential street may raise questions about compliance with local traffic rules and whether that conduct contributed to a crash. Construction trucks, concrete mixers, and dump trucks on the county’s road projects, and refuse trucks, which are City of Detroit or contractor vehicles depending on the community, round out the city cases.

## Who is responsible, and who pays

**The carrier, not just the driver.** Under federal law the motor carrier is responsible for its driver’s compliance with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations and is liable for the driver’s negligence in the course of employment. Michigan adopts many Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations for intrastate commercial motor carriers under MCL 480.11a, subject to statutory modifications and exceptions. A Michigan-only hauler may therefore be subject to many of the same driver-safety, qualification, inspection, and operating requirements as an interstate carrier. **[ATTORNEY CHECK]** The rules that decide Wayne County cases most often:

| Rule | 49 CFR | What it means for the case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hours of service | Part 395 | 11 hours driving in a 14-hour window after 10 off; 60/70-hour weekly limits; recorded on an electronic logging device (ELD). A driver over hours is a fatigued driver, and the ELD proves it. |
| Driver qualification | Part 391 | CDL, medical certificate, and a background check the carrier must do and keep. Negligent hiring and retention are claims against the carrier separate from the driver’s fault. |
| Post-crash drug and alcohol testing | Part 382 | Required after a fatal crash and after an injury or tow-away crash where the driver was cited; alcohol within 8 hours, drugs within 32. |
| Inspection and maintenance | Part 396 | Pre-trip inspections, defect reports, and maintenance records. Brake and tire cases are maintenance cases. |
| Cargo securement | Part 393 | Load securement standards; shifted loads cause rollovers and jackknifes on the freeway curves. |
| Handheld phone ban; hazardous conditions | 392.82, 392.14 | Commercial drivers may not use a handheld phone; must reduce speed or stop in snow and ice. |
| Minimum insurance | Part 387 | $750,000 for general freight; $1 million for oil; $5 million for hazardous materials, which the refinery and chemical traffic in Southwest Detroit and Downriver carries. |

**Michigan no-fault still applies to you.** Your own auto insurer pays your medical bills, 85 percent of lost wages up to the monthly cap, replacement services, and attendant care under MCL 500.3107 regardless of fault, with written notice due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)); if you have no household policy, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan pays (MCL 500.3172). Your claim against the trucker and the carrier for pain and suffering and excess losses requires a threshold injury under MCL 500.3135 and must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The difference from a car case is the money available to pay it: the carrier’s $750,000-plus policy, and often an excess policy above it, against the $50,000 minimum a private driver may carry. Michigan law permits certain properly configured 11-axle truck combinations to weigh up to 164,000 pounds on eligible routes, subject to axle-weight and other restrictions under MCL 257.722. This exceeds the general federal 80,000-pound interstate weight standard, although the higher limit does not apply to every truck or road. **[ATTORNEY CHECK]**

**Other defendants.** The shipper or broker that loaded the trailer or hired an unsafe carrier; the maintenance contractor; the manufacturer of a failed component; the owner of the trailer, which is often a different company from the tractor’s; and, for a government truck, the City of Detroit, Wayne County, or MDOT under the Governmental Tort Liability Act’s motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405). A crash with an Ontario carrier raises the question of which insurer and which court; Canadian carriers operating in the United States must carry the same federal minimums and file proof with the FMCSA.

## The evidence, and how fast it disappears

Trucking companies must keep hours-of-service records for six months (49 CFR 395.8) and driver inspection reports for three months (49 CFR 396.11); after that they may lawfully be destroyed. Bridge plaza, freeway, plant-gate, and business camera footage is overwritten in days to weeks. The truck’s engine control module and event data recorder can be cleared when the truck is repaired and returned to service. The first act in a Wayne County truck case is a **preservation letter** to the carrier, its insurer, the trailer owner, and the shipper, demanding that they keep the ELD data, the ECM and EDR data, the dash camera footage, the driver’s qualification file and drug-test results, the dispatch and bill-of-lading records, the maintenance file, and the truck itself for inspection. A carrier that fails to preserve evidence relevant to pending or reasonably foreseeable litigation may face spoliation sanctions in Michigan courts, depending on the circumstances, the importance of the evidence, and whether its loss prejudiced the opposing party. **[ATTORNEY CHECK]** We also send Freedom of Information Act requests to the Michigan State Police Commercial Vehicle Enforcement Division and the investigating agency for the post-crash inspection and any citations, and we request the bridge authority’s and MDOT’s camera footage before it cycles.

## What to do after a Wayne County truck crash

1. **Call 911 and get medical care the same day**, even if you feel able to drive home. Truck-crash injuries to the neck, back, and brain often present days later.
2. **Photograph the truck**: the cab, the trailer, the DOT number and carrier name on the door, the license plates of both tractor and trailer, the load, and the skid marks. The DOT number identifies the carrier and its safety record.
3. **Get the police report.** The UD-10 is purchased at michigan.gov/tcps for $15; for a fatal or serious crash, the MSP Commercial Vehicle Enforcement Division may prepare a separate post-crash inspection report.
4. **Do not speak to the carrier’s insurer or its investigator.** They will contact you within days and ask for a recorded statement. Decline.
5. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** within the first week.
6. **Call a lawyer within days**, so the preservation letter goes out before the records expire.

Most Wayne County truck cases are filed in the Third Judicial Circuit Court at the Coleman A. Young Municipal Center in Detroit. Cases against out-of-state carriers may be filed in or removed to the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan when federal jurisdictional requirements are satisfied, including applicable citizenship rules and an amount in controversy exceeding $75,000 under 28 USC 1332 and 1441, and claims against the State (MDOT trucks, state trunkline defects) go to the Court of Claims. ## Frequently asked questions

**How many truck accidents happen in Wayne County?** 3,916 heavy trucks and buses were involved in Wayne County crashes in 2024, according to the Michigan State Police, more than Oakland (2,081) and Macomb (1,257) combined.

**The truck was from Ontario. Can I still sue?** Yes. Canadian carriers operating in the United States must register with the FMCSA, carry the same federal minimum insurance, and comply with the same safety regulations, and they can be sued in Michigan for a Michigan crash. Where the suit is filed and which insurer responds take some sorting out, which is one reason to call early.

**Who pays my medical bills after a truck crash in Michigan?** Your own no-fault insurer, regardless of fault, after you file an Application for No-Fault Benefits; the Assigned Claims Plan if no household policy applies. The carrier’s insurer pays for pain and suffering and losses beyond no-fault once fault and a threshold injury are established.

**Why does the lawyer need to act within days?** Because the records that prove the case have expiration dates: six months for the driver’s hours-of-service logs, three months for inspection reports, days or weeks for camera footage, and the truck’s electronic data can be cleared on repair. A preservation letter puts the carrier on notice that relevant evidence must be preserved for anticipated litigation, even when ordinary record-retention periods would otherwise permit its destruction.

**Is a truck case worth more than a car case?** The injuries are usually worse and the insurance is usually larger: $750,000 minimum for a general freight carrier against $50,000 for a minimally insured car. The value still depends on the injury, fault, and the record.

**I was hit by a City of Detroit garbage truck or a county plow. Is that different?** Yes. The claim is against the city or county under the Governmental Tort Liability Act’s motor vehicle exception, with its own procedures and defenses, and the vehicle’s video and GPS data must be requested immediately.
