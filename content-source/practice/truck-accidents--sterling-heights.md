Sterling Heights’ economy moves by truck. ## Where Sterling Heights truck crashes happen

**Mound Road and the plant gates.** Mound is the plant corridor, rebuilt from I-696 to M-59 in the Innovate Mound project, finished in December 2023. The crashes there are trucks turning wide into a gate across two lanes; trucks stopped or slowing in a travel lane while waiting for gate clearance, which drivers behind them do not expect; trucks pulling out of a gate into fast traffic with limited sight lines; and rear-end crashes at the signals. When the gate layout, the plant’s traffic control, or a delivery schedule that stacks trucks in the road contributed, the plant or its logistics provider may share responsibility with the carrier.

**Van Dyke and the M-53 transition.** Through-freight and plant deliveries on Van Dyke, ending at 18½ Mile, the multilane roundabout with the most crashes in Michigan in 2025, where the surface road becomes the M-53 freeway. Trucks changing lanes in the roundabout, trucks merging at freeway speed, and trucks turning across Van Dyke’s lanes at the plant entrances are the recurring crashes.

**Hall Road (M-59).** The east-west freight route across the north of the city, shared with retail traffic: trucks changing lanes into a car’s blind spot, rear-end crashes into traffic stopped at the Van Dyke, Schoenherr, and Hayes signals, and delivery trucks entering and leaving the shopping centers.

**The mile roads and Dequindre.** Box trucks and tractor-trailers serving the industrial parcels along Fifteen through Nineteen Mile and the Dequindre corridor at the Troy line: turning and backing crashes that injure drivers of smaller vehicles, cyclists, and pedestrians (see our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents)).

**Whose road it is.** Van Dyke, Hall Road, and the M-53 freeway are MDOT’s; a road-defect claim against the State goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). The mile roads and Mound are generally the Macomb County Department of Roads’, and local streets the City of Sterling Heights’, with 120 days’ notice (MCL 691.1404). Mound is a Department of Roads road (the County ran Innovate Mound), and Van Dyke and Hall Road are State trunklines.

## Who is responsible, and who pays

**The carrier, not just the driver.** The motor carrier is responsible for its drivers’ compliance with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations and is liable for a driver’s negligence in the course of employment. Michigan applies the federal rules to intrastate carriers (MCL 480.11a), so a local parts hauler running between suppliers is held to the same standards as an interstate carrier. The rules that decide truck cases most often:

| Rule | 49 CFR | What it means for the case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hours of service | Part 395 | 11 hours driving in a 14-hour window after 10 off; 60/70-hour weekly limits; recorded on an electronic logging device. Waiting time at a plant gate counts as on-duty time. |
| Driver qualification | Part 391 | CDL, medical certificate, driving record, background check; negligent hiring and retention are claims against the carrier. |
| Post-crash testing | Part 382 | Alcohol within 8 hours and drugs within 32 after a fatal crash, or an injury or tow-away crash with a citation. |
| Inspection and maintenance | Part 396 | Pre-trip inspections, defect reports, maintenance records; brake and tire cases are maintenance cases. |
| Cargo securement | Part 393 | Securement standards; shifted steel coils, stamped parts, and racks cause rollovers and spilled loads. |
| Handheld phone ban; hazardous conditions | 392.82, 392.14 | No handheld phone use; reduce speed or stop in snow, ice, and fog. |
| Minimum insurance | Part 387 | $750,000 general freight; $1 million oil; $5 million hazardous materials. |

**The owner of the truck or trailer.** In Michigan, a vehicle’s owner is liable for its negligent operation by someone driving with the owner’s consent (MCL 257.401). When the tractor, the trailer, and the driver belong to different companies, which is common in plant freight, each may be a defendant, although a company in the business of renting or leasing vehicles is generally shielded from owner liability by federal law (49 USC 30106).

**Michigan no-fault still applies to you.** Your own auto insurer pays your medical bills, 85 percent of lost wages up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each October, replacement services, and attendant care regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107), with written notice due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). The Michigan Assigned Claims Plan pays if no household policy applies (MCL 500.3172). Your claim against the driver and the carrier for pain and suffering and losses beyond no-fault requires a threshold injury (MCL 500.3135) and must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The difference from a car case is the money available: the carrier’s $750,000-plus policy, often with excess coverage above it. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

**Other defendants.** The shipper or broker that loaded the trailer or hired an unsafe carrier; the plant or logistics provider whose gate operations contributed; the maintenance contractor; the manufacturer of a failed component ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability)); and, for a government truck (City of Sterling Heights, the Department of Roads, MDOT, SMART), the agency under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405).

**If you were the truck driver.** Workers’ compensation pays your benefits, and your employer usually cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). If you were injured in your employer’s truck, no-fault benefits generally come from the insurer of that truck (MCL 500.3114(3)), coordinated with comp, which is paid first (MCL 500.3109(1)). You can also sue a third party, such as another driver or a plant whose gate operation caused the crash. See our [Sterling Heights workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).

## The evidence, and how fast it disappears

Carriers must keep hours-of-service records for six months (49 CFR 395.8) and driver inspection reports for three months (49 CFR 396.11); after that they may lawfully be destroyed. Plant-gate cameras, MDOT freeway cameras, business cameras along Mound and Van Dyke, and dash cameras overwrite in days to weeks. The truck’s engine control module and event data recorder can be cleared when it is repaired and returned to service.

The first act in a Sterling Heights truck case is a **preservation letter**: to the carrier, its insurer, the tractor and trailer owners, the shipper or broker, and, where the crash happened at or near a plant, the plant and its logistics provider. It demands the ELD and engine data, dash camera footage, the driver’s qualification file and post-crash test results, dispatch records and bills of lading, the gate’s appointment and arrival logs, maintenance files, and the truck itself for inspection. A party that destroys evidence after receiving the letter faces spoliation sanctions.

## What to do after a Sterling Heights truck crash

1. **Call 911 and get medical care the same day.** Henry Ford Macomb in Clinton Township and McLaren Macomb in Mount Clemens treat many of the area’s seriously injured; Corewell Health Troy and Henry Ford Warren Hospital treat others.
2. **Photograph the truck**: the cab, the trailer, the USDOT number and carrier name on the door, both plates, the load, and the gate or driveway if the truck was entering or leaving one.
3. **Get the police report.** Sterling Heights Police reports are sold through LexisNexis BuyCrash, and State Police reports through michigan.gov/tcps. A serious crash may produce a separate MSP Commercial Vehicle Enforcement inspection report.
4. **Do not speak to the carrier’s insurer or its investigator.**
5. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** within the first week.
6. **Call a lawyer within days**, so the preservation letter goes out before the records expire.

## The courts and deadlines

Most Sterling Heights truck cases are filed in the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court at 40 N. Main Street in Mount Clemens. Cases against out-of-state carriers may be filed in or removed to the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan. A crash on Dequindre, or with an Oakland County defendant, may allow filing in the Oakland County Circuit Court (MCL 600.1629). Claims against the State go to the Court of Claims; smaller claims go to the 41A District Court on Dodge Park Road. A fatal crash is brought by the estate; see our [Sterling Heights wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death/sterling-heights).

## Frequently asked questions

**How many truck accidents happen in Sterling Heights?** Countywide, 1,257 heavy trucks and buses were involved in Macomb County crashes in 2024.

**A truck stopped in the lane outside a plant gate and I hit it. Is that my fault?** Not necessarily. Following too closely is usually the claim against the rear driver, but a truck stopped in a travel lane without warning, or a plant whose gate operation routinely stacks trucks in the road, can carry fault too. The gate logs, the cameras, and the truck’s data decide it.

**Who pays my medical bills after a truck crash in Sterling Heights?** Your own no-fault insurer, regardless of fault, after you file an Application for No-Fault Benefits; the Assigned Claims Plan if no household policy applies. The carrier’s insurer pays for pain and suffering and losses beyond no-fault once fault and a threshold injury are established.

**I drive a truck for a Sterling Heights supplier and was hurt in a crash. What are my claims?** Workers’ compensation through your employer, no-fault benefits coordinated with comp, and a lawsuit against any third party who caused the crash.

**Why does the lawyer need to act within days?** Because the records expire: six months for hours-of-service logs, three months for inspection reports, days or weeks for camera footage, and the truck’s electronic data can be cleared on repair.

**Is a truck case worth more than a car case?** The injuries are usually worse and the insurance is usually larger: a $750,000 minimum for a general freight carrier, against $50,000 for a minimally insured car.
