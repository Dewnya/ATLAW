## Personal Injury Law Firm Serving Royal Oak, MI

Royal Oak is a city of about 58,000 on Woodward Avenue, between Eight Mile’s inner-ring suburbs and the Birmingham-Bloomfield corridor, with a downtown of restaurants and bars that draws the region on weekend nights, the Detroit Zoo at its southern tip, and Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital, Oakland County’s Level I trauma center and one of the busiest hospitals in Michigan, at Thirteen Mile and Woodward. Its injuries follow that mix. In 2024, according to the Michigan State Police, Royal Oak recorded five traffic deaths, in a year when Oakland County recorded 871 alcohol-involved crashes. In 2024, Royal Oak recorded 1,992 traffic crashes, with 441 people injured and five killed.

## Where Royal Oak injuries happen

**Woodward Avenue (M-1).** Eight lanes through the length of the city, divided, with Michigan-left turnarounds and signals at Eleven, Twelve, Thirteen, and Fourteen Mile and at the downtown cross streets, carrying commuter traffic to Detroit and the northern suburbs and, one weekend each August, the Dream Cruise. Turnaround and crossover crashes at the Michigan lefts, left-turn and red-light crashes at the mile roads, pedestrian crashes where downtown meets Woodward, and speed-related crashes on the straightaways. Woodward is MDOT’s.

**Downtown and the bar district.** Main Street, Washington, Fourth, Fifth, and Lafayette, with several dozen bars and restaurants in a few blocks, the Royal Oak Music Theatre and Emagine, the Farmers Market, and the parking structures that serve them. Pedestrian crashes at the crossings on Main and Eleven Mile late at night, drunk-driving crashes leaving downtown, falls in the structures and on the sidewalks, assaults and negligent-security claims against venues, and the dram shop claims that follow a drunk driver back to the bar that served them (MCL 436.1801, 120-day notice). See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**I-696.** The Reuther along the city’s southern edge, with the Woodward and Campbell/Hilton interchanges and the I-75 interchange at the city’s southeast corner. Merge and lane-change crashes at the interchanges and rear-end crashes in the stop-and-go, and the trucks that use I-696 and Stephenson Highway to reach the Troy and Madison Heights industrial parks. See our [Royal Oak truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents).

**The mile roads, Crooks, Coolidge, Rochester Road, Campbell, and Main.** Eleven, Twelve, Thirteen, and Fourteen Mile across; Crooks, Coolidge, Campbell, Main, and Rochester Road north and south. Intersection crashes at the signals, pedestrian and bicycle crashes near the schools and on the city’s heavily walked residential streets, and the crashes around the hospital campus at Thirteen Mile and Woodward. Responsibility for these roads is divided between the Road Commission for Oakland County and the City of Royal Oak, depending on the segment.

**The hospital.** Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital, with its emergency department, its Level I trauma center, one of the highest birth volumes in the state, and the medical-office campus around it. Medical negligence and birth-injury claims arising from care at the hospital are governed by Michigan’s medical malpractice statutes, with the 182-day notice of intent (MCL 600.2912b), the affidavit of merit (MCL 600.2912d), the two-year limitations period, with a limited six-month discovery rule and generally a six-year statute of repose (MCL 600.5805(8), 600.5838a), and the 2026 noneconomic caps of $596,400 and $1,065,000 (MCL 600.1483). See our [medical malpractice](/personal-injury/medical-malpractice) and [birth injury](/personal-injury/birth-injuries) pages.

**The neighborhoods and the new apartments.** Royal Oak’s housing is early-twentieth-century bungalows and mid-century subdivisions, with a wave of new apartment buildings downtown and along Woodward and Main. Royal Oak Ordinance §650-5C places sidewalk maintenance responsibilities on the adjacent owner, but a claim against that owner requires an independent basis for liability. See our [tenant injury](/personal-injury/tenant-injuries), [premises liability](/personal-injury/premises-liability), and [dog bite](/personal-injury/dog-bites) pages.

**The Zoo and the venues.** The Detroit Zoo, the Royal Oak Music Theatre, the Farmers Market, and the festivals that close downtown streets through the summer produce premises claims against the operators and crowd-related injuries. The Zoo is operated by the Detroit Zoological Society on land owned by the City of Detroit, which affects who is sued.

## What is different about a Royal Oak claim

**Alcohol and the dram shop.** A drunk-driving crash in or leaving Royal Oak adds a potential defendant: under Michigan’s dram shop act, MCL 436.1801, a bar or restaurant that served a visibly intoxicated person is liable for the injuries that person causes, and the claim requires written notice within 120 days of retaining a lawyer. The bar’s point-of-sale records, its cameras, and the witnesses who saw the driver served are the evidence, and they are gone within weeks. See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**Pedestrians at night.** A pedestrian struck downtown is covered by no-fault, through an applicable personal or household no-fault policy first, or the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan if no such coverage applies (MCL 500.3114 and 500.3115); the pain-and-suffering claim is against the driver, with noneconomic damages reduced by the pedestrian’s share of fault and barred if that share exceeds 50 percent (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)), which is why the signal phase, the crosswalk, and the bar-district cameras matter. See our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents).

**Coverage.** Royal Oak’s drivers carry more insurance than the regional average, and the $250,000/$500,000 default limits under MCL 500.3009(1) are common; the downtown traffic, however, comes from everywhere. Your own no-fault insurer pays your medical bills and lost wages regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107), or the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan does if no household policy applies (MCL 500.3172, capped at $250,000); the claim against the at-fault driver requires a threshold injury (MCL 500.3135) and is worth no more than the insurance available unless you carry uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage, with the UIM consent-to-settle handled before any settlement. See our [Royal Oak car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents), [no-fault insurance guide](/resources/michigan-no-fault-guide), and [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Road agencies and government vehicles.** Woodward and I-696 are MDOT’s; the mile roads are the Road Commission for Oakland County’s; the local streets and some arterials are the City of Royal Oak’s. A crash caused by a defect in the traveled roadway may implicate the highway exception to governmental immunity, MCL 691.1402. Signal malfunctions and sight obstructions require separate legal analysis. Royal Oak Police and Fire, SMART buses on Woodward and the mile roads and at the Royal Oak Transit Center, Royal Oak Schools buses, and RCOC trucks are claims under the motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405). See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

**The 44th District and the Sixth Circuit.** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims for vehicle damage (MCL 500.3135(3)(e), up to $3,000), are filed in the 44th District Court at 400 E. Eleven Mile Road, which serves Royal Oak and Berkley. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac. Wrongful death cases may require estate proceedings in Oakland County Probate Court, while the lawsuit itself proceeds in the court with jurisdiction. Settlements involving minors require court approval under MCR 2.420.

**The hospital, for treatment.** Most of Royal Oak’s seriously injured are treated at Corewell Health Royal Oak, a few minutes from anywhere in the city; Corewell Health Troy and Henry Ford Providence Southfield treat some of the rest. We obtain the full record, including the Royal Oak Fire Department EMS run sheet.

## Frequently asked questions

**A drunk driver leaving downtown Royal Oak hit me. Can I sue the bar?** Possibly. Under Michigan’s dram shop act, a bar or restaurant that served a visibly intoxicated person can be liable for the injuries that person causes, with written notice required within 120 days of retaining a lawyer. The bar’s sales records, cameras, and staff are the evidence, and they disappear quickly. The drunk driver’s own insurance is pursued as well.

**I was hit crossing Main Street or Woodward at night. Who pays?** Your own no-fault insurer or a household member’s pays your medical bills and lost wages; if none, the striking vehicle’s insurer, then the Assigned Claims Plan. Your pain-and-suffering claim is against the driver, reduced by any share of fault the evidence assigns you, which is why the signal phase and the cameras matter.

**I was hurt in a Michigan-left turnaround crash on Woodward. Who is at fault?** Usually the driver who failed to yield at the turnaround or ran the signal, but these crashes are disputed often, and the signal phase and sight lines matter. Photograph the turnaround and the signals, get the witnesses, and note that Woodward is MDOT’s road.

**Something went wrong with my care or my baby’s delivery at Corewell Health Royal Oak. Is that a malpractice case?** Possibly. Medical malpractice has a two-year deadline with a six-month discovery rule, a mandatory 182-day notice of intent before suit, an affidavit of merit, and noneconomic damage caps ($596,400 for 2026, or $1,065,000 for the most severe injuries). Birth injuries to young children have a special limitations rule. Cases are expert-driven and screened carefully.

**Where would my Royal Oak injury case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, in the 44th District Court on Eleven Mile; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court in Pontiac.

**I fell in a downtown parking structure or on a Royal Oak sidewalk. Who is responsible?** In a structure, the owner or operator and often a maintenance contractor; since Kandil-Elsayed (2023), the obviousness of the hazard is considered in evaluating breach of duty and comparative fault rather than automatically ending the claim. On a sidewalk, possibly the city under the two-inch rule with 120-day notice, while a claim against an adjacent owner requires an independent legal basis beyond the sidewalk maintenance ordinance.

**How much does it cost to hire you?** Nothing up front, and no fee unless we recover money for you. Michigan caps contingency fees in personal injury cases at one-third of the recovery (MCR 8.121).

**How long do I have?** Three years for most injury suits, one year to notify your no-fault insurer in writing, two years for medical malpractice, and as little as 120 days for a dram shop notice or a claim against the city or the RCOC.
