## Macomb County Personal Injury Lawyers

Macomb County is Michigan’s third most populous county, about 880,000 people in 27 cities, villages, and townships between Eight Mile Road and the St. Clair County line, with Lake St. Clair along its eastern edge. In 2024, according to the Michigan State Police, 21,960 traffic crashes were reported in Macomb County; 6,596 people were injured in them, 333 seriously, and 70 were killed. Macomb’s fatality count is higher than Oakland County’s despite 14,000 fewer crashes, a pattern that follows from its long, fast arterials: Van Dyke, Gratiot, Hall Road, Mound, and Groesbeck.

ATLAW is a personal injury firm based in Dearborn. In 13 years the firm has handled more than 10,000 cases for injured people in southeast Michigan and recovered more than $100 million for them. This page explains what is particular about a Macomb County claim: where the injuries happen, which courts in Mount Clemens hear the cases, which agencies and insurers are usually on the other side, and the deadlines that apply. ## Where Macomb County injuries happen

**The arterials.** Macomb County is laid out on a mile-road grid crossed by wide north-south state trunklines, and the arterials, not the freeways, are where most of the county’s serious crashes occur. Van Dyke (M-53) from Warren to Romeo; Gratiot (M-3) from Eastpointe to Mount Clemens and New Baltimore; Hall Road (M-59) across Sterling Heights, Clinton Township, and Macomb Township, lined with the Lakeside and Partridge Creek retail corridors; Groesbeck (M-97); Mound Road, rebuilt through Sterling Heights and Warren in the Innovate Mound project; and the mile roads, Metro Parkway (16 Mile), 23 Mile, and 26 Mile. Major intersections along Hall Road, Gratiot, and Van Dyke are among Macomb County’s documented high-crash locations, with intersection collisions and pedestrian safety among the concerns along these busy corridors.

**The freeways.** I-94 along the lakeshore from Eastpointe through St. Clair Shores, Roseville, Harrison Township, and Chesterfield to New Baltimore, carrying freight to and from the Blue Water Bridge at Port Huron; I-696 across Warren and Roseville; and I-75 at the county’s western edge. The 1,257 trucks and buses involved in Macomb County crashes in 2024 were concentrated on I-94 and on the plant corridors: the General Motors Technical Center and Warren Truck Assembly, Stellantis Sterling Heights Assembly and Sterling Stamping, and the supplier and defense-contractor plants along Van Dyke and Mound. Our [Macomb County truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/macomb-county) covers them.

**The lake.** Lake St. Clair draws boaters from the whole region to the marinas in St. Clair Shores, Harrison Township, and New Baltimore and to the Lake St. Clair Metropark. Boating, jet-ski, and dock injuries are claims under Michigan’s Marine Safety Act (MCL 324.80101 and following) and general negligence law, and a crash involving an intoxicated boater may support a dram shop claim against a licensed alcohol retailer if the requirements of MCL 436.1801 are met.

**The workplaces.** Macomb County is Michigan’s manufacturing county, with the auto plants above, hundreds of tool-and-die and supplier shops, and the defense industry around Selfridge Air National Guard Base and the Army’s Detroit Arsenal in Warren. Workers’ compensation is the exclusive remedy against the employer (MCL 418.131), but a worker injured by another company’s driver, a contractor, or a defective machine has a lawsuit as well; see our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation/macomb-county) and [product liability page](/personal-injury/product-liability). Certain injuries caused by negligent federal employees acting within the scope of their duties at Selfridge or the Detroit Arsenal may give rise to claims against the United States under the Federal Tort Claims Act, subject to applicable exceptions. A claimant generally must present an administrative claim to the appropriate federal agency within two years under 28 USC 2401(b) and 2675, and a separate six-month deadline generally applies after a final written denial.

**The neighborhoods and the stores.** Falls at the big-box stores and malls along Hall Road and Gratiot, apartment and condominium injuries in Warren, Sterling Heights, and Clinton Township, dog bites, and sidewalk injuries. See our [premises liability](/personal-injury/premises-liability) and [dog bite](/personal-injury/dog-bites/macomb-county) pages.

## What is different about a Macomb County claim

**The Macomb County Department of Roads.** Macomb County’s county roads are maintained by the Macomb County Department of Roads, a county department since 2011 (it was the Road Commission of Macomb County before that), which maintains more than 1,700 miles of roadway, including county-maintained mile roads and arterials that are not state trunklines. A claim for a road defect on a county road is a claim against Macomb County under the highway exception to governmental immunity, MCL 691.1402, with written notice within 120 days under 691.1404. Van Dyke, Gratiot, Hall Road, Groesbeck, and I-94 are state trunklines under MDOT jurisdiction; qualifying state highway-defect claims generally require notice within 120 days under MCL 691.1404, filed with the Court of Claims as required by the statute. Identifying the right agency early is often decisive.

**SMART, school districts, and the county.** SMART operates the fixed-route and paratransit buses in Macomb County. Crashes involving SMART buses, school buses, county sheriff and municipal police vehicles, and county road equipment proceed under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity, MCL 691.1405, with the Governmental Tort Liability Act’s defenses and procedures. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents). Certain personal injury and property damage claims against SMART also require written notice within 60 days under MCL 124.419, a requirement separate from the general governmental motor vehicle exception.

**Coverage.** Macomb County’s uninsured-driver rate sits between Wayne’s and Oakland’s, and the typical at-fault driver carries a modest liability policy; many carry the $50,000 per person minimum in MCL 500.3009(1). A client’s own uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage is therefore often the difference between a recovery that reflects the injury and one that reflects the other driver’s policy; see our [uninsured and underinsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims). When no household policy applies, no-fault benefits come through the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan, capped at $250,000 (MCL 500.3172).

**Venue.** Under MCL 600.1629, venue for a personal injury lawsuit follows a statutory order of priority, generally beginning with the county where the original injury occurred and where a defendant resides, conducts business, or maintains a registered office. A crash in Sterling Heights between two Macomb residents goes to the Sixteenth Circuit in Mount Clemens; a crash near the Wayne or Oakland county line requires applying the statutory venue rules to determine the proper county. The choice affects the jury pool, the judge, scheduling, and facilitation practice, and it is made with the client.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many car accidents happen in Macomb County?** 21,960 in 2024, according to the Michigan State Police, with 6,596 people injured and 70 killed.

**Where would my Macomb County injury lawsuit be filed?** Most are filed in the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court at 40 N. Main Street in Mount Clemens, which hears civil cases over $25,000 from all 27 Macomb County communities. Smaller claims go to the district court for the community where the defendant lives or the injury occurred. Some cases can be filed in Wayne or Oakland instead, depending on where the defendant lives or does business.

**I was hurt because of a pothole or missing signal on a Macomb County road. Who do I claim against?** On a mile road or other county road, the Macomb County Department of Roads, with written notice within 120 days. MDOT, with a 120-day highway-defect notice generally required under MCL 691.1404 and filed with the Court of Claims. On a city street, the city. A lawyer can identify the agency from the location in a day.

**Do you have an office in Macomb County?** Our office is in Dearborn. We serve clients throughout Macomb County and welcome clients to our Dearborn office for a consultation. If you are unable to visit, home or hospital visits can be arranged as needed.

**I was injured in a boating accident on Lake St. Clair. Is that a personal injury case?** Yes. Boat operators owe the same duty of reasonable care as drivers, Michigan’s Marine Safety Act sets operating rules whose violation is evidence of negligence, and available liability coverage depends on the boat owner’s watercraft insurance, any applicable homeowners policy, and the specific policy terms. Michigan automobile no-fault benefits generally do not apply to a boating-only accident. A boating injury claim may proceed under Michigan negligence and vessel-liability law, with a three-year filing period generally applicable to personal injury claims, subject to the circumstances and any applicable maritime law.

**I was hurt at a plant in Warren or Sterling Heights. Can I sue?** Not your employer, in most cases; workers’ compensation is the exclusive remedy. You can sue a third party whose negligence caused the injury, such as another contractor on site, the manufacturer of a defective machine, or a driver who hit you on company business, and you can pursue both claims at once.

**How much does it cost to hire you?** Nothing up front, and no fee unless we recover money for you. Michigan caps contingency fees in personal injury cases at one-third of the recovery (MCR 8.121).

**How long do I have?** Three years for most injury suits, one year to notify your no-fault insurer in writing, and as little as 120 days for a claim against the county or a city. See the table above.
