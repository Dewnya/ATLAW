## Livonia Dog Bite Lawyer

Michigan’s dog bite law is short and strict. If a dog bites you without provocation while you are on public property or lawfully on private property, the owner is liable for your damages, regardless of whether the dog had ever bitten before or whether the owner knew it was dangerous (MCL 287.351). You do not have to prove negligence. In Livonia the bites happen in the yards and driveways of the subdivisions, on the sidewalks and in the City parks, on the trails of Hines Park, in the condominium and apartment communities along Seven Mile, Newburgh, and Farmington, and on mail and delivery routes. The victim is often a child, a neighbor out for a walk, or a worker at the door. And the person who pays is usually not the owner: when the owner is insured, the claim is paid by the owner’s homeowner’s or renter’s insurance.

## The statute, and the two defenses

**Strict liability.** Under MCL 287.351 you prove three things. First, a dog bit you. Second, you were on public property or lawfully on private property. That includes the owner’s own property as a guest, a customer, a tenant, a delivery driver, a utility worker, a mail carrier, or a child playing with permission; the statute specifically covers people performing a duty imposed by law or by the postal service. Third, you did not provoke the dog. The dog’s history and the owner’s knowledge do not matter. There is no “one free bite” in Michigan.

**Provocation.** The owner’s main defense. Michigan courts judge it by whether the person’s conduct would provoke a dog, not by what the person intended, so it can be unintentional: stepping on a dog’s tail by accident can be provocation. Petting a dog, walking past a fence, entering a yard with permission, or knocking on a door is not; hitting, teasing, or cornering a dog may be. The witnesses to the seconds before the bite decide it.

**Trespass.** A person unlawfully on private property is not covered by the statute. A child who wanders into a neighbor’s yard is the contested case; a delivery driver or a guest is lawfully present.

**Comparative fault.** Michigan courts have held that the general comparative-fault rule does not reduce a claim under the dog bite statute; the owner’s defenses are limited to trespass and provocation (*Hill v Sacka*, Mich App 2003).

**Injuries without a bite.** A knockdown by a jumping dog, a fall while fleeing, or a bicycle crash caused by a chasing dog is not covered by the statute. It is a negligence claim, and comparative fault applies. The owner’s knowledge of the dog’s tendencies, a dog off its leash in violation of the City’s rule, or a broken fence are the evidence. ## Livonia Animal Control, the City’s dog rules, and why they matter to the claim

**Who handles a Livonia bite.** Livonia’s Animal Control office is part of the City’s Public Service Division, not the Police Department, and its officers enforce the City’s animal ordinances and investigate bites. It works from the Public Works building near Schoolcraft and Farmington. During weekday business hours, a bite is reported to Public Service at (734) 466-2655, which records an animal bite report; after hours and on weekends, the Livonia Police Department takes the report at (734) 466-2470. Include any medical records with the report. The report starts the rabies observation, creates the official record, and surfaces prior complaints about the dog. We obtain it, and any prior complaints, with a Freedom of Information Act request to the City.

**The City’s rules.** Every dog in Livonia must be licensed by the City, and a license is issued only with a current rabies vaccination certificate; the license year runs from March 1 through the end of February. Michigan law separately requires every dog four months or older to be licensed and vaccinated (MCL 287.266(1), (8)). A dog must be on a leash held by a responsible person on streets, sidewalks, parks, and other public places, and on someone else’s property without permission, and must be leashed in City parks. A household may keep up to three dogs.

A violation does not create the claim, the statute does that, but a dog loose in violation of the leash rule is evidence in a negligence claim for a non-bite injury, and the City’s licensing and vaccination records identify an owner who denies the dog is theirs.

**Dangerous dogs.** Michigan’s Dangerous Animals Act (MCL 287.321 to 287.323) allows a sworn complaint asking the district court to declare a dog that has caused serious injury dangerous, with destruction or strict confinement as the remedies. A finding is strong evidence in the civil case, but the civil case does not depend on it.

## Who pays in Livonia

**Homeowner’s and renter’s insurance.** Dog bite liability is covered by the standard homeowner’s policy and many renter’s policies, often with $100,000 to $500,000 in liability coverage. The owner reports the bite, the insurer pays, and the insurer cannot require the owner to give up the dog. That is why a claim against a neighbor or a relative is less of a barrier than it sounds, and why many bite claims settle without a lawsuit. Some policies exclude certain breeds or dogs with a bite history, and coverage depends on the policy; it must be read.

**Apartments, condominiums, and landlords.** Livonia’s condominium and apartment communities along Seven Mile, Newburgh, Farmington, and Haggerty are where bites happen in shared hallways, courtyards, walks, and parking lots. In a condominium, the association’s bylaws usually regulate pets, and an association that knew of a dangerous dog and had the power to require its removal may face the same kind of claim as a landlord. A tenant who owns the dog is liable under the statute, but many tenants carry no renter’s insurance. A landlord is generally not liable for a tenant’s dog, except where the landlord knew the dog was dangerous and had the ability to have it removed, through the lease or otherwise, and did not (*Szkodzinski v Griffin*, Mich App 1988). Prior complaints to the management office, pet registration records, and the complex’s pet policy are the evidence, and we ask for them early. See our [tenant injury page](/personal-injury/tenant-injuries).

**Delivery and utility workers.** Package and food delivery drivers, meter readers, and home health aides bitten on a Livonia route have the statutory claim against the owner and a workers’ compensation claim against their employer at the same time. The comp insurer has a lien on the recovery (MCL 418.827(5)). **Postal carriers** are federal employees: their work-injury benefits come under the Federal Employees’ Compensation Act, not Michigan’s Act, and the federal government is repaid from any recovery against the owner (5 USC 8101 et seq., 8132). Gig drivers who are classified as independent contractors may have no comp coverage, which makes the owner’s insurance the main source. See our [Livonia workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation/livonia).

**Uninsured owners.** Where the owner rents and carries no insurance, the claim is against the owner personally and is worth what the owner can pay. That is when the search for another responsible party matters: the landlord, a co-owner or keeper of the dog, or a business that kept it on its property (see our [premises liability page](/personal-injury/premises-liability)).

## Injuries, children, and scarring

Dog bites cause puncture and crush wounds, torn tendons and nerves, hand and forearm fractures, and facial injuries. Wounds are often left open and treated with antibiotics, and infection is a routine complication. The biting dog is observed for ten days; if it cannot be found, the victim receives post-exposure rabies treatment. In Livonia, adults are often treated at Trinity Health Livonia Hospital, Corewell Health Farmington Hills, or Garden City Hospital; serious facial injuries in children are often treated by pediatric plastic surgeons in Royal Oak, Detroit, or Ann Arbor. For older adults, a knockdown by a dog can mean a hip or wrist fracture, which is a negligence claim even without a bite.

**Children** are often bitten on the face and head. A facial bite on a child can mean scar revision surgeries over years as the child grows, and psychological injury that Michigan law compensates and a counselor should document. A minor’s settlement must be approved by the court, with the funds protected until adulthood (MCR 2.420); for a Livonia child that is usually the Wayne County Probate Court or the circuit court where the case is pending. A child has until the later of the ordinary three years or one year after the 18th birthday to sue (MCL 600.5851(1)). The evidence does not keep that long; settle or sue when the plastic surgeon’s projection of future revisions is clear.

**Damages** include medical treatment and future scar revision, lost wages (including a parent’s time off work), pain and suffering, permanent disfigurement, and psychological injury, with no cap. A fatal attack is a wrongful death claim brought by the estate; see our [Livonia wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death/livonia).

## What to do after a dog bite in Livonia

1. **Get medical care the same day**, even for a bite that looks minor; the record dated that day is the case.
2. **Identify the dog and the owner**: name, address or unit number, and vaccination status. If the owner refuses, note the address and the dog’s description; the City’s license records can identify the owner.
3. **Report the bite** to Livonia Animal Control through Public Service at (734) 466-2655 during business hours, or to the Livonia Police Department at (734) 466-2470 after hours. In an apartment or condominium community, also report it to the management office in writing and keep a copy.
4. **Photograph the wounds** that day and as they heal, and the place where the bite happened.
5. **Get witness names.** Provocation is decided by what witnesses say happened in the seconds before the bite.
6. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the owner’s insurer or accept an early offer; facial scarring is not valued until it has matured.
7. **Ask about the owner’s insurance** and, in a rental, the landlord’s.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I report a dog bite in Livonia?** Call Livonia Animal Control through Public Service at (734) 466-2655 during weekday business hours; after hours and on weekends, call the Livonia Police Department at (734) 466-2470. Animal Control is part of the City’s Public Service Division and investigates bites.

**The dog had never bitten anyone before. Does the owner still have to pay?** Yes. Michigan’s statute makes the owner liable regardless of the dog’s history or the owner’s knowledge, as long as you were lawfully present and did not provoke the dog.

**The owner is my neighbor and I do not want to sue them.** In an insured case the claim is paid by the owner’s homeowner’s or renter’s insurance, not by the neighbor, and the insurer cannot make them give up the dog. Many cases settle without a lawsuit.

**I was bitten by another resident’s dog in my condominium complex. Is the association responsible?** Possibly. The dog’s owner is liable under the statute. A landlord or association may be liable if it knew the dog was dangerous, for example from earlier complaints, and could have required its removal but did not.

**I am a mail carrier and was bitten on my Livonia route. What are my claims?** Two: federal workers’ compensation under the Federal Employees’ Compensation Act, through the Postal Service, and a claim against the dog’s owner. The federal government is repaid part of its benefits from the owner recovery, so the two must be coordinated.

**My child was bitten on the face. How long do we have?** Until the later of three years or one year after the child’s 18th birthday. The case should still be brought when the medical picture is clear, and a child’s settlement is approved by the court with the funds protected until adulthood.

**What counts as provocation?** Conduct that would provoke a dog, judged by what happened rather than by intent: hitting, teasing, cornering, stepping on it. Petting a dog, walking past it, or knocking on a door is not provocation.
