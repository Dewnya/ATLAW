## Waterford, MI Motorcycle Accident Lawyer

A motorcycle crash in Waterford Township is governed by a rule many riders learn only after they are hurt: under Michigan no-fault, a motorcycle is not a “motor vehicle.” That definition decides who pays your medical bills, how much they pay, whether you have a no-fault claim at all, and how the insurer will argue the helmet question. ## Where Waterford motorcycle crashes happen

**The left turn on M-59.** A driver turning left across oncoming traffic, misjudging the speed of a motorcycle or not seeing it at all, is the recurring account in rider cases. Under Michigan law a driver must yield to oncoming traffic before turning left (MCL 257.650). On Highland Road the turning driver has several lanes to cross, often looking past a stopped vehicle in the opposite turn lane that hides an approaching rider, at the Crescent Lake, Airport Road, Williams Lake, and Pontiac Lake signals and at the retail driveways between them. Highland at Crescent Lake ranked ninth in Oakland County for crashes in 2024, with 22 people injured.

**Dixie Highway.** Drivers turn left across Dixie into dealerships, yards, and strip centers between the Walton, Sashabaw, Scott Lake, and Andersonville signals; trucks swing wide into the same driveways; and the heavy aggregate haulers that use Dixie can leave gravel and stone on the pavement. A rider who goes down on spilled load has a claim against the hauler that failed to secure it. See our [Waterford truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/waterford).

**The lake roads.** Walton, Sashabaw, Williams Lake, Pontiac Lake, Elizabeth Lake, Cass Lake, Cooley Lake, Scott Lake, Hatchery, and Andersonville are the roads riders choose in summer, and the ones with the most hazards for them: curves and hills where a driver crossing the center line meets a rider head-on, driveways and lake-access drives on short sight lines where a driver pulls out without seeing a narrow headlight, and stop-controlled side streets where a driver looks for cars and misses a motorcycle. Sand and gravel wash onto curves and collect at driveway aprons, and summer traffic includes drivers towing boats and trailers who need more room to stop and turn.

**Deer.** A rider who hits a deer has no other vehicle involved and therefore no PIP claim; the bills go to health insurance and the motorcycle policy’s own medical coverage. If a car swerved into the rider to avoid a deer, the car is involved and its insurer is first in line.

**The road itself.** A single-vehicle crash caused by a pothole, a broken joint, a pavement drop-off, a steel plate left by road work, or loose gravel from a defective repair is a claim against the agency with jurisdiction under the highway exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1402). In Waterford that means one of two agencies, and never the township:

- **MDOT** for Highland Road (M-59), Dixie Highway, and Telegraph (US-24): written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **The Road Commission for Oakland County** for Walton, Sashabaw, Crescent Lake, Williams Lake, Pontiac Lake, Elizabeth Lake, Cass Lake, Airport Road, Scott Lake, Andersonville, and most subdivision streets: written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).

A defect that was readily apparent to an ordinary observer for 30 days or more is conclusively presumed known to the agency (MCL 691.1403) Notice to the wrong agency is not notice, and a notice sent to Waterford Township for a road defect is sent to an agency that does not own the road. Sand or gravel that washed onto the road naturally is usually not a defect in the road itself.

**Bars and restaurants.** 31 percent of Michigan’s 2024 rider deaths involved alcohol. A bar, restaurant, or lakeside establishment that over-served the driver who hit a rider can be liable under the dram shop act: written notice within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, and suit within two years (MCL 436.1801(3)). See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

## Who pays a Waterford rider’s medical bills

**If a car or truck was involved, you have no-fault benefits, but not from your motorcycle insurer.** Under MCL 500.3114(5), your Personal Injury Protection benefits come, in order, from:

1. the insurer of the owner or registrant of the motor vehicle involved;
2. the insurer of that vehicle’s operator;
3. the motor vehicle insurer of the motorcycle’s operator;
4. the motor vehicle insurer of the motorcycle’s owner or registrant.

“Involved” does not require contact; a car that turned across M-59 and forced you down is involved even if it never touched you. If no insurer in that chain exists, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan pays (MCL 500.3172), with its $250,000 medical cap, unless you owned the motorcycle and it was uninsured (MCL 500.3113(b))

The benefits are the ones a driver would get under MCL 500.3107: medical expenses up to the PIP limit on the policy that pays, 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year, $20 a day in replacement services, attendant care, and mileage. Written notice is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

The first medical limit is set by a policy you did not choose. Since the 2019 reform, the PIP medical level on the policy first in priority pays first; if the driver who hit you chose $250,000, that policy pays up to $250,000, and a lower-priority policy with more coverage, such as your own car policy, may then pay the rest.

**If no car or truck was involved, no-fault does not apply.** A single-vehicle crash on a curve, a pothole, gravel, or a deer produces no PIP claim. Your bills go to your health insurance and any medical coverage you bought on the motorcycle policy, and your injury claim is against whoever caused the hazard, if anyone did. A rider hit by a car, by contrast, usually has a full PIP claim, and which insurer pays it is the first question.

**Your motorcycle.** Your own collision or comprehensive coverage pays for the bike. The mini tort claim does not cover a motorcycle, because a motorcycle is not a “motor vehicle” under the no-fault act (*Nerat v Swacker*, 150 Mich App 61, 1986).

## Your claim against the driver who hit you

For pain and suffering and economic losses beyond no-fault, you sue the at-fault driver if your injury is a serious impairment of body function, permanent serious disfigurement, or death (MCL 500.3135(1)). Motorcycle injuries clear that threshold more often than car injuries: fractures, road rash requiring grafts, torn ligaments, [brain injuries](/personal-injury/brain-injury), and [spinal cord injuries](/personal-injury/spinal-cord-injury). You also recover excess economic damages above the PIP limit that applied to you, and wage loss above the no-fault cap or beyond three years (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)). You have three years to sue (MCL 600.5805(2)); a fatal crash is brought by the estate (see our [wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death)).

**What the claim is worth depends on the other driver’s policy.** Liability limits on M-59 and Dixie range from the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)) to large policies, and some drivers carry nothing. The recovery then turns on your own **uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage**, which must be on the motorcycle policy; many car policies exclude the insured while riding an owned motorcycle. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Comparative fault is the fight.** Above 50 percent at fault you recover no noneconomic damages; below that your award is reduced by your share, and at exactly 50 percent you recover half (MCL 500.3135(2)(b), 600.2959). Economic damages beyond no-fault are reduced by your share as well. Insurers argue speed, lane position, speed for a curve, lane splitting (illegal in Michigan), and “came out of nowhere.” The police report, witnesses, the other driver’s phone records, store and gas-station cameras along M-59 and Dixie, home and doorbell cameras on the lake roads, and the rider’s own camera answer it.

## The helmet question

Since 2012, Michigan has let riders 21 and older ride without a helmet if they carry at least $20,000 in first-party medical coverage and have either held a motorcycle endorsement for two years or passed an approved safety course (MCL 257.658). Riders under 21 must wear a helmet. If you met those conditions, riding helmetless was legal, so it was not a traffic violation. In a head-injury case the insurer will argue a helmet would have reduced the injury, which is a damages argument for the jury, not a bar; in a leg or shoulder case it is irrelevant.

## What to do after a Waterford motorcycle crash

1. **Get medical care the same day.** McLaren Oakland and Trinity Health Oakland in Pontiac are both Level II trauma centers; the most serious injuries may go to Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak, a Level I center.
2. **Get the police report** from the Waterford Township Police Department, or from the State Police or Oakland County Sheriff where they worked the crash. If a car caused the crash without contact, make sure the report says so. **[VERIFY: report request process]**
3. **Identify every vehicle involved**, including the one that never touched you, and any truck that left material on the road.
4. **Photograph the bike, the scene, the intersection, curve, or driveway, the road surface and any gravel or debris, your gear, and your injuries**, and note every business and home with a camera facing the road. Do not repair or sell the bike.
5. **Read your motorcycle policy for first-party medical, UM/UIM, and collision coverage**, and your car policy’s PIP level.
6. **Apply for no-fault benefits with the right insurer within the first week**, and do not give a recorded statement to the other driver’s insurer.
7. **If a road defect was involved, call within days.** The 120-day Road Commission notice is already running.

## Frequently asked questions

**A driver turned left in front of me on M-59. Who is at fault?** Usually the turning driver, who must yield to oncoming traffic. The insurer will argue your speed; the police diagram, store cameras, and witnesses answer it. The car is “involved” whether or not it touched you, so its owner’s insurer is first in line for your medical bills.

**Are motorcycles covered by no-fault?** A motorcycle is not a “motor vehicle” under the no-fault act, so a rider who crashes alone has no PIP claim. A rider hit by a car or truck does: from that vehicle’s insurer first, under the priority rule.

**I went down on gravel on a lake road. Do I have a claim?** It depends where the gravel came from. Gravel dropped by a truck is a claim against the hauler; gravel left by a defective road repair may be a claim against the Road Commission, with notice within 120 days; sand or gravel that washed onto the road on its own usually is not anyone’s legal fault. With no car involved, there is no PIP claim.

**I hit a deer. Does no-fault pay?** Not if no car or truck was involved. Your health insurance and the medical coverage on your motorcycle policy pay. If a car swerved into you, its insurer pays PIP.

**I wasn’t wearing a helmet. Can I still recover?** Yes, if you were 21 or older with the required $20,000 medical coverage and either two years of endorsement or a safety course. The insurer can argue a helmet would have reduced a head injury, which goes to damages, not to whether you have a claim.

**I went down on a pothole on Williams Lake Road. Do I notify Waterford Township?** No. Williams Lake Road is a county road. Written notice goes to the Road Commission for Oakland County within 120 days, stating the exact location, the defect, the injury, and the witnesses.

**How long do I have?** One year to notify the responsible no-fault insurer, three years to sue the at-fault driver, two years to sue a bar, 120 days for a notice to the Road Commission, and 120 days to file notice of a road-defect claim against the State.
