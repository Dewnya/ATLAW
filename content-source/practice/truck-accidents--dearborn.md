## Where Dearborn truck crashes happen

**I-94 and the Southfield Freeway.** I-94 across the south of the city, with the Rotunda, Oakwood, and Greenfield interchanges serving the Rouge and Ford; the I-94/M-39 interchange; and the Southfield Freeway’s short ramps north through the city. Merge and lane-change crashes at the interchanges, rear-end and underride crashes in stopped traffic, and rollovers on the ramps.

**The Rouge and the industrial south.** Rotunda Drive, Oakwood Boulevard, Miller Road, Schaefer south of Michigan Avenue, and the plant gates and truck entrances of the Rouge complex and the steel works. Turning trucks, backing trucks, and trucks entering and leaving gates and yards: the low-speed, high-weight crashes that injure drivers of smaller vehicles, cyclists, and pedestrians.

**Michigan Avenue, Ford Road, and Telegraph.** Through-trucks on the state trunklines and delivery trucks serving the east-side business district and Fairlane. A truck turning across Michigan Avenue’s pedestrian traffic between Wyoming and Schaefer is a common Dearborn risk.

**Whose road it is.** Michigan Avenue, Ford Road, Telegraph, M-39, and I-94 are MDOT’s; a road-defect claim goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). Rotunda, Oakwood, and the neighborhood streets are the City’s or the County’s, with 120 days’ notice (MCL 691.1404). **[ATTORNEY CHECK: jurisdiction of Rotunda and Oakwood]**

## Who is responsible, and who pays

**The carrier, not just the driver.** The motor carrier is responsible for its drivers’ compliance with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations and is liable for a driver’s negligence in the course of employment; Michigan has adopted the federal rules for intrastate carriers (MCL 480.11a), so a hauler running between the Rouge and a Downriver yard is held to the same standards as a long-haul carrier. The rules that decide Dearborn cases most often:

| Rule | 49 CFR | What it means for the case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hours of service | Part 395 | 11 hours driving in a 14-hour window after 10 off; 60/70-hour weekly limits; recorded on an electronic logging device. A driver over hours is a fatigued driver, and the ELD proves it. |
| Driver qualification | Part 391 | CDL, medical certificate, driving record, background check. Negligent hiring and retention are claims against the carrier separate from the driver’s fault. |
| Post-crash testing | Part 382 | Alcohol within 8 hours and drugs within 32 after a fatal crash, or an injury or tow-away crash with a citation. |
| Inspection and maintenance | Part 396 | Pre-trip inspections, defect reports, maintenance records. Brake and tire cases are maintenance cases. |
| Cargo securement | Part 393 | Securement standards; shifted steel coils and auto parts cause rollovers. |
| Handheld phone ban; hazardous conditions | 392.82, 392.14 | No handheld phone use; reduce speed or stop in snow, ice, and fog. |
| Minimum insurance | Part 387 | $750,000 general freight; $1 million oil; $5 million hazardous materials. |

**Michigan no-fault still applies to you.** Your own auto insurer pays your medical bills, 85 percent of lost wages up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each October, replacement services, and attendant care under MCL 500.3107 regardless of fault, with written notice due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)); the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan pays if no household policy applies (MCL 500.3172). Your claim against the driver and the carrier for pain and suffering and losses beyond no-fault requires a threshold injury under MCL 500.3135 and must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The difference from a car case is the money available to pay it: the carrier’s $750,000-plus policy, and often an excess policy above it, against the $50,000 minimum a private driver may carry. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

**Other defendants.** The shipper or broker that loaded the trailer or hired an unsafe carrier; the maintenance contractor; the manufacturer of a failed component ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability)); the trailer’s owner, often a different company from the tractor’s; and, for a government truck (City of Dearborn, Wayne County, MDOT, SMART), the agency under the Governmental Tort Liability Act’s motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405).

**If you were the truck driver.** A Dearborn driver injured on the job claims workers’ compensation from the employer (MCL 418.131) and can still sue a third party whose negligence caused the crash, the other driver, the shipper that loaded the trailer, the yard owner. ## The evidence, and how fast it disappears

Carriers must keep hours-of-service records for six months (49 CFR 395.8) and driver inspection reports for three months (49 CFR 396.11); after that they may lawfully be destroyed. Plant-gate, MDOT freeway, and business camera footage is overwritten in days to weeks. The truck’s engine control module and event data recorder can be cleared when the truck is repaired and returned to service. The first act in a Dearborn truck case is a **preservation letter** to the carrier, its insurer, the trailer owner, and the shipper, demanding that they keep the ELD data, the ECM and EDR data, the dash camera footage, the driver’s qualification file and post-crash test results, the dispatch and bill-of-lading records, the maintenance file, and the truck itself for inspection. A carrier that destroys evidence after receiving the letter faces spoliation sanctions. We also request the Michigan State Police Commercial Vehicle Enforcement Division’s post-crash inspection and the Dearborn Police report, and the plant’s gate footage before it cycles. **[CONFIRM: firm’s preservation-letter practice]**

## What to do after a Dearborn truck crash

1. **Call 911 and get medical care the same day.** Corewell Health Dearborn Hospital on Oakwood Boulevard is where many of the city’s seriously injured are treated. **[CONFIRM: hospital list]** Truck-crash injuries to the neck, back, and brain often present days later.
2. **Photograph the truck**: the cab, the trailer, the USDOT number and carrier name on the door, the plates of both tractor and trailer, the load, and the skid marks.
3. **Get the police report.** Dearborn Police or the State Police respond to injury crashes; a Dearborn Police UD-10 is purchased through CLEMIS, and a State Police UD-10 for $15 at michigan.gov/tcps. For a fatal or serious crash, the MSP Commercial Vehicle Enforcement Division may prepare a separate post-crash inspection report.
4. **Do not speak to the carrier’s insurer or its investigator.** Decline the recorded statement.
5. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** within the first week.
6. **Call a lawyer within days**, so the preservation letter goes out before the records expire.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many truck accidents happen in Dearborn?** Countywide, 3,916 heavy trucks and buses were involved in Wayne County crashes in 2024, according to the Michigan State Police, up from 3,558 in 2023.

**Who pays my medical bills after a truck crash in Dearborn?** Your own no-fault insurer, regardless of fault, after you file an Application for No-Fault Benefits; the Assigned Claims Plan if no household policy applies. The carrier’s insurer pays for pain and suffering and losses beyond no-fault once fault and a threshold injury are established.

**Why does the lawyer need to act within days?** Because the records that prove the case expire: six months for hours-of-service logs, three months for inspection reports, days or weeks for camera footage, and the truck’s electronic data can be cleared on repair. A preservation letter stops the clock.

**I was hit by a truck leaving the Rouge complex. Who is responsible?** The carrier that operated the truck, which may be Ford’s contractor, a steel hauler, or a third-party logistics company, and possibly the shipper that loaded it. Identifying the carrier from the USDOT number on the door is the first step; the plant’s gate records and footage are the second.

**Is a truck case worth more than a car case?** The injuries are usually worse and the insurance is usually larger: $750,000 minimum for a general freight carrier against $50,000 for a minimally insured car. The value still depends on the injury, fault, and the record.

**I drive a truck and was hurt on the job in Dearborn. What are my options?** Workers’ compensation from your employer for medical care and wage loss, and a separate suit against any third party whose negligence caused the crash.
