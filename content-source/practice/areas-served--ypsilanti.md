## Personal Injury Law Firm Serving Ypsilanti, MI

When people say “Ypsilanti,” they can mean either of two places. The **City of Ypsilanti** had 20,648 residents in the 2020 Census. It covers downtown, Depot Town, Eastern Michigan University, and the neighborhoods along the Huron River. **Ypsilanti Charter Township** had 55,670 residents. It wraps around the city on three sides and takes in Ford Lake, the Michigan Avenue and Ecorse Road corridors, Willow Run Airport, and the subdivisions south of I-94.

## Where Ypsilanti injuries happen

**Downtown’s one-way streets.** Three of the county’s ten highest-crash intersections in 2024 were in the City of Ypsilanti, all on the downtown grid:

- **Hamilton and Washtenaw**, at the top of the county list, is where Washtenaw Avenue comes in from the west and meets the one-way pairs that carry traffic through downtown.
- **Michigan Avenue at Huron Street**, in the heart of downtown.
- **Michigan Avenue at Prospect Street**, east of downtown. It had fewer crashes than most intersections on the list but the most injuries, 20 from 30 crashes.

The typical crashes are drivers turning the wrong way or into the wrong lane on a one-way street, red-light (MCL 257.612) and left-turn crashes (MCL 257.650)), lane changes into an occupied lane (MCL 257.642(1)(a)), and pedestrians struck in crosswalks near the transit center and Depot Town. See our [Ypsilanti car accident page](about:blank) and our [pedestrian accident page](about:blank).

**Washtenaw Avenue and the EMU campus.** Washtenaw Avenue (M-17) runs from Ann Arbor past the shopping centers in Pittsfield and Ypsilanti townships and into the city past Eastern Michigan’s campus. The campus streets and crossings carry students on foot, on bikes, and on scooters. **and into the city past Eastern Michigan’s campus.** See our [bicycle accident page](about:blank).

**Michigan Avenue and Ecorse Road in the township.** Michigan Avenue (US-12) runs east toward Belleville and Wayne County, past strip retail, motels, and gas stations. Ecorse Road runs past the Willow Run industrial area. Both are multi-lane roads with frequent driveways. Typical crashes are left turns across traffic, vehicles pulling out of driveways, and pedestrians crossing between signals.

**I-94.** The freeway runs through the township south of the city, with interchanges at Huron Street, Michigan Avenue, and Rawsonville Road. Crashes there are rear-end, lane-change, and truck crashes, many of them in construction zones and at the ramps. See our [Ypsilanti truck accident page](about:blank) and our [motorcycle accident page](about:blank).

**Rental housing.** Both the city and the township have many renters, from older houses near campus to apartment complexes in the township. The recurring claims are:

- falls on broken stairs and porches
- missing handrails
- unlit parking lots
- fires
- failed smoke and carbon monoxide detectors

A private landlord must keep the premises and common areas fit for their intended use and in reasonable repair (MCL 554.139). A lease clause waiving the landlord’s liability for its own negligence is void. EMU residence halls belong to the State. A claim over a defective university building goes to the Court of Claims under the public building exception, which covers only parts of a building open for use by members of the public and requires written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1406).

**Ford Lake and the Huron River.** Ford Lake, in the township, is used for boating and fishing, with a boat launch at Ford Lake Park. The owner of a boat is liable for injuries caused by its negligent operation by anyone the owner allowed to use it. Consent is presumed when the operator is a member of the owner’s family (MCL 324.80157). Injuries in a township park are limited by governmental immunity.

**Workplaces.** Typical workplaces include:

- the University
- the hospitals
- Willow Run Airport’s cargo operations and the warehouses near it
- construction on I-94 and the corridors
- retail and restaurants

Workers’ compensation is the exclusive remedy against the employer (MCL 418.131). A contractor, property owner, or equipment maker who caused the injury can still be sued. See our [workers’ compensation page](about:blank).

## What is different about an Ypsilanti claim

**Who owns the road decides who you sue and how fast.**

- **In the city:** the City of Ypsilanti owns its streets and sidewalks. A claim against it for a road or sidewalk defect requires written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1402a, 691.1404).
- **In the township:** the township owns no roads. Its roads belong to the Washtenaw County Road Commission, which also requires written notice within 120 days. The township does have the duty for sidewalks along those roads (MCL 691.1401, 691.1402a).
- **On state highways:** I-94, US-12, and M-17 belong to MDOT, and a road-defect claim requires written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).

**Eastern Michigan University is the State.** EMU is a constitutional state university. A claim against it is a claim against the State, whether for a crash with a University vehicle or a defective campus building. That claim is filed in the Michigan Court of Claims, decided by a judge without a jury, and requires written notice within six months of the injury (MCL 600.6431, 600.6419), or within 120 days for a defective building (MCL 691.1406). Missing the notice can end the claim. An injury on a private apartment property next to campus is an ordinary claim against the owner.

**The trauma hospitals are just outside the city.**

- **Trinity Health Ann Arbor** (formerly St. Joseph Mercy Ann Arbor) is a Level I trauma center. It has an Ypsilanti mailing address but sits in Superior Township, a few miles north of the city. It is a private hospital, so a malpractice claim against it goes to the circuit court.
- **University of Michigan Health** in Ann Arbor is a Level I trauma center for adults and children. A malpractice claim against it is not barred by governmental immunity (MCL 691.1407(4)) but proceeds against the State in the Court of Claims.
- **The VA Ann Arbor Healthcare System** treats many area veterans. A claim about care there follows the Federal Tort Claims Act.

The Michigan claims follow the 182-day Notice of Intent, the Affidavit of Merit, the two-year deadline, and the 2026 caps of $596,400 and $1,065,000 (MCL 600.2912b, 600.2912d, 600.5805(8), 600.1483). See our [medical malpractice page](about:blank).

**No-fault for people without a car.** Many Ypsilanti residents and EMU students walk, bike, or ride the bus. A pedestrian or cyclist hit by a car is covered first by a no-fault policy in their household, which for a student may be a parent’s policy. If there is none, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan pays (MCL 500.3114, 500.3115, 500.3172).

**Buses.** TheRide runs routes along Washtenaw Avenue and through the city and township from the Ypsilanti Transit Center downtown. A crash with a TheRide bus is a claim under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405). A bus passenger’s no-fault benefits follow the bus-passenger priority rules (MCL 500.3114(2)). See our [bus accident page](about:blank).

**Dog bites.** Michigan holds a dog’s owner liable when the dog bites someone who was lawfully on the property or in a public place and did not provoke it (MCL 287.351). The city and the township each have their own animal ordinances. A local rule broken by the owner, such as a leash requirement, can add to the claim. See our [dog bite page](about:blank).

**Which court.** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, are filed in one of two district courts, depending on where the injury happened:

- **City:** the 14A-2 District Court at 415 W. Michigan Avenue.
- **Township:** the 14B District Court at 7200 S. Huron River Drive.

Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Washtenaw County Trial Court (22nd Circuit) at 101 E. Huron Street in Ann Arbor. The Probate Court in the same building opens estates for wrongful death claims and approves minors’ settlements. Claims against EMU, MDOT, and the State go to the Court of Claims.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I know if I was hurt in the city or the township?** The police report names the jurisdiction, and the responding agency is a clue: the Ypsilanti Police Department in the city, the Sheriff’s Office in the township. A mailing address that says “Ypsilanti” does not settle it. The answer decides who owns the road and which district court hears a small claim.

**I tripped on a broken sidewalk in Ypsilanti Township. Who is responsible?** Usually the township, which has the duty for sidewalks along county roads, and written notice is due within 120 days. A defect in the road itself is the Road Commission’s.

**I was hit by a car while walking near EMU. Who pays my medical bills?** A no-fault policy in your household pays first, possibly a parent’s if you still live with them for insurance purposes. If there is none, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan pays. Your claim against the driver for pain and suffering is separate.

**I was hurt on Eastern Michigan’s campus. Can I sue?** Often, but in the Michigan Court of Claims, before a judge, with written notice within six months, or 120 days for a defective building. Governmental immunity limits which claims can be brought. That is much shorter than the ordinary three years.

**Where would my Ypsilanti case be filed?** - **Claims up to $25,000:** the 14A-2 District Court on W. Michigan Avenue for the city, or the 14B District Court on S. Huron River Drive for the township. - **Larger injury suits:** the Washtenaw County Trial Court in Ann Arbor. - **Claims against EMU or the State:** the Court of Claims.
