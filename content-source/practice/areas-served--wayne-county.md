## Wayne County Personal Injury Lawyers

Wayne County is Michigan’s most populous county, home to about 1.75 million people in 43 cities and townships, and it accounts for a disproportionate share of the state’s serious injuries. In 2024, according to the Michigan State Police, 50,355 traffic crashes were reported in the county, about one in six of the state’s 288,880; 16,458 people were injured in them, 926 seriously, and 177 were killed. Add the falls, dog bites, workplace injuries, and medical errors that occur in a county of this size, and Wayne County produces more personal injury claims than any other county in Michigan.

ATLAW is a personal injury firm based in Dearborn, in the middle of Wayne County. In 13 years the firm has handled more than 10,000 cases for injured people and recovered more than $100 million for them, most of them residents of Wayne County and the surrounding counties. This page explains what is particular about an injury claim in Wayne County: where the crashes happen, which courts hear the cases, which insurers and government agencies are usually on the other side, and the deadlines that apply. The practice-area pages linked throughout explain the law for each kind of case.

## Where Wayne County injuries happen

**The freeways.** I-94 (the Edsel Ford and Detroit Industrial), I-75 (the Fisher and Chrysler), I-96 (the Jeffries), I-275, M-10 (the Lodge), M-39 (the Southfield Freeway), and M-8 (the Davison) carry commuter and freight traffic through the county at volumes among the highest in Michigan. Freight to and from the Ambassador Bridge and the Gordie Howe International Bridge, and to the auto plants in Dearborn, Detroit, and the Downriver communities, puts heavy trucks on every one of them; 3,916 trucks and buses were involved in Wayne County crashes in 2024. Truck cases are governed by federal carrier rules and larger policies, and our [Wayne County truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/wayne-county) covers them.

**The arterials.** Telegraph Road (US-24), Michigan Avenue (US-12), Ford Road (M-153), Eight Mile (M-102), Gratiot (M-3), Woodward (M-1), Grand River, Van Dyke, Fort Street, and Greenfield are where the intersection, left-turn, and pedestrian crashes concentrate. On the Detroit side of Eight Mile and Telegraph, the city’s wide one-way and multi-lane arterials present significant pedestrian-safety concerns. Detroit has one of the highest pedestrian fatality rates among large U.S. cities, according to a study of 2019–2023 pedestrian fatalities.

**The neighborhoods.** Residential falls, dog bites, apartment and rental-housing injuries, and sidewalk injuries. Detroit, Dearborn, and most Wayne County cities place sidewalk maintenance on the adjacent property owner by ordinance, which can make the owner a defendant alongside, or instead of, the city; see our [sidewalk injury page](/personal-injury/sidewalk-injuries).

**The workplaces.** The county’s auto plants, the airport in Romulus, the port and rail yards, the hospitals, and the construction sites. Workers’ compensation is the exclusive remedy against the employer (MCL 418.131), but a worker hurt by a third party, a delivery driver struck by another vehicle or a contractor injured by another contractor’s negligence, has both a compensation claim and a lawsuit; see our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation/wayne-county).

## What is different about a Wayne County claim

**The uninsured-driver rate.** Michigan’s 2019 reform was passed in large part because of Detroit, where state regulators estimated that about 60 percent of drivers were uninsured before the reform. The rate has fallen, but Wayne County still has a far higher share of uninsured and minimally insured drivers than Oakland or Macomb. The practical consequences: more claims go to the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan for no-fault benefits (capped at $250,000 under MCL 500.3172); more at-fault drivers carry the $50,000 per person minimum under MCL 500.3009(1) or nothing; and a client’s own uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage is more often the only real source of recovery for pain and suffering. We check every policy in the household before we tell a Wayne County client what the claim is worth. See our [uninsured and underinsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Government defendants.** Wayne County has more government-owned vehicles and government-maintained roads than any county in the state: DDOT buses (a City of Detroit department), SMART buses, Wayne County Sheriff and 43 municipal police departments, the Wayne County Department of Public Services on county roads, the City of Detroit on city streets, and MDOT on the state trunklines. Claims against them are governed by the Governmental Tort Liability Act, MCL 691.1401 and following: the motor vehicle exception in MCL 691.1405 allows qualifying claims arising from the negligent operation of a government-owned motor vehicle; the highway exception in MCL 691.1402 addresses certain road defects, with written notice generally required within 120 days under MCL 691.1404; and MCL 691.1402a establishes special rules for municipal sidewalks, including the two-inch presumption. Separate notice requirements may apply to transit authorities. For example, MCL 124.419 requires written notice of certain injury or property-damage claims against SMART within 60 days. Identifying the right agency and serving the right notice early is often the whole case.

**Cross-border and out-of-state parties.** The Ambassador Bridge and the Detroit-Windsor Tunnel bring Ontario drivers and passengers into Wayne County every day, and I-75 and I-94 bring Ohio and Illinois traffic. A non-resident injured in a vehicle not registered or insured in Michigan generally has no Michigan PIP claim (MCL 500.3113(c)), and a Michigan resident hit by an Ontario driver may be dealing with an insurer under a different system. These cases turn on which policy applies and where suit can be brought.

**Venue and the courts.** Under MCL 600.1629, venue for a personal injury lawsuit is determined by a statutory order of priority, generally beginning with the county where the original injury occurred and where a defendant resides, conducts business, or maintains its registered office. Other venue rules apply if those requirements are not met. For most Wayne County residents that means the Third Judicial Circuit Court in Detroit.

## The hospitals

Where a client is treated shapes the medical record and, in a serious case, the care that no-fault must pay for. Wayne County has the region’s Level I trauma centers: Detroit Receiving Hospital and Henry Ford Hospital in Detroit, and Children’s Hospital of Michigan for pediatric trauma. Corewell Health Dearborn Hospital (formerly Beaumont Dearborn), Trinity Health Livonia, Henry Ford Wyandotte, Garden City Hospital, DMC Sinai-Grace, and Henry Ford St. John Hospital (formerly Ascension St. John Hospital) are the hospitals most of our clients are taken to. We obtain the complete record from each, including the EMS run sheet, which often contains the first description of how the injury happened.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many car accidents happen in Wayne County?** 50,355 in 2024, according to the Michigan State Police, with 16,458 people injured and 177 killed. That is about one in six Michigan crashes and nearly one in four Michigan crash injuries.

**Where would my Wayne County injury lawsuit be filed?** Most are filed in the Third Judicial Circuit Court at the Coleman A. Young Municipal Center in Detroit, which hears civil cases over $25,000 from all 43 Wayne County communities. Smaller claims, including mini tort claims, go to the district court for the city where the defendant lives or the crash occurred.

**The driver who hit me in Detroit had no insurance. What now?** Your own no-fault insurer pays your medical bills and wage loss regardless. If no one in your household has a policy, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan assigns one, with medical benefits capped at $250,000. Your pain-and-suffering claim depends on whether you carry uninsured motorist coverage; check your declarations page now.

**I was hit by a DDOT or SMART bus. Is that different?** Yes. Both are government entities under the Governmental Tort Liability Act. SMART also has a separate statutory 60-day written notice requirement for certain tort claims under MCL 124.419. Call quickly.

**I fell on a Detroit sidewalk. Who is responsible?** Possibly the city, subject to 120-day written notice and the two-inch rule under MCL 691.1402a, and possibly the adjacent property owner under the city ordinance that places sidewalk maintenance on owners. Often both are named.

**Do you handle cases in Downriver and western Wayne County, or only Dearborn and Detroit?** All of Wayne County.

**How much does it cost to hire you?** Nothing up front, and no fee unless we recover money for you. Michigan caps contingency fees in personal injury cases at one-third of the recovery (MCR 8.121).

**How long do I have?** Three years for most injury suits, one year to notify your no-fault insurer in writing, and as little as 120 days to give notice of a claim against the county, a city, or the state. See the table above.
