## Macomb Township, MI Personal Injury Lawyer

If you were hurt in Macomb Township because someone else was careless, you have a claim under Michigan law, and its deadlines start the day you are hurt:

- **After a crash,** your no-fault insurer must have written notice within one year.
- **A sewer backup** that injured you or damaged your home needs written notice to the agency that owns the sewer within 45 days of when you discovered it.
- A road defect needs written notice within 120 days to Macomb County, which owns the township’s roads. A defect on Hall Road needs written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days.
- **A sidewalk or path defect, or an injury in a township or school building,** needs written notice to that agency within 120 days.
- **A claim against a bar or restaurant** that served a drunk driver needs written notice within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, and suit within two years.
- **A medical malpractice suit,** including many claims against nursing homes, requires 182 days’ notice first and must be filed within two years.
- **Most other claims,** including falls in stores and condominiums, construction-site injuries, dog bites, and crashes, allow three years.

A personal injury lawyer’s first job is to find out which of those deadlines apply to you. The second is to make sure none runs out before your claim is paid.

## Who the defendant is in Macomb Township

Macomb Township is a charter township. It has its own fire department, buildings, parks, and paths, but it owns none of its public roads, and its police are the Macomb County Sheriff’s deputies. Much of its housing is subdivision and site-condominium housing, much of it built in the last thirty years, and new construction continues. Its retail runs along Hall Road. Each of those changes who you claim against.

- **The roads belong to the county and the State.** MDOT: Hall Road (M-59), with written notice of a road defect filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). Macomb County: (the Department of Roads): every other public road, including Romeo Plank, Garfield, Hayes, Card, North Avenue, the mile roads, and the public subdivision streets, with written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404). Private roads: some subdivision and condominium roads are private and maintained by the association. A defect there is a premises claim against the association.
- A notice sent to the township about a road is sent to the wrong defendant.
- **The police are the county’s.** A crash with a Sheriff’s patrol car is a claim against Macomb County under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405). An injury caused by a deputy’s conduct outside a vehicle raises different immunity questions.
- **Sidewalks and safety paths.** The township has built sidewalks and paths along many of its roads. The municipal corporation in which a public sidewalk is installed must keep it in reasonable repair (MCL 691.1402a). A fall on a broken public sidewalk or path is a claim with three limits: the agency must have known, or should have known, of the defect at least 30 days before the injury; a defect of less than two inches is presumed not to be actionable; and written notice is due within 120 days.
- A township is a municipal corporation under the sidewalk statute, so a public sidewalk along a road in Macomb Township is the township’s to maintain (MCL 691.1401(e), 691.1402a(1)). An ordinance that puts repair on the adjacent owner does not by itself make the owner liable to a pedestrian (Levendoski v Geisenhaver, Mich 1965). A private path in a condominium or subdivision is a premises claim against the association. See our [sidewalk injury page](/personal-injury/sidewalk-injuries).
- **Township buildings and parks.** An injury caused by a dangerous condition in a township building, such as the township hall, a community center, or a fire station, falls under the public building exception. It requires that the township knew, or should have known, of the defect and failed to fix it within a reasonable time, and written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1406). An injury elsewhere in a township park or on a trail generally falls outside every exception to governmental immunity.
- **Schools.** The same public building exception covers the buildings of the school districts that serve the township, including Chippewa Valley, Utica, L’Anse Creuse, and New Haven. A school bus crash falls under the motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405). See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).
- **Fire and EMS.** A crash with a Macomb Township Fire Department vehicle is a claim against the township under the motor vehicle exception. EMS treatment claims are limited to gross negligence or willful misconduct (MCL 333.20965).
- **Sewer backups and flooded basements.** Heavy storms have flooded basements in parts of Macomb County. When a sewer system a governmental agency owns or operates backs up because of a defect the agency knew or should have known of and failed to fix in a reasonable time, the agency can be liable for the property damage and any physical injury the backup caused (MCL 691.1416–691.1417). The claimant must give written notice within 45 days after discovering the damage (MCL 691.1419). In the township, the sanitary sewers and the county drains may belong to different agencies, so the first question is which one failed.
- **Stores, restaurants, and parking lots.** A fall in a store, a restaurant, or a parking lot on Hall Road is a premises claim against the owner, the tenant, or both, depending on who controlled the area. Snow and ice are often handled by a contractor, which can be a separate defendant. Since *Kandil-Elsayed v F & E Oil* (2023), an “open and obvious” hazard such as visible ice goes to comparative fault rather than ending the claim. The deadline is three years. See our [slip and fall](/personal-injury/slip-and-fall) and [premises liability](/personal-injury/premises-liability) pages.
- **Condominium associations and landlords.** Many township homes are site condominiums or condominium units, and a growing number are apartments. In a condominium, the association usually controls the common elements, and a fall on a common walkway, stair, or private road is a claim against the association and its management company. A landlord must keep the premises and common areas fit for their intended use and in reasonable repair (MCL 554.139). See our [tenant injury page](/personal-injury/tenant-injuries).
- **The bar that served the drunk driver.** A bar, restaurant, or store that served a visibly intoxicated person, or a minor, is liable for the injuries that person causes (MCL 436.1801). The claim needs written notice within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, the drunk driver named and kept in the suit, and suit within two years. See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).
- **Dog owners.** An owner is liable for a bite when the person bitten was lawfully present and did not provoke the dog (MCL 287.351). In a subdivision, the claim is usually paid by the owner’s homeowner’s or renter’s policy. See our [dog bite page](/personal-injury/dog-bites).
- **Nursing homes and assisted living.** A claim against a nursing home may be ordinary negligence, with a three-year deadline, or medical malpractice, with two years and a 182-day notice, depending on whether the conduct involved professional judgment. See our [nursing home abuse page](/personal-injury/nursing-home-abuse).

## Construction sites, new homes, and the people who build them

Macomb Township has been under construction for a generation: new subdivisions, schools, retail, road widenings, and the water and sewer work that follows them. Construction produces three kinds of injury claims.

**Construction workers.** A worker hurt on the job is covered by workers’ compensation, and the employer usually cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). But a construction site has many companies on it, and a worker can often sue a company that is not their employer:

- the general contractor, which has a duty to take reasonable steps to guard against readily observable and avoidable dangers in common work areas that create a high degree of risk to a significant number of workers
- another subcontractor whose crew created the hazard
- the manufacturer of a defective ladder, scaffold, lift, or tool ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability))
- a driver who struck the worker in a work zone

Michigan’s occupational safety rules (MIOSHA) set standards for fall protection, trenching, scaffolds, and equipment. A violation can be evidence in a third-party case. The workers’ compensation insurer has a lien on any third-party recovery (MCL 418.827). See our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).

**Residents and children near construction sites.** An open foundation, a trench, a stockpile, or an unfenced pond on a construction site can draw children from the houses already built nearby. Michigan follows the rule that a landowner can be liable to a trespassing child for an artificial condition the owner knows children are likely to reach, when the child is too young to appreciate the risk and the owner could have eliminated the danger at slight cost. Dirt, mud, or debris tracked onto a road from a site, or a construction vehicle backing out of a site, can also cause crashes and falls. See our [Macomb Township truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents).

**New homes.** A defect in a new home, such as a stair without a proper railing, a deck that fails, or wiring that causes a fire, can injure the owner, a family member, or a guest. The builder and the subcontractor that did the work can be responsible. These claims raise their own questions: the builder’s contract and warranty, whether the claim is for injury or for the defect itself, and the statute of repose for improvements to real property (MCL 600.5839).

## When the other driver has no insurance, or not enough

Some drivers carry only the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)), and some carry nothing.

- **Against an uninsured driver,** the claim for pain and suffering depends on your own **uninsured motorist coverage**. Without it, the claim is worth what the driver can personally pay. A hit-and-run is usually a UM claim, with short notice terms in the policy.
- **Against an underinsured driver,** your own **underinsured motorist coverage** pays the difference up to your UIM limit, if you follow the steps in order:
 1. The at-fault driver’s insurer offers its limit.
 2. You notify your own insurer and ask for its written consent to settle.
 3. Only then do you accept the limit and sign the release.
 4. The UIM claim then proceeds.

Accepting without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under most policies.

If you have no auto insurance of your own and no household policy, your no-fault benefits may come from the Assigned Claims Plan, with its $250,000 medical cap. Two rules cut the other way:

- An owner who was required to insure a car and did not is generally barred from PIP benefits (MCL 500.3113(b)).
- A driver who was operating their own uninsured vehicle cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, whoever was at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(c)).

See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

## What a Macomb Township claim is worth, and what reduces it

A claim includes two kinds of damages:

- **Economic damages:** medical expenses, lost wages, lost earning capacity, and replacement services.
- **Noneconomic damages:** pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, and disfigurement.

There is no cap except in medical malpractice. In an auto case, noneconomic damages require a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment does not have to be permanent (*McCormick v Carrier*, 2010). Vehicle damage up to $3,000 can be recovered from the at-fault driver through the mini tort claim, without a threshold (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)). A premises, construction, dog bite, or dram shop case has no threshold.

Your share of fault reduces the recovery, and above 50 percent it bars noneconomic damages (MCL 600.2959; in auto cases, MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). At exactly 50 percent, you recover half. Economic damages are reduced by your share but not barred. Where more than one party caused the injury, each defendant generally pays only its own share of fault (MCL 600.2956, 600.6304). That is why the store, the snow-removal contractor, the association, the general contractor, and the subcontractor all have to be identified and named. Michigan does not award punitive damages in ordinary injury cases. Exemplary damages for humiliation or outrage are available only in narrow circumstances.

You cannot sue the at-fault party’s liability insurer directly in Michigan. The claim is against the driver, owner, or business, and its insurer defends and pays.

The practical limit is often insurance:

- **The at-fault driver’s limit** defaults to $250,000/$500,000 unless the driver chose lower limits, down to the $50,000/$100,000 minimum (MCL 500.3009(1)).
- **Your UM and UIM coverage** fill the gap.
- **In a premises, construction, workplace, or nursing home case,** the commercial policy of the store, association, landlord, builder, contractor, or operator pays, and those policies are often larger than an auto policy.
- **In a dog bite or private-home fall,** the homeowner’s or renter’s policy pays.
- **In a dram shop case,** the bar’s liquor liability policy adds to it.

For a worker with a third-party claim, the workers’ compensation insurer has a lien on the recovery (MCL 418.827). Medicare, Medicaid, and health plan liens are negotiated the same way.

## What it costs

Michigan caps contingency fees in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery (MCR 8.121). Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted from the recovery first; the one-third fee is calculated on the remaining amount. You pay nothing up front and nothing if there is no recovery.

The agreement is in writing. Read how costs are handled if the case is lost, because that is where firms differ: filing fees, records, experts, and depositions. A promise that “you pay nothing unless we win” should say whether that includes costs, and whether “win” means the same thing as “settle.” A success rate quoted without saying which cases it counts tells you little, and neither does a recovery total that does not say when or what the cases were.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in Macomb Township?** Three years for most claims. Several deadlines are much shorter: - 45 days to notify the sewer’s owner of a sewer backup - one year to notify your no-fault insurer after a crash - 120 days to notify Macomb County of a road defect, or the responsible agency of a sidewalk, path, or building defect - 120 days for a road-defect notice on Hall Road - 120 days for a dram shop notice after you hire a lawyer, and two years to sue the bar - a 182-day notice and a two-year limit for malpractice

**Is “Macomb, MI” the same as Macomb Township or Macomb County?** “Macomb, MI” is the mailing address for most of Macomb Township. Macomb County is the larger county that includes the township along with Sterling Heights, Warren, Clinton Township, St. Clair Shores, and many other communities. For an injury claim, the difference matters: the township owns no roads, Macomb County owns the township’s roads and provides its police, and both the 41A District Court and the Macomb County Circuit Court hear the township’s cases.

**I fell on a broken sidewalk or path in my subdivision. Who is responsible?** It depends on who owns it. A public sidewalk or path along a road is the township’s responsibility, with written notice within 120 days and the two-inch rule. A private sidewalk or path in a condominium or association is a premises claim against the association. Photograph the defect with a ruler before it is repaired.

**I was hurt on a construction site where I work. Can I sue anyone?** Usually not your own employer, because workers’ compensation is your remedy against them. But you may be able to sue the general contractor, another subcontractor whose crew created the hazard, or the manufacturer of defective equipment. Those claims are separate from your comp claim and can include pain and suffering.

**My child was hurt on a construction site near our house. Is the builder responsible?** Possibly. A builder or landowner can be liable to a child for a dangerous artificial condition the owner knew children were likely to reach, when the child was too young to understand the risk and the danger could have been fixed at slight cost. Photograph the site and the lack of fencing or barriers.

**I fell on ice in a parking lot on Hall Road. Who is responsible?** The owner or tenant that controlled the lot, and often the snow-removal contractor. Photograph the ice and the lot, report the fall, and ask for the incident report.

**My basement flooded with sewage after a storm. Do I have a claim?** Possibly, against the agency that owns the sewer that backed up, if it failed because of a defect the agency knew or should have known of and failed to fix. The written notice is due within 45 days after you discovered the damage.

**Can I sue the other driver’s insurance company?** No. In Michigan, the claim is against the driver, and the driver’s insurer defends and pays. Your own no-fault insurer is a separate claim, for your medical bills and lost wages.

**What does a Macomb Township personal injury attorney charge?** Michigan caps the fee in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery, with no fee if there is no recovery. Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted only from a recovery.

**Where would my Macomb Township case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000 go to the 41A District Court. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court in Mount Clemens. Claims against MDOT go to the Court of Claims.

**If I was partly at fault, do I still have a claim?** Yes, unless you were more than 50 percent at fault, which bars pain-and-suffering damages. Below that, your recovery is reduced by your share, and at exactly 50 percent you recover half. No-fault benefits are paid regardless of fault.

**Do I need a lawyer whose office is in Macomb Township?** No. What matters is that the lawyer knows the courts your case will be in, the agencies, insurers, and property owners involved, and will meet you where you are.
