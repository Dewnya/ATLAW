## Farmington Hills Personal Injury Lawyer

If you were hurt in Farmington Hills because someone else was careless, you have a claim under Michigan law, and its deadlines start the day you are hurt. After a crash, your no-fault insurer must have written notice within one year. A road or sidewalk defect claim against the City of Farmington Hills or the Road Commission for Oakland County needs written notice within 120 days; a defect on I-696, I-96, I-275, M-5, or Grand River needs written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days. A claim against a bar that served a drunk driver must be filed within two years. A medical malpractice suit, including many claims against nursing homes, requires 182 days’ notice first and must be filed within two years. Most other claims allow three years. A personal injury lawyer’s first job is to find out which of those apply to you; the second is to make sure none runs out before your claim is paid.

## What a Farmington Hills personal injury lawyer does

**The first week.** We calendar every deadline and send preservation letters:

- to the at-fault driver’s insurer;
- to a trucking company for its driver logs and electronic data, after crashes at the I-696/I-96/I-275/M-5 interchange;
- to the Road Commission for Oakland County or MDOT for signal timing records on Orchard Lake, Twelve Mile, and Grand River;
- to SMART or the City for vehicle records;
- to a store, an office-campus property manager on Twelve Mile or Halsted, or an apartment or condominium association on Grand River or Orchard Lake, for footage and incident reports, which many overwrite within days;
- to a nursing home or assisted living community for the resident’s chart, incident reports, and staffing records;
- to a bar or restaurant for its receipts if a drunk driver was involved.

We obtain the police report (Farmington Hills Police crash reports are available through the county’s CLEMIS system; freeway crashes may be State Police reports), the Farmington Hills Fire Department EMS run sheet, and the witnesses’ names.

**The first month.** We identify every policy that may pay: your own auto policy for no-fault benefits, household policies, the at-fault party’s liability coverage, your uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage, a property owner’s commercial policy, umbrella policies, and the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan where no auto policy exists (MCL 500.3172). For a workplace injury, we identify whether a third party, such as a contractor, a vendor’s driver, or an equipment manufacturer, caused it, because the employer itself usually cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). We file your Application for No-Fault Benefits inside the one-year window (MCL 500.3145(1)) and handle the insurer’s requests for statements, records, and examinations (MCL 500.3151). You stop talking to adjusters; we do that.

**The treatment period.** The claim’s value is set mostly by the medical record, so we make sure it is complete and the bills are paid while you treat. We collect records from Corewell Health Farmington Hills Hospital on Grand River, Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak, Henry Ford Providence Novi Hospital, Trinity Health Livonia Hospital, and your own physicians and therapists, and document lost wages with your employer. For the engineers and professionals on the Twelve Mile corridor, that includes salary above the no-fault wage cap, bonuses, and benefits, which are recovered from the at-fault party rather than from no-fault (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)).

**The demand, the suit, and the resolution.** When your condition has stabilized, we send a demand with the complete record and a valuation. The decision to accept any offer is yours (MRPC 1.2). If the insurer will not pay fair value, suit is filed in the right court:

- **47th District Court** at 31605 W. Eleven Mile Road, which serves Farmington Hills and Farmington, for claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims.
- **Sixth Judicial Circuit Court** (Oakland County Circuit Court) at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac for injury suits over $25,000. Most injury cases there go through case evaluation (MCR 2.403) and facilitation before trial.
- **Third Judicial Circuit Court** in Detroit where the defendant lives or does business in Wayne County, or the injury happened on Eight Mile at the Livonia line (MCL 600.1629).

At settlement we resolve the liens, including health plans, Medicare, Medicaid, and workers’ compensation, before you are paid. Minors’ settlements and wrongful death distributions are approved by the Oakland County Probate Court, on the same Telegraph Road campus, or by the trial court (MCR 2.420; MCL 600.2922(6)). Compensation for physical injury is generally not taxable income (26 USC 104(a)(2)).

## What a Farmington Hills claim is worth, and what reduces it

A claim includes economic damages (medical expenses, lost wages, lost earning capacity, replacement services) and noneconomic damages (pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, disfigurement). There is no cap except in medical malpractice. In an auto case, noneconomic damages require a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). Your share of fault reduces the recovery, and above 50 percent it bars noneconomic damages (MCL 600.2959; in auto cases MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). At exactly 50 percent you recover half. Where more than one party caused the injury, each defendant generally pays only its own share of fault (MCL 600.2956, 600.6304), so every responsible party has to be identified and named. Michigan does not award punitive damages in ordinary injury cases; exemplary damages for humiliation or outrage are available only in narrow circumstances.

In many Farmington Hills cases the practical limit is insurance: the at-fault driver’s policy, which defaults to $250,000 per person but may be as low as the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(1), (5)); your own underinsured motorist coverage; and, in a premises, workplace, or nursing home case, the commercial policy of the property owner, contractor, or facility. Where UIM is involved, settling with the at-fault driver without your own insurer’s consent can forfeit the UIM claim. For a worker with a third-party claim, the workers’ compensation insurer has a lien on the recovery (MCL 418.827), and how that lien is negotiated changes what you take home. A higher-earning client’s lost-wage claim is often the largest part of the economic damages, so the employer’s payroll records, bonus history, and benefits are gathered early.

## What it costs

Michigan caps contingency fees in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery (MCR 8.121). Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted from the recovery first; the one-third fee is calculated on the remaining amount. The agreement is in writing; read how costs (filing fees, records, experts, depositions) are handled if the case is lost, because that is where firms differ. A promise that “you pay nothing unless we win” should say whether that includes costs.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in Farmington Hills?** Three years for most claims. But you have one year to notify your no-fault insurer after a crash, 120 days to notify the City or the Road Commission of a defect, 120 days for a defect on a state road, two years to sue a bar, and a 182-day notice and two-year limit for malpractice.

**What does a Farmington Hills personal injury attorney charge?** Michigan caps the fee in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery, with no fee if there is no recovery. Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted only from a recovery.

**Where would my Farmington Hills case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000 in the 47th District Court on Eleven Mile; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court in Pontiac, or in some Eight Mile or Wayne County cases the Third Circuit in Detroit.

**I was hurt at work on the Twelve Mile corridor. Do I need a workers’ comp lawyer or an injury lawyer?** Possibly both claims. Workers’ compensation pays your benefits regardless of fault, and your employer usually cannot be sued. If a contractor, a vendor’s driver, a property manager, or a defective product caused the injury, you also have an injury claim against that party, and the comp insurer is repaid from it.

**If I was partly at fault, do I still have a claim?** Yes, unless you were more than 50 percent at fault, which bars pain-and-suffering damages. Below that, your recovery is reduced by your share; at exactly 50 percent you recover half. No-fault benefits are paid regardless of fault.

**My salary is above the no-fault wage cap. Is the rest lost?** No. No-fault pays 85 percent of wages up to the monthly cap for up to three years. Wage loss above the cap, or beyond three years, can be recovered from the at-fault party (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)).

**Do I need a lawyer whose office is in Farmington Hills?** No. What matters is that the lawyer knows the courts your case will be in, the road agencies, employers, facilities, and insurers involved, and will meet you where you are.

**How long will my case take?** A no-fault benefits claim can resolve in months. A disputed injury claim in the Oakland County Circuit Court often takes a year or two from filing.

**The insurer already offered me money. Should I take it?** Not before you know what the claim is worth and what the release gives up, and not before your UIM insurer has consented if UIM may apply.
