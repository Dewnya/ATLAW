## Royal Oak, MI Wrongful Death Lawyer

When someone dies in Royal Oak because another person or company was careless, Michigan’s Wrongful Death Act (MCL 600.2922) gives the family a claim. It does not work the way many families expect:

- The claim belongs to the estate, not to the spouse or children directly.
- It must be brought by a personal representative appointed by the probate court.
- A judge divides the recovery among family members.
- The deadline depends on what caused the death.

Deaths that lead to these claims in Royal Oak include:

- a crash on Woodward, I-696, I-75, or one of the mile roads
- a pedestrian or cyclist struck downtown or on a residential street
- a tractor-trailer or delivery truck crash
- a driver leaving a downtown bar
- a death on a construction site or at a workplace
- a hospital error
- neglect in a nursing home or senior residence

Some of these deadlines are as short as 120 days. This page explains how the process works for a Royal Oak family and what to do in the first weeks.

## How a Royal Oak wrongful death claim works

**The estate.** Only the personal representative can bring the claim (MCL 600.2922(2)).

- **The person lived in Royal Oak:** the estate is opened in the Oakland County Probate Court on the county campus at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac.
- The person lived elsewhere in Michigan but died in Royal Oak: the estate is opened in the county where they lived. That is common here, because Woodward, the freeways, and downtown draw people from across the region. The suit can still be filed in Oakland County.

If there is a will naming a personal representative, that person petitions for appointment. If not, a family member, usually the spouse or an adult child, petitions under Michigan’s priority rules (MCL 700.3203). The court issues letters of authority, and the personal representative then has standing to hire a lawyer for the estate and file. We file the petition as the first legal step.

**When one crash kills more than one person.** Each person who died has a separate estate, a separate personal representative, and a separate claim. Every injured survivor of the same crash also has a claim. All of them may be paid from the same at-fault driver’s policy limit, which a per-accident limit can cap for everyone combined. When the claims together exceed the limit, how and when each estate settles matters. A lawyer who represents more than one of the estates needs each one’s informed consent to that potential conflict (MRPC 1.7), and the order of the underinsured motorist steps described below applies to each one.

**Notice to the family.** Within 30 days after filing suit, the personal representative must notify in writing every person who may share in the recovery (MCL 600.2922(2)–(4)). That list includes:

- the spouse
- children and descendants
- parents and grandparents
- brothers and sisters
- the children of the deceased’s spouse
- anyone named in the will

The list is not limited to dependents. A parent, sibling, or adult child who was not financially dependent can still share for the loss of companionship. The Act does not rank them in a fixed order of priority. Instead, the court divides the recovery according to each person’s loss. A person named in the will may share in the recovery but does not bring the claim; only the personal representative does.

**The court.** The suit is the injury claim the person would have had, brought by the estate against whoever caused the death. Death claims are filed in circuit court, not in the 44th District Court on Eleven Mile, which hears civil cases only up to $25,000.

- **Most deaths in Royal Oak:** the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court (Oakland County Circuit Court) at 1200 N. Telegraph Road, on the same campus as the Probate Court.
- **An out-of-state trucking company or driver:** the case may be filed in or removed to federal court in Detroit.
- **Claims against MDOT:** the Court of Claims.



**The damages** (MCL 600.2922(6)) include:

- medical and hospital expenses from the injury to the death
- funeral and burial expenses
- the person’s **conscious pain and suffering** between the injury and death
- **loss of financial support** over a working life, which is often a major part of the claim when the person who died was a household’s main earner
- **loss of society and companionship** for each family member

Michigan places no cap on these damages except in medical malpractice cases. Michigan does not award punitive damages in wrongful death cases. Damage to the person’s car or other property is a separate claim of the estate, not part of the wrongful death damages. No-fault survivor’s loss benefits, described below, are also a separate insurance claim.

**Comparative fault.** If the person who died was partly at fault, the damages are reduced by that share. If their fault was greater than the combined fault of everyone else, the estate cannot recover noneconomic damages, but it can still recover reduced economic damages (MCL 600.2959). In an auto case, the no-fault act bars noneconomic damages at more than 50 percent fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)).

**The distribution hearing.** After a settlement or verdict, the personal representative proposes a division among the eligible family members, with notice to each.

- If they agree, the court approves it.
- If not, the court decides based on each person’s relationship and loss (MCL 600.2922(6)).

Fees and costs are approved at the same hearing. Because a judge divides the money, no family member should sign anything with an insurer. A release signed by one relative does not settle the estate’s claim.

## The causes of Royal Oak wrongful death claims

**Crashes.** Fatal crashes here happen on Woodward, I-696, I-75, and the mile roads. They include pedestrians and cyclists struck downtown, on Main Street and Washington Avenue, and on residential streets, and crashes during the Woodward Dream Cruise.

A wrongful death claim after a crash does not step outside no-fault. The claim against the at-fault driver still requires a threshold injury, and a death meets it (MCL 500.3135(1)). No-fault benefits are paid alongside it.

**Two policies, in the right order.** Downtown and Woodward traffic comes from across the region, and some drivers carry only the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)), or no insurance at all. A death claim is often worth more than the at-fault driver’s limit. The estate can recover the difference under the family’s underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage, but only if:

- the UIM insurer consents in writing before the estate accepts the at-fault driver’s limit, and
- the probate court approves the settlement.

Settling without consent can forfeit the UIM claim. Against an uninsured or hit-and-run driver, the claim depends on the family’s uninsured motorist coverage, which has short notice terms in the policy. We also check umbrella policies and the policy of any employer whose driver was working.

**No-fault survivor’s loss benefits** (MCL 500.3108) are paid regardless of fault, for up to three years after the death. They are paid by the deceased’s own insurer, or by the insurer that would have paid their PIP. They cover:

- the economic support the person provided, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- $20 a day in replacement services
- funeral expenses up to the policy limit, at least $1,750 and up to $5,000 (MCL 500.3107(1)(a))

Survivor’s loss stops at the cap. Support above it is part of the wrongful death claim. Written notice is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)), so do not wait for the lawsuit. See our [Royal Oak car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents/royal-oak) and, for pedestrian and cyclist deaths, our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents) and [bicycle accident page](/personal-injury/bicycle-accidents).

**Truck and motorcycle deaths.** A fatal crash with a tractor-trailer on I-75 or I-696, a delivery truck downtown, or a dump truck at a construction site brings three added elements:

- the federal trucking rules
- the carrier’s policy of $750,000 or more
- evidence that can be overwritten within days

See our [Royal Oak truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/royal-oak). A fatal motorcycle crash follows the rider priority rules. A rider killed in a crash in which no car or truck was involved has no no-fault survivor’s loss claim, because a motorcycle is not a “motor vehicle” under the act. A claim may still lie against a road agency for a defect, with the short notice periods below. See our [Royal Oak motorcycle accident page](/personal-injury/motorcycle-accidents/royal-oak).

**Drunk driving.** The State Police recorded 57 alcohol-involved crashes in Royal Oak in 2024. The claim runs against the driver. Under the dram shop act (MCL 436.1801), it can also run against the bar, restaurant, or store that served a visibly intoxicated person or a minor, including downtown on Main Street and Washington Avenue. The dram shop claim has its own rules:

- written notice to every defendant within 120 days after the family hires a lawyer
- the intoxicated driver must be named and kept in the suit
- the suit must be filed within **two years**, not three

The criminal case proceeds separately, and the family’s claim does not wait for it. See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**Deaths on the job.** Workplace deaths in Royal Oak happen on construction sites for new downtown buildings and road work, at the hospital, and in restaurants, stores, and service businesses. When a worker dies on the job, the employer owes workers’ compensation death benefits to dependents, up to 500 weeks plus burial (MCL 418.321, 418.345). The employer generally cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). There are two important exceptions:

- **A third party** whose negligence caused the death can be sued in a wrongful death action. That includes a general contractor or subcontractor on the site, a property owner, a trucking company, or the manufacturer of a machine with a missing guard or a defective safety device ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability)).
- **The employer itself** can be sued only under the narrow intentional tort exception. That requires actual knowledge that an injury was certain to occur and willful disregard of it (MCL 418.131(1)).

The MIOSHA investigation file is one of the first records requested. See our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).

**Hospital and medical negligence.** Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital on Thirteen Mile is Oakland County’s Level I adult trauma center. Claims can arise there or at the clinics and medical offices around it. They are wrongful death claims with the malpractice rules attached:

- a 182-day Notice of Intent (MCL 600.2912b)
- an Affidavit of Merit (MCL 600.2912d)
- a two-year period (MCL 600.5805(8), 600.5838a)
- the cap (MCL 600.1483)

When a person dies after a crash and also after a medical error, there can be two claims, against the driver and against the provider, with different deadlines. See our [Royal Oak medical malpractice page](/personal-injury/medical-malpractice/royal-oak).

**Nursing homes, senior residences, and premises.** Royal Oak has nursing homes, assisted living, senior apartments, and many older apartment buildings and rental houses.

- **A death from neglect in a nursing home** may be ordinary negligence or malpractice, and that changes the deadline. See our [nursing home abuse page](/personal-injury/nursing-home-abuse).
- **Deaths from fires, carbon monoxide, failed smoke detectors, and falls** follow premises law. For tenants, they also follow MCL 554.139. See our [tenant injury page](/personal-injury/tenant-injuries).

**Deaths involving a government agency.** Royal Oak is a city, and the road defendant can be any of three agencies:

- MDOT for Woodward, I-696, and I-75, with written notice of a road defect filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404; *Champine v Department of Transportation*, Mich 2022).
- The Road Commission for Oakland County for Ten, Twelve, and Fourteen Mile, with written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404)
- The City of Royal Oak for the other streets, including Eleven and Thirteen Mile, with the same 120-day notice.

The City also answers for its sidewalks, its buildings, and its downtown parking structures, with the same 120-day notice (MCL 691.1402a, 691.1406). A privately owned lot or structure is an ordinary premises claim. Vehicle crashes involving the government follow these rules:

- **A Royal Oak Police car, fire truck, or ambulance:** a claim against the City, under the motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405). A claim about EMS treatment itself is limited to gross negligence or willful misconduct (MCL 333.20965).
- SMART buses: these also fall under the motor vehicle exception. Royal Oak Schools’ buses are run by a private contractor, so a claim over one of them is an ordinary negligence claim.

An individual government employee is liable only for gross negligence (MCL 691.1407(2)). The Detroit Zoo, at the city’s southern edge, is operated by the Detroit Zoological Society on land the City of Detroit owns, so the defendant for a death there depends on what caused it.

When the injury and the death happen on the same day, the difference between “from the injury” and “from the death” does not matter. When a person lingers for weeks or months, the ordinary period may already be running before the estate exists. That is what the savings provision is for. It adds only a limited period, and courts apply it strictly. Open the estate and file inside the ordinary deadline.

## What the family should do in the first weeks

1. **Get the death certificate and request the Oakland County Medical Examiner’s report.** Also request the Royal Oak Fire Department EMS run sheet, the police report (the Royal Oak Police Department in the city, the State Police on the freeways), and the hospital record. The Medical Examiner’s office is at 1200 N. Telegraph Road, Building 28E, on the county campus in Pontiac, and Michigan law lets a family object to an autopsy on religious grounds (MCL 52.205a). If the family’s customs call for prompt burial, tell the Medical Examiner’s office at once, or call us.
2. **Keep everything:** the vehicle, the phone, the helmet, the bicycle, the work gear, and the medication bottles. Do not authorize salvage of a vehicle until a lawyer has seen it.
3. **Ask for video quickly.** Downtown businesses, parking operators, and apartment buildings often overwrite camera footage within days.
4. **Notify the no-fault insurer in writing** if a vehicle was involved. Survivor’s loss benefits begin with the claim, not with the lawsuit.
5. **Do not give a recorded statement** to any insurer, and do not sign any document, however it is described. An insurer offering to “cover the funeral” in exchange for a signature may be settling the claim for the price of a funeral.
6. **Open the estate.** The probate petition is the first legal step. The wrongful death suit cannot be filed until letters of authority issue.
7. **Call within days** if the death involved a road, a sidewalk, a parking structure, a government vehicle, a bar, a trucking company, or an employer. Those notice periods are short, and trucking records expire.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who can file a wrongful death lawsuit in Royal Oak?** Only the personal representative of the estate. For a Royal Oak resident, the Oakland County Probate Court appoints that person. A spouse, child, or parent can be appointed, but must be appointed before the claim can be filed.

**Who receives the money?** The spouse, children, descendants, parents, grandparents, siblings, the children of the deceased’s spouse, and anyone named in the will may share, whether or not they were financially dependent. The probate court divides the recovery according to each person’s loss, after notice to each of them.

**How long do we have?** - **Crash, fall, and premises claims:** generally three years from the injury that caused the death, with a limited extension for the estate. - **Medical malpractice:** two years, after a 182-day notice. - **Dram shop claims:** two years. - **No-fault survivor’s loss benefits:** one year to claim. - **Road, sidewalk, or building defects:** 120 days to notify the City or the Road Commission. - **MDOT:** 120 days for a road defect.

**Does the three years run from the date of death?** Not necessarily. For most claims, the period generally runs from the injury that caused the death, with a limited extension for the estate. When the person lived for some time after the injury, waiting until three years after the death can be too late.

**Two people in our family died in the same crash. Is that one claim or two?** Two. Each person who died has a separate estate and a separate claim, and each estate needs its own personal representative. The same person can serve for both. If the at-fault driver’s policy cannot cover every claim from the crash, the settlements and the underinsured motorist claims have to be planned together.

**The other driver’s insurer offered its policy limit. Should the estate accept it?** Not before the family’s underinsured motorist insurer consents in writing and the probate court approves the settlement. Accepting without consent can forfeit the UIM claim.

**My husband died in a crash. Are survivor’s loss benefits part of the lawsuit?** No. Survivor’s loss is paid by the no-fault insurer regardless of fault, up to a monthly cap, for up to three years. The wrongful death claim against the at-fault driver is separate. It covers the losses survivor’s loss does not, including support above the cap and the family’s loss of companionship.

**The driver had been drinking at a downtown bar. Can we bring a claim against the bar?** Possibly, if the bar served the driver while visibly intoxicated, or served a minor. The family must send written notice within 120 days after hiring a lawyer, the driver must stay in the suit, and the suit must be filed within two years.

**A bad road contributed to my son’s crash. Do we sue the City of Royal Oak?** It depends on the road: - **Woodward, I-696, or I-75:** MDOT, with written notice of a road defect filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days. - **Ten, Twelve, and Fourteen Mile:** the Road Commission for Oakland County, with written notice within 120 days. - **Other streets, including Eleven and Thirteen Mile:** the City, with written notice within 120 days.

**Can we get punitive damages?** Michigan does not award punitive damages in wrongful death cases. The family recovers the losses the Act lists, including loss of companionship, which has no cap outside medical malpractice.

**Is there a lawsuit if the driver was charged with a crime?** Yes. The criminal case and the family’s claim are separate. The family does not have to wait for the criminal case, and the civil deadlines keep running.
