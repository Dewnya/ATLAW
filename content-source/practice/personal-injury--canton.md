## Canton, MI Personal Injury Lawyer

If you were hurt in Canton because someone else was careless, you have a claim under Michigan law, and its deadlines start the day you are hurt. After a crash, your no-fault insurer must have written notice within one year. A road defect claim on a county road needs written notice to Wayne County within 120 days; a defect on Ford Road, Michigan Avenue, or I-275 needs notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days; and because Canton is a township that does not own its roads, a road notice sent to the township is sent to the wrong defendant. A sidewalk claim is the exception: it goes to the township, with written notice within 120 days. A claim against a bar that served a drunk driver must be filed within two years. A medical malpractice suit, including many claims against nursing homes, requires 182 days’ notice first and must be filed within two years. Most other claims allow three years. A personal injury lawyer’s first job is to find out which of those apply to you; the second is to make sure none runs out before your claim is paid.

## What a Canton personal injury lawyer does

**The first week.** We calendar every deadline and send preservation letters:

- to the at-fault driver’s insurer;
- to a trucking company for its driver logs and electronic data, which matters on I-275 and the Michigan Avenue distribution corridor;
- to MDOT or Wayne County for signal timing records at the Ford Road intersections and the county roads;
- to Canton Township for records of a Public Safety vehicle or a township building;
- to a store, restaurant, or shopping-center manager on Ford Road, an apartment or condominium association, or an employer on Michigan Avenue for footage and incident reports, which many overwrite within days;
- to a nursing home for the resident’s chart, incident reports, and staffing records;
- to a bar for its receipts if a drunk driver was involved.

We obtain the police report, the Canton Public Safety EMS run sheet, and the witnesses’ names.

**The first month.** We identify every policy that may pay: your own auto policy for no-fault benefits, household policies, the at-fault party’s liability coverage, your uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage, a property owner’s commercial policy, umbrella policies, a rideshare company’s policy, and the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan where no auto policy exists (MCL 500.3172). For a workplace injury, we identify whether a third party, such as a contractor, a vendor’s driver, or an equipment manufacturer, caused it, because the employer itself usually cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). We file your Application for No-Fault Benefits inside the one-year window (MCL 500.3145(1)) and handle the insurer’s requests for statements, records, and examinations (MCL 500.3151). You stop talking to adjusters; we do that.

**The treatment period.** The claim’s value is set mostly by the medical record, so we make sure it is complete and the bills are paid while you treat. Canton has no hospital of its own; we collect records from Trinity Health Ann Arbor, Trinity Health Livonia, University of Michigan Health, the urgent care and imaging centers along Ford Road, and your own physicians and therapists, and document lost wages with your employer, including overtime and shift premiums.

**The demand, the suit, and the resolution.** When your condition has stabilized, we send a demand with the complete record and a valuation. The decision to accept any offer is yours (MRPC 1.2). If the insurer will not pay fair value, suit is filed in the right court:

- **35th District Court** at 660 Plymouth Road in Plymouth, which serves Canton, Plymouth, and Northville, for claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims.
- **Third Judicial Circuit Court** at the Coleman A. Young Municipal Center in Detroit for injury suits over $25,000. Most injury cases there go through case evaluation (MCR 2.403) and facilitation before trial.
- **Washtenaw or Oakland County Circuit Court** where a defendant lives or does business there, or the injury happened at the county line (MCL 600.1629).

At settlement we resolve the liens, including health plans, Medicare, Medicaid, and workers’ compensation, before you are paid. Minors’ settlements and wrongful death distributions are approved by the Wayne County Probate Court or the trial court (MCR 2.420; MCL 600.2922(6)). Compensation for physical injury is generally not taxable income (26 USC 104(a)(2)).

## What a Canton claim is worth, and what reduces it

A claim includes economic damages (medical expenses, lost wages, lost earning capacity, replacement services) and noneconomic damages (pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, disfigurement). There is no cap except in medical malpractice. In an auto case, noneconomic damages require a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). Your share of fault reduces the recovery, and above 50 percent it bars noneconomic damages (MCL 600.2959; in auto cases MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). At exactly 50 percent you recover half. Michigan does not award punitive damages in ordinary injury cases; exemplary damages for humiliation or outrage are available only in narrow circumstances.

In many Canton cases the practical limit is insurance: the at-fault driver’s policy, which defaults to $250,000 per person but may be as low as the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(1), (5)); your own underinsured motorist coverage; and, in a premises, workplace, or nursing home case, the commercial policy of the property owner, contractor, or facility. Where UIM is involved, settling with the at-fault driver without your own insurer’s consent can forfeit the UIM claim. For a worker with a third-party claim, the workers’ compensation insurer has a lien on the recovery (MCL 418.827), and how that lien is negotiated changes what you take home.

## What it costs

Michigan caps contingency fees in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery (MCR 8.121). Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted from the recovery first; the one-third fee is calculated on the remaining amount. The agreement is in writing; read how costs (filing fees, records, experts, depositions) are handled if the case is lost, because that is where firms differ.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in Canton, Michigan?** Three years for most claims. But you have one year to notify your no-fault insurer after a crash, 120 days to notify Wayne County of a defect on a county road, 120 days for a defect on a state road, two years to sue a bar, and a 182-day notice and two-year limit for malpractice.

**My injury was caused by a road defect in Canton. Do I file against the township?** No. Canton is a township and does not own its roads. Ford Road, Michigan Avenue, and I-275 are MDOT’s; the county roads are Wayne County’s. A claim involving a township sidewalk, a township building, or a Canton Public Safety vehicle does go to the township.

**What does a Canton personal injury attorney charge?** Michigan caps the fee in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery, with no fee if there is no recovery. Ask how costs are handled.

**Where would my Canton case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000 in the 35th District Court on Plymouth Road in Plymouth; injury suits over $25,000 in the Third Judicial Circuit Court in Detroit, or in some cases in Washtenaw or Oakland County.

**If I was partly at fault, do I still have a claim?** Yes, unless you were more than 50 percent at fault, which bars pain-and-suffering damages. Below that, your recovery is reduced by your share; at exactly 50 percent you recover half. No-fault benefits are paid regardless of fault.

**I was hurt at a Michigan Avenue warehouse. Do I need a workers’ comp lawyer or an injury lawyer?** Possibly both claims. Workers’ compensation pays your benefits regardless of fault, and your employer usually cannot be sued. If a contractor, a vendor’s driver, or a defective machine caused the injury, you also have an injury claim against that party, and the comp insurer is repaid from it.

**How long will my case take?** A no-fault benefits claim can resolve in months. A disputed injury claim in the Third Circuit often takes a year or two from filing.

**The insurer already offered me money. Should I take it?** Not before you know what the claim is worth and what the release gives up, and not before your UIM insurer has consented if UIM may apply.
