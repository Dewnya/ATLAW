## Where Oakland County truck crashes happen

**I-75 and its interchanges.** The I-75 corridor from Eight Mile to M-59 was rebuilt between 2016 and 2023, and the interchanges at I-696, Big Beaver, Crooks, Square Lake, and M-59 in Auburn Hills carry heavy merging truck traffic. North of M-59, through Clarkston, Springfield, and Holly, I-75 is a two-direction freight route to Flint, Saginaw, and the north, and the truck crashes there are high-speed rear-end, lane-change, and jackknife crashes, often in winter conditions.

**I-696.** The Reuther Freeway across Southfield, Lathrup Village, Oak Park, Huntington Woods, Royal Oak, and Madison Heights, with the Telegraph, Lodge, Southfield, Greenfield, Woodward, and I-75 interchanges in eleven miles. The Restore the Reuther project has brought major reconstruction, detours, lane closures, and work-zone restrictions to I-696 between Lahser Road and I-75 since 2025. As of October 2026, westbound I-696 has reopened between I-75 and M-10, while eastbound I-696 remains closed through late 2026, with further project work scheduled for 2027. A truck driver’s moving violation in a work zone may carry enhanced penalties under MCL 257.601b, and evidence of the violation may be relevant to determining fault in a civil case.

**M-59, M-24, and Dixie Highway.** Hall Road and Highland Road across the center of the county, Lapeer Road north from Pontiac through Orion and Oxford, and Dixie Highway from Pontiac through Waterford, Clarkston, and Holly. These are the gravel-train and plant-freight routes, with trucks turning across traffic into pits, plants, and job sites. A fully loaded eleven-axle gravel train can weigh substantially more than a conventional five-axle tractor-trailer, making vehicle weight, speed, braking performance, and stopping distance important factors in a crash investigation. Heavy trucks turning or stopping at intersections along M-59 and M-24 also create potential collision risks.****

**I-96, I-275, and M-5.** Through Novi, Farmington Hills, and Wixom, where the distribution warehouses along Grand River and Wixom Road generate tractor-trailer traffic around the clock, and the I-96/I-275/M-5 interchange funnels it.

**The downtowns and the mile roads.** Delivery trucks, refuse trucks, and construction vehicles in Royal Oak, Ferndale, Birmingham, Troy, and Southfield, and on the Road Commission’s mile roads, where turning and backing crashes with pedestrians and cyclists occur.

## Who is responsible, and who pays

**The carrier, not just the driver.** The motor carrier is responsible for its driver’s compliance with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations and is liable for the driver’s negligence in the course of employment; Michigan adopts many Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations for intrastate commercial motor carriers under MCL 480.11a, subject to statutory modifications and exceptions. An Oakland County gravel hauler operating solely within Michigan may therefore be subject to many of the same driver-safety, qualification, inspection, and operating requirements as an interstate carrier.****

| Rule | 49 CFR | What it means for the case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hours of service | Part 395 | 11 hours driving in a 14-hour window after 10 off; 60/70-hour weekly limits; recorded on an electronic logging device (ELD). An over-hours driver is a fatigued driver, and the ELD proves it. |
| Driver qualification | Part 391 | CDL, medical certificate, background check the carrier must perform and keep. Negligent hiring and retention are claims against the carrier. |
| Post-crash drug and alcohol testing | Part 382 | Required after a fatal crash and after an injury or tow-away crash where the driver was cited; alcohol within 8 hours, drugs within 32. |
| Inspection and maintenance | Part 396 | Pre-trip inspections, defect reports, maintenance records. Brake and tire cases are maintenance cases, and gravel trains are brake-intensive vehicles. |
| Cargo securement | Part 393 | Securement standards; shifted or spilled aggregate and unsecured flatbed loads cause the crashes behind the truck. |
| Handheld phone ban; hazardous conditions | 392.82, 392.14 | No handheld phone use; reduce speed or stop in snow and ice. |
| Minimum insurance | Part 387 | $750,000 for general freight; $1 million for oil; $5 million for hazardous materials. Many carriers, and most large aggregate and plant-freight carriers, carry more. |

**Michigan’s weight laws.** Michigan law permits certain properly configured eleven-axle truck combinations to weigh up to 164,000 pounds on eligible routes, subject to axle-weight, spacing, and other restrictions under MCL 257.722. This exceeds the general federal 80,000-pound interstate weight limit under Michigan’s grandfathered weight provisions. The Michigan State Police Commercial Vehicle Enforcement Division and the Road Commission for Oakland County’s weighmasters enforce applicable vehicle-weight requirements within their respective jurisdictions. An overweight or improper-loading citation may be relevant evidence in a crash investigation, but whether the violation contributed to the collision depends on the circumstances.

**Michigan no-fault still applies to you.** Your own auto insurer pays your medical bills, 85 percent of lost wages up to the monthly cap, replacement services, and attendant care under MCL 500.3107 regardless of fault, with written notice due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)); the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan pays if no household policy applies (MCL 500.3172). Your claim against the driver and carrier for pain and suffering and excess losses requires a threshold injury under MCL 500.3135 and must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The difference from a car case is the insurance available to pay it.

**Other defendants.** The shipper or broker that hired the carrier or loaded the trailer; the pit or plant operator whose site traffic plan put trucks across a public road without a signal; the trailer owner, often a different company from the tractor’s; the maintenance contractor; the manufacturer of a failed component; and, for a government truck, the Road Commission for Oakland County, a city, or MDOT under the Governmental Tort Liability Act’s motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405). Where a road or signal defect contributed, the RCOC (county roads) or MDOT (I-75, I-696, M-59, M-24, Telegraph, Woodward) may be a defendant under the highway exception, MCL 691.1402, with 120-day and six-month notice requirements.

## The evidence, and how fast it disappears

Trucking companies must keep hours-of-service records for six months (49 CFR 395.8) and driver inspection reports for three months (49 CFR 396.11); after that they may lawfully be destroyed. MDOT freeway cameras, the RCOC’s signal cameras, plant-gate and pit-scale cameras, and business cameras along the mile roads are overwritten in days to weeks. The truck’s engine control module and event data recorder can be cleared when the truck is repaired and returned to service. The first act in an Oakland County truck case is a **preservation letter** to the carrier, its insurer, the trailer owner, and the shipper or pit operator, demanding that they keep the ELD data, ECM and EDR data, dash camera footage, the driver’s qualification file and post-crash test results, dispatch records, bills of lading and scale tickets, the maintenance file, and the truck itself for inspection. A carrier that fails to preserve evidence relevant to pending or reasonably foreseeable litigation may face spoliation sanctions in Michigan courts, depending on the circumstances of the loss, the relevance of the evidence, and whether a reasonable explanation for its destruction exists. We also request the MSP Commercial Vehicle Enforcement Division’s post-crash inspection report, the RCOC’s signal timing and camera data where a signal is involved, and MDOT’s camera footage before it cycles.

## What to do after an Oakland County truck crash

1. **Call 911 and get medical care the same day.** Truck-crash injuries to the neck, back, and brain often present days later.
2. **Photograph the truck**: the cab, the trailer, the DOT number and carrier name on the door, the plates of tractor and trailer, the load, and the skid marks. For a gravel train, photograph the axle configuration and any spilled aggregate.
3. **Get the police report.** The UD-10 is purchased at michigan.gov/tcps for $15; for a serious crash, ask whether the MSP Commercial Vehicle Enforcement Division conducted a post-crash inspection.
4. **Do not speak to the carrier’s insurer or its investigator.** Decline the recorded statement.
5. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** within the first week.
6. **Call a lawyer within days**, so the preservation letter goes out before the records expire.

## The courts

Most Oakland County truck cases are filed in the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac. Cases against out-of-state carriers may be filed in or removed to the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan when federal jurisdictional requirements are satisfied, including applicable citizenship requirements and an amount in controversy exceeding $75,000 under 28 USC 1332 and 1441, and claims against MDOT go to the Court of Claims. Venue in a Michigan personal injury lawsuit follows the statutory order of priority in MCL 600.1629, generally beginning with the county where the original injury occurred and where a defendant resides, conducts business, or maintains a registered office.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many truck accidents happen in Oakland County?** 2,081 heavy trucks and buses were involved in Oakland County crashes in 2024, according to the Michigan State Police, up from 1,906 in 2023. Only Wayne County had more.

**What is a gravel train, and why does it matter?** A tractor pulling two aggregate trailers on up to eleven axles, legal in Michigan at up to 164,000 pounds gross, about twice a conventional semi. The weight means longer stopping distances and more severe crashes, and Michigan’s weight and axle rules give the case fault evidence a conventional truck crash does not have.

**Who pays my medical bills after a truck crash in Michigan?** Your own no-fault insurer, regardless of fault, after you file an Application for No-Fault Benefits; the Assigned Claims Plan if no household policy applies. The carrier’s insurer pays for pain and suffering and losses beyond no-fault once fault and a threshold injury are established.

**Why does the lawyer need to act within days?** Because the records that prove the case expire: six months for hours-of-service logs, three months for inspection reports, days or weeks for camera footage, and the truck’s electronic data can be cleared on repair. A preservation letter puts the carrier on notice that relevant evidence should be preserved for anticipated litigation, even if its ordinary record-retention period would otherwise permit destruction.

**I was hit in the I-696 construction zone. Does that change the case?** It can. Truck drivers must follow applicable work-zone speed limits, signs, and lane restrictions. Under MCL 257.601b, moving violations committed in a work zone generally carry doubled fines, with additional penalties possible when certain violations cause injury or death. The contractor’s traffic-control plan, construction records, and available camera footage may be important evidence. A claim against a contractor depends on its actual duties and any negligence that contributed to the crash; a claim against MDOT must also satisfy Michigan’s governmental-immunity rules and an applicable statutory exception.

**Is a truck case worth more than a car case?** The injuries are usually worse and the insurance is usually larger: $750,000 minimum for a general freight carrier against $50,000 for a minimally insured car. The value still depends on the injury, fault, and the record.
