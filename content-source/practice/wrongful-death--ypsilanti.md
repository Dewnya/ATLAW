## Ypsilanti Wrongful Death Lawyer

When someone dies in Ypsilanti because another person, company, or public body was careless, Michigan’s Wrongful Death Act (MCL 600.2922) gives the family a claim, but not in the way many families expect:

- **The claim belongs to the estate,** not to the spouse or children directly.
- **It must be brought by a personal representative** appointed by the probate court.
- **The recovery is divided among family members by a judge.**
- **The deadline depends on what caused the death and who caused it.** Some deadlines are 120 days or six months.

In Ypsilanti, who caused the death changes more than usual:

- **A death involving Eastern Michigan University,** including a crash with a University vehicle or a defective University building, is a claim against the State in the Court of Claims, with a six-month notice, or 120 days for a building defect.
- **A death on a road** may involve the City of Ypsilanti, the Washtenaw County Road Commission, or MDOT, depending on whether it happened in the city, the township, or on I-94, US-12, or M-17.
- **A death after hospital care** may involve a private hospital, the University of Michigan, or the VA, each in a different court.

## How an Ypsilanti wrongful death claim works

**The estate.** Only the personal representative can bring the claim (MCL 600.2922(2)). Where the person lived in Washtenaw County, including the City of Ypsilanti and Ypsilanti Township, the estate is opened in the Washtenaw County Probate Court in Ann Arbor. The steps are:

1. If there is a will naming a personal representative, that person petitions for appointment.
2. If not, a family member, usually the spouse or an adult child, petitions under Michigan’s priority rules (MCL 700.3203).
3. The court issues letters of authority.
4. The personal representative then has standing to hire a lawyer for the estate and file.

We file the petition as the first legal step.

**When the person who died lived somewhere else.** An EMU student whose parents live in another state, or a driver passing through on I-94, may have been domiciled elsewhere. The estate is generally opened where the person was domiciled, and a Michigan claim may then need an appointment or filing here as well. We work out where the estate should be opened before anything is filed.

**Notice to the family.** Within 30 days after filing suit, the personal representative must notify in writing every person who may share in the recovery (MCL 600.2922(2)–(4)):

- the spouse
- children and descendants
- parents and grandparents
- brothers and sisters
- the children of the deceased’s spouse
- anyone named in the will

The list is not limited to dependents. A parent, sibling, or adult child who was not financially dependent can still share for the loss of companionship. The Act does not rank them in a fixed order; the court divides the recovery according to each person’s loss. A person named in the will may share in the recovery but does not bring the claim. Only the personal representative does.

**The damages** (MCL 600.2922(6)):

- medical and hospital expenses from the injury to the death
- funeral and burial expenses
- the person’s **conscious pain and suffering** between the injury and death
- **loss of financial support** the family would have received
- **loss of society and companionship** for each family member

**What the family cannot recover: the decedent’s lost future earnings.** In *Daher v Prime Healthcare Services-Garden City* (July 2024), the Michigan Supreme Court held unanimously that the Wrongful Death Act does not allow recovery of the lost future earnings the person who died would have made. What the family can recover is the financial support it would actually have received. For a young person or a student, that difference can be large, and it is why the evidence of support matters from the start.

Michigan places no cap on these damages except in medical malpractice and product liability cases (MCL 600.1483, 600.2946a). The malpractice caps in MCL 600.1483 do not apply to a death caused by a driver, a landlord, or an employer. Michigan does not award punitive damages in wrongful death cases.

**Comparative fault.** If the person who died was partly at fault, the damages are reduced by that share. If their fault was greater than the combined fault of everyone else, the estate cannot recover noneconomic damages, but can still recover reduced economic damages (MCL 600.2959). Fault above 50 percent does not end the whole claim. In an auto case, the no-fault act bars noneconomic damages at more than 50 percent fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)).

**The distribution hearing.** After a settlement or verdict, the personal representative proposes a division among the eligible family members, with notice to each. If they agree, the court approves it. If not, the court decides based on each person’s relationship and loss (MCL 600.2922(6)). Fees and costs are approved at the same hearing. Because a judge divides the money, no family member should sign anything with an insurer; a release signed by one relative does not settle the estate’s claim.

## The causes of Ypsilanti wrongful death claims

**Crashes in the city and the township.** Ypsilanti’s fatal crashes happen on I-94 through the township, on Michigan Avenue and Ecorse Road, on Washtenaw Avenue, on the downtown grid, and on the township’s county roads. A wrongful death claim after a crash does not step outside no-fault. The claim against the at-fault driver requires a threshold injury, and death meets the threshold (MCL 500.3135(1)). No-fault benefits are paid alongside it.

The at-fault driver’s liability insurer pays, subject to policy limits that default to $250,000/$500,000 unless the driver chose lower limits, down to the $50,000/$100,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(1), (5)). We also check the deceased’s own underinsured motorist coverage, umbrella policies, and the policy of an employer whose driver was working.

**No-fault survivor’s loss benefits** (MCL 500.3108) are paid regardless of fault by the insurer that would have paid the person’s PIP, for up to three years after the death:

- the economic support the person provided, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- $20 a day in replacement services
- funeral expenses up to the policy limit, at least $1,750 and up to $5,000 (MCL 500.3107(1)(a))

Survivor’s loss stops at the cap. Support above it is part of the wrongful death claim. Written notice is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)); do not wait for the lawsuit. See our [Ypsilanti car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents/ypsilanti).

**Pedestrians and cyclists.** Many people in Ypsilanti walk, bike, or ride the bus, along Michigan Avenue, Washtenaw Avenue, and near the EMU campus and the Ypsilanti Transit Center. For a pedestrian or cyclist, the no-fault insurer may be the family’s own household policy or a parent’s policy for a student, and if there is none, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3114, 500.3115(1), 500.3172). See our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents).

**Truck and motorcycle deaths.** A fatal crash with a tractor-trailer on I-94 or Michigan Avenue, or with a truck leaving a warehouse near Willow Run, brings the federal trucking rules, the carrier’s commercial policy, and evidence that can be overwritten within days. See our [Ypsilanti truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/ypsilanti). A fatal motorcycle crash follows the rider priority rules, and a rider’s death in a crash with no other vehicle, such as a deer strike, may carry no survivor’s loss claim at all. See our [Ypsilanti motorcycle accident page](/personal-injury/motorcycle-accidents/ypsilanti).

**Drunk driving.** The family may have claims against the driver and, under the dram shop act (MCL 436.1801), the bar, restaurant, or store that served a visibly intoxicated person or a minor. The dram shop claim has its own rules:

- Written notice must go to every defendant within 120 days after the family hires a lawyer.
- The intoxicated driver must be named and kept in the suit.
- The suit must be filed within **two years**, not three.
- The family of the person who was served, including a minor, generally has no dram shop claim for that person’s own death. The claim belongs to the people that person hurt.

The criminal case proceeds separately, and the family’s claim does not wait for it. See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**Ford Lake and the Huron River.** Ford Lake, in the township, is used for boating, fishing, and swimming. The owner of a boat is liable for a death caused by its negligent operation by anyone the owner allowed to use it, and consent is presumed when the operator is a member of the owner’s family (MCL 324.80157). A drowning at a township park is limited by governmental immunity unless an exception applies. A drowning in a private pool, at an apartment complex, or on private shoreline may be a premises claim against the owner.

**Deaths on the job.** Ypsilanti’s workplaces include EMU, the hospitals, the warehouses, distribution centers, and air cargo operations around Willow Run, the industrial area along Rawsonville Road, construction on I-94 and the corridors, and retail and restaurants. When a worker dies on the job, the employer owes workers’ compensation death benefits to dependents, up to 500 weeks plus burial (MCL 418.321, 418.345), and generally cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). There are two important openings:

- **A third party** whose negligence caused the death can be sued in a wrongful death action. That includes a carrier or driver at a loading dock, a warehouse operator that is not the employer, a general contractor, another subcontractor, a property owner, or the manufacturer of defective equipment ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability)).
- **The employer itself** can be sued only under the narrow intentional tort exception, where the employer had actual knowledge that an injury was certain to occur and willfully disregarded it (MCL 418.131(1)).

The MIOSHA investigation file is one of the first records requested. See our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).

**Hospital and medical deaths.** Ypsilanti families are treated at three systems, each in a different court:

| Provider | Who it is | Where the claim goes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trinity Health Ann Arbor (Ypsilanti mailing address, in Superior Township) | A private hospital | Washtenaw County Trial Court, with the malpractice rules |
| University of Michigan Health | The State | Court of Claims, with the six-month notice and the malpractice rules |
| VA Ann Arbor Healthcare System | The federal government | Federal Tort Claims Act: administrative claim within two years |

The Michigan malpractice rules are:

- a 182-day Notice of Intent (MCL 600.2912b)
- an Affidavit of Merit (MCL 600.2912d)
- a two-year period, with a six-month discovery rule and six-year repose (MCL 600.5805(8), 600.5838a)
- the cap (MCL 600.1483)

A public hospital is not immune from a malpractice claim (MCL 691.1407(4)), but a claim against the University must also satisfy the six-month Court of Claims notice. A VA claim follows the federal deadlines, which Michigan’s estate savings provision does not extend (28 USC 2401(b), 2675). See our [Ann Arbor wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death/ann-arbor) and [medical malpractice page](/personal-injury/medical-malpractice).

**Nursing homes, rental housing, and premises.** A death from neglect in a nursing home, assisted living, or memory care community may be ordinary negligence or malpractice, which changes the deadline. See our [nursing home abuse page](/personal-injury/nursing-home-abuse). Deaths in homes and on other property follow premises law:

- fires, falls, and carbon monoxide in rental houses and apartments, including student rentals near campus and complexes in the township, where the landlord must keep the premises fit and in reasonable repair (MCL 554.139)
- falls from balconies, porches, and stairs

See our [Ypsilanti slip and fall page](/personal-injury/slip-and-fall).

## Deaths involving EMU or a public agency

**Eastern Michigan University is the State.** EMU is a constitutional state university. A death involving the University, such as a crash with a University vehicle or an EMU police car, or a fall caused by a defective University building, is a claim against the State:

- It is filed in the Michigan Court of Claims and decided by a judge, not a jury.
- A verified notice, or the claim itself, must be filed with that court within six months (MCL 600.6431). For a defective University building, written notice is due there within 120 days (MCL 691.1406).
- The Michigan Supreme Court has held that the notice applies to any claim against the State, wherever it is filed (*Christie v Wayne State University*, 2023).



**Roads and public vehicles.**

- **City streets** belong to the City of Ypsilanti, with written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404).
- **Township roads** belong to the Washtenaw County Road Commission, with written notice within 120 days. Townships do not own roads. The township has the duty for sidewalks along those roads.
- **I-94, US-12, M-17, and state trunklines** belong to MDOT: written notice of a road defect filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **City, township, Sheriff, Road Commission, and TheRide vehicles** are claims under the motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405). The township’s police services are provided by the Washtenaw County Sheriff’s Office.

An individual government employee is liable only for gross negligence (MCL 691.1407(2)).

| Claim | Deadline | Statute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crash, fall, fire, boating, construction, product, and premises claims | 3 years, generally from the injury that caused the death | MCL 600.5805(2), 600.5827, 600.2922 |
| Medical malpractice (Trinity Health Ann Arbor, private practices) | Notice of Intent, then 2 years, with a 6-month discovery rule and 6-year repose | MCL 600.2912b, 600.5805(8), 600.5838a |
| Claim against EMU, the University of Michigan, MDOT, or the State | Verified Court of Claims notice within 6 months, plus the malpractice rules for a medical death; 120 days for a road or building defect | MCL 600.6431, 691.1404, 691.1406 |
| VA Ann Arbor Healthcare System | Administrative claim (SF-95) within 2 years; suit within 6 months of denial | 28 USC 2401(b), 2675 |
| Savings provision for the estate | Up to 2 years after letters of authority, but no more than 3 years after the ordinary period ran | MCL 600.5852 |
| No-fault survivor’s loss benefits | Written notice within 1 year | MCL 500.3145(1) |
| Dram shop claim | Notice within 120 days of hiring a lawyer; suit within 2 years | MCL 436.1801(3) |
| Road defect: City of Ypsilanti or Washtenaw County Road Commission | Written notice within 120 days | MCL 691.1404 |
| Sidewalk or building defect: City or Ypsilanti Charter Township | Written notice within 120 days | MCL 691.1402a, 691.1404, 691.1406 |
| Workers’ compensation death benefits | Claim within 2 years of the death | MCL 418.381 |

When the injury and the death happen on the same day, the difference between “from the injury” and “from the death” does not matter. When a person lingers for weeks or months in the hospital, the ordinary period may already be running before the estate exists, which is what the savings provision is for. It adds only a limited period, and courts apply it strictly. Open the estate and file inside the ordinary deadline. The savings provision is a Michigan rule; it does not extend the federal VA deadline.

## What the family should do in the first weeks

1. **Get the death certificate and request the Washtenaw County Medical Examiner’s report,** along with the police crash or incident report (the Ypsilanti Police Department in the city, the Sheriff’s Office in the township, EMU police on campus, or the State Police on I-94), the EMS run sheet, and the hospital record. If the family’s customs call for prompt burial, tell the Medical Examiner’s office at once, or call us.
2. **Keep everything:** the vehicle, the bike, the phone, the helmet, the work gear, the medication bottles, the discharge papers. Do not authorize salvage of a vehicle until a lawyer has seen it.
3. **Notify the no-fault insurer in writing** if a vehicle was involved. Survivor’s loss benefits begin on the claim, not on the lawsuit.
4. **Do not give a recorded statement** to any insurer, hospital risk manager, or claims office, and do not sign any document, however it is described. An insurer offering to “cover the funeral” in exchange for a signature may be settling the claim for the price of a funeral.
5. **Open the estate.** The probate petition is the first legal step; the wrongful death suit cannot be filed until letters issue. If the person lived in another state, ask us before filing anywhere.
6. **Call within days** if the death involved EMU, the University of Michigan, the VA, a road, a government vehicle, a bar, a trucking company, a warehouse, or an employer. Those notice periods are short, and the trucking and site records expire.

## The courts

- **Death claims against private defendants** are filed in the Washtenaw County Trial Court (22nd Circuit) at 101 East Huron Street in Ann Arbor, not the 14A-1 or 14B District Court, which hear civil cases only up to $25,000.
- **A case against an out-of-state trucking company or driver** may be filed in, or removed to, the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan.
- **Claims against EMU, the University of Michigan, or the State** go to the Court of Claims.
- **VA claims,** after the administrative claim, go to federal court.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who can file a wrongful death lawsuit for an Ypsilanti resident?** Only the personal representative of the estate, appointed by the Washtenaw County Probate Court. A spouse, child, or parent can be appointed, but must be appointed before the claim can be filed.

**Who receives the money?** The spouse, children, descendants, parents, grandparents, siblings, the children of the deceased’s spouse, and anyone named in the will may share, whether or not they were financially dependent. The probate court divides the recovery according to each person’s loss, after notice to each of them.

**How long do we have?** - Generally three years from the injury that caused the death for crash, fall, boating, construction, and premises claims, with a limited extension for the estate. - Two years, with a 182-day notice, for medical malpractice. - Six months for a notice to the Court of Claims in a claim against EMU or the University of Michigan, and 120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT or a building-defect notice against EMU. - Two years to file the administrative claim for a VA death. - Two years for a dram shop claim. - One year to claim no-fault survivor’s loss benefits. - 120 days to notify the City or the Road Commission of a road defect.

**Can we recover the income our son would have earned over his career?** Not as such. In 2024 the Michigan Supreme Court held that the Wrongful Death Act does not allow the decedent’s lost future earnings. The family can recover the financial support it would have received, along with medical and funeral costs, his conscious pain and suffering, and each family member’s loss of companionship.

**Can we get punitive damages?** No. Michigan does not award punitive damages in wrongful death cases. The family recovers the losses the Act lists, which have no cap outside medical malpractice and product liability cases.

**Our daughter was an EMU student, and our home is in another state. Where is the estate opened?** Usually where she was domiciled, which may be your home state. A Michigan claim may then need a Michigan appointment or filing as well. We sort that out before anything is filed.

**My husband died in a crash on I-94. Are survivor’s loss benefits part of the lawsuit?** No. Survivor’s loss is paid by the no-fault insurer regardless of fault, up to a monthly cap, for up to three years. The wrongful death claim against the at-fault driver or trucking company is separate. It covers the losses survivor’s loss does not, including support above the cap and the family’s loss of companionship.

**If our son was partly at fault, do we lose the whole claim?** No. His share of fault reduces the damages. If it was greater than everyone else’s combined, the estate cannot recover noneconomic damages such as loss of companionship, but it can still recover reduced economic damages, such as lost support and funeral costs.

**A bad road contributed to the crash. Do we sue Ypsilanti Township?** No. Townships do not own roads. A township road claim goes to the Washtenaw County Road Commission, with written notice within 120 days. A city street claim goes to the City of Ypsilanti, and an I-94, US-12, or M-17 claim goes to MDOT, with written notice filed in the Court of Claims within 120 days.

**Is there a lawsuit if the driver was charged with a crime?** Yes. The criminal case and the family’s claim are separate. The family does not have to wait for the criminal case, and the civil deadlines keep running.
