## The two programs

**Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI)** is for people who worked and paid Social Security taxes: generally 40 work credits, 20 of them in the ten years before disability, fewer for younger workers. The benefit is based on your earnings record, with Medicare after 24 months of entitlement. Because the benefit tracks earnings, a plant worker with a long union career often has a substantial SSDI benefit, and the back pay matters accordingly. See our [SSDI page](about:blank).

**Supplemental Security Income (SSI)** is for people with little income and few assets, whether or not they worked: $994 a month for an individual in 2026 and $1,029 in 2027, reduced by other income, with a $2,000 asset limit (home and one vehicle excluded) and Michigan Medicaid. Some claimants qualify for both. See our [SSI page](about:blank).

## The five-step test, as it applies to a Sterling Heights claim

Social Security uses a five-step sequential evaluation (20 CFR 404.1520), deciding the claim at the first step where the answer is clear.

1. **Are you working above the substantial gainful activity level?** Earning more than $1,690 a month in 2026 ($1,770 in 2027) generally ends the claim at step one. Part-time or accommodated work below that level does not.
2. **Is your impairment severe and expected to last 12 months or result in death?** A diagnosis is not enough; the record must show functional limits.
3. **Does your condition meet or equal a Listing?** Meeting one wins the claim here. See our [qualifying impairments page](about:blank); some conditions are fast-tracked under [Compassionate Allowances](about:blank).
4. **Can you do your past relevant work?** Social Security assesses your residual functional capacity (RFC), what you can still do despite your limitations, and compares it to the jobs you held in the past five years. Line and shop work is usually medium or heavy exertion, with repetitive use of the shoulders, hands, and back. A back, shoulder, knee, or hand condition that rules out that work usually wins step four; the question becomes step five.
5. **Can you do any other work?** Using your RFC, age, education, and work experience under the Medical-Vocational Guidelines (the “grids”). Age matters: at 50 and above, a claimant limited to sedentary work whose past work was unskilled or semi-skilled, with no transferable skills, is generally found disabled under the grids; at 55 and above, the same is true for many claimants limited to light work. Many Sterling Heights claimants are in exactly that position, so the age at onset and the skill level of the past work are often the whole case. Supervisory, skilled-trades, or quality-control roles can produce transferable skills, and that is contested at the hearing.

## Back pay and the fee

SSDI benefits begin five full months after the established onset date; SSI is not paid for months before the application. SSDI back pay reaches to the onset date, up to 12 months before the application, and the onset date your lawyer proves sets the amount. The fee is set by federal law: 25 percent of back benefits, capped at the current limit, and nothing if the claim is not approved (42 USC 406(a)). Social Security pays it directly from back pay; ongoing monthly benefits are not subject to it. We advance the cost of obtaining your medical records.

## How a Sterling Heights disability claim interacts with other claims

- **Union and employer disability benefits.** Automaker and supplier union contracts often provide sickness-and-accident and extended disability benefits, and salaried employees may have group long-term disability insurance. Many of these plans require you to apply for SSDI, reduce their benefit by the SSDI award, and may claim repayment from your back pay. An ERISA-governed plan denial has its own 180-day appeal deadline (29 CFR 2560.503-1), and the plan file and the Social Security file should tell the same story.
- **Workers’ compensation** offsets SSDI (42 USC 424a), and a comp redemption should be written to limit the offset; see our [Sterling Heights workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation/sterling-heights).
- **A personal injury settlement** does not reduce SSDI but can affect SSI; see our [Sterling Heights personal injury lawyer page](/personal-injury/personal-injury/sterling-heights).
- **VA disability** is evidence Social Security must consider but does not bind it; see our [VA benefit claims page](about:blank).

## What to do if you are applying or were denied

1. **Apply as soon as you stop working** or drop below the SGA level; SSDI back pay reaches back no more than 12 months before the application.
2. **If denied, appeal within 60 days.** Do not file a new application.
3. **Keep treating** and tell every provider how your condition limits you day to day; gaps in treatment are a common reason claims are denied.
4. **Keep your employer’s records**: the last job description, restricted-duty and leave records, plant medical visits, and any union disability or LTD file.
5. **Attend the consultative examination** and tell us what happened in it.
6. **Call a lawyer before the hearing is scheduled**, not after the notice arrives. See our [how to win your claim page](about:blank).

## Frequently asked questions

**Where is the Social Security office in Sterling Heights?** Social Security’s Sterling Heights field office is at 7385 Triangle Drive. You can also apply or appeal online at ssa.gov or by phone at 1-800-772-1213, without visiting.

**I worked on the line for 25 years and can’t do it anymore. Will I get disability?** Possibly. If the medical evidence shows you can no longer do that work, the question is whether you can do other work. At 50 and older, limited to sedentary work, and at 55 and older, limited to light work, Social Security’s age rules often favor a worker whose past jobs were unskilled or semi-skilled.

**My union disability plan told me to apply for Social Security. Why?** Because many union and employer disability plans reduce their benefit by the SSDI award and may ask you to repay overlapping months from your back pay. Read the plan before you sign a repayment agreement, and make sure both claims describe your limits the same way.

**How long do I have to appeal?** Sixty days from the date on the denial notice, plus five days for mailing, at every stage.

**Where will my hearing be?** At the Oak Park hearing office, which serves the Sterling Heights field office, in person, by video, or by phone. You can ask to appear in person.

**What changes in 2027?** From January 2027, the SGA limit rises to $1,770 a month, the trial work month to $1,270, and the SSI federal benefit to $1,029 for an individual, with a 3.6 percent increase in benefits.
