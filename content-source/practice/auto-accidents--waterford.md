## Where Waterford crashes happen

**Highland Road (M-59).** The township’s spine, from Telegraph and the Pontiac line west past the Crescent Lake, Airport Road, Williams Lake, and Pontiac Lake signals. The crashes are left-turn crashes across several lanes of oncoming traffic, red-light crashes, rear-ends in the stacked turn lanes and at the signals, driveway crashes at the strip centers, and pedestrian crashes where people cross between the stores and the apartment complexes (see our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents)). **Dixie Highway (US-24) and Telegraph.** From the Telegraph junction northwest through Walton, Sashabaw, Scott Lake, and Andersonville toward Clarkston: crashes at dealership and retail driveways, left-turn and signal crashes, and turning-truck crashes from the gravel and freight traffic that uses Dixie (see our [Waterford truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents)). Farther up the same corridor, Dixie at I-75 in Springfield Township ranked fourth in the county in 2024. Telegraph along the township’s eastern side carries the traffic between Pontiac, the county complex, and Dixie.

**The lake roads.** Walton, Sashabaw, Williams Lake, Pontiac Lake, Elizabeth Lake, Cass Lake, Cooley Lake, Scott Lake, Hatchery, and Airport Road run two to five lanes between the lakes, with curves, hills, driveways and lake-access drives on short sight lines, and stop-controlled crossings. The crashes are intersection crashes where these roads meet M-59 and Dixie, head-on and run-off-road crashes on the curves, and, in summer, crashes involving trailers, motorcycles, and visitors unfamiliar with the roads (see our [motorcycle accident page](/personal-injury/motorcycle-accidents)). In winter, the same hills and curves produce ice and snow crashes, where fault depends on speed for conditions.

**Whose road it is: never the township’s.** Michigan townships do not own public roads. Highland Road (M-59), Dixie Highway, and Telegraph (US-24) are MDOT’s; a road-defect claim against MDOT goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). Walton, Sashabaw, Crescent Lake, Williams Lake, Pontiac Lake, Elizabeth Lake, Cass Lake, Airport Road, Scott Lake, Andersonville, and most subdivision streets are generally the Road Commission for Oakland County’s; a claim against the Road Commission takes written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404). Signals on the state highways may be operated by the Road Commission under contract with MDOT, which affects where the signal records are kept. A pothole or other defect in the roadbed can make the agency a defendant alongside the other driver; on State and county roads, signals, signs, and sight obstructions generally fall outside the highway exception. A notice sent to Waterford Township for a road defect is sent to the wrong agency. The township does have one road-adjacent duty: it must keep the public sidewalks in repair, including those beside county and state roads (MCL 691.1401(d), 691.1402a), which matters for a pedestrian who falls, not for a crash. ## Fault is the case

In a rear-end crash, fault is rarely disputed. At an M-59 left turn, a Dixie driveway, or a lake-road stop sign where the view is blocked, it is disputed often, and the other driver’s insurer may try to assign you half or more of the fault. That number matters: if you are found more than 50 percent at fault, you lose your claim for pain and suffering, and below that your recovery is reduced by your share (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). At exactly 50 percent you recover half. The evidence that decides it is perishable:

- the Waterford Township Police report and the officer’s diagram, or the State Police or Oakland County Sheriff’s report where they worked the crash;
- **signal timing data** from MDOT or the Road Commission for Oakland County;
- store, dealership, gas-station, and apartment-complex cameras along M-59 and Dixie, many overwritten within days;
- the vehicles’ **event data recorders**, which store speed, braking, and throttle in the seconds before impact;
- the other driver’s phone records, for a distracted-driving claim (see our [distracted driving page](/personal-injury/distracted-driving-accidents));
- photographs of the sight line at a lake-road intersection before vegetation, a parked trailer, or a snowbank changes it;
- witnesses in the other lanes.

## Who pays after a Waterford crash

**Your no-fault (PIP) claim.** Your own auto insurer pays your medical expenses up to the PIP level you chose, 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year, replacement services at $20 a day, and attendant care, regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107). If you have no policy, a resident relative’s policy pays (MCL 500.3114); if none exists, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan does (MCL 500.3172), capped at $250,000 for medical benefits. The insurer must receive written notice within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)) and must pay each bill within 30 days of reasonable proof or owe 12 percent interest (MCL 500.3142). Since the 2019 reform, PIP medical coverage is no longer unlimited unless you chose it, so the level on your declarations page matters. An older driver on Medicare may have opted out of PIP medical entirely; in that case Medicare pays first and its rules apply. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance) and [Michigan no-fault guide](about:blank).

**Your claim against the at-fault driver.** For pain and suffering, you need a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment is an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life; it does not have to be permanent (MCL 500.3135(5); *McCormick v Carrier*, 2010). You can also recover lost wages beyond the three years or above the monthly cap, and medical bills above your chosen PIP level (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)). The suit must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The mini tort recovers up to $3,000 in vehicle damage not covered by your policy (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)), filed in the 51st District Court if the at-fault driver’s insurer will not pay it.

**Coverage on the other side, and UIM sequencing.** Liability limits on M-59 and Dixie range from the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)) to large policies. When the at-fault driver’s limit is too small for the injury, your own **underinsured motorist coverage** pays the difference, but most policies require your insurer’s written consent before you accept the at-fault driver’s limit; settling without it can forfeit the UIM claim. We read every policy in the household, including umbrella coverage, before valuing the claim. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Drunk drivers and dram shop.** Oakland County recorded 871 alcohol-involved crashes in 2024. A crash caused by a drunk driver leaving a bar, restaurant, or lakeside establishment adds a claim against the business that served a visibly intoxicated person or a minor. Written notice must go to the retailer within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, the drunk driver must be named and kept in the suit, and the suit must be filed within two years, not three (MCL 436.1801(3), (4)). See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**Government vehicles.** Waterford Township Police and the Waterford Regional Fire Department, Oakland County Sheriff and Road Commission vehicles, SMART buses, and Waterford School District buses fall under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405). See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

## What to do after a Waterford crash

1. **Call 911 and get the police report.** Michigan requires a report for any crash with injury or $1,000 in damage (MCL 257.622). Most township crashes are worked by the Waterford Township Police Department; some by the Michigan State Police or the Oakland County Sheriff’s Office. **[VERIFY: report request process]**
2. **Photograph the vehicles, plates, the other driver’s license and insurance card, the signals, the lane markings, and the view from your stop line.** Note every business with a camera facing the scene; footage is kept for days, not weeks.
3. **Get medical care the same day.** McLaren Oakland and Trinity Health Oakland in Pontiac are both Level II trauma centers; the most serious injuries may go to Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak, a Level I center.
4. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** within the first week.
5. **Read your declarations page** for PIP level, UM/UIM, umbrella, and collision coverage.
6. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the other driver’s insurer, sign a release, or accept a policy-limits offer before your UIM insurer consents.
7. **Call a lawyer within days** if fault is disputed or the road played a part; the camera footage and signal data are the first evidence lost, and the 120-day Road Commission notice is already running.

## The courts

Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, are filed in the 51st District Court at 5100 Civic Center Drive in Waterford, which serves Waterford Township. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac. A case against an out-of-state driver or carrier may be filed in or removed to the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan. Defect claims against MDOT go to the Court of Claims. A fatal crash is brought by the estate through the Oakland County Probate Court; see our [wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death).

## Frequently asked questions

**Which Waterford intersections have the most crashes?** In the State Police data for 2024, Highland Road (M-59) at Crescent Lake Road ranked ninth in Oakland County, with 60 crashes and 22 injuries. **[CONFIRM: data: Waterford’s other highest-crash locations from the MTCF query tool]**

**How many car accidents happen in Waterford each year?** Waterford Township recorded 1,546 crashes, 497 injuries, and 3 deaths in 2024. Oakland County as a whole recorded 35,951 crashes, 9,139 injuries, and 68 deaths in 2024.

**A pothole or a bad signal caused my crash. Do I file against Waterford Township?** No. Townships do not own roads. A defect in the roadbed on M-59, Dixie Highway, or Telegraph is MDOT’s, with notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days; on the county roads it is the Road Commission for Oakland County’s, with written notice within 120 days. A signal problem on a State or county road generally falls outside the highway exception.

**A driver turning left across M-59 hit me. Who is at fault?** Usually the turning driver, who must yield to oncoming traffic (MCL 257.650). A yellow or red light, your speed, or a blocked view can shift part of the fault, which is why the signal timing data and the nearby cameras matter.

**I slid on ice on a lake road and the other driver says it was my fault. Is it?** Not automatically. Michigan drivers must drive at a speed that lets them stop within the assured clear distance ahead (MCL 257.627), and weather does not excuse either driver. Fault is decided by each driver’s speed, following distance, and reaction, and a share of fault reduces your recovery rather than ending it unless it is more than 50 percent.

**The other driver’s insurer offered their policy limit. Should I take it?** Not before checking your own underinsured motorist coverage and getting your insurer’s written consent to settle. Accepting the limit without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under most policies.

**I searched for a Waterford car accident lawyer and found firms in Connecticut and New York. Does that matter?** Yes. Michigan’s no-fault law, its threshold for pain-and-suffering claims, its deadlines, and its courts are different from those states’. A crash in Waterford, Michigan needs a lawyer licensed and practicing in Michigan.

**Where would my Waterford case be filed?** Mini tort and claims up to $25,000 in the 51st District Court on Civic Center Drive; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court in Pontiac.

**How long do I have?** One year to give your no-fault insurer written notice, three years to sue the at-fault driver, two years to sue a bar, 120 days for a notice to the Road Commission, and 120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT..
