## Personal Injury Law Firm Serving St. Clair Shores, MI

St. Clair Shores is a lakeshore city of about 58,000 along Lake St. Clair in southeastern Macomb County, long and narrow between Eight Mile and Thirteen Mile, I-94 and the water. Jefferson Avenue runs its length along the lake, past the marinas, restaurants, and bars of the Nautical Mile and the city’s lakefront parks; Harper and Greater Mack run parallel inland; I-94 forms the western edge with interchanges at every mile road; and the mile roads cross between them. The Nautical Mile’s restaurants, bars, and waterfront attractions make alcohol-related crashes and potential dram shop claims an important concern for injured people in St. Clair Shores. In 2024, St. Clair Shores recorded 1,356 traffic crashes, with 365 people injured and three killed.

Drunk-driving crashes leaving the bars, pedestrian crashes at the Jefferson crossings on summer nights, parking-lot crashes at the marinas and restaurants, and the dram shop claims that follow a drunk driver back to the bar that served them (MCL 436.1801, 120-day notice). **The mile roads and Little Mack.** Eight through Thirteen Mile between I-94 and the lake, and Little Mack along the freeway. ## What is different about a St. Clair Shores claim

**Alcohol and the dram shop.** A drunk-driving crash in or leaving St. Clair Shores adds a potential defendant: under Michigan’s dram shop act, MCL 436.1801, a licensed bar or restaurant that unlawfully served a visibly intoxicated person may be liable if that service contributed to the resulting injury, and the claim requires written notice within 120 days after entering an attorney-client relationship to pursue a dram shop claim. The bar’s point-of-sale records, its cameras, and the witnesses who saw the driver served are the evidence, and they may be lost or deleted quickly. The same act reaches a marina or club that served a drunk boater.

**Coverage.** St. Clair Shores’ drivers carry a mix of policies, and the summer traffic to the Nautical Mile comes from everywhere. An insurer determined under Michigan’s no-fault priority rules may pay covered medical expenses and wage-loss benefits regardless of fault, subject to applicable limits and exclusions. The Michigan Assigned Claims Plan may apply when no other eligible PIP insurer is available. We review every policy in the household before we value a St. Clair Shores claim. See our [St. Clair Shores car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents), [no-fault insurance guide](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance), and [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Road agencies.** I-94 is MDOT’s; Jefferson, Harper, Greater Mack, Little Mack, and the mile roads are the Macomb County Department of Roads’ or the City of St. Clair Shores’ by segment. A crash caused by a qualifying defect in the traveled roadway may implicate the highway exception to governmental immunity. Signal malfunctions and sight obstructions require separate legal analysis. The city’s lakefront parks and municipal boat launches are city facilities. Injury claims may be subject to governmental immunity, recreational-use defenses, or, where a qualifying public-building defect is involved, the public-building exception under MCL 691.1406.

**Government vehicles.** St. Clair Shores Police and Fire, SMART buses on Jefferson, Harper, and Nine Mile, school buses from the Lakeview, Lake Shore, and South Lake districts, and Department of Roads trucks are claims under the Governmental Tort Liability Act’s motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405). See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

**The 40th District and the Sixteenth Circuit.** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims for vehicle damage (MCL 500.3135(3)(e), up to $3,000), are filed in the 40th District Court at 27701 Jefferson Avenue, which serves St. Clair Shores. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court at 40 N. Main Street in Mount Clemens. Wrongful death cases may require estate proceedings in Macomb County Probate Court at 40 N. Main Street in Mount Clemens, while the lawsuit proceeds in the court with jurisdiction. Settlements involving minors require court approval under MCR 2.420. A crash near the Wayne County boundary may raise venue questions under MCL 600.1629, depending on where the injury occurred and the parties' connections to the county.

**The hospitals.** Henry Ford St. John Hospital (formerly Ascension St. John) on Moross in Detroit, a few minutes south, and Henry Ford Macomb in Clinton Township treat seriously injured St. Clair Shores residents; Henry Ford Warren and Corewell Health Grosse Pointe are also regional treatment options. We obtain the full record from each, including the St. Clair Shores Fire Department EMS run sheet.

## Frequently asked questions

**A drunk driver leaving the Nautical Mile hit me. Can I sue the bar?** Possibly. Under Michigan’s dram shop act, a bar or restaurant that served a visibly intoxicated person can be liable for the injuries that person causes, with written notice generally required within 120 days after entering an attorney-client relationship to pursue the dram shop claim. The bar’s sales records, cameras, and staff are the evidence, and those records may be lost or deleted quickly. The drunk driver’s own insurance is pursued as well.

**I was injured in a boating accident on Lake St. Clair. Is that a personal injury case?** Yes. Boat operators owe the same duty of reasonable care as drivers, the Marine Safety Act sets operating rules whose violation is evidence of negligence, and available coverage depends on the applicable watercraft, homeowners’, marina, or other liability policies. Michigan automobile no-fault generally does not apply to boating-only accidents, so these claims usually proceed under negligence law, generally with a three-year deadline; if the operator was intoxicated, a licensed bar or marina that unlawfully served the visibly intoxicated operator may also face a dram shop claim, subject to its special notice requirement.

**Why does St. Clair Shores have so many alcohol-involved crashes?** The Michigan State Police data for 2024 shows about one crash in twenty-five in the city involved alcohol, the highest share among the 24 cities ATLAW serves. The Nautical Mile’s bars and restaurants, the marinas, and the summer lake traffic are the obvious reasons. For an injured person, the practical consequence is that a dram shop claim is more often available here than elsewhere.

**I was hurt in a crash at an I-94 interchange or on the service drive. Who is responsible for the road?** MDOT for the freeway and its ramps, with written notice of a road defect filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days; Macomb County or the City for the mile roads, depending on the segment, with written notice within 120 days. The service drives may belong to MDOT or to the county or City, so the owner of that stretch has to be confirmed before notice goes out. The claim against the other driver is unaffected.

**Where would my St. Clair Shores injury case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, in the 40th District Court on Jefferson; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court in Mount Clemens.

**Who pays my medical bills after a crash in St. Clair Shores?** Your own no-fault insurer, regardless of fault, up to your PIP level, after you file an Application for No-Fault Benefits within one year; a household member’s insurer if you have none; the Assigned Claims Plan if no policy applies.

**How much does it cost to hire you?** Nothing up front, and no fee unless we recover money for you. Michigan caps contingency fees in personal injury cases at one-third of the recovery (MCR 8.121).

**How long do I have?** Three years for most injury suits, one year to notify your no-fault insurer in writing, and as little as 120 days for a dram shop notice or a claim against the city or the county.
