## Rochester Hills, MI Personal Injury Lawyer

If you were hurt in Rochester Hills because someone else was careless, you have a claim under Michigan law. Its deadlines start the day you are hurt:

- **After a crash,** your no-fault insurer must have written notice within one year.
- **A road or sidewalk defect claim** needs written notice within 120 days if the road belongs to the City of Rochester Hills or the Road Commission for Oakland County. A defect on M-59, or on Rochester Road north of M-59, needs written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days.
- **An injury on the Oakland University campus** is a claim against the State, with notice within six months, or within 120 days for a defective road or building (MCL 691.1404, 691.1406).
- **An injury in a City, county, or school building** needs written notice to that agency within 120 days.
- **A claim against a bar** that served a drunk driver must be filed within two years.
- **A medical malpractice suit,** including many claims against nursing homes, requires 182 days’ notice first and must be filed within two years.
- **Most other claims** allow three years.

A personal injury lawyer’s first job is to find out which of those deadlines apply to you. The second is to make sure none runs out before your claim is paid.

## Who the defendant is in Rochester Hills

Rochester Hills is a city whose police are the County’s, whose roads are split four ways, and whose university is the State’s. Each of those changes who you claim against.

- **The roads are split four ways.** M-59, and Rochester Road (M-150) from M-59 north to Tienken, belong to MDOT, with written notice of a road defect filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). Many major roads, including Adams, Crooks, Livernois, Avon, Tienken, Walton, and Rochester Road south of M-59, belong to the Road Commission for Oakland County. Others, including Hamlin and John R, belong to the City of Rochester Hills, along with the subdivision streets. A claim against the Road Commission or the City requires written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404). Oakland University’s campus roads belong to the State. The notice must go to the agency that has jurisdiction over that stretch of road.
- **The sidewalks and pathways are the City’s.** The City must keep public sidewalks in reasonable repair (MCL 691.1402a). A fall on a broken public sidewalk is a claim against the City, with three conditions: the City must have known, or should have known, of the defect at least 30 days before the injury; a defect of less than two inches is presumed not to be actionable; and written notice is due within 120 days. Whether the City’s off-road pathway network counts as “sidewalk” under the statute is a separate question.
- **The police are Oakland County.** The Oakland County Sheriff’s Office polices Rochester Hills under contract. A crash with a patrol car in the city is a claim against Oakland County, under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405), not against the City.
- **Fire and EMS are the City’s.** A crash with a Rochester Hills Fire Department apparatus or ambulance is a claim against the City, under the same exception. EMS treatment claims are limited to gross negligence or willful misconduct (MCL 333.20965).
- **Oakland University is the State.** A claim against the university is a claim against the State of Michigan. That includes a crash with a university vehicle, an injury on a campus road, or a fall in a university building. It is filed in the Court of Claims, decided by a judge without a jury, and requires written notice within six months (MCL 600.6431, 600.6419), or within 120 days for a defective campus road or building (MCL 691.1404, 691.1406). A claim against a student, a visitor, or a private contractor on campus is an ordinary claim.
- **City, county, and school buildings are separate agencies.** An injury caused by a dangerous or defective condition in one of these buildings falls under the public building exception. That exception requires the agency to have known of the defect and failed to fix it, and it requires written notice to that agency within 120 days (MCL 691.1406). It covers a City of Rochester Hills building, a county building, and a Rochester Community Schools or Avondale Schools building.
- **The trails.** The Clinton River Trail and Paint Creek Trail cross the city’s roads at grade. A cyclist or walker hit by a car at a crossing has an ordinary no-fault and negligence claim. An injury caused by the trail itself or by another trail user is harder to bring. Governmental immunity applies to the public agencies that own the trails. The Recreational Land Use Act may limit a landowner’s liability to gross negligence or willful and wanton misconduct for people using land for recreation without charge (MCL 324.73301). See our [bicycle accident page](/personal-injury/bicycle-accidents).
- **Private property owners** are ordinary defendants, with a three-year deadline. That includes stores, the retail centers on Rochester Road and Adams, condominium associations, apartment owners, and the technology campuses. A private landlord must keep the premises and common areas in reasonable repair (MCL 554.139). Since *Kandil-Elsayed v F & E Oil* (2023), an “open and obvious” hazard goes to comparative fault rather than ending the claim.

## Two policies, in the right order

In a serious Rochester Hills crash, the question is often not whether the other driver had insurance but whether their limit, often $250,000 or more, covers the claim. If it does not, your own **underinsured motorist coverage** pays the difference up to your UIM limit. The sequence is:

1. The at-fault driver’s insurer offers its limit.
2. You notify your own insurer and ask for its written consent to settle.
3. Only then do you accept the limit and sign the release.
4. The UIM claim then proceeds against your own insurer.

Accepting the limit without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under most policies.

Some drivers still carry only the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)) Against an uninsured driver, the claim for pain and suffering depends on **uninsured motorist coverage**. Two rules cut the other way:

- An owner who was required to insure a car and did not is generally barred from PIP benefits for injuries in it (MCL 500.3113(b)).
- A driver who was operating their own uninsured vehicle cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, whoever was at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(c)).

See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

## What a Rochester Hills claim is worth, and what reduces it

A claim includes two kinds of damages:

- **Economic damages:** medical expenses, lost wages, lost earning capacity, and replacement services.
- **Noneconomic damages:** pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, and disfigurement.

There is no cap except in medical malpractice. In an auto case, noneconomic damages require a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A premises or dog bite case has no such threshold.

Your share of fault reduces the recovery, and above 50 percent it bars noneconomic damages (MCL 600.2959; in auto cases, MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). At exactly 50 percent, you recover half. Where more than one party caused the injury, each defendant generally pays only its own share of fault (MCL 600.2956, 600.6304), so every responsible party has to be identified and named. Michigan does not award punitive damages in ordinary injury cases. Exemplary damages, for humiliation or outrage, are available only in narrow circumstances.

Lost earning capacity is often a major part of the claim for a professional or engineer whose career was interrupted. It is proved with employment records and, in serious cases, a vocational and economic expert.

The at-fault driver’s liability limit defaults to $250,000/$500,000 unless the driver chose lower limits, down to the $50,000/$100,000 minimum (MCL 500.3009(1)). Your UIM and umbrella coverage fill the gap. In a premises, workplace, or nursing home case, the commercial policy of the property owner, contractor, or nursing home operator pays. For a worker with a third-party claim, the workers’ compensation insurer has a lien on the recovery (MCL 418.827). How that lien is negotiated changes what you take home, and Medicare, Medicaid, and ERISA plan liens are negotiated the same way.

## What it costs

Michigan caps contingency fees in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery (MCR 8.121). Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted from the recovery first; the one-third fee is calculated on the remaining amount.

The agreement is in writing. Read how costs are handled if the case is lost, because that is where firms differ. Costs include filing fees, records, experts, and depositions. A promise that “you pay nothing unless we win” should say whether that includes costs, and whether “win” means the same thing as “settle.”

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in Rochester Hills?** Three years for most claims. But several deadlines are shorter: - one year to notify your no-fault insurer after a crash - 120 days to notify the City, the Road Commission, or a school district of a defect - 120 days for a road-defect notice on M-59 or for a road or building defect at Oakland University - six months for other claims against the university - two years to sue a bar - a 182-day notice and a two-year limit for malpractice

**A Sheriff’s deputy crashed into me in Rochester Hills. Do I claim against the City?** No. The Sheriff’s Office polices Rochester Hills under contract, so the claim is against Oakland County, under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity.

**I fell on the Oakland University campus. Who do I claim against?** The State of Michigan, in the Court of Claims, with written notice within 120 days if a defect in a building or campus road caused the fall. Governmental immunity limits these claims, and the notice period is much shorter than the ordinary three years. A fall at a private apartment complex near campus is an ordinary claim against the owner.

**I was hurt on the Paint Creek Trail. Do I have a case?** If a car hit you at a road crossing, yes, as in any crash. If the trail surface or another trail user caused the injury, the claim is harder. Governmental immunity and the Recreational Land Use Act can limit it. Photograph the location and report it the same day. **[ATTORNEY CHECK]**

**The other driver’s insurer offered its policy limit. Should I take it?** Not before checking your own underinsured motorist coverage and getting your insurer’s written consent to settle. Accepting without consent can forfeit the UIM claim.

**What does a Rochester Hills personal injury attorney charge?** Michigan caps the fee in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery, with no fee if there is no recovery. Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted only from a recovery.

**Where would my Rochester Hills case be filed?** - **Up to $25,000:** the 52-3 District Court on Barclay Circle. - **Over $25,000:** the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court on Telegraph Road in Pontiac. - **Against MDOT or Oakland University:** the Court of Claims.

**If I was partly at fault, do I still have a claim?** Yes, unless you were more than 50 percent at fault, which bars pain-and-suffering damages. Below that, your recovery is reduced by your share, and at exactly 50 percent you recover half. No-fault benefits are paid regardless of fault.

**Do I need a lawyer whose office is in Rochester Hills?** No. What matters is that the lawyer knows the courts your case will be in and the agencies, insurers, and property owners involved, and will meet you where you are.
