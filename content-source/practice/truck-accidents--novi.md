## Novi, MI Truck Accident Lawyer

Novi’s heavy trucks travel three kinds of roads.

**The freeways.**

- **I-96** crosses the city east to west. It is one of the state’s main freight routes between Detroit and western Michigan, with interchanges at Novi Road and Beck Road, and at Wixom Road on the Wixom side.
- **I-275 and M-5** run along the east side to the interchange with I-96 and I-696 at the Farmington Hills line, where four freeways meet.

**The warehouse roads.** Warehouses, distribution centers, and light industrial buildings sit in business parks across the city. Tractor-trailers turn into and out of their driveways and sometimes stage on the shoulders waiting for dock appointments.

**The retail and event roads.** Novi Road, Grand River, and Twelve Mile carry:

- box trucks and tractor-trailers supplying Twelve Oaks Mall, the stores, and the restaurants
- freight for trade shows and events at the Suburban Collection Showplace
- construction trucks for new subdivisions, commercial buildings, and road work
- garbage, recycling, and delivery trucks in the subdivisions

In 2024, according to the Michigan State Police, 2,081 heavy trucks and buses were involved in crashes in Oakland County. Statewide, 110 people were killed and 3,613 injured in heavy truck and bus crashes. In Novi, 549 of the city’s 1,984 crashes, more than a quarter, were on the interstates.

A truck crash is not a car crash with a larger vehicle:

- **The rules are federal.** The driver and the carrier are governed by federal safety regulations, and a violation is evidence of negligence.
- **The insurance is larger.** The carrier must carry $750,000 or more in insurance.
- **The records expire.** The driver’s electronic logs and the truck’s engine data, the records that prove the case, can be lawfully discarded within months.
- **The other side moves fast.** The carrier’s insurer will have an investigator on the case within days.

## Where Novi truck crashes happen

**I-96 and the four-freeway interchange.** The freeway crashes are:

- **Backups:** rear-end and underride crashes in the stop-and-go near the I-96, I-696, I-275, and M-5 interchange and at the Novi Road exit.
- **Lane changes:** trucks moving into a car in the blind spot on the right side of the cab, where exits and entrances come close together.
- **Merges:** crashes on the ramps, where a loaded truck needs time and distance to reach freeway speed.
- **Weather:** jackknife and loss-of-control crashes on snow and ice. The federal rules require a driver to reduce speed or stop in hazardous conditions (49 CFR 392.14).
- **Work zones:** crashes in lane closures during freeway rebuilds and resurfacing.

A car that strikes the side or rear of a trailer can go under it. That is an **underride** crash, which is often fatal or catastrophic. Trailers must carry rear impact guards that meet federal standards (49 CFR 393.86). A missing, damaged, or substandard guard is evidence against the carrier and the trailer’s owner. In a work zone, the contractor can share responsibility.

**The warehouse driveways.** At the warehouses, the crashes happen where trucks meet the road:

- **Wide turns:** a tractor-trailer swings left before turning right into a driveway, or right before turning left, and a car moves into the gap.
- **Slow turns across traffic:** a truck turning left into a driveway blocks oncoming lanes, and a driver coming over a rise or around a curve cannot stop.
- **Pulling out:** a truck leaving a driveway cannot see past parked or staged trucks, or misjudges the speed of oncoming traffic.
- **Staged trucks:** a truck parked on the shoulder waiting for a dock appointment, without warning devices, is struck from behind (49 CFR 392.22).
- **Night conspicuity:** a trailer crossing the road at night without working lights or reflective tape cannot be seen (49 CFR 393.11).

When the warehouse’s driveway design, its truck-staging practice, or its dock scheduling put trucks in the road, the warehouse operator or property owner can share responsibility with the carrier.

**At the loading dock.** Workers, drivers, and visitors are hurt at the docks themselves:

- a truck pulls away while a forklift or worker is still in the trailer
- a trailer creeps or rolls because it was not chocked or restrained
- a driver is struck in the yard by a yard tractor or a backing truck
- a load shifts and falls when the doors are opened

A warehouse worker hurt at the dock usually has a workers’ compensation claim against the employer and no suit against it (MCL 418.131). A suit can still be brought against a third party, such as the carrier whose driver pulled away, the company that loaded the trailer, or the maker of a failed dock restraint. See our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).

**The mall, the stores, and the expo center.** Box trucks and tractor-trailers supply the mall and the stores along Novi Road and Grand River, often early in the morning. During trade-show move-in and move-out, freight trucks queue near the expo center. The crashes involve:

- trucks backing into and out of service drives and loading areas
- wide right turns at Novi Road, Twelve Mile, and Grand River that catch a car on the inside of the turn
- pedestrians struck in parking lots by trucks heading to the loading areas

On designated Michigan routes, heavy trucks can weigh up to 164,000 pounds, about twice the federal Interstate limit. They need far more distance to stop.

**Construction trucks.** New subdivisions, commercial buildings, and road projects bring dump trucks, cement mixers, and equipment haulers onto the mile roads and residential streets. Their crashes involve trucks pulling out of sites, trucks backing without a spotter, and mud, gravel, or debris left on the road.

**Garbage trucks and delivery vans in the subdivisions.** Garbage and recycling trucks, package vans, and furniture trucks work the subdivision streets. The crashes are backing crashes, crashes with vehicles stopped in the lane, and children and pedestrians struck by a truck pulling away from the curb. Delivery vans are often operated by contracted delivery companies rather than by the retailer named on the package. The van’s operator, its insurer, and the contract between them have to be identified.

**Lost and spilled loads.** Cargo, equipment, construction debris, or gravel that falls from a truck is a violation of the federal cargo securement rules (49 CFR 393.100 and following) and of Michigan law (MCL 257.720). The carrier and the company that loaded the truck can both be responsible.

**Whose road it is.** Novi is a city, and the road owner can be any of three agencies:

- **MDOT** for I-96, I-275, and M-5. A road-defect claim against the State goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404; *Champine v Department of Transportation*, Mich 2022).
- **The Road Commission for Oakland County** for Haggerty, Eight Mile, Napier, Pontiac Trail, and Fourteen Mile. Written notice is due within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404).
- **The City of Novi** for the other public roads, including Grand River, Beck, and Wixom Road, and the subdivision streets. Written notice is due within 120 days.

## Who is responsible, and who pays

**The carrier, not just the driver.** The motor carrier is responsible for its drivers’ compliance with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations. It is liable for a driver’s negligence in the course of employment. Michigan applies the federal rules to intrastate carriers (MCL 480.11a). A local hauler shuttling trailers between a Novi warehouse and a rail yard, or a construction trucker that never leaves Oakland County, is held to the same standards as an interstate carrier.

These are the rules that decide truck cases most often:

| Rule | 49 CFR | What it means for the case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hours of service | Part 395 | 11 hours driving in a 14-hour window after 10 hours off; 60/70-hour weekly limits; recorded on an electronic logging device. Some short-haul drivers are exempt from logs but not from the limits. Time spent waiting at a dock counts toward the 14-hour window. |
| Driver qualification | Part 391 | CDL, medical certificate, driving record, background check. Negligent hiring and retention are claims against the carrier. |
| Post-crash testing | Part 382 | Alcohol within 8 hours and drugs within 32 hours after a fatal crash, or after an injury or tow-away crash with a citation. |
| Inspection and maintenance | Part 396 | Pre-trip inspections, defect reports, maintenance records. Brake, tire, lighting, and reflective-tape cases are maintenance cases. |
| Cargo securement | 393.100–393.136 | Loads must be contained or secured so they cannot shift or fall. A spilled load is evidence of a violation. |
| Equipment: rear impact guards, lighting, reflectors | Part 393 | Underride guards (393.86) and conspicuity marking. A trailer that cannot be seen at night or lacks a working guard is a defective trailer. |
| Stopped vehicles; hazardous conditions; phones | 392.22, 392.14, 392.82 | Warning devices for a stopped truck; reduce speed or stop in snow, ice, and fog; no handheld phone use. |
| Minimum insurance | Part 387 | $750,000 general freight; $1 million oil; $5 million hazardous materials. |

**The owner of the truck or trailer.** In Michigan, a vehicle’s owner is liable for its negligent operation by someone driving with the owner’s consent (MCL 257.401). At a warehouse, the tractor, the trailer, and the driver often belong to different companies, and a trailer may sit at a dock for days before a different carrier hauls it. Each may be a defendant, although a company in the business of renting or leasing vehicles is generally shielded from owner liability by federal law (49 USC 30106).

**Michigan no-fault still applies to you.** Your own auto insurer pays these benefits regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107):

- your medical bills
- 85 percent of lost wages, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- replacement services
- attendant care

Written notice is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)).

Your claim against the driver and the carrier is for pain and suffering and for losses beyond no-fault, including lost wages above the PIP cap. It requires a threshold injury (MCL 500.3135) and must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). Michigan’s comparative fault rule is modified, not pure:

- **More than 50 percent at fault:** you cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages.
- **50 percent or less:** your share reduces them (MCL 500.3135(2)(b); MCL 600.2959).

The noneconomic damages caps apply to medical malpractice and to product liability claims, such as a claim against the maker of a failed part (MCL 600.1483, 600.2946a), not to the claim against the driver and carrier.

The difference from a car case is the money available: the carrier’s $750,000-plus policy, often with excess coverage above it. If the carrier’s coverage still falls short, your own underinsured motorist coverage may add to it. Your insurer’s written consent is needed before you accept the carrier’s limit. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

**Other defendants.**

- the shipper or broker that loaded the truck or hired an unsafe carrier
- the warehouse operator or property owner whose driveway, staging, or dock practice contributed
- the general contractor on a construction site whose trucks were involved
- the maintenance contractor
- the manufacturer of a failed component, a defective guard, or a failed dock restraint ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability))
- for a government truck (MDOT, the Road Commission, the City of Novi, or a bus a school district runs itself), the agency, under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405)

**If you were the truck driver.** Workers’ compensation pays your benefits, and your employer usually cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). If you were injured in your employer’s truck, no-fault benefits generally come from the insurer of that truck (MCL 500.3114(3)). They are coordinated with comp, which is paid first (MCL 500.3109(1)). You can also sue a third party, such as another driver or a warehouse whose dock or yard caused the injury. See our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).

## The evidence, and how fast it disappears

Carriers must keep two kinds of records for a limited time:

- hours-of-service records, for six months (49 CFR 395.8)
- driver inspection reports, for three months (49 CFR 396.11)

After that, the regulations no longer require them. Other evidence goes faster:

- warehouse gate, yard, and dock cameras, store and mall cameras, and dash cameras overwrite in days to weeks
- warehouse gate logs and dock appointment records may be kept only as long as the warehouse’s own policy requires
- the truck’s engine control module and event data recorder can be cleared when the truck is repaired and returned to service
- a spilled load is swept up within hours
- a damaged rear guard can be replaced before anyone measures it

The first act in a Novi truck case is a **preservation letter**. It goes to the carrier, its insurer, the tractor and trailer owners, and the shipper or broker. Where the crash happened at a warehouse driveway, a dock, a store’s loading area, or a construction site, it also goes to the warehouse operator, the property owner, or the contractor. It demands:

- the ELD and engine data
- dash camera footage
- the driver’s qualification file and post-crash test results
- dispatch records, bills of lading, gate logs, and dock appointment records
- maintenance files
- the truck and trailer themselves, for inspection of the brakes, lights, load securement, and rear guard

Once a party knows a claim is coming, destroying that evidence exposes it to spoliation sanctions.

## What to do after a Novi truck crash

1. **Call 911 and get medical care the same day.** Henry Ford Providence Novi Hospital, on Grand River near I-96 and Beck Road, is a Level II adult trauma center. The most severe injuries may go to a Level I center such as Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak.
2. **Photograph the truck:** the cab and trailer, the USDOT number and carrier name on the door, both plates and the trailer number if it differs, the load and anything that fell from it, the rear guard and reflective tape, and the driveway, dock, or construction entrance if the truck was entering or leaving one.
3. **Get the police report.** Crashes on city roads are worked by the Novi Police Department. Freeway crashes may be worked by the Michigan State Police. A serious crash may produce a separate commercial vehicle inspection report from the State Police.
4. **Do not speak to the carrier’s insurer or its investigator.**
5. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** promptly. If you were hurt at work, report the injury to your employer as well.
6. **Call a lawyer within days,** so the preservation letter goes out before the records expire and the truck is repaired.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many truck accidents happen in Novi?** The State’s county profile does not break out truck crashes by city. Countywide, 2,081 heavy trucks and buses were involved in Oakland County crashes in 2024. Statewide, 15,888 crashes involved a heavy truck or bus, and 110 people were killed. In Novi, more than a quarter of all crashes were on the interstates.

**A semi turning into a warehouse driveway swung wide and I hit it. Whose fault is that?** It depends on the facts. A truck driver must make a wide turn safely and signal it, and a driver who turns across lanes without checking can be at fault. A driver who passes a truck that is signaling a turn can carry fault too. The trailer’s lights and markings, and the truck’s dash camera, are often the deciding evidence.

**I hit a truck parked on the shoulder outside a warehouse at night. Is the truck at fault?** It may be. A truck stopped on the shoulder must put out warning devices, and a trailer must have working lights and reflective tape. If the warehouse’s practice was to have trucks wait on the road, the warehouse may share responsibility.

**I was hurt at work when a truck pulled away from the dock. Can I sue?** Usually not your employer, which owes workers’ compensation. You may have a suit against the carrier whose driver pulled away, the company that owns the trailer, or the maker of a dock restraint that failed.

**My car went under the back of a trailer on I-96. Is that the truck’s fault?** It may be partly or wholly. Trailers must carry rear impact guards built to federal standards. A guard that is missing, bent, or too high to stop a car is evidence against the carrier and the trailer’s owner. The trailer has to be inspected before it is repaired.

**Is there a cap on what I can recover from a trucking company?** Not on pain and suffering against the driver or carrier. Michigan’s damages caps apply to medical malpractice and product liability claims. In practice, the limit is the insurance available: the carrier’s policy, any excess policy, and your own underinsured coverage.

**Why does the lawyer need to act within days?** Because the records expire. Hours-of-service logs must be kept for six months and inspection reports for three. Warehouse camera footage and gate logs may last only days or weeks. The truck’s electronic data and the trailer’s damage can disappear when the truck is repaired.
