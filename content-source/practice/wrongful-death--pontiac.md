## Pontiac, MI Wrongful Death Lawyer

When someone dies in Pontiac because another person or company was careless, Michigan’s Wrongful Death Act (MCL 600.2922) gives the family a claim, but not in the way many families expect. The claim belongs to the estate, not to the spouse or children directly. It must be brought by a personal representative appointed by the probate court. The recovery is divided among family members by a judge. And the deadline depends on what caused the death: a crash on the Woodward Loop, Telegraph, or M-59, a pedestrian struck on a city street, a truck at a warehouse gate, a driver leaving a downtown bar, a death on a job site, a hospital error, a fire or carbon monoxide in a rental home. Some of those deadlines are 120 days. This page explains how the process works for a Pontiac family and what to do in the first weeks.

## How a Pontiac wrongful death claim works

**The estate.** Only the personal representative can bring the claim (MCL 600.2922(2)). Where the person lived in Pontiac, the estate is opened in the Oakland County Probate Court, which sits in the city on the county campus at 1200 N. Telegraph Road. Where the person lived elsewhere in Michigan but died in Pontiac, the estate is opened in the county where they lived; the suit can still be filed in Oakland County. If there is a will naming a personal representative, that person petitions for appointment; if not, a family member, usually the spouse or an adult child, petitions under Michigan’s priority rules (MCL 700.3203). The court issues letters of authority, and the personal representative then has standing to hire a lawyer for the estate and file. We file the petition as the first legal step.

**Notice to the family.** Within 30 days after filing suit, the personal representative must notify in writing every person who may share in the recovery: the spouse, children, descendants, parents, grandparents, brothers and sisters, the children of the deceased’s spouse, and anyone named in the will (MCL 600.2922(2)–(4)). The list is not limited to dependents; a parent, sibling, or adult child who was not financially dependent can still share for the loss of companionship. The Act does not rank them in a fixed order of priority; the court divides the recovery according to each person’s loss. A person named in the will may share in the recovery, but does not bring the claim; only the personal representative does.

**The court.** The suit is the injury claim the person would have had, brought by the estate against whoever caused the death. Death claims are filed in circuit court, not the 50th District Court on Saginaw Street, which hears civil cases only up to $25,000. For a death in Pontiac that is usually the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court (Oakland County Circuit Court) at 1200 N. Telegraph Road, on the same campus as the Probate Court. A case against an out-of-state trucking company or driver may be filed in or removed to federal court in Detroit. Claims against the State go to the Court of Claims.

**The damages** (MCL 600.2922(6)): medical and hospital expenses from the injury to the death; funeral and burial expenses; the person’s **conscious pain and suffering** between the injury and death; **loss of financial support** over a working life; and **loss of society and companionship** for each family member. Michigan places no cap on these except in medical malpractice cases. Michigan does not award punitive damages in wrongful death cases. No-fault survivor’s loss benefits, described below, are a separate insurance claim, not part of these damages.

**Comparative fault.** If the person who died was partly at fault, the damages are reduced by that share. If their fault was greater than the combined fault of everyone else, the estate cannot recover noneconomic damages, but can still recover reduced economic damages (MCL 600.2959). In an auto case, the no-fault act bars noneconomic damages at more than 50 percent fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)).

**The distribution hearing.** After a settlement or verdict, the personal representative proposes a division among the eligible family members, with notice to each. If they agree, the court approves it; if not, the court decides based on each person’s relationship and loss (MCL 600.2922(6)). Fees and costs are approved at the same hearing. Because a judge divides the money, no family member should sign anything with an insurer; a release signed by one relative does not settle the estate’s claim.

## The causes of Pontiac wrongful death claims

**Crashes** on the Woodward Loop, Telegraph, M-59, I-75, and the city streets, including pedestrians and cyclists struck on streets where many residents walk. A wrongful death claim after a crash does not step outside no-fault: the claim against the at-fault driver still requires a death, which meets the threshold (MCL 500.3135(1)), and no-fault benefits are paid alongside it. The at-fault driver’s liability insurer pays, subject to policy limits that default to $250,000/$500,000 unless the driver chose lower limits, down to the $50,000/$100,000 minimum (MCL 500.3009(1)); the deceased’s own uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage, umbrella policies, and the policy of an employer whose driver was working are checked as well. When the at-fault driver had no insurance, the family’s recovery for loss of companionship depends on UM coverage in the household. Separately, **no-fault survivor’s loss benefits** (MCL 500.3108) are paid regardless of fault by the deceased’s own insurer, or the insurer that would have paid their PIP, for up to three years after the death: the economic support the person provided, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year, $20 a day in replacement services, and funeral expenses up to the policy limit, at least $1,750 and up to $5,000 (MCL 500.3107(1)(a)). Survivor’s loss stops at the cap, and support above it is part of the wrongful death claim. If the person who died had no auto policy and no household policy applied, survivor’s loss may be paid by the insurer of the vehicle involved or by the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3115, 500.3172). Written notice is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)); do not wait for the lawsuit. See our [Pontiac car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents/pontiac) and, for pedestrian deaths, our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents).

**Truck and motorcycle deaths.** A fatal crash with a tractor-trailer on M-59, I-75, or near a warehouse gate, including an underride crash into the side or rear of a trailer, brings the federal trucking rules, the carrier’s policy of $750,000 or more, and evidence that can be overwritten within days; see our [Pontiac truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/pontiac). A fatal motorcycle crash follows the rider priority rules; see our [Pontiac motorcycle accident page](/personal-injury/motorcycle-accidents/pontiac).

**Drunk driving.** The driver, and under the dram shop act (MCL 436.1801) the bar, restaurant, or store that served a visibly intoxicated person or a minor. The dram shop claim has its own rules: written notice to every defendant within 120 days after the family hires a lawyer, the intoxicated driver must be named and kept in the suit, and the suit must be filed within **two years**, not three. The criminal case proceeds separately; the family’s claim does not wait for it. See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents). **[ATTORNEY CHECK]**

**Deaths on the job.** Pontiac’s plants, warehouses and distribution centers, and the road and building construction around downtown are where workplace deaths happen. When a worker dies on the job, the employer owes workers’ compensation death benefits to dependents, up to 500 weeks plus burial (MCL 418.321, 418.345), and generally cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). There are two important openings:

- **A third party** whose negligence caused the death can be sued in a wrongful death action: a contractor or subcontractor on the site, a property owner, a trucking company, or the manufacturer of a machine with a missing guard or a defective safety device ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability)).
- **The employer itself** can be sued only under the narrow intentional tort exception, where the employer had actual knowledge that an injury was certain to occur and willfully disregarded it (MCL 418.131(1)).

The MIOSHA investigation file is one of the first records requested. See our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).

**Hospital and medical negligence** at McLaren Oakland or Trinity Health Oakland, both in the city, or the clinics and medical offices around them: a wrongful death claim with the malpractice rules attached, a 182-day Notice of Intent (MCL 600.2912b), an Affidavit of Merit (MCL 600.2912d), a two-year period (MCL 600.5805(8), 600.5838a), and the cap (MCL 600.1483). See our [Pontiac medical malpractice page](/personal-injury/medical-malpractice/pontiac).

**Housing, nursing homes, and premises.** Many Pontiac households rent: about half, in the most recent Census estimates (ACS 2020–2024). Deaths from fires, carbon monoxide, failed smoke detectors, and falls in rental homes and apartments follow premises law and, for tenants, MCL 554.139; a death caused by a defect inside a Pontiac Housing Commission unit is usually barred by governmental immunity, because courts have held that a public housing unit is not a public building open to the public (*Griffin v City of Detroit*, Mich App 1989); a defect in a part of the building open to the public may be different, with written notice to the Commission within 120 days (MCL 691.1406). See our [tenant injury page](/personal-injury/tenant-injuries). A death from neglect in a nursing home may be ordinary negligence or malpractice, which changes the deadline (see our [nursing home abuse page](/personal-injury/nursing-home-abuse)).

**Deaths involving a government agency.** Pontiac is a city, so the road defendant can be the State, the City, or the Road Commission. Woodward, the Loop, Telegraph, M-59, and I-75 are MDOT’s, with written notice of a road defect filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404); most city streets are the City of Pontiac’s and some roads are the Road Commission for Oakland County’s, each with written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404). The City answers for its sidewalks and buildings with the same 120-day notice (MCL 691.1402a, 691.1406). A crash with a Sheriff’s patrol car is a claim against Oakland County, which polices Pontiac under contract; a fire engine answering a Pontiac call is usually Waterford Township’s, because the Waterford Regional Fire Department serves the city under contract; and SMART and City vehicles fall under the motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405). An individual government employee is liable only for gross negligence (MCL 691.1407(2)). **[ATTORNEY CHECK]**

When the injury and the death happen on the same day, the difference between “from the injury” and “from the death” does not matter. When a person lingers for weeks or months, the ordinary period may already be running before the estate exists, which is what the savings provision is for. It adds only a limited period and courts apply it strictly. Open the estate and file inside the ordinary deadline.

## What the family should do in the first weeks

1. **Get the death certificate and request the Oakland County Medical Examiner’s report**, along with the fire department and ambulance run sheets, the Sheriff’s report, and the hospital record. The Medical Examiner’s office is on the county campus in Pontiac. If the family’s customs call for prompt burial, tell the Medical Examiner’s office at once, or call us.
2. **Keep everything**: the vehicle, the phone, the helmet, the work gear, the medication bottles. Do not authorize salvage of a vehicle until a lawyer has seen it.
3. **Notify the no-fault insurer in writing** if a vehicle was involved; survivor’s loss benefits begin on the claim, not on the lawsuit.
4. **Do not give a recorded statement** to any insurer and do not sign any document, however it is described. An insurer offering to “cover the funeral” in exchange for a signature may be settling the claim for the price of a funeral.
5. **Open the estate.** The probate petition is the first legal step; the wrongful death suit cannot be filed until letters issue.
6. **Call within days** if the death involved a road, a sidewalk, a government vehicle, a bar, a trucking company, or an employer. Those notice periods are short, and the trucking records expire.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who can file a wrongful death lawsuit in Pontiac?** Only the personal representative of the estate, appointed by the Oakland County Probate Court for a Pontiac resident. A spouse, child, or parent can be appointed, but must be appointed before the claim can be filed.

**Who receives the money?** The spouse, children, descendants, parents, grandparents, siblings, the children of the deceased’s spouse, and anyone named in the will may share, whether or not they were financially dependent. The probate court divides the recovery according to each person’s loss after notice to each of them.

**How long do we have?** Generally three years from the injury that caused the death for crash, fall, and premises claims, with a limited extension for the estate; two years with a 182-day notice for medical malpractice; two years for a dram shop claim; one year to claim no-fault survivor’s loss benefits; and 120 days to notify the City or the Road Commission of a defect.

**My husband died in a crash. Are survivor’s loss benefits part of the lawsuit?** No. Survivor’s loss is paid by the no-fault insurer regardless of fault, up to a monthly cap, for up to three years. The wrongful death claim against the at-fault driver is separate and covers the losses survivor’s loss does not, including support above the cap and the family’s loss of companionship.

**A bad road contributed to my son’s crash. Do we sue the City of Pontiac?** It depends on the road. Woodward, the Loop, Telegraph, M-59, and I-75 are MDOT’s, with written notice of a road defect filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days.

**The driver who killed my mother had no insurance. Is there anything we can recover?** Yes. Survivor’s loss benefits and funeral expenses are paid by the no-fault system regardless, through the family’s insurer, the insurer of the vehicle involved, or the Assigned Claims Plan. The claim for loss of companionship depends on uninsured motorist coverage in the household; without it, it is worth what the driver can personally pay.

**Can we get punitive damages?** Michigan does not award punitive damages in wrongful death cases. The family recovers the losses the Act lists, including loss of companionship, which has no cap outside medical malpractice.

**Is there a lawsuit if the driver was charged with a crime?** Yes. The criminal case and the family’s claim are separate. The family does not have to wait for the criminal case, and the civil deadlines keep running.
