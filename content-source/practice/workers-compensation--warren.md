## Warren Workers’ Compensation Lawyer

A worker hurt on the job in Warren is owed benefits under Michigan’s Workers’ Disability Compensation Act regardless of fault: medical care in full, 80 percent of after-tax wages while unable to work, vocational rehabilitation, and scheduled payments for the loss of a body part. What differs in Warren is who is on the other side. General Motors at the Technical Center and Stellantis at Warren Truck Assembly and Warren Stamping are self-insured and administer claims through their own adjusters and plant medical departments. The supplier, tool-and-die, and logistics shops along Mound, Van Dyke, Groesbeck, and Stephens are smaller employers, many of which staff their lines through temporary agencies. And civilian employees at the Detroit Arsenal are not under Michigan’s Act at all.

## Where Warren workers are hurt, and who handles the claim

**The assembly and stamping plants.** Repetitive-motion injuries to shoulders, wrists, and backs; crush and amputation injuries from presses, robotics, and conveyors; forklift strikes; falls; and burns. The automakers are self-insured, and the first 28 days of care are typically at plant medical. Union contracts add grievance rights and sickness-and-accident benefits that interact with comp (MCL 418.354).

**The Tech Center and engineering campuses.** Engineering, design, and test work: vehicle-testing and shop injuries, falls on icy lots and stairs, and repetitive strain. Contractors’ and vendors’ employees on the campus are covered by their own employer’s comp, and the campus owner can be a third-party defendant; see our [Warren slip and fall page](/personal-injury/slip-and-fall/warren).

**Supplier shops and staffing agencies.** Tool-and-die, machining, plating, and logistics operations, where a large share of the workforce may be placed by a staffing agency. A placed worker is the agency’s employee for comp, and the host plant may also be protected by the exclusive-remedy bar under Michigan’s economic-reality test, which affects whether the plant can be sued as a third party. Some small shops carry no workers’ compensation insurance. An uninsured employer loses the exclusive-remedy protection: the worker may claim comp benefits and may also sue the employer in circuit court, and officers can be personally liable (MCL 418.641, 418.647).

**The Detroit Arsenal.** Civilian federal employees at the Arsenal are covered by the Federal Employees’ Compensation Act, administered by the U.S. Department of Labor, not by Michigan’s Act: written notice on Form CA-1 or CA-2 within 30 days, a claim within three years, and no lawsuit against the United States for the injury (5 USC 8101 et seq.). Employees of private defense contractors on the base are under Michigan’s Act through their own employer.

**City, schools, and the hospital.** Warren Police, Fire, and Public Works; the school districts serving the city; and Henry Ford Warren Hospital: public-safety injuries, patient-handling injuries, and assaults. Public employers have pension and duty-disability provisions that run alongside comp, and police officers and firefighters have a statutory presumption for certain heart and respiratory conditions (MCL 418.405).

**Truck drivers and delivery workers.** Drivers hurt in crashes on Mound, Van Dyke, and I-696 while working, which brings Michigan no-fault into the claim (see below). Independent-contractor drivers may have no comp claim; see our [Warren truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/warren).

## What a Warren worker is owed

**Medical care** in full, for as long as needed, with no deductible or co-pay (MCL 418.315), including attendant care at home. For the first 28 days the employer chooses the doctor; after that you may treat with your own physician on notice to the employer (MCL 418.315(1)).

**Wage loss** at 80 percent of your after-tax average weekly wage, computed from the highest 39 of the 52 weeks before the injury (MCL 418.351, 418.371), capped at the state maximum. For plant workers, overtime and shift premiums count toward the average weekly wage, so the pay records matter. Partial wage loss is paid if you return at lower pay (MCL 418.361(1)), and the seven-day waiting period is paid from day one if the disability lasts two weeks (MCL 418.311).

**Specific loss** for a body part or its use, regardless of wage loss, which matters for the amputation injuries presses cause (MCL 418.361(2)); total and permanent disability for at least 800 weeks (MCL 418.361(3)). **Vocational rehabilitation** up to 52 weeks, extendable to 104 (MCL 418.319). **Death benefits** to dependents for 500 weeks plus burial (MCL 418.321, 418.345), alongside the third-party wrongful death claim on our [Warren wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death/warren).

## Where Warren claims are denied, and how they are won

- **“Disability”** means a limitation in wage-earning capacity in work suitable to your qualifications and training (MCL 418.301(4)); a written job-search log within your restrictions is the evidence (MCL 418.301(5)).
- **Pre-existing conditions and wear and tear.** Years on a line produce degenerative shoulders, backs, and knees. The question is whether work contributed to or aggravated the condition in a significant manner (MCL 418.301(2)); the pre- and post-injury records decide it.
- **Reasonable employment.** Refusing a job within your restrictions without good reason can end benefits (MCL 418.301(9)). Plants often offer restricted work; whether it is real and within your limits is a common dispute.
- **The IME** (MCL 418.385): the insurer’s doctor says you have recovered; your treating physician’s records are the answer, which is why the 28-day rule matters.
- **Late notice.** 90 days from the injury or from when you knew it was work-related (MCL 418.381(1)). A verbal report to a team leader counts in law but is hard to prove; put it in writing and keep a copy.
- **Retaliation** for filing is prohibited (MCL 418.301(13)) and is a separate lawsuit.

## The hearing, and the lawsuit comp does not bar

When an employer or insurer denies a claim or stops paying, the worker files an Application for Mediation or Hearing (Form WC-104A) with the Workers’ Disability Compensation Agency. Disputed claims go to mediation and then to a magistrate, with appeal to the Michigan Compensation Appellate Commission. Macomb County claims are heard at the agency’s Detroit hearing site at Cadillac Place, 3026 W. Grand Boulevard, Suite 3-700. A contested claim often takes a year or more, which is why many disputed claims resolve by **redemption**, a lump-sum settlement approved by the magistrate that closes the claim, including future medical care (MCL 418.835). Do not sign one without advice.

Workers’ compensation is your exclusive remedy against an insured employer (MCL 418.131), with one narrow exception: an employer that specifically intended an injury, meaning it had actual knowledge that an injury was certain to occur and willfully disregarded that knowledge (MCL 418.131(1)). You can sue anyone else whose negligence caused the injury (MCL 418.827):

- the other driver in a crash on the job, with workers’ compensation primary and no-fault paying the difference (MCL 500.3109(1)); see our [Warren car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents/warren);
- a general contractor or another subcontractor on a construction or plant-retooling job;
- the manufacturer of a press, robot, conveyor, or lift with a missing guard or a defective safety device ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability));
- a property owner whose hazard caused a fall;
- a dog owner, for a delivery driver ([Warren dog bite page](/personal-injury/dog-bites/warren)).

The comp insurer has a lien on that recovery (MCL 418.827(5)), reduced by its share of fees and costs; negotiating it is part of the work. The third-party suit is filed in the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court in Mount Clemens within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). A permanent injury may also qualify for Social Security Disability, coordinated with comp under the federal offset (42 USC 424a); see our [Social Security Disability page](/disability/social-security-disability).

## What to do after a work injury in Warren

1. **Report it in writing the same day**, to your supervisor and to HR or plant medical, and keep a copy.
2. **Get treatment**, at plant medical or the employer’s clinic for the first 28 days if required, and tell every provider exactly how the injury happened at work.
3. **Count 28 days.** On day 29, if you are not satisfied with the employer’s doctor, see your own and notify the employer in writing.
4. **If you were placed by a staffing agency, report to the agency too**, and keep the names of both employers.
5. **Keep your pay stubs**, including overtime and shift premiums, and, once released with restrictions, a written log of every job you apply for.
6. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the adjuster without advice, and do not sign a redemption agreement without understanding that it ends your medical coverage.
7. **If a machine, a contractor, a driver, or a property hazard was involved, call within days.** The third-party evidence, including the machine itself, disappears on the same schedule as in any injury case.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to report a work injury in Warren?** Ninety days from the injury, or from when you knew it was work-related, to notify your employer, and two years to file a claim. Report it in writing the day it happens.

**I was hurt at a plant. Is plant medical my only option?** For the first 28 days, generally yes. After that you may treat with your own physician on written notice. Your union contract may also provide sickness-and-accident benefits and grievance rights that run alongside the comp claim.

**I was placed at the plant by a staffing agency. Who pays my comp?** Usually the staffing agency’s insurer. Whether you can also sue the plant depends on how much control it had over your work; if it counts as your employer too, comp is your only remedy against it.

**I work at the Arsenal. Does Michigan workers’ comp apply?** Not if you are a federal civilian employee. Your claim is under the Federal Employees’ Compensation Act, with notice within 30 days. If you work for a private contractor on the base, Michigan’s Act applies through your employer.

**Can I sue my employer?** Not if it is insured, except in the narrow case where it knew an injury was certain to occur and willfully disregarded that. You can sue any other company or person whose negligence caused the injury, including the maker of a defective machine, and an uninsured employer directly.

**I was in a crash on I-696 while driving for work. Who pays?** Workers’ compensation pays your medical and wage-loss benefits first, your no-fault insurer pays what comp does not cover, and the at-fault driver is liable for pain and suffering if you have a threshold injury.

**What does a Warren workers’ comp lawyer cost?** Nothing up front. Fees are limited by state rule to a percentage of the benefits recovered and must be approved by the magistrate. You owe nothing if no benefits are recovered.
