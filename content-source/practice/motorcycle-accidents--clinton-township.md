## Clinton Township Motorcycle Accident Lawyer

A motorcycle crash in Clinton Township is governed by a rule many riders learn only after they are hurt: under Michigan no-fault, a motorcycle is not a “motor vehicle.” That definition decides who pays your medical bills, how much they pay, whether you have a no-fault claim at all, and how the insurer will argue the helmet question. ## Where Clinton Township motorcycle crashes happen

**Hall Road’s crossovers.** Along the township’s northern edge, drivers turn left by passing the intersection and using a median crossover, then merging into the far lanes. A driver leaving a crossover, looking for a gap among cars, often misses a motorcycle in that gap, and a rider in the near lane is the one most often hit. A driver leaving a crossover must yield to traffic in the lanes being entered. Hall at Hayes and Hall at Garfield ranked fifth and ninth in Macomb County for crashes in 2024.

**The left turn on Gratiot and the county roads.** A driver turning left across oncoming traffic, misjudging the speed of a motorcycle or not seeing it at all, is the recurring account in rider cases. Under Michigan law a driver must yield to oncoming traffic before turning left (MCL 257.650). On Gratiot, drivers turn left into strip centers, restaurants, and dealerships between signals; on Romeo Plank, Garfield, Cass, Harper, and the mile roads, they turn left at intersections where a rider’s speed is easy to misjudge against the background of a straight, flat road.

**Groesbeck’s angled intersections.** Groesbeck crosses the mile roads at an angle, so a driver entering from a mile road has to look back over a shoulder to see Groesbeck traffic. A rider’s narrow profile is easy to miss at that angle, and the industrial driveways and trucks along Groesbeck add blind spots. See our [Clinton Township truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/clinton-township).

**I-94.** On the freeway at the township’s southeastern corner, drivers merging and changing lanes, and rear-end crashes in congestion, are the rider’s risks; a rear-end impact a car absorbs throws a rider.

**The road itself.** A single-vehicle crash caused by a pothole, a broken concrete joint, a pavement drop-off, a steel plate left by road work, or gravel is a claim against the agency with jurisdiction under the highway exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1402). In Clinton Township that means one of two agencies, and never the township:

- **MDOT** for Hall Road (M-59), Gratiot (M-3), Groesbeck (M-97), and I-94: written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **The Macomb County Department of Roads** for Romeo Plank, Garfield, Hayes, Harper, Moravian, Cass, Utica Road, Metro Parkway, the mile roads, and most subdivision streets: written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).

A defect that was readily apparent to an ordinary observer for 30 days or more is conclusively presumed known to the agency (MCL 691.1403). Notice to the wrong agency is not notice, and a notice sent to Clinton Township for a road defect is sent to an agency that does not own the road.

**Bars and restaurants.** 31 percent of Michigan’s 2024 rider deaths involved alcohol. A bar or restaurant on Hall Road or Gratiot that over-served the driver who hit a rider can be liable under the dram shop act: written notice within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, and suit within two years (MCL 436.1801(3)). See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

## Who pays a Clinton Township rider’s medical bills

**If a car or truck was involved, you have no-fault benefits, but not from your motorcycle insurer.** Under MCL 500.3114(5), your Personal Injury Protection benefits come, in order, from:

1. the insurer of the owner or registrant of the motor vehicle involved;
2. the insurer of that vehicle’s operator;
3. the motor vehicle insurer of the motorcycle’s operator;
4. the motor vehicle insurer of the motorcycle’s owner or registrant.

“Involved” does not require contact; a car that pulled out of a crossover and forced you down is involved even if it never touched you. If no insurer in that chain exists, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan pays (MCL 500.3172), with its $250,000 medical cap, unless you owned the motorcycle and it was uninsured (MCL 500.3113(b)).

The benefits are the ones a driver would get under MCL 500.3107: medical expenses up to the PIP limit on the policy that pays, 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year, $20 a day in replacement services, attendant care, and mileage. Written notice is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

The first medical limit is set by a policy you did not choose. Since the 2019 reform, the PIP medical level on the policy first in priority pays first; if the driver who hit you chose $250,000, that policy pays up to $250,000, and a lower-priority policy with more coverage, such as your own car policy, may then pay the rest.

**If no car or truck was involved, no-fault does not apply.** A single-vehicle crash on a pothole, a broken joint, or gravel produces no PIP claim. Your bills go to your health insurance and any medical coverage you bought on the motorcycle policy, and your injury claim is against whoever caused the hazard. A rider hit by a car, by contrast, usually has a full PIP claim, and which insurer pays it is the first question.

**Your motorcycle.** Covered by your own collision coverage, or by the mini tort claim against the at-fault driver for up to $3,000 (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)), filed in the 41B District Court if the insurer will not pay it.

## Your claim against the driver who hit you

For pain and suffering and economic losses beyond no-fault, you sue the at-fault driver if your injury is a serious impairment of body function, permanent serious disfigurement, or death (MCL 500.3135(1)). Motorcycle injuries clear that threshold more often than car injuries: fractures, road rash requiring grafts, torn ligaments, [brain injuries](/personal-injury/brain-injury), and [spinal cord injuries](/personal-injury/spinal-cord-injury). You also recover excess economic damages above the PIP limit that applied to you, and wage loss above the no-fault cap or beyond three years (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)). You have three years to sue (MCL 600.5805(2)); a fatal crash is brought by the estate (see our [Clinton Township wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death)).

**What the claim is worth depends on the other driver’s policy.** Liability limits on Hall Road and Gratiot range from the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)) to large policies, and some drivers carry nothing. The recovery then turns on your own **uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage**, which must be on the motorcycle policy; many car policies exclude the insured while riding an owned motorcycle. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Comparative fault is the fight.** Above 50 percent at fault you recover no noneconomic damages; below that your award is reduced by your share, and at exactly 50 percent you recover half (MCL 500.3135(2)(b), 600.2959). Economic damages beyond no-fault are reduced by your share as well. Insurers argue speed, lane position, lane splitting (illegal in Michigan), and “came out of nowhere.” The police report, witnesses, the other driver’s phone records, store and gas-station cameras along Hall Road and Gratiot, and the rider’s own camera answer it.

## The helmet question

Since 2012, Michigan has let riders 21 and older ride without a helmet if they carry at least $20,000 in first-party medical coverage and have either held a motorcycle endorsement for two years or passed an approved safety course (MCL 257.658). Riders under 21 must wear a helmet. If you met those conditions, riding helmetless was legal, so it was not a traffic violation. In a head-injury case the insurer will argue a helmet would have reduced the injury, which is a damages argument for the jury, not a bar; in a leg or shoulder case it is irrelevant.

## What to do after a Clinton Township motorcycle crash

1. **Get medical care the same day.** Henry Ford Macomb Hospital in the township and McLaren Macomb in Mount Clemens are both Level II trauma centers.
2. **Get the police report** from the Clinton Township Police Department, or from the State Police on I-94. If a car caused the crash without contact, make sure the report says so. **[VERIFY: report request process]**
3. **Identify every vehicle involved**, including the one that never touched you.
4. **Photograph the bike, the scene, the crossover or intersection, the road surface, your gear, and your injuries**, and note every business with a camera facing the road. Do not repair or sell the bike.
5. **Read your motorcycle policy for first-party medical, UM/UIM, and collision coverage**, and your car policy’s PIP level.
6. **Apply for no-fault benefits with the right insurer within the first week**, and do not give a recorded statement to the other driver’s insurer.
7. **If a road defect was involved, call within days.** The 120-day county notice is already running.

## Frequently asked questions

**A car pulled out of a Hall Road crossover and I went down. Do I have a claim?** Yes. A driver leaving a crossover must yield to traffic in the lanes being entered, and the car is “involved” under no-fault whether or not it touched you, so its owner’s insurer is first in line for your medical bills. Get the plate and witnesses before the car is gone.

**A driver turned left in front of me on Gratiot. Who is at fault?** Usually the turning driver, who must yield to oncoming traffic. The insurer will argue your speed; the police diagram, store cameras, and witnesses answer it.

**Are motorcycles covered by no-fault?** A motorcycle is not a “motor vehicle” under the no-fault act, so a rider who crashes alone has no PIP claim. A rider hit by a car or truck does: from that vehicle’s insurer first, under the priority rule.

**I wasn’t wearing a helmet. Can I still recover?** Yes, if you were 21 or older with the required $20,000 medical coverage and either two years of endorsement or a safety course. The insurer can argue a helmet would have reduced a head injury, which goes to damages, not to whether you have a claim.

**I went down on a pothole on Romeo Plank. Do I notify Clinton Township?** No. Romeo Plank is a Macomb County road. Written notice goes to the Macomb County Department of Roads within 120 days, stating the exact location, the defect, the injury, and the witnesses.

**How long do I have?** One year to notify the responsible no-fault insurer, three years to sue the at-fault driver, two years to sue a bar, 120 days for a notice to the Department of Roads, and 120 days to file notice of a road-defect claim against the State.
