## Canton, MI Workers’ Compensation Lawyer

A worker hurt on the job in Canton is owed benefits under Michigan’s Workers’ Disability Compensation Act regardless of fault: medical care in full, 80 percent of after-tax wages while unable to work, vocational rehabilitation, and scheduled payments for the loss of a body part. ## Where Canton workers are hurt, and who handles the claim

**Warehouses, distribution centers, and staffing agencies.** Order picking, loading, and forklift work at the warehouses and distribution centers along Michigan Avenue, Haggerty, and Lotz: lifting and back injuries, dock-plate and trailer falls, pallet-jack and forklift injuries, conveyor injuries, crush injuries at the dock, and repetitive strain from scanning and packing at production rates. A large share of this workforce may be placed by a staffing agency. A placed worker is the agency’s employee for comp, and the host company may also be protected by the exclusive-remedy bar under Michigan’s economic-reality test, which affects whether it can be sued as a third party. Some small employers carry no workers’ compensation insurance. An uninsured employer loses the exclusive-remedy protection: the worker may claim comp benefits and may also sue the employer in circuit court, and officers can be personally liable (MCL 418.641, 418.647).

**Delivery drivers.** Drivers who load vans at a delivery station and carry packages to Canton’s subdivisions are hurt lifting, slipping on porches and steps, bitten by dogs, and in crashes. Whose comp covers them depends on who employs them: a retailer’s own driver, a delivery contractor’s driver, or a gig driver classified as an independent contractor, who may have no comp coverage at all. Under the Act, a worker paid as a contractor is still an employee for comp purposes unless he or she maintains a separate business, holds himself or herself out to the public to perform the work, or is an employer subject to the Act (MCL 418.161(1)(n)).

**Construction.** Framing, roofing, concrete, and site work in new subdivisions and commercial projects: falls from roofs, ladders, and scaffolds, trench and equipment injuries, and struck-by injuries. A worker for a subcontractor has comp through that subcontractor, and the general contractor or another trade whose negligence caused the injury can be sued as a third party. A general contractor can also be liable for comp benefits to an uninsured subcontractor’s employees (MCL 418.171).

**Nursing homes, assisted living, and home care.** Canton has no hospital of its own, but its nursing homes, assisted living communities, and home-care agencies employ aides who suffer patient-handling injuries to backs and shoulders from lifting and transfers; needlesticks and exposures; slips on wet floors; and assaults by patients or residents. Health care workers’ injuries are often cumulative, and the employer’s insurer may argue the condition is degenerative rather than work-related (see “wear and tear” below). Home-care aides injured in a client’s home have a comp claim against their agency and, where a hazard in the home caused the injury, possibly a premises claim against the homeowner.

**Ford Road stores and restaurants.** Stock, warehouse, and kitchen work: lifting injuries, burns, cuts, and falls, in high-turnover jobs where injuries often go unreported past the 90 days. Some small employers carry no comp insurance; see the uninsured-employer rule above.

**The township and the schools.** Canton Public Safety police and fire, township public works and parks staff, and Plymouth-Canton, Wayne-Westland, and Van Buren school employees: public-safety injuries, custodial and maintenance injuries, and assaults. Public employers have pension and duty-disability provisions that run alongside comp, and police officers and firefighters have a statutory presumption for certain heart and respiratory conditions (MCL 418.405).

**Truck drivers.** Drivers hurt in crashes on I-275, Michigan Avenue, and Ford Road while working, which brings Michigan no-fault into the claim (see below).

## What a Canton worker is owed

**Medical care** in full, for as long as needed, with no deductible or co-pay (MCL 418.315), including attendant care at home. For the first 28 days the employer chooses the doctor; after that you may treat with your own physician on notice to the employer (MCL 418.315(1)).

**Wage loss** at 80 percent of your after-tax average weekly wage, computed from the highest 39 of the 52 weeks before the injury (MCL 418.351, 418.371), capped at the state maximum. Overtime and shift premiums count toward the average weekly wage, so the pay records matter. Partial wage loss is paid if you return at lower pay (MCL 418.361(1)), and the seven-day waiting period is paid from day one if the disability lasts two weeks (MCL 418.311).

**Specific loss** for a body part or its use, regardless of wage loss, which matters for the amputation injuries machinery causes (MCL 418.361(2)); total and permanent disability for at least 800 weeks (MCL 418.361(3)). **Vocational rehabilitation** up to 52 weeks, extendable to 104 (MCL 418.319). **Death benefits** to dependents for 500 weeks plus burial (MCL 418.321, 418.345), alongside the third-party wrongful death claim on our [Canton wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death/canton).

## Where Canton claims are denied, and how they are won

- **“Disability”** means a limitation in wage-earning capacity in work suitable to your qualifications and training (MCL 418.301(4)); a written job-search log within your restrictions is the evidence (MCL 418.301(5)).
- **Pre-existing conditions and wear and tear.** Years on a warehouse floor, a delivery route, a roof, or a nursing unit produce degenerative shoulders, backs, and knees. The question is whether work contributed to or aggravated the condition in a significant manner (MCL 418.301(2)); the pre- and post-injury records decide it.
- **Reasonable employment.** Refusing a job within your restrictions without good reason can end benefits (MCL 418.301(9)). Employers often offer restricted work; whether it is real and within your limits is a common dispute.
- **The IME** (MCL 418.385): the insurer’s doctor says you have recovered; your treating physician’s records are the answer, which is why the 28-day rule matters.
- **Late notice.** 90 days from the injury or from when you knew it was work-related (MCL 418.381(1)). A verbal report to a supervisor can satisfy the law but is hard to prove; put it in writing and keep a copy.
- **Retaliation** for filing is prohibited (MCL 418.301(13)) and is a separate lawsuit.

## The hearing, and the lawsuit comp does not bar

When an employer or insurer denies a claim or stops paying, the worker files an Application for Mediation or Hearing (Form WC-104A) with the Workers’ Disability Compensation Agency. Disputed claims go to mediation and then to a magistrate, with appeal to the Michigan Compensation Appellate Commission. Wayne County claims are heard at the agency’s Detroit office in Cadillac Place. A contested claim often takes a year or more, which is why many disputed claims resolve by **redemption**, a lump-sum settlement approved by the magistrate that closes the claim, including future medical care (MCL 418.835). Do not sign one without advice.

Workers’ compensation is your exclusive remedy against an insured employer (MCL 418.131), with one narrow exception: an employer that specifically intended an injury, meaning it had actual knowledge that an injury was certain to occur and willfully disregarded that knowledge (MCL 418.131(1)). You can sue anyone else whose negligence caused the injury (MCL 418.827):

- the other driver in a crash on the job, with workers’ compensation primary and no-fault paying the difference (MCL 500.3109(1)); see our [Canton car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents/canton);
- a general contractor or another trade on a construction site;
- the manufacturer of a conveyor, forklift, scissor lift, ladder, or nail gun with a defect or a missing guard ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability));
- a property owner whose hazard caused a fall, including a homeowner’s icy steps for a delivery driver or a client’s home for a home-care aide ([Canton slip and fall page](/personal-injury/slip-and-fall/canton));
- a dog owner, for a delivery driver ([Canton dog bite page](/personal-injury/dog-bites/canton)).

The comp insurer has a lien on that recovery (MCL 418.827(5)), reduced by its share of fees and costs; negotiating it is part of the work. The third-party suit is filed in the Third Judicial Circuit Court in Detroit within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). A permanent injury may also qualify for Social Security Disability, coordinated with comp under the federal offset (42 USC 424a); see our [Social Security Disability page](/disability/social-security-disability).

## What to do after a work injury in Canton

1. **Report it in writing the same day**, to your supervisor and to HR, and keep a copy. A delivery driver should report to the company that employs them, not only the one whose packages they carry.
2. **Get treatment**, at the employer’s designated clinic for the first 28 days if required, and tell every provider exactly how the injury happened at work.
3. **Count 28 days.** On day 29, if you are not satisfied with the employer’s doctor, see your own and notify the employer in writing.
4. **If you were placed by a staffing agency, report to the agency too**, and keep the names of both companies.
5. **Keep your pay stubs**, including overtime and shift premiums, and, once released with restrictions, a written log of every job you apply for.
6. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the adjuster without advice, and do not sign a redemption agreement without understanding that it ends your medical coverage.
7. **If a machine, a contractor, a driver, or a property hazard was involved, call within days.** The third-party evidence, including the machine itself, disappears on the same schedule as in any injury case.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to report a work injury in Canton?** Ninety days from the injury, or from when you knew it was work-related, to notify your employer, and two years to file a claim. Report it in writing the day it happens.

**I work at a Michigan Avenue warehouse through a staffing agency. Who pays my comp?** Usually the staffing agency’s insurer. Whether you can also sue the warehouse depends on how much control it had over your work; if it counts as your employer too, comp is your only remedy against it.

**I deliver packages in Canton and the company says I’m an independent contractor. Do I have comp?** Possibly. Michigan looks at the work, not the label: you are treated as an employee unless you maintain your own business, offer the service to the public, or are yourself an employer covered by the Act. If you are in fact an employee, you have a comp claim, and if you were hurt by someone else’s negligence, a third-party claim too.

**I was hurt working for a subcontractor that has no insurance. Who pays?** The general contractor may be responsible for your comp benefits, and you may also be able to sue the uninsured subcontractor directly.

**Can I sue my employer?** Not if it is insured, except in the narrow case where it knew an injury was certain to occur and willfully disregarded that. You can sue any other company or person whose negligence caused the injury, including the maker of a defective machine, and an uninsured employer directly.

**I was in a crash on I-275 while driving for work. Who pays?** Workers’ compensation pays your medical and wage-loss benefits first, your no-fault insurer pays what comp does not cover, and the at-fault driver is liable for pain and suffering if you have a threshold injury.

**What does a Canton workers’ comp lawyer cost?** Nothing up front. Fees are limited by state rule to a percentage of the benefits recovered and must be approved by the magistrate. You owe nothing if no benefits are recovered.
