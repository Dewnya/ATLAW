## Dearborn Workers’ Compensation Lawyer

A worker hurt on the job in Dearborn is owed benefits under Michigan’s Workers’ Disability Compensation Act regardless of fault: medical care in full, 80 percent of after-tax wages while unable to work, retraining, and scheduled payments for the loss of a body part. What differs in Dearborn is who is on the other side. Ford, the Rouge complex’s contractors, the steel works, and Corewell Health are self-insured and administer claims through their own adjusters and clinics. The restaurants, bakeries, groceries, and shops along Michigan Avenue and Warren are small employers, some of whom carry no coverage, which changes the claim entirely. **Michigan Avenue and Warren.** Restaurants, bakeries, groceries, auto repair, and retail: burns, cuts, lifting injuries, and falls in high-turnover workforces where injuries often go unreported past the 90 days. ## What a Dearborn worker is owed

**Medical care** in full, for as long as needed, with no deductible or co-pay (MCL 418.315), including attendant care at home (56 hours a week for family-provided care). For the first 28 days the employer chooses the doctor; after that you may treat with your own physician on notice to the employer (MCL 418.315(1)).

**Wage loss** at 80 percent of your after-tax average weekly wage, computed from the highest 39 of the 52 weeks before the injury (MCL 418.351, 418.371), capped at the state maximum; partial wage loss if you return at lower pay (418.361(1)); seven-day waiting period, paid from day one if the disability lasts two weeks (418.311).

**Specific loss** for a body part or its use, regardless of wage loss (418.361(2)); total and permanent disability for at least 800 weeks (418.361(3)). **Vocational rehabilitation** up to 52 weeks, extendable to 104 (418.319). **Death benefits** to dependents for 500 weeks plus burial (418.321, 418.345), alongside the third-party wrongful death claim on our [Dearborn wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death/dearborn).

## Where Dearborn claims are denied, and how they are won

- **“Disability”** means a limitation in wage-earning capacity in work suitable to your qualifications and training (MCL 418.301(4)); a written job-search log within your restrictions is the evidence (418.301(5)).
- **Pre-existing conditions**: the aggravation must be medically distinguishable and work must have contributed in a significant manner (418.301(2)); the pre- and post-injury records decide it.
- **Reasonable employment**: refusing a job within your restrictions without good reason can end benefits (418.301(9)).
- **The IME** (418.385): the insurer’s doctor says you have recovered; your treating physician’s records are the answer, which is why the 28-day rule matters.
- **Late notice**: 90 days from the injury or from when you knew it was work-related (418.381(1)). A verbal report to a shift lead counts in law but is hard to prove; put it in writing, in English, and keep a copy.
- **Retaliation** for filing is prohibited (418.301(13)) and is a separate lawsuit.

## The hearing, and the lawsuit comp does not bar

When a Dearborn employer or insurer denies a claim or stops paying, the worker files an Application for Mediation or Hearing (Form WC-104A) with the Workers’ Disability Compensation Agency; disputed claims go to mediation and then to a magistrate at the agency’s Detroit hearing office in Cadillac Place, 3026 West Grand Boulevard **[VERIFY]**, with appeal to the Michigan Compensation Appellate Commission. A contested claim typically takes 12 to 18 months, which is why most disputed claims resolve by **redemption**, a lump-sum settlement approved by the magistrate that closes the claim, including future medical care; do not sign one without advice.

Workers’ compensation is your exclusive remedy against an insured employer (MCL 418.131), but you can sue anyone else whose negligence caused the injury (418.827): the other driver in a crash on the job (with workers’ comp primary and no-fault paying the difference under MCL 500.3109(1); see our [Dearborn car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents/dearborn) and [Dearborn truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/dearborn)), the general contractor or another subcontractor at the Rouge or a construction site, the manufacturer of a defective press or lift ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability)), a property owner whose hazard caused a fall ([Dearborn slip and fall](/personal-injury/slip-and-fall/dearborn)), or a dog owner for a delivery driver ([Dearborn dog bite](/personal-injury/dog-bites/dearborn)). The comp insurer has a lien on that recovery (418.827(5)), reduced by its share of fees and costs; negotiating it is part of the work. The third-party suit is filed in the Third Judicial Circuit Court within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). **[ATTORNEY CHECK: 3109 coordination]** A permanent injury may also qualify for Social Security Disability, coordinated with comp under the federal offset; see our [Dearborn Social Security Disability page](/disability/social-security-disability/dearborn).

## What to do after a work injury in Dearborn

1. **Report it in writing the same day**, to your supervisor and to HR, and keep a copy. If you write it in Arabic, write it in English too, or have us do it.
2. **Get treatment**, at the employer’s clinic for the first 28 days if required, and tell every provider exactly how the injury happened at work.
3. **Count 28 days.** On day 29, if you are not satisfied with the employer’s doctor, see your own and notify the employer in writing.
4. **Ask whether your employer is insured.** If it is not, call immediately; the remedies are different and broader.
5. **Keep your pay records** and, once released with restrictions, a written log of every job you apply for.
6. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the adjuster without advice, in any language, and do not sign a redemption agreement without understanding that it ends your medical coverage.
7. **If another company’s employee, a driver, a product, or a property hazard was involved, call within days**; the third-party evidence disappears on the same schedule as in any injury case.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to report a work injury in Dearborn?** Ninety days from the injury, or from when you knew it was work-related, to notify your employer, and two years to file a claim. Report it in writing the day it happens.

**I was hurt at the Rouge. Is the plant’s medical department my only option?** For the first 28 days, generally yes. After that you may treat with your own physician on written notice. Your union contract may also provide sickness-and-accident benefits and grievance rights that run alongside the comp claim.

**My employer on Warren Avenue has no workers’ comp insurance. What happens?** You may still claim comp benefits, and because the employer is uninsured it loses the protection that normally bars a lawsuit: you can sue it in circuit court for your full damages, and its officers can be personally liable. Call immediately.

**I was in a crash on I-94 while driving for work. Who pays?** Workers’ compensation pays your medical and wage-loss benefits first, your no-fault insurer pays what comp does not cover, and the at-fault driver is liable for pain and suffering if you have a threshold injury. All three claims run at once.

**Can I sue my employer?** Not if it is insured, except for a deliberate intent to injure. You can sue any other company or person whose negligence caused the injury, and an uninsured employer directly.

**What is a redemption, and should I take one?** A lump-sum settlement approved by a magistrate that closes the claim, including future medical care. Whether to accept depends on what treatment you will need. Do not sign one without advice.

**I reported my injury in Arabic to my supervisor. Does that count?** Notice to the employer in any language counts if it was given and understood, but proving it is the problem. Put it in writing in English as well, and keep a copy; we will do it for you. **[ATTORNEY CHECK]**

**What does a Dearborn workers’ comp lawyer cost?** Nothing up front. Fees are limited by state rule to a percentage of the benefits recovered and must be approved by the magistrate. You owe nothing if no benefits are recovered. **[CONFIRM]**
