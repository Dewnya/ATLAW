## Where Royal Oak crashes happen

**Woodward and the Michigan lefts.** Woodward has no direct left turns at most intersections. A driver who wants to turn left goes past the cross street, makes a U-turn at a turnaround in the median, and comes back. The crashes are:

- **at the turnaround:** a driver pulls out of it into oncoming lanes without yielding, or a truck too long to clear it blocks lanes
- **in the crossover:** drivers crossing the median at a signal
- **lane changes:** drivers moving left to reach a turnaround (MCL 257.642(1)(a))
- **signals:** red-light crashes at Eleven, Twelve, Thirteen, and Fourteen Mile
- **straightaways:** rear-end crashes at the signals and speed-related crashes between them

Woodward belongs to MDOT. Each August, the Woodward Dream Cruise brings crowds, slow traffic, and pedestrians to the corridor. **[VERIFY]**

**Downtown and the bars.** Main Street, Washington, Fourth, Fifth, Lafayette, and Eleven Mile hold dozens of bars, restaurants, a concert venue, and a cinema, with parking structures between them. The crashes are:

- pedestrians struck at the downtown crossings late at night
- drivers backing out of angled and parallel spaces
- crashes in and around the parking structures
- drunk-driving crashes as the bars close

A driver who caused a crash after being served while visibly intoxicated brings a second defendant into the case: the bar. See the dram shop section below and our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents).

**I-696 and I-75.** The Walter P. Reuther Freeway runs along the city’s southern edge. Its crashes come from:

- merges and lane changes at the Woodward, Campbell, and I-75 interchanges
- rear-end crashes in rush-hour backups
- trucks bound for the Troy and Madison Heights industrial areas

See our [Royal Oak truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/royal-oak).

**The mile roads and the neighborhoods.** Royal Oak’s grid of bungalow neighborhoods produces:

- left-turn crashes at the signals on the mile roads and on Crooks, Coolidge, Campbell, Main, and Rochester Road (MCL 257.650)
- crashes at uncontrolled and stop-sign intersections on residential streets
- cyclist and pedestrian crashes near schools and parks

The hospital campus at Thirteen Mile and Woodward adds ambulance traffic and visitors unfamiliar with the roads. See our [bicycle accident page](/personal-injury/bicycle-accidents).

**Whose road it is: the State, the county, or the City.** Royal Oak is a city, and road ownership is split three ways:

- **MDOT** owns Woodward, I-696, and I-75. A road-defect claim against MDOT goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404; *Champine v Department of Transportation*, Mich 2022).
- **The Road Commission for Oakland County** owns Ten, Twelve, and Fourteen Mile.
- **The City of Royal Oak** owns the other streets, including Eleven and Thirteen Mile, Crooks, Coolidge, Campbell, Main, and Rochester Road.

A claim against the Road Commission or the City requires written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404). A pothole or other defect in the road can make the agency a defendant alongside the other driver; on State and county roads, signals, signs, and sight obstructions generally fall outside the highway exception (*Nawrocki v Macomb County Road Commission*, Mich 2000). The notice must go to the agency that actually has jurisdiction over that stretch of road, or it is no notice. For a pedestrian who falls, the City’s sidewalk duty matters (MCL 691.1402a). Royal Oak’s code makes the adjacent owner keep the sidewalk in repair (City Code § 650-5), but that duty to the City does not by itself make the owner liable to a pedestrian (*Levendoski v Geisenhaver*, Mich 1965). See our [sidewalk injury page](/personal-injury/sidewalk-injuries).

## Fault is the case

In a rear-end crash, fault is rarely disputed. At a Woodward turnaround, a downtown crossing, or a mile-road left turn, it often is, and the other driver’s insurer may try to assign you half or more of the fault. That share matters (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)):

- **More than 50 percent at fault:** you lose your claim for pain and suffering.
- **50 percent or less:** your recovery is reduced by your share. At exactly 50 percent, you recover half.

The evidence that decides fault is perishable:

- the Royal Oak Police Department crash report and diagram, or the State Police report on the freeway
- **signal timing data** from MDOT, the Road Commission, or the City, including the turnaround signals
- cameras at downtown businesses, parking structures, gas stations, and homes, many overwritten within days
- the vehicles’ **event data recorders**, which store speed, braking, and throttle in the seconds before impact
- the other driver’s phone records, for a distracted-driving claim (see our [distracted driving page](/personal-injury/distracted-driving-accidents))
- bar receipts and the bar’s own cameras, if alcohol was involved
- witnesses, including other pedestrians downtown

We move promptly to request the camera footage, signal data, bar records, and vehicle data.

## Who pays after a Royal Oak crash

**Your no-fault (PIP) claim.** Your own auto insurer pays these benefits regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107):

- your medical expenses, up to the PIP level you chose
- 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- replacement services at $20 a day
- attendant care

If you have no policy, a resident relative’s policy pays (MCL 500.3114). If none exists, an occupant claims from the insurer of the vehicle they were in (MCL 500.3114(4)), and then the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3172), capped at $250,000 for medical benefits. A pedestrian or cyclist hit by a car, with no household policy, claims through the Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3115)

The insurer must receive written notice within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). It must pay each bill within 30 days of reasonable proof, or owe 12 percent interest (MCL 500.3142). Since the 2019 reform, PIP medical coverage is no longer unlimited unless you chose it, so the level on your declarations page matters. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance) and our [Michigan no-fault guide](/resources/michigan-no-fault-guide).

**Your claim against the at-fault driver.** For pain and suffering, you need a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment is an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life. It does not have to be permanent (MCL 500.3135(5); *McCormick v Carrier*, 2010).

You can also recover economic losses that no-fault does not cover (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)):

- lost wages beyond the three years or above the monthly cap
- medical bills above your chosen PIP level

The suit must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The mini tort claim recovers up to $3,000 in vehicle damage not covered by your policy (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)). If the at-fault driver’s insurer will not pay it, the claim is filed in the 44th District Court.

**Drunk drivers and the dram shop claim.** A bar, restaurant, or store that serves a visibly intoxicated person, or a minor, is liable for the injuries that person causes under Michigan’s dram shop act (MCL 436.1801). For a crash after a night downtown, the bar’s commercial policy is often larger than the drunk driver’s auto policy. The claim has its own rules:

- Written notice must go to the business within 120 days after you hire a lawyer.
- The drunk driver must be named and kept in the suit.
- The suit must be filed within two years, not three (MCL 436.1801(3), (4)).
- “Visibly intoxicated” has to be shown by what the server could see, so the evidence matters: point-of-sale records, the bar’s cameras, ride-share and parking records, and witnesses who saw the driver served.

That evidence is overwritten or forgotten within weeks. See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**Two policies, in the right order.** In a serious crash, the at-fault driver’s liability limit may be less than the claim is worth. If you carry **underinsured motorist coverage**, your own insurer pays the difference up to your UIM limit. Follow these steps in order:

1. The at-fault driver’s insurer offers its limit.
2. You notify your own insurer and ask for its written consent to settle.
3. Only then do you accept the limit and sign the release.
4. The UIM claim then proceeds.

Accepting without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under most policies. Against an uninsured driver, or in a hit-and-run, the claim for pain and suffering depends on **uninsured motorist coverage**, whose notice terms are often short. Two rules cut the other way:

- An owner who was required to insure a car and did not is generally barred from PIP benefits (MCL 500.3113(b)).
- A driver who was operating their own uninsured vehicle cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, whoever was at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(c)).

See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Rideshare.** Downtown Royal Oak is a rideshare pickup zone on weekend nights. A crash with a rideshare driver involves the driver’s own policy and the platform’s commercial coverage. Which policy applies depends on whether the app was on and whether a ride was in progress. See our [rideshare accident page](/personal-injury/rideshare-accidents).

**Police cars, fire trucks, and buses.** All of these fall under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405):

- Royal Oak Police and Fire vehicles
- SMART buses on Woodward and the mile roads and at the Royal Oak Transit Center
- Road Commission trucks

Royal Oak Schools’ buses are run by a private contractor, so a claim over one of them is an ordinary negligence claim with no immunity question. Dash camera and dispatch records are overwritten on short schedules, so we request them by FOIA promptly. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

## What to do after a Royal Oak crash

1. **Call 911 and get the police report.** Michigan requires a report for any crash with injury or $1,000 in damage (MCL 257.622). Crashes in the city are worked by the Royal Oak Police Department. Freeway crashes may be worked by the Michigan State Police.
2. **Photograph the scene.** Get the vehicles, plates, the other driver’s license and insurance card, the turnaround or crossing, the signals, and the lane markings. Note every business, parking structure, and home with a camera facing the scene. Footage is kept for days, not weeks.
3. **If you think the other driver had been drinking,** tell the officer, note where the driver said they had been, and write down the names of witnesses.
4. **Get medical care the same day.** Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital, on Thirteen Mile at Woodward, is Oakland County’s Level I adult trauma center.
5. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** promptly.
6. **Read your declarations page** for PIP level, UM/UIM, umbrella, and collision coverage.
7. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the other driver’s insurer, sign a release, or accept a policy-limits offer before your UIM insurer consents.
8. **Call a lawyer within days** if fault is disputed, alcohol was involved, the other driver’s limit looks too low, or the road played a part. The camera footage and bar records are the first evidence lost, and the 120-day dram shop and road-defect notices are already running.

## The courts

- **Claims up to $25,000,** including mini tort claims, go to the 44th District Court at 400 E. Eleven Mile Road, which serves Royal Oak and Berkley.
- **Injury suits over $25,000** go to the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac, with a jury drawn from all of Oakland County.
- **Defect claims against MDOT** go to the Court of Claims.
- **A fatal crash** is brought by the estate through the Oakland County Probate Court. See our [Royal Oak wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death).

## Frequently asked questions

**A driver pulled out of a Woodward turnaround and hit me. Who is at fault?** Usually the driver leaving the turnaround, who must yield to traffic on Woodward. These crashes are often disputed, though. The turnaround’s signal or sign, the sight lines, and your speed all matter. Photograph the turnaround and get the witnesses. Woodward belongs to MDOT.

**The driver who hit me had just left a bar downtown. Can I sue the bar?** Possibly. A bar that served a visibly intoxicated person can be liable for the injuries that person causes. Written notice is due within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, and suit within two years. The bar’s sales records and cameras are the evidence, and they disappear quickly.

**I was hit crossing Main Street at night. Who pays my medical bills?** Your own household’s auto policy pays first. If you have none, the Assigned Claims Plan pays. (MCL 500.3115.) Your pain-and-suffering claim is against the driver, reduced by any share of fault the evidence assigns you. That is why the signal phase and the downtown cameras matter.

**How many crashes are there in Royal Oak?** The State Police recorded 1,992 crashes in Royal Oak in 2024. They killed 5 people and seriously injured 28, and 57 involved alcohol.

**A pothole caused my crash.** **Do I file against the City of Royal Oak?** It depends on the road: - **Woodward, I-696, and I-75** belong to MDOT, with notice filed in the Court of Claims within 120 days. - **Ten, Twelve, and Fourteen Mile belong to the Road Commission for Oakland County**, with written notice within 120 days. - **Most other streets** belong to the City, with the same 120 days.

**Where would my Royal Oak case be filed?** Mini tort claims and claims up to $25,000 go to the 44th District Court on Eleven Mile. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court in Pontiac.

**How long do I have?** - One year to give your no-fault insurer written notice. - 120 days to send a dram shop notice, and two years to sue a bar. - Three years to sue the at-fault driver. - 120 days for a notice to the City or the Road Commission. - 120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT.
