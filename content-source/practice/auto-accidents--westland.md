## Where Westland crashes happen

**Ford Road.** From Venoy to Hix, Ford Road has signals at Venoy, Wayne Road, Newburgh, and Hix, and dozens of driveways between them. The crashes are:

- left-turn crashes at the signals, where a driver turning across five or seven lanes misjudges oncoming traffic (MCL 257.650)
- red-light and late-yellow crashes
- rear-end crashes in the turn lanes and at the signals
- crashes at retail and restaurant driveways, where a driver leaving a lot must yield to traffic on the road (MCL 257.652)
- pedestrians struck crossing between the apartment complexes and the stores, often away from a signal

Ford Road belongs to MDOT. See our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents).

**Wayne Road, Warren Road, and the mall.** The Westland Shopping Center sits near Wayne and Warren, with City Hall nearby on Warren Road and the 18th District Court on Ford Road. The crashes are intersection crashes at Wayne and Warren and at Wayne and Ford, crashes at the mall entrances, and parking-lot crashes and pedestrian strikes in the lots. A crash in a parking lot is still a no-fault claim for your medical bills.

**Michigan Avenue.** US-12 along the southern edge carries through traffic and trucks. The crashes are left turns into businesses, rear-end crashes, and crashes at the signals. Michigan Avenue belongs to MDOT.

**The county-road grid.** Cherry Hill, Joy, Palmer, and Warren, and Inkster, Middlebelt, Merriman, Venoy, Newburgh, and Hix, carry commuters between the subdivisions and the arterials. The crashes are intersection crashes at the signals and four-way stops, school-zone crashes, and crashes with pedestrians and cyclists near the schools and parks.

**Hines Drive.** The county parkway along the Rouge winds through Hines Park, with curves, trail crossings, and low spots that flood. The crashes are run-off-road and head-on crashes on the curves, and cyclists and pedestrians struck where the trail meets the drive. Hines Drive belongs to Wayne County. See our [bicycle accident page](/personal-injury/bicycle-accidents).

**Whose road it is: the State, the county, or the City.** Westland is a city, and road ownership is split three ways:

- **MDOT** owns Ford Road and Michigan Avenue. A road-defect claim against MDOT goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **Wayne County** owns Wayne Road, Hines Drive, and other major roads.
- **The City of Westland** owns the subdivision streets.

A claim against the county or the City requires written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404). A pothole or other defect in the road can make the agency a defendant alongside the other driver; on State and county roads, signals, signs, and sight obstructions generally fall outside the highway exception (Nawrocki v Macomb County Road Commission, Mich 2000). The notice must go to the agency that actually has jurisdiction over that stretch of road, or it is no notice.

## Fault is the case

In a rear-end crash, fault is rarely disputed. At a Ford Road left turn or a retail driveway, it often is, and the other driver’s insurer may try to assign you half or more of the fault. That share matters (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)):

- **More than 50 percent at fault:** you lose your claim for pain and suffering.
- **50 percent or less:** your recovery is reduced by your share. At exactly 50 percent, you recover half.

The evidence that decides fault is perishable:

- the Westland Police Department crash report and diagram
- **signal timing data** from MDOT or Wayne County
- cameras at the stores, restaurants, gas stations, and apartment complexes along Ford Road and Wayne Road, many overwritten within days
- the vehicles’ **event data recorders**, which store speed, braking, and throttle in the seconds before impact
- the other driver’s phone records, for a distracted-driving claim (see our [distracted driving page](/personal-injury/distracted-driving-accidents))
- witnesses, including other drivers stopped at the signal

We move promptly to request the camera footage, signal data, and vehicle data.

## Who pays after a Westland crash

**Your no-fault (PIP) claim.** Your own auto insurer pays these benefits regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107):

- your medical expenses, up to the PIP level you chose
- 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- replacement services at $20 a day
- attendant care

If you have no policy, a resident relative’s policy pays (MCL 500.3114). If none exists, an occupant claims from the insurer of the vehicle they were in (MCL 500.3114(4)), and then the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3172), capped at $250,000 for medical benefits. A pedestrian or cyclist hit by a car, with no household policy, claims through the Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3115).

The insurer must receive written notice within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). It must pay each bill within 30 days of reasonable proof, or owe 12 percent interest (MCL 500.3142). Since the 2019 reform, PIP medical coverage is no longer unlimited unless you chose it, so the level on your declarations page matters. Providers’ charges are also subject to the fee schedule in MCL 500.3157. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

**If you had no insurance.** A person who owned a car and was required to insure it, but did not, is generally barred from PIP benefits for their own injuries (MCL 500.3113(b)). A driver who was operating their own uninsured vehicle cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, whoever was at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(c)). A passenger or pedestrian who did not own an uninsured car may still be covered through a household policy or the Assigned Claims Plan.

**Your claim against the at-fault driver.** For pain and suffering, you need a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment is an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life. It does not have to be permanent (MCL 500.3135(5); *McCormick v Carrier*, 2010).

You can also recover economic losses that no-fault does not cover (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)):

- lost wages beyond the three years or above the monthly cap
- medical bills above your chosen PIP level

The suit must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The mini tort claim recovers up to $3,000 in vehicle damage not covered by your policy (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)). If the at-fault driver’s insurer will not pay it, the claim is filed in the 18th District Court.

**When the other driver has no insurance, or not enough.** Some drivers on Ford Road and Michigan Avenue carry only the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)), and some carry nothing.

- **Against an uninsured driver:** your pain-and-suffering claim is worth what that driver can personally pay, unless you carry **uninsured motorist coverage**. A hit-and-run is usually a UM claim, with short notice terms in the policy.
- **Against an underinsured driver:** your own **underinsured motorist coverage** pays the difference up to your UIM limit, if you follow the steps in order:
 1. The at-fault driver’s insurer offers its limit.
 2. You notify your own insurer and ask for its written consent to settle.
 3. Only then do you accept the limit and sign the release.
 4. The UIM claim then proceeds.

Accepting without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under most policies. We read every policy in the household before valuing the claim. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Drunk drivers and the dram shop claim.** A bar, restaurant, or store that serves a visibly intoxicated person, or a minor, is liable for the injuries that person causes under Michigan’s dram shop act (MCL 436.1801). The State Police recorded 48 alcohol-involved crashes in Westland in 2024. The claim has its own rules:

- Written notice must go to the business within 120 days after you hire a lawyer.
- The drunk driver must be named and kept in the suit.
- The suit must be filed within two years, not three.

See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**Police cars, fire trucks, and buses.** All of these fall under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405):

- Westland Police and Fire vehicles
- SMART buses on Ford Road and Michigan Avenue
- school buses for the districts that serve the city
- Wayne County road and parks vehicles

## What to do after a Westland crash

1. **Call 911 and get the police report.** Michigan requires a report for any crash with injury or $1,000 in damage (MCL 257.622). Crashes in the city are worked by the Westland Police Department.
2. **Photograph the scene.** Get the vehicles, plates, the other driver’s license and insurance card, the intersection or driveway, the signals, and the lane markings. Note every store and apartment building with a camera facing the scene. Footage is kept for days, not weeks.
3. **If the other driver has no insurance card,** get the plate, the name, and the address, and tell the officer. Your own policy’s uninsured motorist notice terms may be short.
4. **Get medical care the same day.** Garden City Hospital, on Inkster Road just across the city line, and Corewell Health Wayne Hospital have emergency departments. The most serious injuries may go to a trauma center on the State’s list, such as Trinity Health Livonia or Corewell Health Dearborn (Level II), or a Level I center in Detroit.
5. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** promptly. If you have no policy, a lawyer can identify the insurer next in priority or the Assigned Claims Plan.
6. **Read your declarations page** for PIP level, UM/UIM, umbrella, and collision coverage.
7. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the other driver’s insurer, sign a release, or accept a policy-limits offer before your UIM insurer consents.
8. **Call a lawyer within days** if fault is disputed, the other driver is uninsured, or the road played a part. The camera footage is the first evidence lost, and the 120-day notice to Wayne County or the City is already running.

## Frequently asked questions

**A driver turned left in front of me on Ford Road. Who is at fault?** Usually the driver turning left, who must yield to oncoming traffic. These crashes are often disputed, though: the signal phase, your speed, and whether the view was blocked all matter. Store cameras along Ford Road can settle it, but footage is kept only days.

**A car pulled out of a store driveway on Ford Road and hit me. Who is at fault?** Usually the driver leaving the driveway, who must yield to traffic on the road. Your own speed and any obstruction to the sight line can still be argued.

**The driver who hit me had no insurance. What now?** Your own no-fault insurer, a household member’s insurer, or the Assigned Claims Plan pays your medical bills and lost wages regardless of fault. Your pain-and-suffering claim depends on whether you carry uninsured motorist coverage. Without it, the claim is worth what the uninsured driver can personally pay.

**I was hit crossing Ford Road. Who pays my medical bills?** Your own household’s auto policy pays if you have one. If not, the Assigned Claims Plan pays. Your pain-and-suffering claim is against the driver, reduced by any share of fault the evidence assigns you.

**How many crashes are there in Westland?** The State Police recorded 1,604 crashes in Westland in 2024. They killed 3 people, seriously injured 17, and injured 545 in all. 48 involved alcohol, 19 a pedestrian, and 16 a cyclist.

**A pothole caused my crash. Do I file against the City of Westland?** It depends on the road: - **Ford Road and Michigan Avenue** belong to MDOT, with notice filed in the Court of Claims within 120 days. - Wayne Road, Hines Drive, and other major roads belong to Wayne County, with written notice within 120 days. - **Subdivision streets** belong to the City, with the same 120 days.

**Where would my Westland case be filed?** Mini tort claims and claims up to $25,000 go to the 18th District Court on Ford Road. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Third Judicial Circuit Court in Detroit.

**How long do I have?** - One year to give your no-fault insurer written notice. - Three years to sue the at-fault driver. - 120 days for a notice to Wayne County or the City. - 120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT. - Two years to sue a bar, with a 120-day notice.
