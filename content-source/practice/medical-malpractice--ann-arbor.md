## Ann Arbor, MI Medical Malpractice Lawyer

A medical malpractice claim in Michigan has rules no other injury claim has:

- a two-year deadline instead of three
- a written Notice of Intent served on each provider at least 182 days before suit
- a sworn Affidavit of Merit from a qualified expert, filed with the complaint
- strict rules on who that expert can be
- a cap on pain-and-suffering damages

A case that misses any one of them can be lost before its merits are heard.

In Ann Arbor there is a sixth rule, and it comes first. **If the care was given by the University of Michigan, the claim is against the State.** It is filed in the Michigan Court of Claims, and a notice of intention, or the claim itself, must be filed with that court within six months. A Notice of Intent mailed to the hospital is not the same thing. For a child’s birth injury, the minority rules that give a child years to sue do not extend the six-month notice.

So the first question in an Ann Arbor case is who gave the care:

- **University of Michigan Health,** including its hospitals and health centers: the State, in the Court of Claims.
- **Trinity Health Ann Arbor** and private practices: the ordinary malpractice rules, in the Washtenaw County Trial Court.
- **The VA Ann Arbor Healthcare System:** the United States, under federal law.

## The rules, by the numbers

| Rule | Figure | Statute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Claim against the University of Michigan: notice or claim filed with the Court of Claims | Within 6 months of the event | MCL 600.6431 |
| Deadline to file suit | 2 years from the act or omission | MCL 600.5805(8) |
| Discovery rule | 6 months from when the claim was or should have been discovered, if later; never more than 6 years after the act (exceptions for fraud and reproductive injury) | MCL 600.5838a(2) |
| Notice of Intent before suit | 182 days; the provider must respond in writing within 154 days | MCL 600.2912b |
| Affidavit of Merit | Filed with the complaint | MCL 600.2912d |
| Defendant’s Affidavit of Meritorious Defense | Within 91 days after the answer | MCL 600.2912e |
| Expert qualification | Same specialty; same board certification if the defendant is certified; majority of professional time in that specialty in the year before | MCL 600.2169 |
| 2026 cap on noneconomic damages | $596,400 (standard) / $1,065,000 (catastrophic) | MCL 600.1483 |
| Child injured before age 8 (ordinary claims) | Deadline runs to the 10th birthday | MCL 600.5851(7) |
| VA Ann Arbor Healthcare System | Administrative claim (SF-95) within 2 years; suit after denial or 6 months | 28 USC 2401(b), 2675 |

Economic damages, including medical bills, lost earnings, and the cost of future care, are not capped. The Affidavit of Merit itself is required by MCL 600.2912d; MCL 600.2169 decides who is qualified to sign it.

## What malpractice is, and is not

A bad outcome is not malpractice. Malpractice is a bad outcome caused by a provider who failed to do what a reasonably competent provider in the same specialty would have done in the same circumstances (MCL 600.2912a). You must prove:

- the standard of care
- a breach
- that the breach more likely than not caused the injury
- damages

Michigan requires proof that the patient would have had a better outcome. A lost chance of a better outcome is compensable only where that chance was greater than 50 percent (MCL 600.2912a(2)).

A qualified physician’s written review of the records decides, before anything is filed, whether a case exists. Many do not, and we say so.

## Care at University of Michigan Health

**Who is the State.** The University of Michigan, governed by its Board of Regents, is a constitutional state institution. Its hospitals and clinics in Ann Arbor, including University Hospital, C.S. Mott Children’s Hospital, Von Voigtlander Women’s Hospital, and the Frankel Cardiovascular Center, and its health centers across the region, are part of the University. So is University Health Service, where students are treated. A malpractice claim against the University is a claim against the State.

University of Michigan Health also includes hospitals elsewhere in Michigan that joined it through affiliations, such as in Lansing and the Grand Rapids area. Whether each is the University itself or a separate corporation decides whether the State’s rules apply. We confirm the legal entity from the records before any notice is filed.

**Immunity does not bar the claim.** Michigan’s governmental immunity law does not shield a public hospital from a medical malpractice claim (MCL 691.1407(4)). The claim can be brought. The question is how.

**The court.** A claim against the University is filed in the Michigan Court of Claims and decided by a judge, not a jury (MCL 600.6419, 600.6443).

**The six-month notice.** For a personal injury claim against the State, the claimant must file, with the clerk of the Court of Claims, either the claim itself or a written notice of intention to file it, within six months after the event that gave rise to the claim (MCL 600.6431). The notice must be signed and verified under oath and must state the time, place, nature of the claim, and the injuries.

- **It is a filing with the court, not a letter to the hospital.** The Notice of Intent under MCL 600.2912b is served on the provider. It is a separate requirement, and serving it does not by itself satisfy the Court of Claims notice.
- **It is not extended for children.** In *Taylor v University of Michigan Board of Regents* (Mich App, August 18, 2022; unpublished), the Court of Appeals held that the minority tolling statute (MCL 600.5851) does not extend the six-month notice, and ordered a child’s injury claim against the University and C.S. Mott Children’s Hospital dismissed because the notice was filed years late. An unpublished opinion is not binding, but the University will cite it.
- **It applies wherever the claim is filed.** The Michigan Supreme Court held in *Christie v Wayne State University* (May 2, 2023) that any claim against the State, wherever it is filed, must comply with the notice requirements of MCL 600.6431.
- **It runs from the event.** The period runs from the event giving rise to the claim. Where an injury was not apparent at once, whether the period can run from a later date is contested. Do not count on it.

**The malpractice rules still apply.** The Notice of Intent, the 182-day waiting period, the Affidavit of Merit, the expert rules, and the caps all apply to a claim against the University as well. The Court of Claims notice is in addition to them, not instead of them.

**Residents, fellows, and individual physicians.** University of Michigan Health is a teaching hospital, and much of the care is given by residents and fellows under attending physicians. The physicians and nurses are University employees. Whether a claim against an individual University physician must also follow the Court of Claims rules, and where it is filed, depends on how the claim is pleaded.

**What this means for an Ann Arbor family.** If the care was at a University hospital or clinic, call within weeks, not months. The six months can pass while a family is still waiting for records or for a child’s diagnosis to become clear.

## Care at Trinity Health Ann Arbor, private practices, and surgery centers

**Trinity Health Ann Arbor,** formerly St. Joseph Mercy Ann Arbor, is a private, nonprofit hospital in Superior Township. Its employed physicians, nurses, and staff, and the physician groups that practice there, are private defendants. Claims are filed in the Washtenaw County Trial Court, with the ordinary malpractice rules:

- the 182-day Notice of Intent and 154-day response
- the Affidavit of Merit
- the two-year period, with the six-month discovery rule and six-year repose
- the cap

Health-system names and ownership change. The correct legal entity for the Notice of Intent must be identified from current corporate filings and from the name the provider used at the time of care, not from the sign or the letterhead.

**Emergency physicians and other contracted groups.** Many hospitals staff their emergency departments, anesthesia, and radiology through separate physician groups. The Notice of Intent must name the group as well as the hospital. Where a patient came to the hospital for care and reasonably believed the doctor worked for it, Michigan law can hold the hospital responsible under ostensible agency (*Grewe v Mt Clemens General Hospital*, 1978).

**Medical offices, urgent care, surgery centers, and imaging centers.** Primary-care and specialty practices, urgent care clinics, and outpatient surgery, endoscopy, and imaging centers serve Ann Arbor along Washtenaw Avenue, State Street, Plymouth Road, and Jackson Road. The frequent claims are:

- a delayed diagnosis in an office practice
- a missed fracture or infection at urgent care
- a surgical complication at an outpatient center

The employer of each provider who touched the chart must be identified. A University-employed physician and an independent physician in the same building are different defendants, with different rules, courts, and deadlines.

## Care at the VA Ann Arbor Healthcare System

Veterans treated at the VA Ann Arbor Healthcare System, or one of its outpatient clinics, have a claim against the United States under the Federal Tort Claims Act, not a Michigan malpractice claim:

- An administrative claim on Standard Form 95, stating a specific dollar amount, must be filed with the Department of Veterans Affairs within two years after the claim accrues (28 USC 2401(b)).
- There is no Michigan Notice of Intent.
- Suit can be filed in federal court only after the VA denies the claim, or six months pass without a decision, and within six months of a denial (28 USC 2675).
- The case is tried to a federal judge, without a jury.

Serving a Michigan Notice of Intent and waiting 182 days while the federal clock runs can lose the case. A veteran may also qualify for disability compensation under 38 USC 1151 where the injury was caused by VA carelessness, negligence, or error, or by an event that was not reasonably foreseeable.

**Federally funded community health centers.** Community health centers that receive federal Health Center Program funding, including centers serving Ann Arbor and Ypsilanti, can have their providers deemed federal employees for malpractice purposes. A claim against one follows the same federal path, with the administrative claim filed with the Department of Health and Human Services.

## Other Ann Arbor providers

**EMS.** Emergency ambulance service in Ann Arbor is provided by a private nonprofit ambulance operator, and the Ann Arbor Fire Department responds to medical calls. Claims against EMS personnel, and against the agency that employs them, public or private, are limited by the Emergency Medical Services Act to gross negligence or willful misconduct (MCL 333.20965(1)).

**Nursing homes, rehabilitation, and assisted living.** Ann Arbor has nursing homes, short-term rehabilitation units, and assisted living and memory care communities. Three Medicare-certified nursing homes have an Ann Arbor address Their claims are sometimes malpractice and sometimes ordinary negligence, and which one it is decides whether the Notice of Intent, the two-year period, and the cap apply. A nurse’s wound-care decision is malpractice. Leaving a resident unsupervised against the care plan is usually negligence. See our [nursing home abuse page](/personal-injury/nursing-home-abuse).

That includes University employees treated by University occupational health. See our [Ann Arbor workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation/ann-arbor).

## How an Ann Arbor malpractice case proceeds

1. **Identify who gave the care** in the first days: the University, a private hospital or practice, a physician group, the VA, a federally funded clinic, or EMS. If it was the University, the Court of Claims notice is drafted, verified, and filed first. If it was the VA, the SF-95 is prepared.
2. **The records,** including the electronic audit trail, from every provider, requested promptly, under whatever name the provider used at the time of care.
3. **Expert review** by a physician in the defendant’s specialty, in writing, before the Notice of Intent is served.
4. **The Notice of Intent** (MCL 600.2912b), served at least 182 days before suit. A timely notice pauses the two-year period during the notice period (MCL 600.5856(c)). If the provider does not respond within 154 days, suit may be filed then.
5. **The complaint and Affidavit of Merit** (MCL 600.2912d): in the Washtenaw County Trial Court for private providers, or the Court of Claims for the University. The defense then files its own affidavit (MCL 600.2912e).
6. **Discovery, case evaluation, and facilitation,** and, for cases that do not settle, trial. Against a private provider, the trial is to a Washtenaw County jury. Against the University, it is to a judge. Two to four years is typical.
7. **Resolution,** with minors’ and estates’ settlements approved by the court or the Washtenaw County Probate Court, and Medicare, Medicaid, and health plan liens resolved before distribution.

## Frequently asked questions

**I was harmed at a University of Michigan hospital. What is the deadline?** The first deadline is six months. A verified notice of intention, or the claim itself, must be filed with the Court of Claims within six months of the event. The two-year malpractice period, the 182-day Notice of Intent, and the Affidavit of Merit also apply. Call within weeks.

**My child was injured at birth at a University hospital. Don’t children get more time?** For the lawsuit against a private hospital, a child injured before age eight has until the tenth birthday. For a claim against the University, the Court of Appeals has held that the minority rules do not extend the six-month Court of Claims notice. Do not wait for the child’s diagnosis to be final before calling.

**Can I sue the University of Michigan for malpractice? Isn’t it immune?** You can. Michigan’s immunity law does not protect a public hospital from a malpractice claim. The claim is heard in the Court of Claims by a judge, and the six-month notice applies.

**Is a claim against Trinity Health Ann Arbor different?** Yes. Trinity Health Ann Arbor is a private hospital. The claim is filed in the Washtenaw County Trial Court, with a jury, under the ordinary malpractice rules. There is no six-month Court of Claims notice. The correct legal entity must be identified from the records.

**I’m a veteran and was treated at the VA in Ann Arbor. Is that different?** Yes. A claim against VA care follows the Federal Tort Claims Act: an administrative claim within two years, no Michigan Notice of Intent, and suit in federal court without a jury. A veteran may also qualify for disability compensation for an injury caused by VA error or an unforeseeable event.

**Is the Notice of Intent “six months” or 182 days?** 182 days, which is not the same as six calendar months. A notice served on the last day of a six-month count can be late. Count the days. And the Notice of Intent is separate from the six-month Court of Claims notice for a University claim.

**A resident made the mistake, not my doctor. Who is responsible?** In a teaching hospital, the hospital is responsible for its residents, and the attending physician may be responsible for supervising them. At a University hospital, both are University employees, and the claim follows the University’s rules.

**I was transferred to Ann Arbor from another hospital. Which hospital is responsible?** It depends on where the decision or delay that caused the harm happened: the first hospital’s diagnosis and decision to transfer, the transport, or the care after arrival. Each provider is a separate defendant with its own notice, and if one was a University hospital, its six-month notice applies.

**Is there a cap on what I can recover?** On pain-and-suffering damages only: $596,400 in the standard case and $1,065,000 for the catastrophic permanent injuries the statute lists, in 2026, adjusted each year. Medical bills, lost earnings, and future care are not capped.
