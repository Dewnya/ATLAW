## Personal Injury Law Firm Serving Ann Arbor, MI

Ann Arbor is a city of about 120,000 people and the home of the University of Michigan, whose students, staff, and visitors fill its streets, sidewalks, buses, and bike lanes. In 2024, according to the Michigan State Police, 2,770 crashes were reported in the city, more than in any other Washtenaw County community; 423 of them injured someone, 36 people were seriously injured, and 5 were killed. The city’s injuries follow its shape: pedestrian, bicycle, and scooter crashes around campus and downtown; intersection crashes on State Street, Washtenaw Avenue, Packard, Stadium, Huron Parkway, and Ellsworth; freeway crashes where I-94, US-23, and M-14 ring the city; falls in older rental houses and apartment buildings; and injuries in buildings, buses, and a stadium that belong to the State.

## Where Ann Arbor injuries happen

**Campus and downtown streets.** State Street, South University, East and North University, Washtenaw Avenue at the Hill, Packard, Liberty, Main, Huron, Division, and Fuller and Observatory near the medical campus carry more people on foot and on bikes than almost anywhere in Michigan. The crashes are drivers turning across crosswalks, drivers who stop for a pedestrian while the next lane does not, cyclists struck by an opening door or a right-turning vehicle, and scooter riders on uneven pavement. **Ann Arbor has its own crosswalk rule**: where no traffic signal is operating, the city’s ordinance requires drivers to stop and yield not only to pedestrians in a crosswalk but to pedestrians stopped at the curb, curb line, or ramp leading to it, which goes further than state law (Ann Arbor City Code, Chapter 126, Section 10:148(a)). **The corridors and the roundabouts.** Washtenaw Avenue east toward Ypsilanti, State Street and Ellsworth near Briarwood, Stadium Boulevard, Packard, Plymouth Road, Jackson Avenue, and Huron Parkway. The State Street and Ellsworth roundabout had 85 crashes in 2024, the second most in the county, but only 5 injuries; Huron Parkway at Washtenaw had fewer crashes, 35, but 15 injuries. Ann Arbor’s car crashes are covered on our [Ann Arbor car accident page](about:blank).

**The freeways.** I-94 along the south side, US-23 along the east, and M-14 along the north, with interchanges at State Street, Ann Arbor-Saline Road, Jackson Road, Washtenaw Avenue, Plymouth Road, and Main Street. Rear-end, lane-change, and truck crashes in the backups at the interchanges; see our [Ann Arbor truck accident page](about:blank).

**Football Saturdays and big events.** Home games at Michigan Stadium bring crowds of more than 100,000 people into the stadium neighborhood, with closed streets, shuttle buses, tailgates, and pedestrians on roads not built for them. Injuries at the stadium itself, which the University owns, are claims against the State; injuries at private tailgates, bars, and parking lots are ordinary claims. Bars and stores that serve visibly intoxicated or underage customers who then cause a crash can be liable under the dram shop act (MCL 436.1801). See our [drunk driving accident page](about:blank).

**Rental houses, apartments, and student housing.** Much of the near-campus housing is older houses converted to rentals and high-rise student apartments. Falls on broken porch steps and stairs, missing handrails, fires, carbon monoxide, and failed smoke detectors are the recurring claims. A private landlord must keep the premises and common areas fit for their intended use and in reasonable repair (MCL 554.139); a lease clause waiving the landlord’s liability for its own negligence is void. University residence halls are the State’s. A claim for a defective University building goes to the Court of Claims under the public building exception, which covers only parts of a building open for use by members of the public and requires written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1406).

**Workplaces.** The University and its hospitals, research labs, construction sites, retail, and restaurants. Workers’ compensation is the exclusive remedy against the employer (MCL 418.131), but a contractor, property owner, or equipment maker who caused the injury can be sued. See our [Ann Arbor workers’ compensation page](about:blank).

## What is different about an Ann Arbor claim

**The University is the State.** The University of Michigan is a constitutional state institution, and so is its health system. A claim against it, whether for a crash with a University bus or vehicle, a defective University building, a fall at the stadium, or medical malpractice at Michigan Medicine, is a claim against the State: it is filed in the Michigan Court of Claims, decided by a judge without a jury, and requires written notice within six months of the injury (MCL 600.6431), or within 120 days for a defective building (MCL 691.1406). Missing that notice can end the claim.

**Three hospitals, three different rules.** University of Michigan Health, a Level I trauma center for adults and children, treats the most seriously injured people from across the region; a malpractice claim against it is not barred by governmental immunity (MCL 691.1407(4)) but proceeds against the State in the Court of Claims. Trinity Health Ann Arbor (formerly St. Joseph Mercy Ann Arbor), a Level I trauma center in Superior Township, is a private hospital, so a claim against it goes to the circuit court. The VA Ann Arbor Healthcare System is federal, so a claim about a veteran’s care there follows the Federal Tort Claims Act: an administrative claim within two years and suit in federal court. All three follow Michigan’s malpractice standard of care, and the Michigan claims follow the 182-day Notice of Intent, the Affidavit of Merit, the two-year deadline, and the 2026 caps of $596,400 and $1,065,000 (MCL 600.2912b, 600.2912d, 600.5805(8), 600.1483). See our [Ann Arbor medical malpractice page](about:blank).

**Who owns the road.** Ann Arbor is a city and owns its sidewalks and most of its streets; Washtenaw Avenue, Huron Street, and North Main Street carry Business Loop I-94 and Business US-23, which are State routes; a claim against the City for a road or sidewalk defect takes written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1402a, 691.1404). I-94, US-23, and M-14 are MDOT’s, with written notice of a road defect filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). University roads and walks on campus are the State’s.

**Students and no-fault.** Many students do not own a car. A student hit while walking or biking is covered by a policy in their household, which may be a parent’s policy if the student is still domiciled with the parent; if no household policy applies, by the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan. (MCL 500.3115.) (MCL 500.3114, 500.3115, 500.3172). A scooter or e-bike rider’s no-fault position depends on whether a motor vehicle was involved. An out-of-state student hit in Ann Arbor is not entitled to Michigan PIP benefits unless the student is a Michigan resident or owns a vehicle registered and insured in Michigan (MCL 500.3113(c)); whether a student living here is a Michigan resident depends on the facts. --- Since the 2019 reform, MCL 500.3113(c) bars PIP for a nonresident "unless the person owned a motor vehicle that was registered and insured in this state," and the old rule making certified out-of-state insurers pay Michigan PIP (MCL 500.3163) was removed.

**Buses.** TheRide (the Ann Arbor Area Transportation Authority) runs the city’s buses, and the University runs its own. A crash with a TheRide bus is a claim under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405); a crash with a University bus is a Court of Claims case. A bus passenger’s no-fault benefits follow the bus-passenger priority rules (MCL 500.3114(2)). See our [bus accident page](about:blank).

**The courts are on Huron Street.** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, are filed in the 15th District Court at the Ann Arbor Justice Center, 301 E. Huron Street. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Washtenaw County Trial Court (22nd Circuit) at 101 E. Huron Street, where the Probate Court, which opens estates for wrongful death claims and approves minors’ settlements, also sits. Claims against the University, MDOT, and the State go to the Court of Claims; federal claims and cases against out-of-state defendants may be heard in the federal courthouse in Ann Arbor.

## Frequently asked questions

**I was hit by a car while crossing near campus. Who pays my medical bills?** A policy in your household, possibly a parent’s if you are still domiciled with them; if none, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan. Your claim against the driver for pain and suffering is separate.

**Does Ann Arbor’s crosswalk rule matter in my case?** It can. At crosswalks without a working signal, Ann Arbor requires drivers to stop and yield to pedestrians stopped at the curb waiting to cross, not only to those already in the crosswalk. A driver who did not is evidence of negligence.

**I was hurt by a University of Michigan bus, building, or employee. Can I sue?** Often, but in the Michigan Court of Claims, before a judge, with written notice within six months, or 120 days for a defective building. Governmental immunity limits which claims can be brought, and the notice deadline is much shorter than the ordinary three years.

**Can I bring a malpractice claim against Michigan Medicine?** Yes, under Michigan’s malpractice rules, in the Court of Claims. A claim against Trinity Health Ann Arbor goes to the circuit court, and a claim against the VA follows federal law.

**I fell on the porch of my rental house. Is the landlord responsible?** A landlord must keep the premises and common areas in reasonable repair and fit for their use (MCL 554.139), and a lease clause waiving that duty is void. Photograph the defect and report it in writing.

**Where would my Ann Arbor case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000 in the 15th District Court at 301 E. Huron Street; injury suits over $25,000 in the Washtenaw County Trial Court at 101 E. Huron Street; claims against the University or the State in the Court of Claims.

**Do you have an office in Ann Arbor?** Our office is at 3 Parklane Boulevard in Dearborn, east of Ann Arbor. We welcome clients to our Dearborn office for a consultation. If you are unable to visit, home or hospital visits can be arranged as needed.

**How long do I have?** Three years for most injury suits, one year to notify your no-fault insurer, two years for malpractice, 120 days for a claim against the City, and six months for a claim against the University or the State (120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT or a building-defect notice against the University).
