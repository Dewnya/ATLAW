## The two programs

**Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI)** is for people who worked and paid Social Security taxes: generally 40 work credits, 20 of them in the ten years before disability, fewer for younger workers. The benefit is based on your earnings record, and after 24 months of entitlement you qualify for Medicare regardless of age. A Detroit autoworker, nurse, bus driver, or tradesperson who stopped working in their fifties is a typical SSDI claimant.

**Supplemental Security Income (SSI)** is for people with little income and few assets, whether or not they worked: $994 a month for an individual in 2026, reduced by other income, with a $2,000 asset limit (home and one vehicle excluded), and Michigan Medicaid immediately. Many Detroit claimants qualify for both programs, and a lawyer files both applications together. See our [SSDI page](/disability/social-security-disability-insurance) and [SSI page](/disability/supplemental-security-income).

## The five-step test, as it is applied to a Detroit claim

Social Security uses a five-step sequential evaluation (20 CFR 404.1520), deciding the claim at the first step where the answer is clear.

1. **Are you working at the substantial gainful activity level?** Earning more than $1,690 a month in 2026 ends the claim at step one regardless of your condition.
2. **Is your impairment severe and expected to last 12 months or result in death?** A diagnosis is not enough; the record must show functional limits.
3. **Does your condition meet or equal a Listing?** The Listings of Impairments (20 CFR Part 404, Subpart P, Appendix 1) set criteria for specific conditions; meeting one wins the claim here. See our [qualifying impairments page](/disability/qualifying-impairments). Certain conditions are fast-tracked under [Compassionate Allowances](/disability/compassionate-allowances).
4. **Can you do your past relevant work?** Social Security assesses your residual functional capacity, what you can still do, and compares it to the jobs you held in the past five years.
5. **Can you do any other work?** Using your RFC, age, education, and work experience under the Medical-Vocational Guidelines (the “grids”). This is where claimants over 50 with a history of physical work, which describes a large share of Detroit claimants, win or lose. A 55-year-old former line worker limited to sedentary work is generally found disabled under the grids; a 45-year-old with the same limits is generally not. **[ATTORNEY CHECK: grid summary]**

## Back pay, the waiting period, and what the fee is

SSDI benefits begin five full months after the established onset date; SSI has no waiting period but is not paid for months before the application. When a claim is approved after a long appeal, back pay reaches to the onset date (SSDI up to 12 months before the application), and the onset date your lawyer proves sets the amount.

**The fee is set by federal law.** Under Social Security’s fee agreement process, the attorney receives 25 percent of back benefits, capped at the current limit ($9,200 for agreements approved on or after November 30, 2024), and nothing if the claim is not approved (42 USC 406(a)). Social Security pays it directly from the back pay; you never write a check, and ongoing monthly benefits are not subject to the fee. Costs for medical records are usually modest; we advance them. There is no fee for the consultation.

## What to do if you are applying or were denied in Detroit

1. **Apply as soon as you stop working**, or as soon as your earnings fall below the SGA level. SSDI back pay reaches back no more than 12 months before the application.
2. **If denied, appeal within 60 days.** Do not file a new application.
3. **Keep treating**, and tell every provider how your condition limits you day to day. Gaps in treatment are a common reason Detroit claims are denied at the hearing.
4. **Ask your treating physician for a functional opinion**, in the form the regulations recognize; we provide it.
5. **Attend the consultative examination**, and tell us what happened in it.
6. **Keep a record** of your work attempts, your daily limitations, and your medications and side effects; the judge will ask.
7. **Call a lawyer before the hearing is scheduled**, not after the notice arrives. The record has to be complete before the judge reads it. See our [how to win your claim page](/disability/winning-your-disability-claim).

## Frequently asked questions

**Why was my Detroit disability application denied when my doctor says I cannot work?** Because Social Security decides disability by its own rules, not by your doctor’s conclusion. The question is whether your medical records document limits that rule out all work you could otherwise do. Most denials are for insufficient evidence, gaps in treatment, or earnings above the limit, and many are reversible at a hearing with a complete record.

**How long do I have to appeal?** Sixty days from the date on the denial notice, plus five days for mailing, at every stage. Missing it generally means starting over.

**Where will my hearing be?** At the Detroit hearing office in the McNamara Federal Building downtown or the Livonia hearing office, or by video or phone. You can request to appear in person. **[VERIFY]**

**How long does a Detroit claim take?** Initial decision in roughly six to eight months; if denied, reconsideration in another four to seven; a hearing after that. A claim that goes to hearing commonly takes two years from application. Compassionate Allowance claims take weeks.

**What does a Detroit disability lawyer cost?** Twenty-five percent of your back benefits, capped by federal law, paid by Social Security from the back pay. Nothing if you do not win. No fee is taken from your ongoing monthly benefits.

**I get mental health treatment through DWIHN. Does that count?** Yes. Treatment records from the Detroit Wayne Integrated Health Network’s providers are medical evidence, and for many Detroit claims they are the central evidence. They can be slow to obtain, which is one reason to start early.

**I was hurt in a car crash and cannot work. Should I apply for disability too?** If the injury will keep you from working for a year or more, yes, and as soon as possible. The no-fault, injury, and Social Security claims run on different rules and deadlines, and we coordinate them.

**Can I work at all while my claim is pending?** Below the SGA level, yes, but any work will be examined, and it is safer to discuss it with your lawyer first.

**What is the difference between SSDI and SSI?** SSDI is based on your work record and pays according to your earnings, with Medicare after 24 months. SSI is based on financial need, pays up to $994 a month in 2026, and comes with Medicaid. The disability test is the same for both, and many Detroit claimants qualify for both.
