Rochester Hills’ freight moves on M-59, a freeway through the whole city, carrying tractor-trailers to I-75 in Auburn Hills and east into Macomb County.

Off M-59, trucks serve the city’s industrial, technology, and research parks. They also serve the retail centers on Rochester Road and Adams, and the construction that comes with a growing city.

In 2024, according to the Michigan State Police:

- 2,081 heavy trucks and buses were involved in crashes in Oakland County.
- Statewide, 110 people were killed and 3,613 injured in heavy truck and bus crashes.

The State’s county profile does not break out truck crashes by city.

A truck crash is not a car crash with a larger vehicle:

- **The rules are federal.** The driver and the carrier are governed by federal safety regulations, and a violation is evidence of negligence.
- **The insurance is larger.** The carrier must carry $750,000 or more in insurance.
- **The records expire.** The records that prove the case, the driver’s electronic logs and the truck’s engine data, can be lawfully discarded within months.
- **The other side moves fast.** The carrier’s insurer will have an investigator on the case within days.

## Where Rochester Hills truck crashes happen

**The M-59 freeway.** The freeway crashes are:

- rear-end and underride crashes in the backups at the Adams, Crooks, and Rochester Road interchanges and the approach to I-75
- lane changes into a car in the blind spot on the right side of the cab
- merge crashes on the ramps
- crashes in work zones

A car that strikes the side or rear of a trailer can go under it. That is an **underride** crash, among the deadliest in trucking. Trailers must carry rear impact guards that meet federal standards (49 CFR 393.86). A missing, damaged, or substandard guard is evidence against the carrier and the trailer’s owner. On designated Michigan routes, heavy trucks can weigh up to 164,000 pounds, about twice the federal Interstate limit, and they need far more distance to stop.

**The industrial and technology parks.** Tractor-trailers and box trucks enter and leave loading docks on these roads. Livernois at Hamlin had 60 crashes in 2024, tenth in Oakland County. The truck crashes are:

- trucks turning across traffic into a driveway
- trucks backing into a dock from the road
- trucks parked on the shoulder waiting for a dock

When a site’s dock layout or delivery schedule puts trucks in the road, the property owner or tenant may share responsibility with the carrier.

**Rochester Road and the retail docks.** Stores and restaurants along Rochester Road and Adams are supplied by tractor-trailers and box trucks. Those trucks turn into service drives across traffic and back into docks behind the stores. See our [premises liability page](/personal-injury/premises-liability).

**Construction and dump trucks.** New subdivisions, commercial building, and road work bring dump trucks, cement mixers, and equipment haulers onto two-lane roads. Their crashes involve:

- loads of gravel or debris
- trucks pulling out of construction sites
- trucks with poorly maintained brakes



**Delivery vans, garbage trucks, and the subdivisions.** Package vans, appliance and furniture trucks, and garbage trucks work the subdivision streets. The crashes are:

- backing crashes
- crashes with vans stopped in the lane
- right-turn crashes that catch a cyclist or pedestrian near the trail crossings

Delivery vans are often operated by contracted delivery companies rather than by the retailer named on the package. The van’s operator, its insurer, and the contract between them have to be identified. See our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents) and our [bicycle accident page](/personal-injury/bicycle-accidents).

**Lost and spilled loads.** Cargo, equipment, gravel, or debris that falls from a truck is a violation of the federal cargo securement rules (49 CFR 393.100 and following) and of Michigan law (MCL 257.720). The carrier and the company that loaded the truck can both be responsible.

**Whose road it is.** Rochester Hills is a city, and the road owner can be any of four agencies:

- MDOT for M-59, and for Rochester Road (M-150) from M-59 north to Tienken. A road-defect claim against the State goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404; *Champine v Department of Transportation*, Mich 2022).
- The Road Commission for Oakland County for many major roads, including Adams, Crooks, Livernois, Avon, Tienken, Walton, and Rochester Road south of M-59. Written notice is due within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404)
- The City of Rochester Hills for Hamlin, John R, other City roads, and the subdivision streets. Written notice is due within 120 days.
- **Oakland University** for its campus roads. A claim against it is a claim against the State.



## Who is responsible, and who pays

**The carrier, not just the driver.** The motor carrier is responsible for its drivers’ compliance with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations. It is liable for a driver’s negligence in the course of employment. Michigan applies the federal rules to intrastate carriers (MCL 480.11a). A local hauler, a dump truck operator, or a delivery carrier that never leaves Oakland County is held to the same standards as an interstate carrier.

These are the rules that decide truck cases most often:

| Rule | 49 CFR | What it means for the case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hours of service | Part 395 | 11 hours driving in a 14-hour window after 10 hours off; 60/70-hour weekly limits; recorded on an electronic logging device. Some short-haul drivers are exempt from logs but not from the limits. |
| Driver qualification | Part 391 | CDL, medical certificate, driving record, background check. Negligent hiring and retention are claims against the carrier. |
| Post-crash testing | Part 382 | Alcohol within 8 hours and drugs within 32 hours after a fatal crash, or after an injury or tow-away crash with a citation. |
| Inspection and maintenance | Part 396 | Pre-trip inspections, defect reports, maintenance records. Brake, tire, lift-axle, lighting, and reflective-tape cases are maintenance cases. |
| Cargo securement | 393.100–393.136 | Loads must be contained or secured so they cannot shift or fall. A spilled load is evidence of a violation. |
| Equipment: rear impact guards, lighting, reflectors | Part 393 | Underride guards (393.86) and conspicuity marking. A trailer that cannot be seen at night or lacks a working guard is a defective trailer. |
| Handheld phone ban; hazardous conditions | 392.82, 392.14 | No handheld phone use. Reduce speed or stop in snow, ice, and fog. |
| Minimum insurance | Part 387 | $750,000 general freight; $1 million oil; $5 million hazardous materials. |



**The owner of the truck or trailer.** In Michigan, a vehicle’s owner is liable for its negligent operation by someone driving with the owner’s consent (MCL 257.401). When the tractor, the trailer, and the driver belong to different companies, each may be a defendant, although a company in the business of renting or leasing vehicles is generally shielded from owner liability by federal law (49 USC 30106).

**Michigan no-fault still applies to you.** Your own auto insurer pays these benefits regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107):

- your medical bills
- 85 percent of lost wages, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- replacement services
- attendant care

Written notice is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)).

Your claim against the driver and the carrier is for pain and suffering and for losses beyond no-fault. It requires a threshold injury (MCL 500.3135) and must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). Michigan’s comparative fault rule is modified, not pure:

- **More than 50 percent at fault:** you cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages.
- **50 percent or less:** your share reduces them (MCL 500.3135(2)(b); MCL 600.2959).

The noneconomic damages caps apply to medical malpractice and to product liability claims, such as a claim against the maker of a failed part (MCL 600.1483, 600.2946a), not to the claim against the driver and carrier.

The difference from a car case is the money available: the carrier’s $750,000-plus policy, often with excess coverage above it. If the carrier’s coverage still falls short, your own underinsured motorist coverage may add to it. Your insurer’s written consent is needed before you accept the carrier’s limit. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

**Other defendants.**

- the shipper or broker that loaded the truck or hired an unsafe carrier
- the property owner or tenant whose dock or yard operation contributed
- the general contractor on a construction site whose trucks were involved
- the maintenance contractor
- the manufacturer of a failed component or a defective guard ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability))
- for a government truck (MDOT, the Road Commission, the City, Oakland County, SMART, or a school bus), the agency, under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405)



**If you were the truck driver.** Workers’ compensation pays your benefits, and your employer usually cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). If you were injured in your employer’s truck, no-fault benefits generally come from the insurer of that truck (MCL 500.3114(3)). They are coordinated with comp, which is paid first (MCL 500.3109(1)). You can also sue a third party, such as another driver or a business whose dock or yard caused the crash. See our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).

## The evidence, and how fast it disappears

Carriers must keep two kinds of records for a limited time:

- hours-of-service records, for six months (49 CFR 395.8)
- driver inspection reports, for three months (49 CFR 396.11)

After that, the regulations no longer require them. Other evidence goes faster:

- dock and yard cameras, business cameras along M-59 and Rochester Road, MDOT freeway cameras, and dash cameras overwrite in days to weeks
- the truck’s engine control module and event data recorder can be cleared when the truck is repaired and returned to service
- a spilled load is swept up within hours
- a damaged rear guard can be replaced before anyone measures it

The first act in a Rochester Hills truck case is a **preservation letter**. It goes to the carrier, its insurer, the tractor and trailer owners, and the shipper or broker. Where the crash happened at a dock, yard, or construction site, it also goes to the property owner, the tenant, or the contractor. It demands:

- the ELD and engine data
- dash camera footage
- the driver’s qualification file and post-crash test results
- dispatch records, bills of lading, and dock appointment logs
- maintenance files
- the truck and trailer themselves, for inspection of the brakes, lift axles, load securement, and rear guard

Once a party knows a claim is coming, destroying that evidence exposes it to spoliation sanctions.

## What to do after a Rochester Hills truck crash

1. **Call 911 and get medical care the same day.** Henry Ford Rochester Hospital, on University Drive, describes itself as a Level III adult trauma center. The most serious injuries may go to McLaren Oakland or Trinity Health Oakland in Pontiac, both Level II trauma centers, or to Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak, the county’s Level I adult trauma center. Henry Ford Rochester Hospital, on University Drive, describes itself as a Level III adult trauma center.
2. **Photograph the truck.** Get the cab and trailer, the USDOT number and carrier name on the door, both plates, and the load and anything that fell from it. Photograph the rear guard and reflective tape, and the dock, driveway, or construction entrance if the truck was entering or leaving one.
3. **Get the police report.** Crashes on city streets are worked by the Oakland County Sheriff’s Office, which polices Rochester Hills under contract. Freeway crashes may be worked by the Michigan State Police. A serious crash may produce a separate commercial vehicle inspection report from the State Police.
4. **Do not speak to the carrier’s insurer or its investigator.**
5. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** within the first week.
6. **Call a lawyer within days,** so the preservation letter goes out before the records expire and the truck is repaired.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many truck accidents happen in Rochester Hills?** The State does not publish truck crash figures by city. Countywide, 2,081 heavy trucks and buses were involved in Oakland County crashes in 2024. Statewide, 15,888 crashes involved a heavy truck or bus, and 110 people were killed.

**A dump truck from a construction site dropped gravel and cracked my windshield, then I crashed. Who is responsible?** The truck’s operator, under federal and Michigan load-securement rules, and possibly the contractor that hired it. Note the truck’s company name and number, and photograph the debris before it is cleared.

**A delivery van hit me. Who is responsible?** The van’s driver and the company that employs the driver. That is often a contracted delivery company rather than the retailer whose name is on the package, and that company’s commercial auto policy is usually the coverage.

**My car went under the back of a trailer on M-59. Is that the truck’s fault?** It may be partly or wholly. Trailers must carry rear impact guards built to federal standards. A guard that is missing, bent, or too high to stop a car is evidence against the carrier and the trailer’s owner. The trailer has to be inspected before it is repaired. **[ATTORNEY CHECK]**

**Is there a cap on what I can recover from a trucking company?** Not on pain and suffering against the driver or carrier. Michigan’s damages caps apply to medical malpractice and product liability claims. In practice, the limit is the insurance available: the carrier’s policy, any excess policy, and your own underinsured coverage.

**I was partly at fault. Can I still recover?** Yes, if you were 50 percent at fault or less. Your recovery is reduced by your share, and above 50 percent you lose the claim for pain and suffering. No-fault benefits are paid regardless.

**Why does the lawyer need to act within days?** Because the records expire. Hours-of-service logs must be kept for six months and inspection reports for three. Camera footage lasts days or weeks. The truck’s electronic data and the trailer’s damage can disappear when the truck is repaired.
