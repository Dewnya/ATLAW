## Clinton Township Personal Injury Lawyer

If you were hurt in Clinton Township because someone else was careless, you have a claim under Michigan law, and its deadlines start the day you are hurt. After a crash, your no-fault insurer must have written notice within one year. A road defect claim against the Macomb County Department of Roads needs written notice within 120 days; a defect on Hall Road, Gratiot, Groesbeck, or I-94 needs written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days. A fall on a broken public sidewalk, or in a township building, needs written notice to Clinton Township within 120 days. A claim against a bar that served a drunk driver must be filed within two years. A medical malpractice suit, including many claims against nursing homes, requires 182 days’ notice first and must be filed within two years. Most other claims allow three years. A personal injury lawyer’s first job is to find out which of those apply to you; the second is to make sure none runs out before your claim is paid.

## What a Clinton Township personal injury lawyer does

**The first week.** We calendar every deadline and send preservation letters:

- to the at-fault driver’s insurer;
- to a trucking company for its driver logs and electronic data, after crashes on Groesbeck, Gratiot, or I-94;
- to MDOT or the Macomb County Department of Roads for signal timing records;
- to SMART or the township for vehicle records;
- to Partridge Creek, a Hall Road or Gratiot store, or an apartment or condominium complex’s management, for footage and incident reports, which many overwrite within days;
- to a nursing home or assisted living community for the resident’s chart, incident reports, and staffing records;
- to a bar or restaurant for its receipts if a drunk driver was involved.

We obtain the police report (Clinton Township Police Department reports; some highway crashes are State Police or Macomb County Sheriff reports), the Clinton Township Fire Department and ambulance run sheets, and the witnesses’ names.

**The first month.** We identify every policy that may pay: your own auto policy for no-fault benefits, household policies, the at-fault party’s liability coverage, your uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage, a property owner’s or management company’s commercial policy, umbrella policies, and the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan where no auto policy exists (MCL 500.3172). For a workplace injury, we identify whether a third party, such as a contractor, a vendor’s driver, or an equipment manufacturer, caused it, because the employer itself usually cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). We file your Application for No-Fault Benefits inside the one-year window (MCL 500.3145(1)) and handle the insurer’s requests for statements, records, and examinations (MCL 500.3151). For an older client on Medicare who opted out of PIP medical coverage, we coordinate Medicare’s payment and its lien from the start. You stop talking to adjusters; we do that.

**The treatment period.** The claim’s value is set mostly by the medical record, so we make sure it is complete and the bills are paid while you treat. We collect records from Henry Ford Macomb Hospital, McLaren Macomb in Mount Clemens, and your own physicians and therapists, and document lost wages with your employer, including overtime and shift premiums.

**The demand, the suit, and the resolution.** When your condition has stabilized, we send a demand with the complete record and a valuation. The decision to accept any offer is yours (MRPC 1.2). If the insurer will not pay fair value, suit is filed in the right court:

- **41B District Court** on Starks Drive in Clinton Township, which serves Clinton Township, Harrison Township, and Mount Clemens, for claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims.
- **Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court** (Macomb County Circuit Court) at 40 N. Main Street in Mount Clemens for injury suits over $25,000. Most injury cases there go through case evaluation (MCR 2.403) and facilitation before trial.
- **The U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan**, where an out-of-state defendant removes the case.

At settlement we resolve the liens, including health plans, Medicare, Medicaid, and workers’ compensation, before you are paid. Minors’ settlements and wrongful death distributions are approved by the Macomb County Probate Court, in Mount Clemens, or by the trial court (MCR 2.420; MCL 600.2922(6)). Compensation for physical injury is generally not taxable income (26 USC 104(a)(2)).

## Who the defendant is in a township

Clinton Township is a township, and that changes who you claim against in a way that catches many people out.

- **The roads are not the township’s.** Hall Road, Gratiot, Groesbeck, and I-94 are MDOT’s, with written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). Garfield, Romeo Plank, Hayes, Harper, Moravian, Cass, Utica Road, Metro Parkway, the mile roads, and most subdivision streets are the Macomb County Department of Roads’, with written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404).
- **The sidewalks are.** A township is a “municipal corporation” under the immunity act, and must keep the public sidewalks in reasonable repair, including those beside county and state roads (MCL 691.1401(d), 691.1402a). A fall on a broken public sidewalk is a claim against Clinton Township, with the two-inch rule and 120-day notice.
- **So are the township’s buildings and vehicles.** A fall caused by a defect in a township building proceeds under the public building exception (MCL 691.1406); a crash with a township police car, fire truck, or other vehicle under the motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405).
- **The schools and the college are separate agencies.** The school districts that serve the township and Macomb Community College each take their own 120-day notice for a building defect. The township is served by the Chippewa Valley and Clintondale school districts, with parts in L’Anse Creuse, Fraser, and Mount Clemens. A community college district is a political subdivision under the immunity act (MCL 691.1401(e)).

## What a Clinton Township claim is worth, and what reduces it

A claim includes economic damages (medical expenses, lost wages, lost earning capacity, replacement services) and noneconomic damages (pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, disfigurement). There is no cap except in medical malpractice. In an auto case, noneconomic damages require a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). Your share of fault reduces the recovery, and above 50 percent it bars noneconomic damages (MCL 600.2959; in auto cases MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). At exactly 50 percent you recover half. Where more than one party caused the injury, each defendant generally pays only its own share of fault (MCL 600.2956, 600.6304), so every responsible party has to be identified and named. Michigan does not award punitive damages in ordinary injury cases; exemplary damages for humiliation or outrage are available only in narrow circumstances.

In many Clinton Township cases the practical limit is insurance. The at-fault driver’s liability limit defaults to $250,000/$500,000 unless the driver chose lower limits, down to the $50,000/$100,000 minimum (MCL 500.3009(1)), and some drivers carry nothing; your own underinsured motorist coverage fills the gap; and in a premises, workplace, or nursing home case, the commercial policy of the property owner, contractor, or facility pays. Where UIM is involved, settling with the at-fault driver without your own insurer’s consent can forfeit the UIM claim. For a worker with a third-party claim, the workers’ compensation insurer has a lien on the recovery (MCL 418.827), and how that lien is negotiated changes what you take home. For an older client, the Medicare lien is negotiated the same way.

## What it costs

Michigan caps contingency fees in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery (MCR 8.121). Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted from the recovery first; the one-third fee is calculated on the remaining amount. You pay nothing up front and nothing if there is no recovery. The agreement is in writing; read how costs (filing fees, records, experts, depositions) are handled if the case is lost, because that is where firms differ. A promise that “you pay nothing unless we win” should say whether that includes costs.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in Clinton Township?** Three years for most claims. But you have one year to notify your no-fault insurer after a crash, 120 days to notify the Department of Roads of a road defect or the township of a sidewalk or building defect, 120 days for a defect on a state highway, two years to sue a bar, and a 182-day notice and two-year limit for malpractice.

**I fell on a broken sidewalk along Gratiot. Do I notify MDOT or the township?** Generally the township. MDOT’s duty stops at the roadway; the township must keep the public sidewalk beside it in reasonable repair. It must have known or should have known of the defect for 30 days, a defect under two inches is presumed to be reasonable repair, and written notice must reach the township within 120 days.

**What does a Clinton Township personal injury attorney charge?** Michigan caps the fee in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery, with no fee if there is no recovery. Ask how costs are handled. At ATLAW, case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted only from a recovery.

**Where would my Clinton Township case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000 in the 41B District Court on Starks Drive; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court in Mount Clemens.

**If I was partly at fault, do I still have a claim?** Yes, unless you were more than 50 percent at fault, which bars pain-and-suffering damages. Below that, your recovery is reduced by your share; at exactly 50 percent you recover half. No-fault benefits are paid regardless of fault.

**I’m on Medicare and opted out of no-fault medical coverage. Who pays my bills after a crash?** Medicare pays first for the care it covers, and it will expect to be repaid from any recovery against the at-fault driver. The claim has to be built with that lien in view.

**Do I need a lawyer whose office is in Clinton Township?** No. What matters is that the lawyer knows the courts your case will be in, the road agencies, property owners, facilities, and insurers involved, and will meet you where you are.

**The insurer already offered me money. Should I take it?** Not before you know what the claim is worth and what the release gives up, and not before your UIM insurer has consented if UIM may apply.
