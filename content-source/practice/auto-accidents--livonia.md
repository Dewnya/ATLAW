## Where Livonia crashes happen, and whose road it is

**The I-96 service drives.** Schoolcraft Road is not one road but two: the eastbound and westbound service drives on either side of I-96. Each arterial crosses both, so a driver at Middlebelt passes through two signalized intersections a few hundred feet apart, with freeway ramps feeding into each. The crashes are rear-end collisions in the queues between the two signals, left turns across traffic coming off the ramps, red-light and late-yellow crashes, and lane changes by drivers trying to reach a ramp. The same pattern repeats at Merriman, Farmington, Levan, and Newburgh.

**Six Mile, Laurel Park, and the I-96/I-275 interchange.** Six Mile at the interchange carries the freeway exits, the traffic to Laurel Park Place and the office and hotel cluster around it, and Schoolcraft College to the north on Haggerty. Merge and weave crashes on the freeway, and ramp and signal crashes on Six Mile, are its cases; southbound I-275 near Six Mile is a crash area of its own. Heavy trucks use I-96 and I-275 to reach the Plymouth Road industrial corridor; see our [Livonia truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/livonia).

**Plymouth Road and Middlebelt.** Plymouth Road carries the industrial corridor and the Wonderland Village redevelopment at Middlebelt, and Middlebelt carries the retail strips from Plymouth to Seven Mile. Signal crashes, left turns into driveways, and parking-lot crashes are Middlebelt’s; pedestrian strikes near the shopping centers and bus stops are a risk on both (see our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents)).

**The mile roads and the subdivisions.** Five, Six, Seven, and Eight Mile; Farmington, Merriman, Levan, Newburgh, and Inkster. Signal crashes at Farmington’s intersections with Six and Seven Mile and Plymouth, and pedestrian and bicycle crashes near the schools, Madonna University, the parks, and the subdivision streets (see our [bicycle accident page](/personal-injury/bicycle-accidents)). Eight Mile is the line with Oakland County, so a crash there can involve Farmington Hills drivers and a choice of courts.

**Three road agencies.** I-96, I-275, M-14, and generally the I-96 service drives are MDOT’s; a road-defect claim against MDOT goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). The mile roads, including Eight Mile from Haggerty to Farmington, most of the arterials, and Plymouth Road west of Newburgh are generally Wayne County’s; other streets, including subdivision streets, are generally the City of Livonia’s; both take written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404). A pothole or other defect in the roadbed can make the agency a defendant alongside the other driver; on State and county roads, signals, signs, and sight obstructions generally fall outside the highway exception. Notice to the wrong agency is not notice.

## Fault is the case

In a rear-end crash, fault is rarely disputed. In a left-turn or signal crash at a Livonia intersection, it is disputed often, and the other driver’s insurer may try to assign you half or more of the fault. That number matters: if you are found more than 50 percent at fault, you lose your claim for pain and suffering, and below that your recovery is reduced by your share (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). At exactly 50 percent you recover half. The evidence that decides it is perishable:

- the Livonia Police report and the officer’s diagram;
- **signal timing data** held by MDOT or Wayne County, which show how each light was timed, including how the two service-drive signals at an arterial were coordinated, and, where the controller logs events, what phase it was in;
- traffic, business, and retail cameras along Middlebelt, Plymouth, and Six Mile, many overwritten within days;
- the vehicles’ **event data recorders**, which store speed, braking, and throttle in the seconds before impact;
- witnesses in the other lanes.

## Who pays after a Livonia crash

**Your no-fault (PIP) claim.** Your own auto insurer pays your medical expenses up to the PIP level you chose, 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year, replacement services at $20 a day, and attendant care, regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107). If you have no policy, a resident relative’s policy pays (MCL 500.3114); if none exists, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan does (MCL 500.3172), capped at $250,000 for medical benefits. The insurer must receive written notice within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)) and must pay each bill within 30 days of reasonable proof or owe 12 percent interest (MCL 500.3142). Since the 2019 reform, PIP medical coverage is no longer unlimited unless you chose it, so the level on your declarations page matters. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance) and [Michigan no-fault guide](about:blank).

**Your claim against the at-fault driver.** For pain and suffering, you need a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment is an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life; it does not have to be permanent (MCL 500.3135(5); *McCormick v Carrier*, 2010). You can also recover lost wages beyond the three years and medical bills above your chosen PIP level (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)). The suit must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The mini tort recovers up to $3,000 in vehicle damage not covered by your policy (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)), filed in the 16th District Court if the insurer will not pay it.

**Coverage on the other side.** Many drivers carry the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)). I-96 and I-275 bring drivers from everywhere, and minimum-limits and uninsured drivers are among them. Your own **uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage** often decides what the claim is worth; we read every policy in the household before valuing the claim. Settling with the at-fault driver without your UIM insurer’s consent can forfeit the UIM claim. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Drunk drivers and dram shop.** A crash caused by a drunk driver leaving a Plymouth Road, Six Mile, or Middlebelt restaurant or bar adds a claim against the business that served a visibly intoxicated person or a minor. Written notice must go to the retailer within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, the drunk driver must be named and kept in the suit, and the suit must be filed within two years, not three (MCL 436.1801(3), (4)). See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**Government vehicles.** Livonia Police and Fire, SMART buses, Livonia Public Schools and Clarenceville buses, and county road trucks fall under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405). See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

## What to do after a Livonia crash

1. **Call 911 and get the police report.** Michigan requires a report for any crash with injury or $1,000 in damage (MCL 257.622). Livonia Police crash reports are available online through CLEMIS (payments.clemis.org); a crash on the freeway may be worked by the Michigan State Police instead. **[VERIFY]**
2. **Photograph the vehicles, plates, the other driver’s license and insurance card, the signals, and the lanes.** Note every business with a camera facing the intersection; footage is kept for days, not weeks.
3. **Get medical care the same day.** Trinity Health Livonia Hospital on Five Mile treats many of the city’s seriously injured; Corewell Health Farmington Hills, Garden City Hospital, and the Detroit Level I trauma centers treat others.
4. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** within the first week.
5. **Read your declarations page** for PIP level, UM/UIM, and collision coverage.
6. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the other driver’s insurer or sign a release.
7. **Call a lawyer within days** if fault is disputed; the camera footage and signal data are the first evidence lost.

## The courts

Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, are filed in the 16th District Court at 32765 Five Mile Road. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Third Judicial Circuit Court at the Coleman A. Young Municipal Center in Detroit. A crash on Eight Mile, or with an Oakland County defendant, may allow filing in the Oakland County Circuit Court instead (MCL 600.1629). Defect claims against MDOT go to the Court of Claims. A fatal crash is brought by the estate through the Wayne County Probate Court; see our [Livonia wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death/livonia).

## Frequently asked questions

**Which Livonia intersection has the most crashes?** In the State Police data for 2024, Middlebelt at Schoolcraft, with 95 crashes and 14 injuries, second in Wayne County behind Schoolcraft at Telegraph in Redford Township. The Livonia Police Department also lists Middlebelt and Plymouth, Five Mile and Middlebelt, and Farmington at Six Mile and Seven Mile among the city’s highest-crash intersections.

**How many car accidents happen in Livonia each year?** Livonia recorded 3,033 crashes, 869 injuries, and 10 deaths in 2024. Wayne County as a whole recorded 50,355 crashes, 16,458 injuries, and 177 deaths in 2024.

**The other driver’s insurer says I was half at fault for a left turn on Middlebelt. Is my claim over?** No. You lose the pain-and-suffering claim only if you are found more than 50 percent at fault. At 50 percent you recover half. The diagram, the signal data, the cameras, and the witnesses decide the number, not the adjuster.

**I was hurt on a Schoolcraft service drive. Whose road is that?** Generally MDOT’s, because the service drives are part of the I-96 corridor. A claim that a defect in the road contributed goes to the Court of Claims, with notice filed within 120 days. The claim against the other driver is unaffected.

**I was hit by a driver leaving a Livonia restaurant. Can I sue the restaurant?** If it served a visibly intoxicated person who then caused the crash, yes, under the dram shop act, but notice must go out within 120 days after you hire a lawyer and the suit must be filed within two years.

**The driver who hit me had no insurance. What now?** Your no-fault benefits are unaffected: your own insurer, a household member’s, or the Assigned Claims Plan pays your medical bills and lost wages. Your pain-and-suffering claim depends on your uninsured motorist coverage.

**Where would my Livonia case be filed?** Mini tort and claims up to $25,000 in the 16th District Court on Five Mile Road; injury suits over $25,000 in the Third Judicial Circuit Court in Detroit, or in some Eight Mile cases the Oakland County Circuit Court.

**How long do I have?** One year to give your no-fault insurer written notice, three years to sue the at-fault driver, two years to sue a bar, and 120 days for a notice to the City or Wayne County.
