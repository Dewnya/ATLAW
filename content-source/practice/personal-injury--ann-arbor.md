## Ann Arbor, MI Personal Injury Lawyer

If you were hurt in Ann Arbor because someone else was careless, you have a claim, and its deadlines start the day you are hurt:

- **A claim against the University of Michigan or the State,** including its buses and hospital, needs written notice in the Court of Claims within six months. A defect in a University building, including the stadium, needs written notice filed there within 120 days.
- **A road, signal, or sidewalk defect, or an injury in a City building,** needs written notice to the City of Ann Arbor within 120 days. A defect on I-94, US-23, or M-14 needs written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days.
- **After a crash,** your no-fault insurer must have written notice within one year.
- **A claim against a bar or store** that served a visibly intoxicated person or a minor needs written notice within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, and suit within two years.
- **A medical malpractice suit** against a Michigan provider requires 182 days’ notice first and must be filed within two years. A claim about care at the VA requires a federal administrative claim within two years.
- **Most other claims,** including falls in rentals, stores, and restaurants, dog bites, and crashes with private drivers, allow three years.

A personal injury lawyer’s first job is to find out which of those deadlines apply to you. The second is to make sure none runs out before your claim is paid.

## What an Ann Arbor personal injury lawyer does

The first steps. We calendar every deadline, starting with the six-month Court of Claims notice when the University or the State is involved, and send preservation letters to:

- the at-fault driver’s insurer
- the City or MDOT for signal timing records
- the University’s Division of Public Safety and Security for campus incident reports and camera footage
- TheRide or the University for bus video and records
- a landlord or property manager for maintenance requests, work orders, the City’s rental inspection reports, and smoke and carbon monoxide detector records
- a store, restaurant, or bar for footage, incident reports, receipts, and snow-removal logs, which many overwrite or discard within days
- a snow-removal or maintenance contractor, which is often a separate defendant
- a hospital or clinic for the complete chart, including audit trails

We also obtain:

- the police report from the Ann Arbor Police Department, the University’s police, the Sheriff, or the State Police, depending on where it happened
- the Ann Arbor Fire Department or Huron Valley Ambulance run sheet
- the witnesses’ names

**The first month.** We identify every policy that may pay:

- your own auto policy, or a parent’s policy for a student, for no-fault benefits
- other household policies
- the at-fault party’s liability coverage
- your uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage
- a landlord’s or property manager’s commercial policy
- a store’s, restaurant’s, or bar’s commercial and liquor liability policies
- a homeowner’s or renter’s policy, for a fall or dog bite at a private home
- the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan, where no auto policy exists (MCL 500.3172)

A student’s own health plan, including a University student health plan, may have a lien on the recovery. For a workplace injury, we identify whether a third party caused it, because the employer itself usually cannot be sued (MCL 418.131).

We file your Application for No-Fault Benefits inside the one-year window (MCL 500.3145(1)). We handle the insurer’s requests for statements, records, and examinations (MCL 500.3151). You stop talking to adjusters, and we do that instead.

**The treatment period.** The claim’s value is set mostly by the medical record, so we make sure it is complete and the bills are paid while you treat.

- **University of Michigan Health** is a Level I trauma center for adults and children.
- **Trinity Health Ann Arbor** (formerly St. Joseph Mercy Ann Arbor), in Superior Township, is a Level I trauma center.
- **The VA Ann Arbor Healthcare System** treats veterans.

 We collect records from the hospital and from your own physicians and therapists, and document lost wages with your employer.

**The demand, the suit, and the resolution.** When your condition has stabilized, we send a demand with the complete record and a valuation. The decision to accept any offer is yours (MRPC 1.2). If the insurer will not pay fair value, suit is filed in the right court:

- **15th District Court** at the Ann Arbor Justice Center, 301 East Huron Street, for claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims.
- **Washtenaw County Trial Court** (22nd Circuit) at 101 East Huron Street, for injury suits over $25,000 against private defendants, with a jury drawn from Washtenaw County. Many injury cases there go through case evaluation (MCR 2.403) or facilitation before trial.
- **The Michigan Court of Claims,** for claims against the University of Michigan, MDOT, and the State, heard by a judge without a jury.
- **The U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan,** which sits in Ann Arbor and Detroit, for claims against the VA and cases removed by out-of-state defendants.



At settlement, we resolve the liens before you are paid: health plans, Medicare, Medicaid, and workers’ compensation. Minors’ settlements and wrongful death distributions are approved by the Washtenaw County Probate Court or by the trial court (MCR 2.420; MCL 600.2922(6)). Compensation for physical injury is generally not taxable income (26 USC 104(a)(2)).

## Who the defendant is in Ann Arbor

- **The University of Michigan is the State.** The University is a constitutional state institution, and so is its health system. A claim against it goes to the Michigan Court of Claims, is decided by a judge, and requires written notice or the claim itself within six months (MCL 600.6431). That covers: a crash with a University bus or vehicle, under the motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405); an injury caused by a dangerous or defective condition in a University building, in a University building open for use by members of the public, such as a classroom building or the stadium, under the public building exception, which requires that the University knew, or should have known, of the defect and failed to fix it within a reasonable time, and written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1406); and medical malpractice at Michigan Medicine, which governmental immunity does not bar (MCL 691.1407(4)) but which follows the malpractice rules below.
- Injuries on University grounds outside a building, such as a fall on a campus walk, may fall outside every exception.
- **The City of Ann Arbor.** The City owns its streets and sidewalks, its buildings, and its parks. A road, signal, or sidewalk defect is a claim against the City, with written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1402a, 691.1404). The sidewalk claim requires that the City knew, or should have known, of the defect at least 30 days before the injury, and a defect under two inches is presumed not to be actionable. A dangerous condition in a City building falls under the public building exception (MCL 691.1406). An injury in a City park, on a trail, or at the City’s canoe liveries on the Huron River generally falls outside the exceptions unless it happened in a building or one of them applies.
- **MDOT** for I-94, US-23, M-14, and any state trunkline routes, with written notice of a road defect filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **TheRide.** A crash with a TheRide bus is a claim against the Ann Arbor Area Transportation Authority under the motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405).
- **Landlords and student rentals.** Much of the near-campus housing is older houses converted to rentals, plus apartment buildings and high-rises. A landlord must keep the premises and common areas fit for their intended use and in reasonable repair, and comply with health and safety laws (MCL 554.139). That covers: porches and balconies; stairs and railings; wiring; smoke and carbon monoxide detectors; and snow and ice the landlord controls.
- A lease clause that waives the landlord’s liability for its own negligence is not enforceable. The City’s rental housing inspections, and earlier complaints and work orders, are often strong evidence of what the landlord knew. A guest at a party in a rental may have a premises claim too. See our [tenant injury page](/personal-injury/tenant-injuries).
- **Stores, restaurants, and parking lots.** A fall in a store, a restaurant, or a parking structure is a premises claim against the owner, the tenant, or both, depending on who controlled the area. Snow and ice are often handled by a contractor, which can be a separate defendant. Since *Kandil-Elsayed v F & E Oil* (2023), an “open and obvious” hazard such as visible ice goes to comparative fault rather than ending the claim. A city ordinance requiring owners to clear the public sidewalk in front of their property does not by itself make the owner liable to a pedestrian who falls there. See our [Ann Arbor slip and fall page](/personal-injury/slip-and-fall/ann-arbor).
- **Bars, stores, and service to minors.** A bar, restaurant, or store that serves a visibly intoxicated person, or anyone under 21, is liable for the injuries that person causes (MCL 436.1801). The State Police recorded 63 alcohol-involved crashes in Ann Arbor in 2024. The claim needs written notice within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, the intoxicated person named and kept in the suit, and suit within two years. Michigan generally does not hold a private social host liable for serving adults, but furnishing alcohol to minors can create liability. See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).
- **Scooters, e-bikes, and rental companies.** A rider hurt by a car generally has no-fault benefits like a cyclist. A rider hurt by a defective rental scooter or e-bike may have a claim against the rental company or the manufacturer, and the rental app’s terms may include arbitration and liability clauses that have to be read. See our [product liability page](/personal-injury/product-liability).
- **Dog owners.** An owner is liable for a bite when the person bitten was lawfully present and did not provoke the dog (MCL 287.351). See our [Ann Arbor dog bite page](/personal-injury/dog-bites/ann-arbor).
- **Employers and third parties.** The University, its hospitals, research labs, construction sites, and the city’s restaurants and retail are major workplaces. Workers’ compensation is the remedy against the employer (MCL 418.131), but a contractor, property owner, or equipment maker who caused the injury can be sued. See our [Ann Arbor workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation/ann-arbor).
- **Nursing homes.** A claim against a nursing home may be ordinary negligence, with a three-year deadline, or medical malpractice, with two years and a 182-day notice. See our [nursing home abuse page](/personal-injury/nursing-home-abuse).

## Three hospitals, three different courts

A medical malpractice claim in Ann Arbor follows Michigan’s malpractice rules, but which court hears it depends on who the provider is:

| Provider | Who it is | Where the claim goes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| University of Michigan Health (Michigan Medicine) | The State | Court of Claims, judge only, with the six-month notice and the malpractice rules |
| Trinity Health Ann Arbor | A private hospital | Washtenaw County Trial Court, with a jury |
| VA Ann Arbor Healthcare System | The federal government | Federal Tort Claims Act: an administrative claim within two years, then federal court (28 USC 2401(b), 2675) |

For the Michigan claims, the rules are:

- a 182-day Notice of Intent before filing (MCL 600.2912b)
- an Affidavit of Merit with the complaint (MCL 600.2912d)
- two years from the act or omission, or six months from discovery (MCL 600.5805(8), 600.5838a)
- the 2026 noneconomic damages caps of $596,400 and $1,065,000 (MCL 600.1483)

## When the other driver has no insurance, or not enough

Some drivers carry only the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)), and some carry nothing.

- **Against an uninsured driver,** the claim for pain and suffering depends on your own **uninsured motorist coverage**, or a parent’s, if you are a resident relative under their policy. Without it, the claim is worth what the driver can personally pay. A hit-and-run is usually a UM claim, with short notice terms in the policy.
- **Against an underinsured driver,** your own **underinsured motorist coverage** pays the difference up to your UIM limit, if you follow the steps in order:
 1. The at-fault driver’s insurer offers its limit.
 2. You notify your own insurer and ask for its written consent to settle.
 3. Only then do you accept the limit and sign the release.
 4. The UIM claim then proceeds.

Accepting without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under many policies.

If you have no auto insurance of your own and no household policy, your no-fault benefits may come from the Assigned Claims Plan, with its $250,000 medical cap. Two rules cut the other way:

- An owner who was required to insure a car and did not is generally barred from PIP benefits (MCL 500.3113(b)).
- A driver who was operating their own uninsured vehicle cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, whoever was at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(c)).

See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

## What an Ann Arbor claim is worth, and what reduces it

A claim includes two kinds of damages:

- **Economic damages:** medical expenses, lost wages, lost earning capacity, and replacement services.
- **Noneconomic damages:** pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, and disfigurement.

For a student or a young professional, **lost earning capacity**, meaning the career the injury cut short, is often a large part of the economic loss. It is proved with education records, the field of study, and expert testimony.

There is no cap except in medical malpractice and product liability cases (MCL 600.1483, 600.2946a). In an auto case, noneconomic damages require a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment does not have to be permanent (*McCormick v Carrier*, 2010). Vehicle damage up to $3,000 can be recovered from the at-fault driver through the mini tort claim, without a threshold (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)). A premises, dog bite, or dram shop case has no threshold.

**Comparative fault.**

- **More than 50 percent at fault:** noneconomic damages are barred (MCL 600.2959; in auto cases, MCL 500.3135(2)(b)).
- **50 percent or less:** your recovery is reduced by your share. At exactly 50 percent, you recover half.
- **Economic damages** are reduced by your share but not barred.

Where more than one party caused the injury, each defendant generally pays only its own share of fault (MCL 600.2956, 600.6304). That is why the landlord, the property manager, the snow-removal contractor, and the bar all have to be identified and named. Michigan does not award punitive damages in ordinary injury cases. Exemplary damages for humiliation or outrage are available only in narrow circumstances.

You cannot sue the at-fault party’s liability insurer directly in Michigan. The claim is against the driver, owner, or business, and its insurer defends and pays.

The practical limit is often insurance:

- **The at-fault driver’s limit** defaults to $250,000/$500,000 unless the driver chose lower limits, down to the $50,000/$100,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(1), (5)).
- **Your UM and UIM coverage,** or a parent’s, fills the gap.
- **In a premises, workplace, or nursing home case,** the commercial policy of the landlord, owner, contractor, or operator pays, and those policies are often larger than an auto policy.
- **In a dram shop case,** the bar’s liquor liability policy adds to it.

 For a worker with a third-party claim, the workers’ compensation insurer has a lien on the recovery (MCL 418.827). Medicare, Medicaid, and health plan liens are negotiated the same way.

## What it costs

Michigan caps contingency fees in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery (MCR 8.121). You pay nothing up front and nothing if there is no recovery. Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted from the recovery first; the one-third fee is calculated on the remaining amount.

The agreement is in writing. Read how costs are handled if the case is lost, because that is where firms differ: filing fees, records, experts, and depositions. A promise that “you pay nothing unless we win” should say whether that includes costs, and whether “win” means the same thing as “settle.” A recovery total or a list of verdicts tells you about past cases, not yours, and says nothing unless it says when and what the case was.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in Ann Arbor?** Three years for most claims. Several deadlines are much shorter: - six months for a claim against the University of Michigan or the State, and 120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT or a building-defect notice against the University - 120 days for a notice to the City of a road, sidewalk, or building defect - one year to notify your no-fault insurer after a crash - 120 days for a dram shop notice after you hire a lawyer, and two years to sue the bar - a 182-day notice and a two-year limit for malpractice - two years for a federal claim about VA care

**I was hurt on the University of Michigan campus. Can I sue?** Often, but against the State, in the Court of Claims, before a judge, with notice within six months, or 120 days for a building defect. A crash with a University vehicle and an injury from a defective University building can be claims. A fall on a campus walk outside a building may be barred by governmental immunity.

**I fell on the porch of my rental house. Is the landlord responsible?** Usually, if the porch was in disrepair. A landlord must keep the premises in reasonable repair and fit for their use, and a lease clause waiving that duty is not enforceable. Photograph the defect, report it in writing, and keep earlier complaints.

**I was hurt at a party. Is the host responsible?** It depends. A host can be liable for a dangerous condition on the property, such as a broken railing or an overloaded porch. Michigan generally does not hold a private host liable for serving alcohol to adults who then hurt someone, but serving minors can create liability. A bar or store that served a minor or a visibly intoxicated person is liable under the dram shop act.

**Which court hears a malpractice claim against Michigan Medicine?** The Court of Claims, because the University’s health system is part of the State. A claim against Trinity Health Ann Arbor goes to the Washtenaw County Trial Court, and a claim about VA care follows federal law.

**I slipped on ice on a downtown sidewalk. Who is responsible?** It depends on whose sidewalk it was and why it was icy. The City is responsible for defects in public sidewalks, with 120 days’ notice. A business that created the hazard, or that controlled the area, can be responsible as well. An ordinance requiring owners to clear snow does not by itself make them liable to a pedestrian.

**Can I sue the other driver’s insurance company?** No. In Michigan, the claim is against the driver, and the driver’s insurer defends and pays. Your own no-fault insurer is a separate claim, for your medical bills and lost wages.

**What does an Ann Arbor personal injury attorney charge?** Michigan caps the fee in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery, with no fee if there is no recovery. Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted only from a recovery.

**Where would my Ann Arbor case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000 go to the 15th District Court at 301 East Huron Street. Injury suits over $25,000 against private defendants go to the Washtenaw County Trial Court at 101 East Huron Street. Claims against the University or the State go to the Court of Claims.

**If I was partly at fault, do I still have a claim?** Yes, unless you were more than 50 percent at fault, which bars pain-and-suffering damages. Below that, your recovery is reduced by your share, and at exactly 50 percent you recover half. No-fault benefits are paid regardless of fault.

**Do I need a lawyer whose office is in Ann Arbor?** No. What matters is that the lawyer knows the courts your case will be in, the agencies, insurers, and property owners involved, and will meet you where you are.
