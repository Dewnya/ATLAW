## Southfield Motorcycle Accident Lawyer

A motorcycle crash in Southfield is governed by a rule most riders learn only after they are hurt: under Michigan no-fault, a motorcycle is not a “motor vehicle.” That one definition decides who pays your medical bills, how much they pay, whether you have any no-fault claim at all, and how the insurer will argue the helmet question. ## Where Southfield motorcycle crashes happen

**The left-turn crash on Telegraph and Northwestern.** Telegraph at 12 Mile had 121 crashes in 2025, the most of any Oakland County intersection; drivers turning left across four lanes of oncoming traffic are the recurring account in rider cases, and under Michigan law a driver must yield to oncoming traffic before turning left (MCL 257.650). Northwestern’s office-tower driveways produce the same crash at lower speed.

**I-696 and the work zone.** Five interchanges in four miles, lane shifts, narrowed shoulders, uneven pavement edges, steel plates, and grooved concrete through the reconstruction: hazards a car absorbs and a motorcycle does not. A rider thrown by a work-zone surface condition may have a claim against the construction contractor whose traffic control or surface did not meet the required standards, and against MDOT under the highway exception in the Court of Claims. **[CONFIRM: current I-696 project status; ATTORNEY CHECK: contractor liability and MDOT exposure]**

**The Lodge and Eight Mile.** Stop-and-go traffic where the Lodge meets Northwestern, and the Detroit-line traffic on Eight Mile, where the other driver is more likely to carry the $50,000 minimum or nothing.

**The road itself.** A single-vehicle crash caused by a pothole, a pavement drop-off, or gravel is a claim against the agency with jurisdiction under the highway exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1402), and in Southfield that means three possible defendants: MDOT for Telegraph, Northwestern, the Lodge, Eight Mile, and I-696 (Court of Claims, written notice within 120 days, MCL 691.1404); the RCOC for the mile roads from Nine to Thirteen and for Lahser; and the City for Evergreen and the local streets (120 days’ written notice, MCL 691.1404). A defect that was readily apparent for 30 days or more is presumed known to the agency (MCL 691.1403). Notice to the wrong agency is not notice. **[ATTORNEY CHECK: jurisdiction list; 1403 presumption]**

## Who pays a Southfield rider’s medical bills

**If a car or truck was involved, you have no-fault benefits, but not from your motorcycle insurer.** Under MCL 500.3114(5), your Personal Injury Protection benefits come, in order, from the insurer of the owner or registrant of the motor vehicle involved; then the insurer of that vehicle’s operator; then the motor vehicle insurer of the motorcycle’s operator; then the motor vehicle insurer of the motorcycle’s owner or registrant. “Involved” does not require contact; a car that cut you off is involved even if it never touched you. If no insurer in that chain exists, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan pays (MCL 500.3172), with its $250,000 medical cap. **[ATTORNEY CHECK: 3114(5) wording; MACP eligibility for riders]**

The benefits are the ones a driver would get under MCL 500.3107: medical expenses up to the PIP limit on the policy that pays, 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each October, $20 a day in replacement services, attendant care, and mileage. Written notice is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

The first medical limit is set by a policy you did not choose. Since the 2019 reform, the PIP medical level on the policy first in priority pays first; if the driver who hit you chose $250,000, that policy pays up to $250,000, and a lower-priority policy with more coverage, such as your own car policy, may then pay the rest.

**If no car or truck was involved, no-fault does not apply at all.** A single-vehicle crash on a pothole, a steel plate, or gravel: no PIP claim. Your bills go to your health insurance and any medical coverage you bought on the motorcycle policy, and your injury claim is against whoever caused the hazard.

**Your motorcycle.** Covered by your own collision coverage, or by the mini tort claim against the at-fault driver for up to $3,000 (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)), filed in the 46th District Court if the insurer will not pay it.

## Your claim against the driver who hit you

For pain and suffering and economic losses beyond no-fault, you sue the at-fault driver if your injury is a serious impairment of body function, permanent serious disfigurement, or death (MCL 500.3135). Motorcycle injuries clear the threshold more often than car injuries: fractures, road rash requiring grafts, torn ligaments, [brain injuries](/personal-injury/brain-injury), and [spinal cord injuries](/personal-injury/spinal-cord-injury). You also recover excess economic damages above the PIP limit that applied to you (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)). Three years to sue (MCL 600.5805(2)); a fatal crash is brought by the estate (see our [Southfield wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death/southfield)).

**What the claim is worth depends on the other driver’s policy.** A commuter to the Northwestern towers may carry high limits and an umbrella; an Eight Mile driver may carry the minimum or nothing. The recovery then turns on your own **uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage**, which must be on the motorcycle policy (many car policies exclude the insured while on an owned motorcycle). See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Comparative fault is the fight.** Above 50 percent at fault you recover no noneconomic damages; below that your award is reduced by your share (MCL 500.3135(2)(b), 600.2959). Insurers argue speed, lane position, lane splitting (illegal in Michigan), and “came out of nowhere”; the UD-10, witnesses, the other driver’s phone records, business cameras along Telegraph and Northwestern, and the rider’s own camera answer it.

## The helmet question

Since 2012, Michigan has let riders 21 and older ride without a helmet if they carry at least $20,000 in first-party medical coverage and have either held a motorcycle endorsement for two years or passed an approved safety course (MCL 257.658). If you met those conditions, riding helmetless was legal, so it was not a traffic violation. In a head-injury case the insurer will argue a helmet would have reduced the injury, a damages argument for the jury, not a bar; in a leg or shoulder case it is irrelevant.

## What to do after a Southfield motorcycle crash

1. **Get medical care the same day.** Henry Ford Providence Southfield Hospital (formerly Ascension Providence) on Nine Mile and Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak treat many of the area’s seriously injured. **[CONFIRM: hospital names]**
2. **Get the Southfield Police report** (or the State Police report on I-696); a Southfield Police UD-10 is purchased through Oakland County’s CLEMIS system, and a State Police UD-10 for $15 at michigan.gov/tcps. If a car caused the crash without contact, make sure the report says so.
3. **Identify every vehicle involved**, including the one that never touched you.
4. **Photograph the bike, the scene, the road surface, the work zone if any, your gear, and your injuries.** Do not repair or sell the bike.
5. **Read your motorcycle policy for first-party medical, UM/UIM, and collision coverage**, and your car policy’s PIP level.
6. **Apply for no-fault benefits with the right insurer within the first week**, and do not give a recorded statement to the other driver’s insurer.
7. If a road defect or the work zone was involved, call within days.
## The courts and deadlines

## Frequently asked questions

**A car turned left in front of me on Telegraph and I went down. Do I have a claim?** Yes. A driver turning left must yield to oncoming traffic, and the car is “involved” under no-fault whether or not it touched you, so its owner’s insurer is first in line for your medical bills. Get the plate and witnesses before the car is gone.

**I went down on a steel plate in the I-696 construction zone. Who is responsible?** Possibly the construction contractor, if the plate or the pavement edge did not meet the required standards, and possibly MDOT, in the Court of Claims with notice filed within 120 days. No-fault does not apply if no car was involved. Photograph the surface that day.

**I hit a pothole on 12 Mile. Who do I notify?** The mile roads in Southfield are the Road Commission for Oakland County’s: written notice within 120 days stating the exact location, the defect, the injury, and the witnesses.

**I wasn’t wearing a helmet. Can I still recover?** Yes, if you were 21 or older with the required $20,000 medical coverage and either two years of endorsement or a safety course. The insurer can argue a helmet would have reduced a head injury, which goes to damages, not to whether you have a claim.

**How long do I have?** One year to notify the responsible no-fault insurer, three years to sue the at-fault driver, 120 days for a notice to the City, the RCOC, or the State, and two years to sue a bar, with notice to the bar within 120 days after you hire a lawyer.
