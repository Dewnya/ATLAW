## Westland, MI Truck Accident Lawyer

Westland’s heavy trucks travel three kinds of roads.

**The routes in from I-275.** I-275 runs just west of the city, with interchanges at Ford Road and at Michigan Avenue nearby. Trucks leave the freeway there and head east into Westland.

**Michigan Avenue and Ford Road.**

- **Michigan Avenue (US-12)** runs along the southern edge, a freight route lined with industrial, automotive, and commercial sites in and near the city.
- **Ford Road (M-153)** runs through the center, five to seven lanes of stores, restaurants, and apartment complexes supplied by box trucks and tractor-trailers.

**The county roads and the neighborhoods.** Wayne Road, Merriman, Middlebelt, Newburgh, Cherry Hill, Joy, and Warren carry delivery trucks, construction trucks, garbage trucks, and school buses between the arterials and the subdivisions.

In 2024, according to the Michigan State Police, 3,916 heavy trucks and buses were involved in crashes in Wayne County, compared with 2,081 in Oakland County and 1,257 in Macomb. Statewide, 110 people were killed and 3,613 injured in heavy truck and bus crashes. Westland recorded 1,604 crashes of all kinds, 257 of them on Ford Road and 32 on Michigan Avenue.

A truck crash is not a car crash with a larger vehicle:

- **The rules are federal.** The driver and the carrier are governed by federal safety regulations, and a violation is evidence of negligence.
- **The insurance is larger.** The carrier must carry $750,000 or more in insurance.
- **The records expire.** The driver’s electronic logs and the truck’s engine data, the records that prove the case, can be lawfully discarded within months.
- **The other side moves fast.** The carrier’s insurer will have an investigator on the case within days.

## Where Westland truck crashes happen

**The I-275 ramps and the first miles east.** A truck leaving I-275 at Ford Road or Michigan Avenue is slowing, changing lanes, and turning toward its destination. The crashes are:

- rear-end and underride crashes where traffic backs up from the ramp signals
- lane changes into a car in the blind spot on the right side of the cab
- trucks turning right from the ramp across a lane of traffic
- wide left turns into driveways in the first mile

A car that strikes the side or rear of a trailer can go under it. That is an **underride** crash, which is often fatal or catastrophic. Trailers must carry rear impact guards that meet federal standards (49 CFR 393.86). A missing, damaged, or substandard guard is evidence against the carrier and the trailer’s owner.

**Michigan Avenue.** US-12 carries tractor-trailers, flatbeds, and car haulers along the city’s southern edge. The crashes are:

- wide turns into industrial and commercial driveways that catch a car on the inside of the turn
- slow left turns across oncoming lanes into a plant or yard
- trucks pulling out of a driveway into traffic that cannot stop
- rear-end crashes at the signals, where a loaded truck needs far more distance to stop
- trucks parked or staged on the shoulder without warning devices (49 CFR 392.22)

On designated Michigan routes, heavy trucks can weigh up to 164,000 pounds, about twice the federal Interstate limit. When an industrial site’s driveway design or truck-staging practice puts trucks in the road, the site’s operator or owner can share responsibility with the carrier.

**Ford Road and the retail corridor.** Box trucks, beverage trucks, and tractor-trailers supply the stores, restaurants, and the Westland Shopping Center. The crashes are:

- wide right turns into a store or mall driveway
- trucks backing into and out of loading areas and service drives
- trucks double-parked or stopped in a lane for a delivery
- pedestrians struck by a truck in a parking lot or crossing between the apartment complexes and the stores

Where a business’s delivery arrangements routinely put trucks in the street or the lot’s traffic lanes, the business can share responsibility. See our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents).

**Apartment complexes, construction, and garbage trucks.** Moving trucks, furniture trucks, and package vans serve the city’s many apartment complexes. Construction trucks serve new building and road work. Garbage and recycling trucks work the subdivisions. The crashes are backing crashes, crashes with vehicles stopped in a lane, and children and pedestrians struck by a truck pulling away. Delivery vans are often operated by contracted delivery companies rather than by the retailer named on the package.

**Lost and spilled loads.** Cargo, scrap, gravel, or debris that falls from a truck is a violation of the federal cargo securement rules (49 CFR 393.100 and following) and of Michigan law (MCL 257.720). The carrier and the company that loaded the truck can both be responsible.

**Whose road it is.** Westland is a city, and the road owner can be any of three agencies:

- **MDOT** for Ford Road, Michigan Avenue, and I-275. A road-defect claim against the State goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **Wayne County** for Wayne Road and other major roads. Written notice is due within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404).
- **The City of Westland** for the subdivision streets. Written notice is due within 120 days.

## Who is responsible, and who pays

**The carrier, not just the driver.** The motor carrier is responsible for its drivers’ compliance with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations. It is liable for a driver’s negligence in the course of employment. Michigan applies the federal rules to intrastate carriers (MCL 480.11a). A local hauler running between a Westland yard and a plant down Michigan Avenue is held to the same standards as an interstate carrier.

These are the rules that decide truck cases most often:

| Rule | 49 CFR | What it means for the case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hours of service | Part 395 | 11 hours driving in a 14-hour window after 10 hours off; 60/70-hour weekly limits; recorded on an electronic logging device. Some short-haul drivers are exempt from logs but not from the limits. |
| Driver qualification | Part 391 | CDL, medical certificate, driving record, background check. Negligent hiring and retention are claims against the carrier. |
| Post-crash testing | Part 382 | Alcohol within 8 hours and drugs within 32 hours after a fatal crash, or after an injury or tow-away crash with a citation. |
| Inspection and maintenance | Part 396 | Pre-trip inspections, defect reports, maintenance records. Brake, tire, lighting, and reflective-tape cases are maintenance cases. |
| Cargo securement | 393.100–393.136 | Loads must be contained or secured so they cannot shift or fall. A spilled load is evidence of a violation. |
| Equipment: rear impact guards, lighting, reflectors | Part 393 | Underride guards (393.86) and conspicuity marking. A trailer that cannot be seen at night or lacks a working guard is a defective trailer. |
| Stopped vehicles; hazardous conditions; phones | 392.22, 392.14, 392.82 | Warning devices for a stopped truck; reduce speed or stop in snow, ice, and fog; no handheld phone use. |
| Minimum insurance | Part 387 | $750,000 general freight; $1 million oil; $5 million hazardous materials. |

**The owner of the truck or trailer.** In Michigan, a vehicle’s owner is liable for its negligent operation by someone driving with the owner’s consent (MCL 257.401). When the tractor, the trailer, and the driver belong to different companies, each may be a defendant, although a company in the business of renting or leasing vehicles is generally shielded from owner liability by federal law (49 USC 30106).

**Michigan no-fault still applies to you.** Your own auto insurer pays these benefits regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107):

- your medical bills
- 85 percent of lost wages, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- replacement services
- attendant care

Written notice is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). If you have no household policy, a vehicle occupant claims from the insurer of the vehicle they were in, and then the Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3114(4), 500.3172). A pedestrian or cyclist with no household policy claims through the Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3115).

Your claim against the driver and the carrier is for pain and suffering and for losses beyond no-fault. It requires a threshold injury (MCL 500.3135) and must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). Michigan’s comparative fault rule is modified, not pure:

- **More than 50 percent at fault:** you cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages.
- **50 percent or less:** your share reduces them (MCL 500.3135(2)(b); MCL 600.2959).

The noneconomic damages caps apply to medical malpractice and to product liability claims, such as a claim against the maker of a failed part (MCL 600.1483, 600.2946a), not to the claim against the driver and carrier.

The difference from a car case is the money available: the carrier’s $750,000-plus policy, often with excess coverage above it. If the carrier’s coverage still falls short, your own underinsured motorist coverage may add to it. Your insurer’s written consent is needed before you accept the carrier’s limit. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

**Other defendants.**

- the shipper or broker that loaded the truck or hired an unsafe carrier
- the industrial site, store, or mall whose driveway, staging, or delivery practice contributed
- the general contractor on a construction site whose trucks were involved
- the maintenance contractor
- the manufacturer of a failed component or a defective guard ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability))
- for a government truck (MDOT, Wayne County, the City of Westland, SMART, or a school bus), the agency, under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405)

**If you were the truck driver.** Workers’ compensation pays your benefits, and your employer usually cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). If you were injured in your employer’s truck, no-fault benefits generally come from the insurer of that truck (MCL 500.3114(3)). They are coordinated with comp, which is paid first (MCL 500.3109(1)). You can also sue a third party, such as another driver or a business whose dock or yard caused the crash. See our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).

## The evidence, and how fast it disappears

Carriers must keep two kinds of records for a limited time:

- hours-of-service records, for six months (49 CFR 395.8)
- driver inspection reports, for three months (49 CFR 396.11)

After that, the regulations no longer require them. Other evidence goes faster:

- store, mall, gas-station, and industrial-site cameras along Ford Road and Michigan Avenue, and dash cameras, overwrite in days to weeks
- the truck’s engine control module and event data recorder can be cleared when the truck is repaired and returned to service
- a spilled load is swept up within hours
- a damaged rear guard can be replaced before anyone measures it

The first act in a Westland truck case is a **preservation letter**. It goes to the carrier, its insurer, the tractor and trailer owners, and the shipper or broker. Where the crash happened at a driveway, a loading area, or a construction site, it also goes to the business, the property owner, or the contractor. It demands:

- the ELD and engine data
- dash camera footage
- the driver’s qualification file and post-crash test results
- dispatch records, bills of lading, gate logs, and delivery records
- maintenance files
- the truck and trailer themselves, for inspection of the brakes, lights, load securement, and rear guard

Once a party knows a claim is coming, destroying that evidence exposes it to spoliation sanctions.

## What to do after a Westland truck crash

1. **Call 911 and get medical care the same day.** Garden City Hospital, on Inkster Road just across the city line, and Corewell Health Wayne Hospital have emergency departments. The most serious injuries may go to a trauma center on the State’s list, such as Trinity Health Livonia or Corewell Health Dearborn (Level II), or a Level I center in Detroit.
2. **Photograph the truck.** Get these shots: the cab and trailer; the USDOT number and carrier name on the door; both plates, and the trailer number if it differs; the load and anything that fell from it; the rear guard and reflective tape; and the driveway, loading area, or ramp if the truck was entering or leaving one.
3. **Get the police report.** Crashes in the city are worked by the Westland Police Department, and crashes on I-275 by the State Police. A serious crash may produce a separate commercial vehicle inspection report from the State Police.
4. **Do not speak to the carrier’s insurer or its investigator.**
5. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** promptly.
6. **Call a lawyer within days,** so the preservation letter goes out before the records expire and the truck is repaired.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many truck accidents happen in Westland?** The State’s county profile does not break out truck crashes by city. Countywide, 3,916 heavy trucks and buses were involved in Wayne County crashes in 2024. Statewide, 15,888 crashes involved a heavy truck or bus, and 110 people were killed.

**A semi swung wide turning into a driveway on Michigan Avenue and I hit it. Whose fault is that?** It depends on the facts. A truck driver must make a wide turn safely and signal it, and a driver who turns across lanes without checking can be at fault. A driver who passes a truck that is signaling a turn can carry fault too. The trailer’s lights and markings, and the truck’s dash camera, are often the deciding evidence.

**A delivery truck was stopped in the lane on Ford Road and I hit it, or swerved and hit something else. Whose fault is that?** It depends. A truck stopped in a travel lane without warning, or where stopping is prohibited, can carry fault, and so can a business whose deliveries routinely put it there. Your own speed and attention matter too.

**My car went under the back of a trailer at the I-275 ramp. Is that the truck’s fault?** It may be partly or wholly. Trailers must carry rear impact guards built to federal standards. A guard that is missing, bent, or too high to stop a car is evidence against the carrier and the trailer’s owner. The trailer has to be inspected before it is repaired.

**Is there a cap on what I can recover from a trucking company?** Not on pain and suffering against the driver or carrier. Michigan’s damages caps apply to medical malpractice and product liability claims. In practice, the limit is the insurance available: the carrier’s policy, any excess policy, and your own underinsured coverage.

**Why does the lawyer need to act within days?** Because the records expire. Hours-of-service logs must be kept for six months and inspection reports for three. Camera footage lasts days or weeks. The truck’s electronic data and the trailer’s damage can disappear when the truck is repaired.
