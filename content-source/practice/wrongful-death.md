## Michigan Wrongful Death Lawyer

When a family member dies because someone else was careless, Michigan law gives the family a claim. It does not give it in the way most people expect. The claim belongs to the estate, not to the grieving spouse or child directly; it must be brought by a court-appointed personal representative; the recovery is divided among family members by a probate judge; and the deadline depends on what caused the death. Families who do not know these rules lose time they do not have.

## Who can file a wrongful death claim in Michigan

Michigan’s Wrongful Death Act (MCL 600.2922) allows a claim whenever a death was caused by a wrongful act or negligence that would have supported an injury lawsuit had the person lived.

**Only the personal representative of the estate can file it.** Not the spouse, not the children, not the parents, unless one of them is appointed personal representative. If the person left a will naming an executor, that person petitions the probate court for appointment. If there was no will, a family member (usually the spouse or an adult child) petitions. The probate court in the county where the person lived issues “letters of authority,” and the personal representative then has the standing to bring the claim and to hire a lawyer for the estate. ATLAW handles the probate filing as part of the case. **[CONFIRM: that the firm opens the estate itself rather than referring to probate counsel]**

**Who shares in the recovery** is set by the Act (MCL 600.2922(3)): the spouse, children, descendants, parents, grandparents, brothers and sisters, the children of the deceased’s spouse, and anyone named in the will. Within 30 days of filing the lawsuit, the personal representative must notify every one of these people in writing that the claim exists and that they may be entitled to share in it (600.2922(4)).

**Unborn children.** Michigan allows a wrongful death claim for the death of an embryo or fetus caused by a wrongful act against the mother (MCL 600.2922a), with exceptions for lawful medical procedures.

## What the family can recover

The Act lists the damages (MCL 600.2922(6)), and a jury or a settlement allocates them:

- **Medical and hospital expenses** from the injury to the death.
- **Funeral and burial expenses.**
- **Conscious pain and suffering** the person experienced between the injury and death. If the person was aware and in pain for minutes, hours, or days, this is a substantial element; the medical record and witnesses establish it.
- **Loss of financial support**: the income, benefits, and household contributions the person would have provided over a working life, calculated with an economist for a working-age decedent.
- **Loss of society and companionship**: the family’s loss of the relationship itself, which Michigan recognizes for each eligible family member. There is no cap on this element in Michigan, except in medical malpractice cases.
- **Loss of services**: childcare, household work, care for an aging parent, whatever the person did that now has to be replaced.

In a medical malpractice death, non-economic damages are capped (MCL 600.1483): $596,400 for most cases and $1,065,000 for the highest tier in 2026, adjusted each January. **[VERIFY: 2026 cap figures against the Treasury memo]** Economic damages are not capped.

## How the recovery is divided among family members

This is the part of a Michigan wrongful death case that surprises families. The money does not simply go to the estate and pass under the will. After a settlement or verdict, the personal representative files a motion in the probate court (or the trial court) proposing how to divide the proceeds among the eligible family members, with notice to each of them. If everyone agrees, the court approves the distribution. If they do not, the court holds a hearing and decides, based on each person’s relationship with the deceased and the loss each suffered (MCL 600.2922(6)). A spouse and minor children ordinarily receive the largest shares; an estranged adult child may receive little. Attorney fees and case costs are approved by the court at the same hearing.

Because the division is decided by a court and not by the personal representative alone, families should talk to a lawyer before any family member signs anything with an insurer. An insurer that settles with one family member has not settled the claim.

## Fatal car, truck, and motorcycle crashes: two claims, not one

In 2024, 350 people were killed in traffic crashes in Wayne, Oakland, Macomb, and Washtenaw counties, and 1,099 statewide (Michigan State Police, 2024 Traffic Crash Facts). When a death is caused by a motor vehicle crash, the family has two separate claims under Michigan law.

**No-fault survivor’s loss benefits** (MCL 500.3108) are paid by the deceased person’s own auto insurer, or by the insurer that would have paid their PIP benefits, regardless of fault. For three years after the death, dependents receive the economic support the person would have provided, up to the same monthly maximum that applies to wage loss ($7,455 a month for policies renewing on or after October 1, 2026), plus $20 a day in replacement services. The insurer also pays funeral and burial expenses up to the policy’s limit, which Michigan requires to be at least $1,750 and allows up to $5,000 (MCL 500.3107(1)(a)). Written notice to the insurer within one year is required (MCL 500.3145); the family should not wait for the wrongful death case to resolve before claiming these.

**The wrongful death lawsuit** against the at-fault driver is separate, and recovers everything no-fault does not: pain and suffering before death, loss of society and companionship, and economic support beyond the three years and above the monthly cap. In a crash, the injury threshold does not apply to a death; the family may sue for non-economic damages without proving a serious impairment (MCL 500.3135(1)). Comparative fault still applies: if the deceased was more than 50% at fault, non-economic damages are barred.

For a death caused by a **drunk driver**, the bar or store that served the driver while visibly intoxicated may be liable under Michigan’s dram shop act (MCL 436.1801), with written notice to the retailer required within 120 days and a two-year deadline.

## Other causes of wrongful death in metro Detroit

- **Medical malpractice**: a death from a missed diagnosis, surgical error, or hospital negligence. The malpractice rules apply: two-year deadline, 182-day Notice of Intent, affidavit of merit, same-specialty expert, damages cap. Our medical malpractice page explains them.
- **Nursing home neglect**: falls, pressure sores, dehydration, medication errors in the 85 Medicare-certified nursing homes in the cities ATLAW serves.
- **Workplace deaths**: a worker’s family receives workers’ compensation death benefits from the employer (500 weeks of wage benefits to dependents, plus up to $6,000 in burial costs, MCL 418.321 and 418.345) and cannot sue the employer, but can sue any third party whose negligence caused the death: a contractor, a manufacturer, a property owner.
- **Premises deaths**: falls, fires, drownings, negligent security. The 2023 change to Michigan’s open-and-obvious rule applies.
- **Defective products**: vehicles, parts, drugs, equipment.
- **Deaths on public property or involving a government vehicle**: written notice to the government body within 120 days (MCL 691.1404), or the claim is barred regardless of fault.

The savings provision is the rule families most often misunderstand. It gives a personal representative time to be appointed and to sue, but it has a hard outer limit and courts apply it strictly. Do not rely on it; open the estate and file inside the ordinary deadline.

## What to do after a wrongful death in Michigan

1. **Request the death certificate and the medical examiner’s report**, if there was one. For a crash, get the police report (UD-10) and the names of any witnesses.
2. **Preserve evidence**: the vehicle (do not let the insurer salvage it), the scene photographs, the medical records, the person’s phone. For a truck crash, a preservation letter to the carrier within days. For a nursing home death, the chart and the facility’s incident reports.
3. **Notify the no-fault insurer in writing** if a vehicle was involved; survivor’s loss benefits start then, not after the lawsuit.
4. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the at-fault party’s insurer and do not sign any release, however it is described. An insurer offering to “cover the funeral” in exchange for a signature is trying to settle the claim for the price of a funeral.
5. **Open the estate.** A lawyer handles the probate petition; it is the first legal step and nothing else can happen until it is done.
6. **Keep a record** of the person’s income, benefits, and the things they did for the household. These become the economic damages.

## Frequently asked questions about Michigan wrongful death claims

**Who can file a wrongful death lawsuit in Michigan?** Only the personal representative of the estate, appointed by the probate court (MCL 600.2922). A spouse, child, or parent can be appointed, but must be, before the claim can be filed.

**Who receives the money?** The spouse, children, descendants, parents, grandparents, siblings, stepchildren, and anyone named in the will may share. The probate court approves the division after a hearing, based on each person’s relationship and loss.

**How long do we have?** Three years from the death for most claims; two years (with a six-month Notice of Intent) for medical malpractice; one year to claim no-fault survivor’s loss benefits; 120 days to notify a government body. The savings provision can extend the deadline for an estate in limited circumstances, but should not be relied on.

**My family member died in a car accident. What does no-fault pay?** Survivor’s loss benefits: up to three years of the economic support the person provided, capped at the monthly maximum, plus $20 a day in replacement services and funeral expenses up to the policy limit. These are separate from, and in addition to, the wrongful death claim against the at-fault driver.

**Is there a cap on wrongful death damages in Michigan?** No, except in medical malpractice cases, where non-economic damages are capped under MCL 600.1483. Economic damages are never capped.

**Can we recover for pain and suffering?** Yes, for the pain and suffering the person consciously experienced between the injury and death, and separately for the family’s own loss of society and companionship.

**What if there was no will?** The probate court appoints a personal representative, usually the spouse or an adult child, under Michigan’s intestacy rules. The wrongful death claim proceeds the same way.

**What if my family member was partly at fault?** The recovery is reduced by their percentage of fault. If they were more than 50% at fault, the family cannot recover non-economic damages. Economic damages are still recoverable, reduced by the share.

**Can we sue if the death happened at work?** Not the employer, in most cases; workers’ compensation death benefits apply instead. But the family can sue any third party whose negligence caused the death, and those claims are often the larger recovery.
