## Personal Injury Law Firm Serving Waterford, MI

Waterford is a charter township of approximately 70,000 residents west of Pontiac, built around 34 lakes and crossed by two major state highways: M-59 (Highland Road) and Dixie Highway (US-24). Local roads including Walton Boulevard, Sashabaw Road, Williams Lake Road, Pontiac Lake Road, Elizabeth Lake Road, Cass Lake Road, and Airport Road connect its neighborhoods, shopping areas, and recreational destinations. Oakland County International Airport is also located in the township. Waterford’s injuries follow that geography: high-speed crashes on M-59 and Dixie, intersection crashes where the lake roads meet them, boating and dock injuries on Cass, Elizabeth, Williams, and Loon lakes in summer, and snowmobile and ice injuries in winter. According to Michigan Traffic Crash Facts, Waterford Township recorded 1,546 traffic crashes in 2024, resulting in 497 people injured and three fatalities. The total included 367 injury crashes and two fatal crashes.

Waterford is also a township, and that changes an injury claim in a way no competing page mentions: Michigan townships do not own public roads. M-59 and Dixie Highway are MDOT’s; Walton, Sashabaw, Williams Lake, Pontiac Lake, Elizabeth Lake, Cass Lake, Airport Road, and the rest are the Road Commission for Oakland County’s. A claim for a signal, sight-line, or pavement defect goes to the state or the Road Commission, with a six-month or 120-day notice, never to the township. The township’s own police, fire, buildings, parks, and lake access sites are a different matter.

## Where Waterford injuries happen

**M-59 (Highland Road).** The township’s east-west spine, five to seven lanes from Telegraph and the Pontiac line west past Crescent Lake, Airport Road, Williams Lake, and Pontiac Lake Road toward White Lake and Highland, lined with retail and carrying commuter traffic between Pontiac and the western county at speed. Left-turn and red-light crashes at the Airport Road, Williams Lake, Crescent Lake, and Pontiac Lake signals, rear-end crashes in the stacked turn lanes, and pedestrian crashes at the strips. M-59 is MDOT’s.

**Dixie Highway (US-24).** The northwest diagonal from Telegraph through the township to Clarkston and I-75, lined with dealerships, retail, and the former Summit Place site, and carrying the gravel trains from the northern pits and the plant freight to Pontiac. Intersection crashes at Walton, Sashabaw, Scott Lake, and Andersonville, and turning-truck crashes at the dealerships and the industrial frontage. Dixie is MDOT’s. **Walton, Sashabaw, and the lake roads.** Walton Boulevard across the north of the township to Clarkston; Sashabaw north to I-75; Williams Lake, Pontiac Lake, Elizabeth Lake, Cass Lake, Cooley Lake, Hatchery, Scott Lake, and Airport Road threading between the lakes. Two-lane and five-lane roads with curves, hills, limited sight lines at the lake-access drives, and the intersections with M-59 and Dixie; the crashes are run-off-road, head-on, and intersection crashes, with motorcycle crashes concentrated on the lake roads in summer. **The lakes.** Waterford’s lakes, including Cass Lake, Elizabeth Lake, Williams Lake, Loon Lake, Watkins Lake, Pontiac Lake, and Crescent Lake, are used for boating, personal watercraft, fishing, and other recreational activities. ## What is different about a Waterford claim

**Road responsibility and governmental immunity.** M-59 and Dixie Highway are state highways under MDOT jurisdiction, while many other public roads in Waterford are maintained by the Road Commission for Oakland County. When a crash allegedly results from an unsafe roadway condition, identifying the responsible agency is important, but responsibility for the road does not automatically establish liability. Michigan’s governmental immunity laws limit highway-defect claims to circumstances covered by a recognized statutory exception. Under MCL 691.1404, qualifying highway-defect claims generally require notice within 120 days of the injury, with specific requirements for the notice’s contents and delivery. Claims involving state highways also require compliance with applicable Court of Claims procedures.

**The township’s own agencies.** Claims involving Waterford Township vehicles, buildings, parks, or recreational facilities may raise separate questions under Michigan’s governmental immunity laws. MCL 691.1405 provides an exception for certain injuries caused by negligent operation of government-owned motor vehicles, while MCL 691.1406 addresses qualifying dangerous or defective conditions in public buildings. Accidents involving public school buses, Road Commission vehicles, county parks, or state lake-access facilities may be subject to different governmental responsibilities and immunity rules. The particular facts determine whether an exception applies.

**Coverage.** Michigan’s no-fault system generally provides eligible personal injury protection benefits for accident-related medical expenses and certain wage losses without requiring proof that another driver was at fault. The responsible insurer is determined under Michigan’s statutory priority rules, and medical coverage depends on the applicable policy and coverage elections. In qualifying circumstances, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan may provide benefits when no applicable insurer is available under the priority rules. A separate claim against an at-fault driver may be available for noneconomic damages when the requirements of MCL 500.3135 are satisfied. Liability limits and any available uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage can affect the amount ultimately collected. See our [Waterford car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents), [motorcycle accident page](/personal-injury/motorcycle-accidents), [no-fault insurance guide](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance), and [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**The 51st District and the Sixth Circuit.** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims for vehicle damage (MCL 500.3135(3)(e), up to $3,000), are filed in the 51st District Court at 5100 Civic Center Drive in Waterford, which serves Waterford Township. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac, a few minutes east; wrongful death estates (MCL 600.2922) and minors’ settlements (MCR 2.420) to the Oakland County Probate Court on the same campus.

**The hospitals.** Waterford residents may receive treatment at regional medical facilities such as McLaren Oakland and Trinity Health Oakland in Pontiac or Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak, a Level I adult trauma center. Emergency transport decisions depend on the patient’s medical condition, the circumstances of the emergency, and available medical services. Hospital records, diagnostic imaging, specialist evaluations, and emergency medical services reports can help document the nature and extent of accident-related injuries.

## Frequently asked questions

**I was hurt because of a bad signal or pothole in Waterford. Do I sue the township?** Not necessarily. Roadway responsibility depends on which government agency has legal authority over the location and whether a recognized exception to governmental immunity applies. M-59 and Dixie Highway are state routes under MDOT jurisdiction, while many other public roads in Waterford are maintained by the Road Commission for Oakland County. Under MCL 691.1404, qualifying highway-defect claims generally require notice within 120 days, with specific requirements for claims involving the state. Not every roadway hazard creates a valid claim, so the facts and responsible agency must be evaluated promptly.

**I was injured in a boating accident on Cass Lake or Elizabeth Lake. Is that a personal injury case?** A boating or personal watercraft accident may support an injury claim when the circumstances establish a legally responsible party. Michigan’s Marine Safety Act regulates watercraft operation, and negligence or premises liability rules may also apply depending on how the accident occurred. The type of insurance available depends on the policies and circumstances involved. If alcohol contributed to the accident, a qualifying dram shop claim may also be considered when Michigan’s statutory requirements are met. Filing deadlines and any special notice requirements depend on the particular claim.

**I was on a motorcycle and a car hit me on Williams Lake Road. Who pays my medical bills?** Michigan has special no-fault insurance rules for motorcyclists. Under MCL 500.3114(5), the first priority is generally the insurer of the owner or registrant of the involved motor vehicle, followed by the insurer of that vehicle’s operator. The law then identifies certain motor vehicle policies associated with the motorcycle operator or owner. The applicable policy, coverage elections, and other statutory rules determine which benefits are available. In some circumstances, additional benefits may be pursued from a lower-priority insurer after higher-priority coverage has been exhausted. A separate injury claim against an at-fault driver may also be available. If no qualifying motor vehicle was involved, automobile PIP generally would not apply through the motorcycle priority provision, although separate motorcycle medical-benefits coverage may be available.

**Where would my Waterford injury case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, in the 51st District Court on Civic Center Drive; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court in Pontiac.

**Which hospital will I be taken to from a Waterford crash?** Usually McLaren Oakland or Trinity Health Oakland in Pontiac; Corewell Health Royal Oak for the most severe injuries.

**Who pays my medical bills after a car crash in Waterford?** Your own no-fault insurer, regardless of fault, up to your PIP level, after you file an Application for No-Fault Benefits within one year; a household member’s insurer if you have none; the Assigned Claims Plan if no policy applies.

**How much does it cost to hire you?** Nothing up front. Michigan caps contingency fees in personal injury cases at one-third of the recovery (MCR 8.121). Your initial consultation is free. For qualifying personal injury cases handled on a contingency-fee basis, there is no attorney fee unless we recover money for you. ATLAW’s written representation agreement explains the fee calculation and treatment of case expenses in accordance with Michigan’s applicable rules.

**How long do I have?** Three years for most injury suits, one year to notify your no-fault insurer in writing, and as little as 120 days for a claim against the Road Commission or the township.
