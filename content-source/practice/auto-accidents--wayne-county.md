## Wayne County Car Accident Lawyer

A car crash in Wayne County is a Michigan no-fault claim with Wayne County problems added. The claim itself follows state law: your own insurer pays your medical bills and lost wages regardless of fault, and you can sue the at-fault driver only for a threshold injury. The problems are local. Wayne County has a larger share of uninsured and minimally insured drivers than any other Michigan county, more government-owned vehicles on the road, heavily traveled freeways and more pedestrian crashes than the rest of the metro area combined, and the Third Circuit Court in Detroit, whose docket and practices differ from the suburban counties’. In 2024 the Michigan State Police recorded 50,355 crashes in Wayne County, 16,458 people injured, 926 of them seriously, and 177 killed.

## Who pays after a Wayne County crash

Michigan no-fault gives every injured person two claims, and in Wayne County the first one matters more than anywhere else in the state.

**Your no-fault (PIP) claim, against your own insurer.** Under MCL 500.3107, your own auto policy pays your medical expenses up to the PIP level you selected, 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years (capped at $7,201 a month for crashes in the year beginning October 1, 2025), replacement services at $20 a day, and attendant care, regardless of who caused the crash. If you have no policy, the order is your spouse’s or a resident relative’s policy (MCL 500.3114), and if none exists, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3172), which assigns an insurer and caps medical benefits at $250,000. The insurer must receive **written notice within one year** of the crash (MCL 500.3145(1)) and must pay each bill within 30 days of reasonable proof or owe 12 percent interest (MCL 500.3142). Our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance) covers the application, the benefits, and the denials.

**Your claim against the at-fault driver.** For pain and suffering and economic losses beyond no-fault, you must have a threshold injury under MCL 500.3135: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement. The deadline is three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). Your recovery is reduced by your share of fault and barred if you were more than 50 percent at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(b), 600.2959). For vehicle damage, the **mini tort** under 3135(3)(e) recovers up to $3,000 from the at-fault driver.

## What makes a Wayne County crash claim different

**The other driver is more likely to be uninsured.** Michigan’s 2019 no-fault reform was driven largely by Detroit, where state regulators estimated that about 60 percent of drivers were uninsured before the reform. Rates have fallen since, but Wayne County still has a far larger share of uninsured and minimum-limits drivers than Oakland or Macomb. For your claim this means three things. More Wayne County no-fault claims go through the Assigned Claims Plan. More at-fault drivers carry the $50,000 per person minimum under MCL 500.3009(1), or nothing, so the pain-and-suffering claim is worth the policy rather than the injury. And your own **uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage** is more often the real recovery; we check every policy in the household before we value a Wayne County claim. If you were hit by an uninsured driver or a hit-and-run driver, see our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims) and notify your insurer in writing now; UM policies carry short notice conditions.

**The other vehicle is more likely to be a government vehicle.** DDOT buses, SMART buses, Detroit and 42 other municipal police departments, the Wayne County Sheriff, county road crews, and MDOT. A crash involving one of these government-owned vehicles may support a claim under the Governmental Tort Liability Act’s motor vehicle exception, MCL 691.1405, when the injury results from negligent vehicle operation. Separate statutory requirements may apply; for example, certain tort claims against SMART require written notice within 60 days under MCL 124.419, and the vehicle’s video is routinely overwritten within weeks unless requested. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents). **[ATTORNEY CHECK]**

**The road itself may be the defendant.** A crash caused by a road defect on a Wayne County road is a claim against the Wayne County Department of Public Services; on a Detroit street, against the City; on a state trunkline (the freeways, Telegraph, Michigan Avenue, Eight Mile, Woodward), against MDOT in the Court of Claims. Written notice is due within 120 days to the county or city (MCL 691.1404) and within six months to the state (MCL 600.6431).

**The other driver may be from Ontario or Ohio.** The Ambassador Bridge, the tunnel, and I-75 and I-94 bring out-of-state and Canadian drivers through the county daily. A non-resident in a vehicle not insured in Michigan generally has no Michigan PIP claim (MCL 500.3113(c)), and a Michigan resident hit by an Ontario driver is dealing with an insurer under a different system; which policy applies and where suit can be filed are the first questions.

**The crash is more likely to involve a pedestrian.** Wayne County had 637 pedestrian-involved crashes in 2024, 40 of them fatal, two-thirds of them in Detroit. A pedestrian struck by a car is covered by no-fault, through their own or a household policy first, then the insurer of the vehicle’s owner, then its operator, then the Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3115); see our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents).

**The case will be in the Third Circuit.** Most Wayne County injury suits over $25,000 are filed in the Third Judicial Circuit Court at the Coleman A. Young Municipal Center in Detroit, which has its own case evaluation and facilitation practices and one of the largest civil dockets in the state. Mini tort and other small claims go to the district court for the community: the 36th in Detroit, the 19th in Dearborn, the 16th in Livonia, the 23rd in Taylor, the 18th in Westland, the 35th in Canton and Plymouth, among the county’s twenty district courts. Venue is determined under the statutory order of priority in MCL 600.1629, generally beginning with the county where the original injury occurred and where a defendant resides, conducts business, or maintains a registered office. ## What to do after a Wayne County crash

1. **Stop, call 911 if anyone is hurt, and call police.** Michigan law requires a report for any crash with injury or $1,000 in damage (MCL 257.622). In Detroit, if an officer does not respond to the scene, promptly contact the Detroit Police Department for instructions on filing the required crash report. The report is the UD-10, purchased for $15 at michigan.gov/tcps.
2. **Photograph everything and get witnesses** before the scene clears.
3. **Get medical care the same day** and tell the provider it was a car accident. Detroit Receiving, Henry Ford, Corewell Health Dearborn, Trinity Health Livonia, Henry Ford Wyandotte, and Garden City Hospital see most of the county’s crash patients.
4. **Report to your own insurer and request the Application for No-Fault Benefits.** File it within the first week, not the eleventh month.
5. **Check your declarations page for UM/UIM and collision coverage.** In Wayne County this is the step that decides what the claim is worth.
6. **Do not give a recorded statement to the other driver’s insurer**, sign a medical authorization, or sign a release.
7. **Stay off social media** about the crash and your activities.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many car accidents happen in Wayne County each year?** 50,355 in 2024, with 16,458 people injured and 177 killed, according to the Michigan State Police. That is about one in six Michigan crashes and nearly one in four crash injuries.

**The driver who hit me in Detroit had no insurance. Can I still recover?** Your no-fault benefits are unaffected: your own insurer, a household member’s, or the Assigned Claims Plan pays your medical bills and lost wages. Your pain-and-suffering claim depends on whether you carry uninsured motorist coverage; without it, the claim is worth only what the uninsured driver can personally pay.

**I was hit by a DDOT bus. Is that handled like any other crash?** No. DDOT is a City of Detroit department, so the claim is against the city under the Governmental Tort Liability Act’s motor vehicle exception, with the Act’s procedures and defenses. Request the bus video immediately.

**Where would my Wayne County car accident case be filed?** Most injury suits go to the Third Judicial Circuit Court in Detroit. Mini tort and other claims under $25,000 go to the district court for the community where the crash occurred or the defendant lives.

**I was a passenger in a car with no insurance. Who pays my bills?** Your own policy if you have one, then a household member’s; if none, the Assigned Claims Plan, capped at $250,000 for medical benefits. Since 2019, uninsured passengers no longer claim from the vehicle owner’s insurer. See our [passenger injury page](/personal-injury/passenger-injury).

**How long do I have?** One year to give your no-fault insurer written notice, three years to sue the at-fault driver, and as little as 120 days for a claim against the county or a city.

**Do I need a lawyer for a Wayne County fender-bender?** Not if no one was hurt and the insurer is paying. Call if anyone was injured, the other driver was uninsured or fled, a government vehicle was involved, the police report is wrong, or an insurer has denied or delayed benefits.
