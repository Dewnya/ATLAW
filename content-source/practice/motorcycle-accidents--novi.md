## Novi, MI Motorcycle Accident Lawyer

A motorcycle crash in Novi is governed by a rule many riders learn only after they are hurt: under Michigan no-fault, a motorcycle is not a “motor vehicle.” That definition decides:

- who pays your medical bills, and how much they pay
- whether you have a no-fault claim at all
- how the insurer will argue the helmet question

Novi adds its own risks:

- **I-96, I-275, and M-5**, where drivers merging and changing lanes fail to see a motorcycle in the next lane.

- **Trucks** turning into and out of the city’s warehouses and business parks.
- **Three road agencies**, each with its own notice clock when the road itself caused the crash.

## Where Novi motorcycle crashes happen

**I-96, I-275, and M-5.** More than a quarter of Novi’s 2024 crashes, 549 of 1,984, were on the interstates. For a rider, the freeway crashes are:

- **Merges:** a driver entering from the Novi Road or Beck Road ramps moves into a motorcycle already in the lane.
- **Lane changes:** a driver moving over without checking the blind spot, where a motorcycle fits and a car would not (MCL 257.642(1)(a)).
- **Backups:** rear-end crashes in the stop-and-go near the four-freeway interchange at the Farmington Hills line, where a rider has nowhere to go.
- **Trucks:** blind spots, wind blast, and tire debris. See our [Novi truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/novi).

**M-5 and its crossovers.** North of the Twelve Mile interchange, M-5 becomes a divided boulevard at Thirteen and Fourteen Mile. Drivers leaving a median crossover or turnaround pull into the near lanes without yielding to a motorcycle approaching at speed, and a driver leaving a crossover must yield to traffic in the through lanes.

**Left turns and retail driveways.** The recurring account in rider cases is a driver turning left across oncoming traffic who misjudges the speed of a motorcycle or never sees it. Under Michigan law, a driver must yield to oncoming traffic before turning left (MCL 257.650). A driver leaving a private driveway must yield to traffic on the road (MCL 257.652). Novi produces those crashes every day:

- at the signals on Novi Road, Grand River, and the mile roads
- at the driveways of the mall, the stores, the restaurants, and the hotels around the I-96 and Novi Road interchange
- at subdivision entrances on Beck, Taft, Meadowbrook, and Wixom Road, where a driver pulling out misjudges an oncoming motorcycle

**Deer and the wooded roads.** Novi’s west and north sides have parks, lakes, wetlands, and wooded lots between the subdivisions. Deer cross those roads most often at dawn and dusk, and in the fall. For a rider, a deer strike is often a single-vehicle crash, and that changes the claim:

- **If no car was involved,** there is no no-fault claim. Your bills go to your health insurance and to any medical coverage on the motorcycle policy.
- **If a car was involved,** for example a driver who swerved or braked for the same deer and forced you down, the car is “involved” and no-fault applies through the rider priority rule below. See our [Novi car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents/novi) for deer crashes in cars.

**Gravel, construction, and the warehouse roads.** New subdivisions, commercial buildings, and road projects leave gravel, mud, and debris at site entrances. Trucks turning into the city’s warehouses make wide turns and slow left turns across the lanes. A contractor that tracks gravel onto the road, or a carrier whose truck drops its load, can be responsible.

**The road itself.** A single-vehicle crash caused by a pothole, a broken pavement seam, or a loose surface left by road work is a claim against the agency with jurisdiction, under the highway exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1402). In Novi there are three possible defendants:

- **MDOT** for I-96, I-275, and M-5: written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **The Road Commission for Oakland County** for Haggerty, Eight Mile, Napier, Pontiac Trail, and Fourteen Mile: written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **The City of Novi** for the other public roads, including Novi Road and Grand River, and the subdivision streets: written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).

A defect that was readily apparent to an ordinary observer for 30 days or more is conclusively presumed known to the agency (MCL 691.1403). Notice to the wrong agency is not notice.

**Drinking and riding.** 31 percent of Michigan’s 2024 rider deaths involved alcohol. A restaurant or bar that over-serves a driver who then hits a rider can be liable under the dram shop act. The notice is due within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, and suit within two years (MCL 436.1801(3)). See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

## Who pays a Novi rider’s medical bills

**If a car or truck was involved, you have no-fault benefits, but not from your motorcycle insurer.** Under MCL 500.3114(5), your Personal Injury Protection benefits come, in order, from:

1. the insurer of the owner or registrant of the motor vehicle involved
2. the insurer of that vehicle’s operator
3. the motor vehicle insurer of the motorcycle’s operator
4. the motor vehicle insurer of the motorcycle’s owner or registrant

Priority follows the vehicles’ insurers, not who was at fault. “Involved” does not require contact; a car that cut you off is involved even if it never touched you. If no insurer in that chain exists, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan pays (MCL 500.3172), with its $250,000 medical cap, unless you owned the motorcycle and it was uninsured (MCL 500.3113(b)).

The benefits are the ones a driver would get under MCL 500.3107:

- medical expenses, up to the PIP limit on the policy that pays
- 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- $20 a day in replacement services
- attendant care
- mileage

Written notice is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

The first medical limit is set by a policy you did not choose. Since the 2019 reform, the PIP medical level on the policy first in priority pays first; if the driver who hit you chose $250,000, that policy pays up to $250,000, and a lower-priority policy with more coverage, such as your own car policy, may then pay the rest. This is why optional first-party medical coverage on a motorcycle policy matters.

**If no car or truck was involved, no-fault does not apply.** Several common Novi crashes produce no PIP claim:

- a deer strike with no other vehicle involved
- a slide on gravel or mud at a construction entrance or subdivision corner
- a pothole or broken pavement seam on a city or county road
- a low-speed drop in a parking lot

Your bills go to your health insurance and to any medical coverage you bought on the motorcycle policy. Any injury claim is against whoever caused the hazard, if anyone did.

**Your motorcycle.** Your own collision or comprehensive coverage pays for the bike. The mini tort claim does not cover a motorcycle, because a motorcycle is not a “motor vehicle” under the no-fault act (*Nerat v Swacker*, 150 Mich App 61, 1986).

## Your claim against the driver who hit you

For pain and suffering and economic losses beyond no-fault, you can sue the at-fault driver if your injury meets the threshold: serious impairment of body function, permanent serious disfigurement, or death (MCL 500.3135). A serious impairment is an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life. It does not have to be permanent (*McCormick v Carrier*, 2010).

Motorcycle injuries often clear that threshold. Common examples include:

- fractures
- road rash requiring grafts
- torn ligaments
- [brain injuries](/personal-injury/brain-injury)
- [spinal cord injuries](/personal-injury/spinal-cord-injury)

You also recover excess economic damages above the PIP limit that applied to you, including lost wages above the monthly cap (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)). You have three years to sue (MCL 600.5805(2)). A fatal crash is brought by the estate; see our [wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death).

**What the claim is worth depends on the policies.** The default liability limit is $250,000 per person, but some drivers on I-96 and at the mall carry the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)) or nothing. The recovery above the other driver’s limit turns on your own **uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage**. That coverage must be on the motorcycle policy, because many car policies exclude the insured while riding an owned motorcycle. Your UIM insurer’s written consent is needed before you accept the at-fault driver’s limit. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Comparative fault is the fight.** Michigan’s rule is modified comparative fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(b), 600.2959):

- **More than 50 percent at fault:** you recover no noneconomic damages.
- **50 percent or less:** your award is reduced by your share.
- **Exactly 50 percent:** you recover half.

Economic damages beyond no-fault are reduced by your share as well. Insurers argue speed, lane position, lane splitting (illegal in Michigan), and “came out of nowhere.” The evidence that answers them:

- the Novi Police report, or the State Police report on the freeway, and witnesses
- the other driver’s phone records
- store, mall, hotel, and gas-station cameras along Novi Road and Grand River
- the vehicles’ event data recorders
- the rider’s own camera

## The helmet question

Since 2012, Michigan has let riders 21 and older ride without a helmet if they meet two conditions (MCL 257.658):

- They carry at least $20,000 in first-party medical coverage.
- They have either held a motorcycle endorsement for two years or passed an approved safety course.

Riders under 21 must wear a helmet. If you met those conditions, riding without a helmet was legal, so it was not a traffic violation. In a head-injury case, the insurer will argue a helmet would have reduced the injury. That is a damages and causation argument for the jury, not a bar to the claim. In a leg or shoulder case, it is irrelevant.

## What to do after a Novi motorcycle crash

1. **Get medical care the same day.** Henry Ford Providence Novi Hospital, on Grand River near I-96 and Beck Road, is a Level II adult trauma center. The most severe injuries may go to a Level I center such as Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak.
2. **Get the police report.** Crashes in the city are worked by the Novi Police Department, and freeway crashes may be worked by the State Police. If a car caused the crash without making contact, make sure the report says so. After a deer strike, report the crash and photograph the deer.
3. **Identify every vehicle involved,** including the one that never touched you.
4. **Photograph everything:** the bike, the scene, the ramp, intersection, or driveway, the road surface, your gear, and your injuries. Do not repair or sell the bike.
5. **Read your policies.** Check your motorcycle policy for first-party medical, UM/UIM, collision, and comprehensive coverage, and your car policy’s PIP level.
6. **Apply for no-fault benefits with the right insurer promptly.** Do not give a recorded statement to the other driver’s insurer.
7. **If a road defect, a construction site, or a bar was involved, call within days.** The 120-day road-defect notice is already running, and the dram shop notice runs from the day you hire a lawyer.

## Frequently asked questions


**A driver merged onto I-96 into my lane and I went down. Do I have a claim?** Usually. A driver entering the freeway or changing lanes must make sure the lane is clear. The car is “involved” under no-fault whether or not it touched you, so its owner’s insurer is first in line for your medical bills. Get the plate and witnesses before the car is gone.

**A car pulled out of a store driveway on Grand River in front of me. Who is at fault?** Usually the driver leaving the driveway, who must yield to traffic on the road. Insurers will argue your speed and lane position, so store cameras and witnesses matter, and footage is overwritten within days.

**I hit a deer on my motorcycle. Who pays my medical bills?** If no car or truck was involved, no-fault does not pay. Your health insurance and any medical coverage on your motorcycle policy pay instead, and comprehensive coverage usually pays for the bike. If a car was involved, for example one that braked or swerved and forced you down, that car’s insurer may be first in line.

**Does the at-fault driver’s insurance pay my medical bills?** Not because the driver was at fault. Under the rider priority rule, the insurer of the car involved pays first, whoever caused the crash. Fault matters for your separate claim for pain and suffering.

**I have my own car insurance. Doesn’t that cover me on the motorcycle?** Not first. If a car was involved, that car’s insurer is first in line. Your own car insurer is third, after the involved vehicle’s owner’s and operator’s insurers. A motorcycle policy itself does not carry Michigan PIP. If no car was involved, there is usually no PIP claim at all.

**I slid on gravel at a construction entrance. Is anyone responsible?** Possibly the contractor or developer that left it there, or the road agency if it knew of the hazard and did not act. There is no no-fault claim if no other vehicle was involved. Photograph the gravel and the site entrance before it is swept.

**I wasn’t wearing a helmet. Can I still recover?** Yes, if three conditions were met: - you were 21 or older - you carried the required $20,000 in medical coverage - you had either two years of endorsement or a safety course. The insurer can argue a helmet would have reduced a head injury. That goes to damages, not to whether you have a claim.

**How long do I have?** - One year to notify the responsible no-fault insurer. - Three years to sue the at-fault driver. - 120 days for a notice to the City or the Road Commission. - 120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT. - 120 days for a dram shop notice, and two years to sue a bar.
