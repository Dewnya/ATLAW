## Rochester Hills, MI Medical Malpractice Lawyer

A medical malpractice claim in Michigan has rules no other injury claim has:

- a two-year deadline instead of three
- a written Notice of Intent, served on each provider at least 182 days before suit
- a sworn Affidavit of Merit from a qualified expert, filed with the complaint
- strict rules on who that expert can be
- a cap on pain-and-suffering damages

A case that misses any one of them can be lost before its merits are heard.

## The rules, by the numbers

| Rule | Figure | Statute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deadline to file suit | 2 years from the act or omission | MCL 600.5805(8) |
| Discovery rule | 6 months from when the claim was or should have been discovered; never more than 6 years after the act (exceptions for retained objects, fraud, reproductive injury) | MCL 600.5838a(2) |
| Notice of Intent before suit | 182 days; the provider must respond in writing within 154 days | MCL 600.2912b |
| Affidavit of Merit | Filed with the complaint | MCL 600.2912d |
| Defendant’s Affidavit of Meritorious Defense | Within 91 days after the answer | MCL 600.2912e |
| Expert qualification | Same specialty; same board certification if the defendant is certified; majority of professional time in that specialty in the year before | MCL 600.2169 |
| 2026 cap on noneconomic damages | $596,400 (standard) / $1,065,000 (catastrophic) | MCL 600.1483 |
| Child injured before age 8 | Deadline runs to the 10th birthday | MCL 600.5851(7) |
| Federally funded provider | Administrative claim (SF-95) within 2 years; suit after denial or 6 months | 28 USC 2401(b), 2675 |

Economic damages are not capped. They include medical bills, lost earnings, and the cost of future care. The Affidavit of Merit is required by MCL 600.2912d, and MCL 600.2169 decides who is qualified to sign it.

## What malpractice is, and is not

A bad outcome is not malpractice. Malpractice is a bad outcome caused by a provider who failed to do what a reasonably competent provider in the same specialty would have done in the same circumstances (MCL 600.2912a). You must prove four things:

- the standard of care
- a breach of it
- that the breach more likely than not caused the injury
- damages

Michigan requires proof that the patient would have had a better outcome. A lost chance of a better outcome is compensable only where that chance was greater than 50 percent (MCL 600.2912a(2)). Before anything is filed, a qualified physician’s written review of the records decides whether a case exists. Many do not, and we say so.

## Rochester Hills providers, and who answers for each

**Henry Ford Rochester Hospital.** This is the city’s hospital, with its employed physicians, nurses, and staff. Its name and ownership have changed several times, most recently in the 2024 transaction that brought Ascension’s southeast Michigan hospitals into a joint venture with Henry Ford Health. **[VERIFY]** The correct legal entity for the Notice of Intent must be identified two ways:

- from current corporate filings
- from the name the hospital used at the time of care, not from today’s sign or letterhead

Care given under the Ascension Providence or Crittenton name may involve an entity that no longer appears on the building, or a different one for care before and after the transaction. Claims against the hospital and its employed providers are filed in the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court (Oakland County Circuit Court) in Pontiac.

**Emergency physicians and other contracted groups.** Many hospitals staff their emergency departments, anesthesia, radiology, and hospitalist services through separate physician groups. The Notice of Intent must name the group as well as the hospital. Where a patient came to the hospital for care and reasonably believed the doctor worked for it, Michigan law can hold the hospital responsible under ostensible agency (*Grewe v Mt Clemens General Hospital*, 1978).

**Transfers to a higher-level center.** As a Level III center, Henry Ford Rochester stabilizes and transfers many major trauma, stroke, cardiac, and complex surgical patients. They may go to McLaren Oakland, Trinity Health Oakland, Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital, or another hospital in the patient’s system. A transfer case can involve two hospitals, two sets of physicians, and the transport between them. The question is which delay or decision caused the harm:

- the first hospital’s diagnosis
- the decision and timing of the transfer
- the transport
- the care after arrival

All of these hospitals are in Oakland County, so the same circuit court hears them.

**Corewell Health Troy and other nearby hospitals.** Many Rochester Hills residents are treated at Corewell Health Beaumont Troy Hospital, across the city line, or at hospitals in their own health plan’s network. The same Michigan rules apply. The defendant entity and the court are identified the same way.

**Medical offices, urgent care, surgery centers, and imaging centers.** This includes the primary-care and specialty practices around the hospital and along Rochester Road and Walton, urgent care clinics, and outpatient surgery, dialysis, and imaging centers. **[VERIFY: outpatient centers in the city]** Frequent claims are:

- a delayed diagnosis in an office practice
- a missed fracture or infection at urgent care
- a surgical complication at an outpatient center

The employer of each provider who touched the chart must be identified. A health-system-employed physician and an independent physician in the same building are different defendants with different insurers. Michigan rules apply to all of them.

**Oakland University’s clinics.** Oakland University is a state university, and its on-campus health clinic is part of it. A malpractice claim against a university-employed provider is not barred by governmental immunity (MCL 691.1407(4)). It proceeds against the State in the Court of Claims, decided by a judge, with the Court of Claims notice requirements in addition to the malpractice rules (MCL 600.6419, 600.6431). A claim against a private physician who teaches at the university’s medical school but practices for a private hospital is an ordinary Michigan malpractice claim.

**Federally funded community health centers.** Some Oakland County residents get primary, prenatal, and dental care at community health centers that receive federal Health Center Program funding. Providers at a funded center can be deemed federal employees for malpractice purposes. **[VERIFY: deemed status of centers serving Rochester Hills, by name, at intake; do not name on the page]** A claim against one follows the Federal Tort Claims Act, not Michigan procedure:

- an administrative claim on Standard Form 95 to the Department of Health and Human Services, within two years (28 USC 2401(b))
- no Notice of Intent
- suit in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan, tried to a judge without a jury

Identifying a deemed clinic early is the difference between a timely claim and none.

**Veterans.** Veterans treated at the John D. Dingell VA Medical Center in Detroit or a VA outpatient clinic have an FTCA claim on the same path. They may also qualify for disability compensation under 38 USC 1151 where the injury was caused by VA carelessness, negligence, or error, or by an event that was not reasonably foreseeable. ## How a Rochester Hills malpractice case proceeds

1. **The records,** including the electronic audit trail, requested from every provider promptly, under whatever name the provider used at the time of care.
2. **Expert review** by a physician in the defendant’s specialty, in writing, before anything is filed.
3. **Identify the defendants and the forum.** A hospital entity, physician group, surgery center, urgent care operator, or independent practice goes to the Sixth Circuit for Oakland County care. Oakland University goes to the Court of Claims. A deemed clinic or the VA follows the SF-95 route to federal court. EMS is subject to the EMS Act’s gross-negligence standard. Serving a Notice of Intent on a federal provider while the federal two-year clock runs can lose the case.
4. **The Notice of Intent** (MCL 600.2912b), served at least 182 days before suit. A timely notice pauses the two-year period during the notice period (MCL 600.5856(c)). If the provider does not respond within 154 days, suit may be filed then.
5. **The complaint and Affidavit of Merit** (MCL 600.2912d). The defense then files its own affidavit (MCL 600.2912e).
6. **Discovery, case evaluation, and facilitation.** Cases that do not settle go to trial before a jury in the Sixth Circuit at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac. Two to four years is typical.
7. **Resolution.** Minors’ and estates’ settlements are approved by the court or the Oakland County Probate Court. Medicare, Medicaid, ERISA plan, and health plan liens are resolved before distribution.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to sue a doctor or hospital in Rochester Hills?** Two years from the malpractice, with a six-month discovery rule capped at six years. The Notice of Intent must be served 182 days before suit, and the expert review comes first, so call well inside the first year.

**Is the Notice of Intent “six months” or 182 days?** 182 days, which is not the same as six calendar months. A notice served on the last day of a six-month count can be late. Count the days.

**My care was at “Ascension Providence Rochester” or “Crittenton.” Does the name change matter?** It can. The Notice of Intent and the complaint must name the legal entity responsible for your care at the time it was given. After the hospital’s transactions and rebranding, that may not be the name on the building today. We identify it from the records and the corporate filings.

**I was seen at Henry Ford Rochester and transferred to another hospital. Which hospital is responsible?** It depends on where the decision or delay that caused the harm happened: the first hospital’s diagnosis and decision to transfer, the transport, or the care after arrival. Each provider is a separate defendant with its own Notice of Intent.

**I was treated at Oakland University’s clinic. Is that different?** Yes. The university is the State, so the claim is filed in the Court of Claims and decided by a judge. Its notice requirements apply in addition to the malpractice rules.

**I’m a veteran and was treated at the VA. Is that different?** Yes. A claim against VA care follows the Federal Tort Claims Act: an administrative claim within two years, no Notice of Intent, and suit in federal court without a jury. A veteran may also qualify for disability compensation for an injury caused by VA error or an unforeseeable event.

**Is there a cap on what I can recover?** On pain-and-suffering damages only. In 2026, the cap is $596,400 in the standard case and $1,065,000 for the catastrophic permanent injuries the statute lists, adjusted each year. Medical bills, lost earnings, and future care are not capped.

**My child was injured at birth. How long do we have?** A child injured before age eight has until the tenth birthday to file. The parents’ own claims are subject to the ordinary two-year period, so the family should not wait.
