## Rochester Hills, MI Wrongful Death Lawyer

When someone dies in Rochester Hills because another person or company was careless, Michigan’s Wrongful Death Act (MCL 600.2922) gives the family a claim. It does not work the way many families expect:

- The claim belongs to the estate, not to the spouse or children directly.
- It must be brought by a personal representative appointed by the probate court.
- A judge divides the recovery among family members.
- The deadline depends on what caused the death.

Deaths that lead to these claims in Rochester Hills include:

- a crash on M-59, Rochester Road, or a wooded two-lane road
- a cyclist or walker struck at a trail crossing
- a truck at an industrial park
- a driver leaving a bar
- a death on a construction site or at a workplace
- a hospital error
- neglect in a senior residence

Some of these deadlines are as short as 120 days. This page explains how the process works for a Rochester Hills family and what to do in the first weeks.

## How a Rochester Hills wrongful death claim works

**The estate.** Only the personal representative can bring the claim (MCL 600.2922(2)).

- **The person lived in Rochester Hills:** the estate is opened in the Oakland County Probate Court on the county campus at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac.
- The person lived elsewhere in Michigan but died in Rochester Hills: the estate is opened in the county where they lived. The suit can still be filed in Oakland County.

If there is a will naming a personal representative, that person petitions for appointment. If not, a family member, usually the spouse or an adult child, petitions under Michigan’s priority rules (MCL 700.3203). The court issues letters of authority, and the personal representative then has standing to hire a lawyer for the estate and file. We file the petition as the first legal step. **[ATTORNEY CHECK: EPIC appointment summary]**

**Notice to the family.** Within 30 days after filing suit, the personal representative must notify in writing every person who may share in the recovery (MCL 600.2922(2)–(4)). That list includes:

- the spouse
- children and descendants
- parents and grandparents
- brothers and sisters
- the children of the deceased’s spouse
- anyone named in the will

The list is not limited to dependents. A parent, sibling, or adult child who was not financially dependent can still share for the loss of companionship. The Act does not rank them in a fixed order of priority. Instead, the court divides the recovery according to each person’s loss. A person named in the will may share in the recovery but does not bring the claim; only the personal representative does.

**The court.** The suit is the injury claim the person would have had, brought by the estate against whoever caused the death. Death claims are filed in circuit court, not in the 52-3 District Court on Barclay Circle, which hears civil cases only up to $25,000.

- **Most deaths in Rochester Hills:** the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court (Oakland County Circuit Court) at 1200 N. Telegraph Road, on the same campus as the Probate Court.
- A death on Dequindre, the county line: if the fatal injury happened on the Macomb side, the Sixteenth Circuit (MCL 600.1629).
- **An out-of-state trucking company or driver:** the case may be filed in or removed to federal court in Detroit.
- **Claims against MDOT or Oakland University:** the Court of Claims.



**The damages** (MCL 600.2922(6)) include:

- medical and hospital expenses from the injury to the death
- funeral and burial expenses
- the person’s **conscious pain and suffering** between the injury and death
- **loss of financial support** over a working life, which in a household where the person who died was a professional or engineer is often a major part of the claim
- **loss of society and companionship** for each family member

Michigan places no cap on these damages except in medical malpractice cases. Michigan does not award punitive damages in wrongful death cases. Damage to the person’s car or other property is a separate claim of the estate, not part of the wrongful death damages. No-fault survivor’s loss benefits, described below, are also a separate insurance claim.

**Comparative fault.** If the person who died was partly at fault, the damages are reduced by that share. If their fault was greater than the combined fault of everyone else, the estate cannot recover noneconomic damages, but it can still recover reduced economic damages (MCL 600.2959). In an auto case, the no-fault act bars noneconomic damages at more than 50 percent fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)).

**The distribution hearing.** After a settlement or verdict, the personal representative proposes a division among the eligible family members, with notice to each.

- If they agree, the court approves it.
- If not, the court decides based on each person’s relationship and loss (MCL 600.2922(6)).

Fees and costs are approved at the same hearing. Because a judge divides the money, no family member should sign anything with an insurer. A release signed by one relative does not settle the estate’s claim.

## The causes of Rochester Hills wrongful death claims

**Crashes.** Fatal crashes here happen on M-59, Rochester Road, the mile roads, and the wooded roads toward Oakland Township. They include pedestrians and cyclists struck at the Clinton River and Paint Creek trail crossings and near Oakland University.

A wrongful death claim after a crash does not step outside no-fault. The claim against the at-fault driver still requires a threshold injury, and a death meets it (MCL 500.3135(1)). No-fault benefits are paid alongside it.

**Two policies, in the right order.** A death claim is often worth more than the at-fault driver’s limit. The estate can recover the difference under the family’s UIM coverage, but only if:

- the UIM insurer consents in writing before the estate accepts the at-fault driver’s limit, and
- the probate court approves the settlement.

Settling without consent can forfeit the UIM claim. We also check umbrella policies and the policy of any employer whose driver was working.

**No-fault survivor’s loss benefits** (MCL 500.3108) are paid regardless of fault, for up to three years after the death. They are paid by the deceased’s own insurer, or by the insurer that would have paid their PIP. They cover:

- the economic support the person provided, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- $20 a day in replacement services
- funeral expenses up to the policy limit, at least $1,750 and up to $5,000 (MCL 500.3107(1)(a))

Survivor’s loss stops at the cap. Support above it is part of the wrongful death claim. Written notice is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)), so do not wait for the lawsuit. See our [Rochester Hills car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents/rochester-hills) and, for pedestrian and cyclist deaths, our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents) and [bicycle accident page](/personal-injury/bicycle-accidents).

**Truck, motorcycle, and deer-related deaths.** A fatal crash with a tractor-trailer, a dump truck, or a delivery truck on M-59 or near an industrial park brings three added elements:

- the federal trucking rules
- the carrier’s policy of $750,000 or more
- evidence that can be overwritten within days

See our [Rochester Hills truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/rochester-hills). A fatal motorcycle crash follows the rider priority rules. A rider killed in a crash involving only a deer has no no-fault survivor’s loss claim, because a motorcycle is not a “motor vehicle” under the act. See our [Rochester Hills motorcycle accident page](/personal-injury/motorcycle-accidents/rochester-hills).

**Drunk driving.** The claim runs against the driver. Under the dram shop act (MCL 436.1801), it can also run against the bar, restaurant, or store that served a visibly intoxicated person or a minor, including along Rochester Road and in downtown Rochester. The dram shop claim has its own rules:

- written notice to every defendant within 120 days after the family hires a lawyer
- the intoxicated driver must be named and kept in the suit
- the suit must be filed within **two years**, not three

The criminal case proceeds separately, and the family’s claim does not wait for it. See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**Deaths on the job.** Workplace deaths in Rochester Hills happen at the city’s engineering, technology, and manufacturing sites; at construction on new subdivisions, commercial buildings, and roads; and at retail and service workplaces. When a worker dies on the job, the employer owes workers’ compensation death benefits to dependents, up to 500 weeks plus burial (MCL 418.321, 418.345). The employer generally cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). There are two important exceptions:

- **A third party** whose negligence caused the death can be sued in a wrongful death action. That includes a general contractor or subcontractor on the site, a property owner, a trucking company, or the manufacturer of a machine with a missing guard or a defective safety device ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability)).
- **The employer itself** can be sued only under the narrow intentional tort exception. That requires actual knowledge that an injury was certain to occur and willful disregard of it (MCL 418.131(1)).

The MIOSHA investigation file is one of the first records requested. See our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).

**Hospital and medical negligence.** These claims can arise at Henry Ford Rochester Hospital on University Drive, at the hospitals that receive the city’s most seriously injured, or at the clinics and medical offices around them. They are wrongful death claims with the malpractice rules attached:

- a 182-day Notice of Intent (MCL 600.2912b)
- an Affidavit of Merit (MCL 600.2912d)
- a two-year period (MCL 600.5805(8), 600.5838a)
- the cap (MCL 600.1483)

See our [Rochester Hills medical malpractice page](/personal-injury/medical-malpractice/rochester-hills).

**Senior residences, nursing homes, and premises.** Rochester Hills has senior communities, assisted living, memory care, and nursing homes, along with condominiums and apartment complexes.

- **A death from neglect in a nursing home** may be ordinary negligence or malpractice, and that changes the deadline. See our [Rochester Hills nursing home abuse page](/personal-injury/nursing-home-abuse/rochester-hills).
- **Deaths from fires, carbon monoxide, failed smoke detectors, and falls** follow premises law. For tenants, they also follow MCL 554.139. See our [tenant injury page](/personal-injury/tenant-injuries).

**Deaths involving a government agency.** Rochester Hills is a city, and the road defendant can be any of four agencies:

- MDOT for M-59, and for Rochester Road (M-150) from M-59 north to Tienken, with written notice of a road defect filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- The Road Commission for Oakland County for many major roads, including Adams, Crooks, Livernois, Avon, Tienken, Walton, and Rochester Road south of M-59, with written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404).
- The City of Rochester Hills for others, including Hamlin and John R, with the same 120-day notice.
- Oakland University for its campus roads and buildings, as the State, in the Court of Claims, with written notice of a road or building defect within 120 days (MCL 691.1404, 691.1406), or within six months for other claims (MCL 600.6431).

The City also answers for its sidewalks and buildings, with the same 120-day notice (MCL 691.1402a, 691.1406). Vehicle crashes involving the government follow these rules:

- **A Sheriff’s patrol car:** a claim against Oakland County, which polices Rochester Hills under contract.
- **A Rochester Hills Fire Department vehicle:** a claim against the City.
- **SMART and school buses:** these fall under the motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405).

An individual government employee is liable only for gross negligence (MCL 691.1407(2)). Public trails add governmental immunity and the Recreational Land Use Act (MCL 324.73301).

| UIM consent before the estate accepts the at-fault driver’s limit | Before settlement | Policy terms |

| Road defect: City of Rochester Hills or Road Commission | Written notice within 120 days | MCL 691.1404 |
| Sidewalk or building defect: City of Rochester Hills | Written notice within 120 days | MCL 691.1402a, 691.1404, 691.1406 |

| Workers’ compensation death benefits | MCL 418.381(1) | MCL 418.381 |

When the injury and the death happen on the same day, the difference between “from the injury” and “from the death” does not matter. When a person lingers for weeks or months, the ordinary period may already be running before the estate exists. That is what the savings provision is for. It adds only a limited period, and courts apply it strictly. Open the estate and file inside the ordinary deadline.

## What the family should do in the first weeks

1. **Get the death certificate and request the Oakland County Medical Examiner’s report.** Also request the Rochester Hills Fire Department EMS run sheet, the Sheriff’s report, and the hospital record. The Medical Examiner’s office is on the county campus in Pontiac. If the family’s customs call for prompt burial, tell the Medical Examiner’s office at once, or call us.
2. **Keep everything:** the vehicle, the phone, the helmet, the bicycle, the work gear, and the medication bottles. Do not authorize salvage of a vehicle until a lawyer has seen it.
3. **Notify the no-fault insurer in writing** if a vehicle was involved. Survivor’s loss benefits begin with the claim, not with the lawsuit.
4. **Do not give a recorded statement** to any insurer, and do not sign any document, however it is described. An insurer offering to “cover the funeral” in exchange for a signature may be settling the claim for the price of a funeral.
5. **Open the estate.** The probate petition is the first legal step. The wrongful death suit cannot be filed until letters of authority issue.
6. **Call within days** if the death involved a road, a sidewalk, a government vehicle, Oakland University, a bar, a trucking company, or an employer. Those notice periods are short, and trucking records expire.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who can file a wrongful death lawsuit in Rochester Hills?** Only the personal representative of the estate. For a Rochester Hills resident, the Oakland County Probate Court appoints that person. A spouse, child, or parent can be appointed, but must be appointed before the claim can be filed.

**Who receives the money?** The spouse, children, descendants, parents, grandparents, siblings, the children of the deceased’s spouse, and anyone named in the will may share, whether or not they were financially dependent. The probate court divides the recovery according to each person’s loss, after notice to each of them.

**How long do we have?** - **Crash, fall, and premises claims:** generally three years from the injury that caused the death, with a limited extension for the estate. - **Medical malpractice:** two years, after a 182-day notice. - **Dram shop claims:** two years. - **No-fault survivor’s loss benefits:** one year to claim. - **Road or building defects:** 120 days to notify the City or the Road Commission. - **MDOT, or a road or building defect at Oakland University:** 120 days. - **Other claims against Oakland University:** six months.

**Does the three years run from the date of death?** Not necessarily. For most claims, the period generally runs from the injury that caused the death, with a limited extension for the estate. When the person lived for some time after the injury, waiting until three years after the death can be too late.

**The other driver’s insurer offered its policy limit. Should the estate accept it?** Not before the family’s underinsured motorist insurer consents in writing and the probate court approves the settlement. Accepting without consent can forfeit the UIM claim.

**My husband died in a crash. Are survivor’s loss benefits part of the lawsuit?** No. Survivor’s loss is paid by the no-fault insurer regardless of fault, up to a monthly cap, for up to three years. The wrongful death claim against the at-fault driver is separate. It covers the losses survivor’s loss does not, including support above the cap and the family’s loss of companionship.

**A bad road contributed to my son’s crash. Do we sue the City of Rochester Hills?** It depends on the road: - **M-59, and Rochester Road north of M-59:** MDOT, with written notice of a road defect filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days. - **Other major roads:** some belong to the City, such as Hamlin, and many to the Road Commission, such as Livernois, each with written notice within 120 days. - **Campus roads:** Oakland University, which is the State.



**Can we get punitive damages?** Michigan does not award punitive damages in wrongful death cases. The family recovers the losses the Act lists, including loss of companionship, which has no cap outside medical malpractice.

**Is there a lawsuit if the driver was charged with a crime?** Yes. The criminal case and the family’s claim are separate. The family does not have to wait for the criminal case, and the civil deadlines keep running.
