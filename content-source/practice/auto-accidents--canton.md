## Canton, MI Car Accident Lawyer

A car crash in Canton is a Michigan no-fault claim shaped by one road. Ford Road (M-153) runs east-west through the township from the I-275 interchange past IKEA and the big-box and restaurant strips to Lilley, Canton Center, and Sheldon, carrying shoppers and commuters from across western Wayne County through signals and driveways at commercial volumes. MDOT plans to rebuild 2.5 miles of it, between Sheldon and Lotz, as a boulevard that eliminates left turns, with work scheduled to begin in spring 2028; the State says the redesign is meant to reduce the high volume of crashes there. Around Ford Road are I-275, with interchanges at Ford Road and Michigan Avenue; Michigan Avenue and its distribution centers; and the mile roads and county arterials that carry subdivision traffic. ## Where Canton crashes happen, and whose road it is

**Ford Road (M-153).** From the I-275 interchange west, Ford Road’s five to seven lanes carry retail traffic past IKEA, the shopping centers, and the restaurant rows, with signals at Haggerty, Lilley, Morton Taylor, Canton Center, and Sheldon and dozens of driveways between them. The crashes are left turns across oncoming traffic into the shopping centers and at the signals, rear-end crashes when a driver ahead brakes for a driveway, red-light and late-yellow crashes, and low-speed crashes and pedestrian strikes in the parking lots (see our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents)). MDOT’s boulevard design replaces direct left turns with turnarounds to remove the left-turn conflict; until it is built, the left turn is the recurring Ford Road case, and during construction in 2028 and 2029, work-zone lane shifts will add their own.

**I-275 and the interchanges.** The western Wayne County freeway, with interchanges at Michigan Avenue and Ford Road in Canton. Merge and weave crashes at the Ford Road interchange, where freeway exits meet the retail traffic within a few hundred feet; rear-end crashes in stop-and-go traffic; and heavy trucks bound for the distribution centers along Michigan Avenue and Haggerty. See our [Canton truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/canton).

**Michigan Avenue (US-12).** The township’s southern spine, with distribution, warehouse, and light industrial sites and their truck driveways, and the I-275 ramps.

**The county roads.** Cherry Hill, Palmer, Warren, Joy, and Geddes; Sheldon, Canton Center, Lilley, Beck, Haggerty, Morton Taylor, and Lotz. Two-lane and five-lane roads carrying subdivision traffic to Ford Road and the schools, with signal crashes, crashes at unsignalized crossings, and pedestrian and bicycle crashes near the schools and parks (see our [bicycle accident page](/personal-injury/bicycle-accidents)).

**Whose road it is: not the township’s.** Canton is a charter township, and Michigan townships do not have jurisdiction over public roads. Ford Road, Michigan Avenue, and I-275 are MDOT’s; a road-defect claim against MDOT goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). The mile roads and the county arterials are the Wayne County Department of Public Services’, with written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404). A pothole or other defect in the roadbed can make the agency a defendant alongside the other driver; on State and county roads, signals, signs, and sight obstructions generally fall outside the highway exception. A notice sent to Canton Township is sent to the wrong defendant, and notice to the wrong agency is not notice.

## Fault is the case

In a rear-end crash, fault is rarely disputed. In a left-turn or signal crash at a Ford Road intersection, it is disputed often, and the other driver’s insurer may try to assign you half or more of the fault. That number matters: if you are found more than 50 percent at fault, you lose your claim for pain and suffering, and below that your recovery is reduced by your share (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). At exactly 50 percent you recover half. The evidence that decides it is perishable:

- the Canton Public Safety police report and the officer’s diagram;
- **signal timing data** held by MDOT or Wayne County, which show how each light was timed and, where the controller logs events, what phase it was in;
- store, restaurant, and parking-lot cameras along Ford Road, many overwritten within days;
- the vehicles’ **event data recorders**, which store speed, braking, and throttle in the seconds before impact;
- witnesses in the other lanes.

## Who pays after a Canton crash

**Your no-fault (PIP) claim.** Your own auto insurer pays your medical expenses up to the PIP level you chose, 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year, replacement services at $20 a day, and attendant care, regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107). If you have no policy, a resident relative’s policy pays (MCL 500.3114); if none exists, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan does (MCL 500.3172), capped at $250,000 for medical benefits. The insurer must receive written notice within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)) and must pay each bill within 30 days of reasonable proof or owe 12 percent interest (MCL 500.3142). Since the 2019 reform, PIP medical coverage is no longer unlimited unless you chose it, so the level on your declarations page matters. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance) and [Michigan no-fault guide](about:blank).

**Your claim against the at-fault driver.** For pain and suffering, you need a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment is an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life; it does not have to be permanent (MCL 500.3135(5); *McCormick v Carrier*, 2010). You can also recover lost wages beyond the three years and medical bills above your chosen PIP level (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)). The suit must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The mini tort recovers up to $3,000 in vehicle damage not covered by your policy from the at-fault driver (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)), filed in the 35th District Court if the driver’s insurer will not pay it.

**Coverage on the other side.** Many drivers carry the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)). Ford Road and I-275 bring drivers from everywhere, and minimum-limits and uninsured drivers are among them. Your own **uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage** often decides what the claim is worth; we read every policy in the household before valuing the claim. Settling with the at-fault driver without your UIM insurer’s consent can forfeit the UIM claim. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Rideshare crashes.** An Uber or Lyft driver’s crash on Ford Road brings the company’s commercial policy into the claim, depending on whether the app was on and a trip was accepted; see our [Canton rideshare accident page](/personal-injury/rideshare-accidents).

**Drunk drivers and dram shop.** A crash caused by a drunk driver leaving a Ford Road restaurant or bar adds a claim against the business that served a visibly intoxicated person or a minor. Written notice must go to the retailer within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, the drunk driver must be named and kept in the suit, and the suit must be filed within two years, not three (MCL 436.1801(3), (4)). See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**Government vehicles.** Canton Public Safety police and fire vehicles, Plymouth-Canton, Wayne-Westland, and Van Buren school buses, and county road trucks fall under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405). See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

## What to do after a Canton crash

1. **Call 911 and get the police report.** Michigan requires a report for any crash with injury or $1,000 in damage (MCL 257.622). Crashes in the township are generally worked by Canton Public Safety; crashes on I-275 may be worked by the Michigan State Police.
2. **Photograph the vehicles, plates, the other driver’s license and insurance card, the signals, and the lanes.** Note every store or restaurant with a camera facing the intersection or the lot; footage is kept for days, not weeks.
3. **Get medical care the same day.** Canton has no hospital of its own. Many of the seriously injured go to Trinity Health Ann Arbor in Superior Township, a Level I trauma center, or to Trinity Health Livonia; University of Michigan Health in Ann Arbor, also a Level I center, treats others.
4. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** within the first week.
5. **Read your declarations page** for PIP level, UM/UIM, and collision coverage.
6. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the other driver’s insurer or sign a release.
7. **Call a lawyer within days** if fault is disputed; the camera footage and signal data are the first evidence lost.

## The courts

Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, are filed in the 35th District Court at 660 Plymouth Road in Plymouth, which serves Canton, Plymouth, and Northville. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Third Judicial Circuit Court at the Coleman A. Young Municipal Center in Detroit. A crash near the Washtenaw County line, or with a Washtenaw or Oakland County defendant, may allow filing there instead (MCL 600.1629). Defect claims against MDOT go to the Court of Claims. A fatal crash is brought by the estate through the Wayne County Probate Court; see our [Canton wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death/canton).

## Frequently asked questions

**Why are there so many crashes on Ford Road in Canton?** Volume, signals, and left turns. The corridor carries retail traffic from across western Wayne County past dozens of driveways and signalized intersections, so left turns across oncoming traffic and rear-end crashes at the driveways are constant. MDOT’s boulevard project, scheduled for 2028 and 2029 between Sheldon and Lotz, is designed to eliminate the direct left turns.

**How many car accidents happen in Canton each year?** Canton Township recorded 1,961 crashes, 550 injuries, and 4 deaths in 2024. Wayne County as a whole recorded 50,355 crashes, 16,458 injuries, and 177 deaths in 2024.

**A pothole or a bad signal on a Canton road caused my crash. Do I notify the township?** No. Canton is a township and does not own its roads. A defect in the roadbed on Ford Road, Michigan Avenue, or I-275 is MDOT’s, with notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days; on the county roads it is Wayne County’s, with written notice within 120 days. A signal problem on a State or county road generally falls outside the highway exception.

**The other driver’s insurer says I was half at fault for a left turn on Ford Road. Is my claim over?** No. You lose the pain-and-suffering claim only if you are found more than 50 percent at fault. At 50 percent you recover half. The diagram, the signal data, the cameras, and the witnesses decide the number, not the adjuster.

**I was hit by a driver leaving a Ford Road restaurant. Can I sue the restaurant?** If it served a visibly intoxicated person who then caused the crash, yes, under the dram shop act, but notice must go out within 120 days after you hire a lawyer and the suit must be filed within two years.

**Where would my Canton case be filed?** Mini tort and claims up to $25,000 in the 35th District Court on Plymouth Road in Plymouth; injury suits over $25,000 in the Third Judicial Circuit Court in Detroit.

**How long do I have?** One year to give your no-fault insurer written notice, three years to sue the at-fault driver, two years to sue a bar, 120 days for a notice to Wayne County, and 120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT.
