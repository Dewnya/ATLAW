## Personal Injury Law Firm Serving Livonia, MI

Livonia is a city of about 93,000 in western Wayne County, laid out on a grid of mile roads from Five Mile to Eight Mile and north-south arterials from Inkster to Haggerty, and cut by two freeways, I-96 and I-275, whose interchange near the city’s western edge is a major regional connection for commuters and commercial traffic. The Plymouth Road and Schoolcraft corridors along I-96 hold the city’s industry; Laurel Park Place, the Middlebelt and Seven Mile retail strips, and the Wonderland redevelopment hold its commerce; and approximately 22 percent of its residents are age 65 or older, served by five Medicare-certified nursing homes. Livonia is also where the Social Security Administration hears disability appeals for much of western Wayne County, at the Laurel Park hearing office. In 2024, Livonia recorded 3,033 traffic crashes, with 10 people killed, according to Michigan Traffic Crash Facts.

In 13 years the firm has handled more than 10,000 cases and recovered more than $100 million for clients across southeast Michigan, Livonia residents among them. ## Where Livonia injuries happen

**The I-96/I-275 interchange and the freeways.** I-96, the Jeffries, runs the length of the city along Schoolcraft with interchanges at Inkster, Middlebelt, Merriman, Farmington, Levan, and Newburgh before meeting I-275 and M-14 at the western edge in a stacked interchange that carries traffic between Detroit, Ann Arbor, Lansing, and the northern suburbs. Merge, weave, and lane-change crashes at the interchange; rear-end crashes in the stop-and-go east of it; and the heavy trucks that use I-96 and I-275 to reach the Plymouth Road industrial corridor and the distribution centers in Livonia, Plymouth, and Canton. See our [Livonia truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/livonia).

**The mile roads and the arterials.** Five Mile, Six Mile, Seven Mile, and Eight Mile; Plymouth Road and Schoolcraft; Inkster, Middlebelt, Merriman, Farmington, Levan, Newburgh, and Haggerty. The intersections at Middlebelt and Seven Mile, Middlebelt and Plymouth, Farmington and Six Mile, and Newburgh and Six Mile around Laurel Park carry the left-turn, red-light, and rear-end crashes; the Plymouth Road and Middlebelt strips carry the parking-lot crashes and the pedestrian strikes. The mile roads and most of the arterials are Wayne County’s; Eight Mile and the freeways are MDOT’s.

**The industrial corridor.** Plymouth Road and Schoolcraft from Inkster to Newburgh: the Ford Livonia Transmission Plant, the engineering and supplier operations around it, and the warehouses and light industry along the I-96 service drives. Workers’ compensation is the exclusive remedy against the employer (MCL 418.131), but a worker injured by a contractor, a vendor’s driver, or a defective machine has a lawsuit as well. See our [workers’ compensation](/personal-injury/workers-compensation/livonia) and [product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability) pages.

**The retail and the venues.** Laurel Park Place and the office and hotel cluster around it at Six Mile and Newburgh; the Middlebelt and Seven Mile strips; the Wonderland Village redevelopment at Plymouth and Middlebelt; and the restaurants along Plymouth Road and Farmington. Parking-lot crashes, falls in stores and on walkways, snow and ice, and dram shop claims against restaurants that over-served a driver (MCL 436.1801, 120-day notice). See our [premises liability](/personal-injury/premises-liability) and [drunk driving accident](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents) pages.

**The neighborhoods.** Livonia’s housing is mid-century subdivision stock, with approximately 87 percent of occupied homes owner-occupied, according to the 2020–2024 American Community Survey, with condominiums and senior apartments along Seven Mile, Newburgh, and Farmington. Falls on stairs and ice, condominium common-area injuries, dog bites under MCL 287.351, and sidewalk injuries under the two-inch rule (MCL 691.1402a). See our [dog bite page](/personal-injury/dog-bites/livonia).

**The senior-care facilities.** Five Medicare-certified nursing homes, plus assisted-living and memory-care communities. Falls during transfers, pressure injuries, medication errors, dehydration, and alleged neglect may give rise to claims involving ordinary negligence, medical malpractice, or both. The distinction depends on whether the alleged misconduct involves professional medical judgment or matters within ordinary knowledge and experience. Because the applicable procedures and filing deadlines differ, each claim must be evaluated based on its specific facts. ## What is different about a Livonia claim

**Coverage.** Livonia’s drivers carry more insurance on average than drivers to the east, and the typical Livonia at-fault driver has a policy worth pursuing; the I-96 and I-275 traffic, however, comes from everywhere, and minimum-limits and uninsured drivers are on both freeways. Your own no-fault insurer pays your medical bills and lost wages regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107), or the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan does if no household policy applies (MCL 500.3172, capped at $250,000); the claim against the at-fault driver requires a threshold injury (MCL 500.3135) and is worth no more than the insurance available unless you carry uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage. When the at-fault driver’s limit is too small for the injury, your own UIM coverage pays the difference, with the insurer’s consent required before any settlement. We review every policy in the household before we value a Livonia claim. See our [Livonia car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents/livonia), [no-fault insurance guide](about:blank), and [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Two road agencies, plus the city.** MDOT has jurisdiction over state highways such as I-96, I-275, and M-14. Wayne County maintains many of Livonia’s major roads, including Five Mile, Six Mile, Seven Mile, Middlebelt, Merriman, Farmington, and Inkster, as well as designated portions of Eight Mile and Plymouth Road. The City of Livonia maintains local streets under its jurisdiction. Responsibility for any particular roadway or segment should be confirmed against the applicable state, county, and city road-jurisdiction records. A qualifying defect in the improved portion of a public roadway may support a claim against the responsible governmental agency under the highway exception to governmental immunity, MCL 691.1402. Claims based solely on malfunctioning traffic signals, inadequate signs, or obstructed sight lines generally do not fall within that exception. Qualifying highway-defect claims generally require written notice within 120 days under MCL 691.1404, including claims against MDOT, for which notice must be filed with the Court of Claims as required by statute.

**Government vehicles.** Crashes involving Livonia Police or Fire Department vehicles, SMART buses, public school buses, or Wayne County road trucks may support claims under the Governmental Tort Liability Act’s motor vehicle exception, MCL 691.1405, when injuries result from negligent operation of government-owned vehicles. Certain tort claims against SMART also require written notice within 60 days under MCL 124.419. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

**The 16th District and the Third Circuit.** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims for vehicle damage (MCL 500.3135(3)(e), up to $3,000), are filed in the 16th District Court at 32765 Five Mile Road. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Third Judicial Circuit Court at the Coleman A. Young Municipal Center in Detroit; wrongful death estates (MCL 600.2922) and minors’ settlements (MCR 2.420) to the Wayne County Probate Court in the same building. For crashes near Eight Mile Road or Haggerty Road, or involving defendants from another county, venue is determined under the statutory order of priority in MCL 600.1629, generally beginning with the county where the original injury occurred and where a defendant resides, conducts business, or maintains a registered office.

**The hospitals.** Trinity Health Livonia Hospital (formerly St. Mary Mercy) on Five Mile is the city’s trauma center and treats most of Livonia’s seriously injured; Corewell Health Farmington Hills, Garden City Hospital, and the Detroit Level I centers treat the rest. **[CONFIRM: verify Trinity Health Livonia’s trauma designation and that the list reflects where clients are treated]** We obtain the full record from each, including the Livonia Fire Department EMS run sheet.

**The Social Security hearing office.** The Social Security Administration operates an Office of Hearings Operations at 19575 Victor Parkway in Livonia. It handles disability hearings assigned from several Michigan field offices, including Livonia, Inkster, Farmington Hills, Ann Arbor, Detroit Northwest, and designated Pontiac-area locations. A Livonia resident whose injury prevents them from working may also qualify for Social Security disability benefits, subject to the program’s eligibility requirements; see our [Social Security disability page](/disability/social-security-disability/livonia).

## Frequently asked questions

**Where are the crashes in Livonia?** At the I-96/I-275 interchange and along I-96, and at the mile-road intersections on Middlebelt, Farmington, and Newburgh, especially around Laurel Park and the Seven Mile and Plymouth Road retail strips. Livonia recorded 3,033 traffic crashes, 869 people injured, and 10 traffic deaths in 2024. Wayne County as a whole recorded 50,355 crashes, 16,458 injuries, and 177 deaths in 2024.

**My parent was injured in a Livonia nursing home. Is that malpractice or negligence?** It depends on the circumstances and the type of care involved. Under Michigan law, ordinary negligence generally concerns conduct that can be evaluated using common knowledge, while medical malpractice involves professional medical judgment or specialized standards of care. A fall during a transfer, a pressure injury, or dehydration could involve either type of claim, depending on the allegations and evidence. Ordinary negligence claims generally have a three-year limitations period, while medical malpractice claims generally have a two-year period and additional procedural requirements, including a notice of intent. Because a shorter deadline may apply, prompt legal review is important.

**I was hurt in a crash at the I-96/I-275 interchange. Who is responsible for the road?** MDOT has jurisdiction over I-96 and I-275, although specific maintenance responsibilities may involve other agencies. A qualifying defect in the roadway may support a claim under Michigan’s highway exception to governmental immunity, generally requiring notice within 120 days under MCL 691.1404. Claims based solely on roadway design or inadequate traffic signs are subject to significant governmental-immunity limitations. A separate negligence claim against another driver depends on the facts of the collision.

**I fell at Laurel Park or in a Middlebelt store. Who is responsible?** Responsibility depends on who possessed or controlled the property, who was responsible for maintaining the area, and whether negligence contributed to the injury. Since Kandil-Elsayed v. F & E Oil, Inc. (2023), an open-and-obvious hazard does not automatically eliminate a property possessor’s duty; its obviousness may be considered when determining breach of duty and comparative fault. Incident reports and available security-camera footage may provide important evidence, so preservation should be requested promptly.

**Where would my Livonia injury case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, in the 16th District Court on Five Mile Road; injury suits over $25,000 in the Third Judicial Circuit Court in Detroit.

**My injury means I cannot work again. Do I have a disability claim too?** Possibly, and the two claims run on different tracks. Social Security disability depends on your work history and medical inability to work for at least twelve months, not on fault, and the Livonia hearing office decides appeals for the area. We handle both.

**How much does it cost to hire you?** Nothing up front, and no fee unless we recover money for you. Michigan caps contingency fees in personal injury cases at one-third of the recovery (MCR 8.121).

**How long do I have?** Three years for most injury suits, one year to notify your no-fault insurer in writing, two years for nursing home clinical negligence, and as little as 120 days for a claim against the City of Livonia or Wayne County.
