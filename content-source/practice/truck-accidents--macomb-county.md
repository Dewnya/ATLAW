## Where Macomb County truck crashes happen

**Mound Road and Van Dyke.** The plant corridor. Mound was rebuilt from I-696 to M-59 in the Innovate Mound project, with new signals and dedicated turn lanes, but the traffic it carries is the same: tractor-trailers turning into and out of plant gates at the Tech Center, Warren Truck, Sterling Heights Assembly, and Sterling Stamping at shift changes, mixed with commuter traffic. Van Dyke (M-53) runs roughly parallel to Mound Road and includes two of Michigan’s highest-crash intersections in 2024: 11 Mile Road/I-696 at Van Dyke in Warren/Center Line, with 185 crashes, and 18½ Mile Road at Van Dyke in Sterling Heights, with 162 crashes. Truck movements around plant entrances can create turning, merging, and rear-end collision risks, particularly where freight traffic mixes with commuters near busy intersections.

**I-94.** From Eastpointe through St. Clair Shores, Roseville, Harrison Township, and Chesterfield to New Baltimore and the St. Clair County line, the through-freight route to the Blue Water Bridge, the second-busiest truck crossing on the U.S.–Canada border. The I-94/I-696 interchange in Roseville and the 23 Mile and 26 Mile interchanges serving the Chesterfield and New Baltimore warehouses are the merging points; lane-change, rear-end, and jackknife crashes on the lakeshore stretch, often in lake-effect snow, are the freeway cases.

**Hall Road and Gratiot.** Delivery trucks to the Lakeside and Partridge Creek retail corridors and the big-box strips; refuse and construction trucks; and the Gratiot (M-3) route through Roseville, Clinton Township, and Mount Clemens to the northern county. Turning and backing crashes with pedestrians and passenger vehicles in parking lots and at the signals.

**The northern county.** M-53 north of M-59 through Shelby, Washington, and Bruce townships to Romeo, and 26 Mile Road and M-19 serving northern Macomb County, where aggregate-hauling and construction trucks also use two-lane roads.

**Selfridge and the Arsenal.** Military and contractor trucks serving Selfridge Air National Guard Base in Harrison Township and the Army’s Detroit Arsenal in Warren. Certain crashes caused by negligent federal employees acting within the scope of their duties at Selfridge or the Detroit Arsenal may support claims against the United States under the Federal Tort Claims Act, subject to applicable exceptions. A claimant generally must present an administrative claim to the appropriate federal agency within two years under 28 USC 2401(b) and 2675. A separate six-month lawsuit deadline generally applies after a final written denial.****

## Who is responsible, and who pays

**The carrier, not just the driver.** The motor carrier is responsible for its driver’s compliance with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations and is liable for the driver’s negligence in the course of employment; Michigan adopts many Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations for intrastate commercial motor carriers under MCL 480.11a, subject to statutory modifications and exceptions. A Macomb County shuttle carrier operating solely within Michigan may therefore be subject to many of the same driver-safety, qualification, inspection, and operating requirements as an interstate carrier.****

| Rule | 49 CFR | What it means for the case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hours of service | Part 395 | 11 hours driving in a 14-hour window after 10 off; 60/70-hour weekly limits; recorded on an electronic logging device (ELD). An over-hours driver is a fatigued driver, and the ELD proves it. |
| Driver qualification | Part 391 | CDL, medical certificate, background check the carrier must perform and keep. Negligent hiring and retention are claims against the carrier. |
| Post-crash drug and alcohol testing | Part 382 | Required after a fatal crash and after an injury or tow-away crash where the driver was cited; alcohol within 8 hours, drugs within 32. |
| Inspection and maintenance | Part 396 | Pre-trip inspections, defect reports, maintenance records. Brake and tire cases are maintenance cases. |
| Cargo securement | Part 393 | Securement standards; shifted loads cause rollovers and jackknifes, and unsecured parts racks and coils cause the crashes behind the truck. |
| Handheld phone ban; hazardous conditions | 392.82, 392.14 | No handheld phone use; reduce speed or stop in snow and ice. |
| Minimum insurance | Part 387 | $750,000 for general freight; $1 million for oil; $5 million for hazardous materials. Automotive logistics carriers commonly carry more. |

**Plant and shipper responsibility.** Automotive freight in Macomb County runs on just-in-time schedules set by the plants and their logistics providers. Delivery schedules that cannot reasonably be met within federal hours-of-service limits may raise safety and compliance concerns. Under 49 CFR 390.6, motor carriers, shippers, receivers, and transportation intermediaries are prohibited from coercing drivers to violate applicable safety regulations, including through threats or adverse work-related actions after a driver identifies the violation. Dispatch records, load tenders, and gate logs show whether the schedule left room to comply. **[ATTORNEY CHECK]**

**Michigan no-fault still applies to you.** Your own auto insurer pays your medical bills, 85 percent of lost wages up to the monthly cap, replacement services, and attendant care under MCL 500.3107 regardless of fault, with written notice due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)); the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan pays if no household policy applies (MCL 500.3172). Your claim against the driver and carrier for pain and suffering and excess losses requires a threshold injury under MCL 500.3135 and must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The difference from a car case is the insurance available to pay it.

**Other defendants.** The trailer owner, often a different company from the tractor’s; the maintenance contractor; the manufacturer of a failed component; and, for a government truck, the Macomb County Department of Roads, a city, or MDOT under the Governmental Tort Liability Act’s motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405). Where a signal or road defect contributed, the Department of Roads (county roads) or MDOT (Van Dyke, Gratiot, Hall Road, Groesbeck, I-94) may be a defendant under the highway exception, MCL 691.1402, with 120-day and six-month notice requirements.

## The evidence, and how fast it disappears

Trucking companies must keep hours-of-service records for six months (49 CFR 395.8) and driver inspection reports for three months (49 CFR 396.11); after that they may lawfully be destroyed. Plant-gate cameras, MDOT freeway cameras, the Department of Roads’ signal cameras, and business cameras along Hall Road and Gratiot are overwritten in days to weeks. The truck’s engine control module and event data recorder can be cleared when the truck is repaired and returned to service. The first act in a Macomb County truck case is a **preservation letter** to the carrier, its insurer, the trailer owner, and the shipper or plant logistics provider, demanding that they keep the ELD data, ECM and EDR data, dash camera footage, the driver’s qualification file and post-crash test results, dispatch records and load tenders, gate logs, the maintenance file, and the truck itself for inspection. A carrier that fails to preserve evidence relevant to pending or reasonably foreseeable litigation may face spoliation sanctions in Michigan courts, depending on the circumstances of the loss, the importance of the evidence, and whether a reasonable explanation for its destruction exists.**** We also request the MSP Commercial Vehicle Enforcement Division’s post-crash inspection report and the signal timing data where a signal is involved.

## What to do after a Macomb County truck crash

1. **Call 911 and get medical care the same day.** Truck-crash injuries to the neck, back, and brain often present days later.
2. **Photograph the truck**: the cab, the trailer, the DOT number and carrier name on the door, the plates of tractor and trailer, the load, and the skid marks. For a plant shuttle, photograph the trailer’s placard or routing tag.
3. **Get the police report.** The UD-10 is purchased at michigan.gov/tcps for $15; for a serious crash, ask whether the MSP Commercial Vehicle Enforcement Division conducted a post-crash inspection.
4. **Do not speak to the carrier’s insurer or its investigator.** Decline the recorded statement.
5. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** within the first week.
6. **Call a lawyer within days**, so the preservation letter goes out before the records expire.

## The courts

Most Macomb County truck cases are filed in the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court at 40 N. Main Street in Mount Clemens. Cases against out-of-state carriers may be filed in or removed to the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan when federal jurisdictional requirements are satisfied, including applicable citizenship requirements and an amount in controversy exceeding $75,000 under 28 USC 1332 and 1441; Federal Tort Claims Act cases are filed there as well; and claims against MDOT go to the Court of Claims. Venue in a Michigan personal injury lawsuit follows the statutory order of priority in MCL 600.1629, generally beginning with the county where the original injury occurred and where a defendant resides, conducts business, or maintains a registered office.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many truck accidents happen in Macomb County?** 1,257 heavy trucks and buses were involved in Macomb County crashes in 2024, according to the Michigan State Police, about the same as 2023.

**I was hit by a truck turning into a plant on Mound or Van Dyke. Who is responsible?** The driver and the carrier, and possibly the plant or its logistics provider if the gate layout, the traffic control, or the delivery schedule contributed. Plant-gate cameras usually recorded the crash and are overwritten quickly; the preservation letter goes to the plant as well as the carrier.

**Who pays my medical bills after a truck crash in Michigan?** Your own no-fault insurer, regardless of fault, after you file an Application for No-Fault Benefits; the Assigned Claims Plan if no household policy applies. The carrier’s insurer pays for pain and suffering and losses beyond no-fault once fault and a threshold injury are established.

**Why does the lawyer need to act within days?** Because the records that prove the case expire: six months for hours-of-service logs, three months for inspection reports, days or weeks for camera footage, and the truck’s electronic data can be cleared on repair. A preservation letter puts the recipient on notice that relevant evidence should be preserved for anticipated litigation, even if ordinary record-retention periods would otherwise permit its destruction.

**The truck was a military or Selfridge vehicle. Is that different?** Yes. If the crash was caused by a qualifying federal employee acting within the scope of federal employment, a claim may be available against the United States under the Federal Tort Claims Act, subject to applicable exceptions. The claimant generally must submit an administrative claim within two years before filing suit. FTCA lawsuits against the United States are heard in federal court without a jury under 28 USC 2402. Claims involving contractors or different military and National Guard duty statuses require separate analysis.

**Is a truck case worth more than a car case?** The injuries are usually worse and the insurance is usually larger: $750,000 minimum for a general freight carrier against $50,000 for a minimally insured car. The value still depends on the injury, fault, and the record.

**Do you handle truck cases in northern Macomb County and along I-94 toward New Baltimore?** Throughout the county.
