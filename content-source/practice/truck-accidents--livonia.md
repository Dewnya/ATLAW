## Livonia Truck Accident Lawyer

Livonia sits on two of metro Detroit’s main freight routes. I-96 runs the length of the city along Schoolcraft Road, and I-275 runs along its western edge, where the two meet M-14 in the interchange near Laurel Park. Between them, the Plymouth Road and Schoolcraft corridors hold manufacturing plants, suppliers, warehouses, and light industry that receive and ship freight by tractor-trailer and box truck every day. In 2024, according to the Michigan State Police, 3,916 heavy trucks and buses were involved in crashes in Wayne County, more than Oakland and Macomb counties combined, and 110 people were killed and 3,613 injured in heavy truck and bus crashes statewide.

A truck crash is not a car crash with a larger vehicle. The driver and the carrier are governed by federal safety regulations whose violation is evidence of negligence. The carrier must hold $750,000 or more in insurance. ## Where Livonia truck crashes happen

**I-96 and the Schoolcraft service drives.** I-96 carries freight between Detroit, Lansing, and Grand Rapids, with interchanges at Inkster, Middlebelt, Merriman, Farmington, Levan, and Newburgh. The freeway crashes are rear-end collisions in stop-and-go traffic, including underride crashes into the back of a trailer; lane changes into a car in the truck’s blind spot; and rollovers and jackknifes on the ramps and curves. Local news has reported semi crashes that closed I-96 in Livonia, including a truck that struck the Farmington Road overpass in October 2011, closing the eastbound lanes at Newburgh, and a rollover near Levan in May 2016.

**I-275, M-14, and the interchange.** I-275 is the western bypass for freight moving between I-75, I-94, the airport cargo terminals in Romulus, and the northern suburbs, and the I-96/I-275/M-14 interchange at the city’s western edge is where those flows merge and split. Merge and weave crashes, trucks changing lanes for an exit, and backups at the ramps are the interchange’s cases; southbound I-275 near Six Mile is a crash area the Livonia Police Department identifies separately. **[VERIFY]**

**Plymouth Road, Schoolcraft, and the industrial parcels.** Tractor-trailers and box trucks turning wide into loading docks and driveways across two lanes; trucks backing into docks from the road; trucks stopped in a travel lane waiting to enter; and trucks pulling out into fast traffic with limited sight lines. When a facility’s dock layout, traffic control, or delivery schedule puts trucks in the road, the facility or its logistics provider may share responsibility with the carrier.

**The mile roads and the retail corridors.** Delivery trucks serving Laurel Park Place, the Middlebelt and Seven Mile strips, and the Six Mile office and hotel cluster: turning and backing crashes that injure drivers of smaller vehicles, cyclists, and pedestrians (see our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents)).

**Whose road it is.** I-96, I-275, M-14, and generally the I-96 service drives are MDOT’s; a road-defect claim against the State goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). The mile roads, including Eight Mile from Haggerty to Farmington, most arterials, and Plymouth Road west of Newburgh are generally Wayne County’s, and other local streets the City of Livonia’s, with 120 days' notice (MCL 691.1404).

## Who is responsible, and who pays

**The carrier, not just the driver.** The motor carrier is responsible for its drivers’ compliance with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations and is liable for a driver’s negligence in the course of employment. Michigan applies the federal rules to intrastate carriers (MCL 480.11a), so a local hauler running between Livonia suppliers is held to the same standards as an interstate carrier. The rules that decide truck cases most often:

| Rule | 49 CFR | What it means for the case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hours of service | Part 395 | 11 hours driving in a 14-hour window after 10 off; 60/70-hour weekly limits; recorded on an electronic logging device. Waiting time at a dock counts as on-duty time. |
| Driver qualification | Part 391 | CDL, medical certificate, driving record, background check; negligent hiring and retention are claims against the carrier. |
| Post-crash testing | Part 382 | Alcohol within 8 hours and drugs within 32 after a fatal crash, or an injury or tow-away crash with a citation. |
| Inspection and maintenance | Part 396 | Pre-trip inspections, defect reports, maintenance records; brake and tire cases are maintenance cases. |
| Cargo securement | Part 393 | Securement standards; shifted loads cause rollovers on ramps and spilled cargo on the freeway. |
| Handheld phone ban; hazardous conditions | 392.82, 392.14 | No handheld phone use; reduce speed or stop in snow, ice, and fog. |
| Minimum insurance | Part 387 | $750,000 general freight; $1 million oil; $5 million hazardous materials. |

**The owner of the truck or trailer.** In Michigan, a vehicle’s owner is liable for its negligent operation by someone driving with the owner’s consent (MCL 257.401). When the tractor, the trailer, and the driver belong to different companies, which is common in freight, each may be a defendant, although a company in the business of renting or leasing vehicles is generally shielded from owner liability by federal law (49 USC 30106).

**Michigan no-fault still applies to you.** Your own auto insurer pays your medical bills, 85 percent of lost wages up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year, replacement services, and attendant care regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107), with written notice due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). The Michigan Assigned Claims Plan pays if no household policy applies (MCL 500.3172). Your claim against the driver and the carrier for pain and suffering and losses beyond no-fault requires a threshold injury (MCL 500.3135) and must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). If you were more than 50 percent at fault, you cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, and below that your share reduces them (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). The difference from a car case is the money available: the carrier’s $750,000-plus policy, often with excess coverage above it. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

**Other defendants.** The shipper or broker that loaded the trailer or hired an unsafe carrier; the facility or logistics provider whose dock operations contributed; the maintenance contractor; the manufacturer of a failed component ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability)); and, for a government truck (City of Livonia, Wayne County, MDOT, SMART), the agency under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405).

**If you were the truck driver.** Workers’ compensation pays your benefits, and your employer usually cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). If you were injured in your employer’s truck, no-fault benefits generally come from the insurer of that truck (MCL 500.3114(3)), coordinated with comp, which is paid first (MCL 500.3109(1)). You can also sue a third party, such as another driver or a facility whose dock operation caused the crash. See our [Livonia workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation/livonia).

## The evidence, and how fast it disappears

Carriers must keep hours-of-service records for six months (49 CFR 395.8) and driver inspection reports for three months (49 CFR 396.11); after that the regulations no longer require them. MDOT freeway cameras, business cameras along Plymouth Road and the service drives, and dash cameras overwrite in days to weeks. The truck’s engine control module and event data recorder can be cleared when it is repaired and returned to service.

The first act in a Livonia truck case is a **preservation letter**: to the carrier, its insurer, the tractor and trailer owners, the shipper or broker, and, where the crash happened at or near a facility, the facility and its logistics provider. It demands the ELD and engine data, dash camera footage, the driver’s qualification file and post-crash test results, dispatch records and bills of lading, dock appointment and arrival logs, maintenance files, and the truck itself for inspection. Once a party knows a claim is coming, destroying that evidence exposes it to spoliation sanctions.

## What to do after a Livonia truck crash

1. **Call 911 and get medical care the same day.** Trinity Health Livonia Hospital on Five Mile treats many of the city’s seriously injured; Corewell Health Farmington Hills, Garden City Hospital, and the Detroit Level I trauma centers treat others.
2. **Photograph the truck**: the cab, the trailer, the USDOT number and carrier name on the door, both plates, the load, and the dock or driveway if the truck was entering or leaving one.
3. **Get the police report.** The Michigan State Police generally work freeway crashes; Livonia Police work the surface roads and have a motor carrier officer for commercial vehicle inspections. A serious crash may produce a separate commercial vehicle inspection report. Livonia Police reports are available through CLEMIS.
4. **Do not speak to the carrier’s insurer or its investigator.**
5. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** within the first week.
6. **Call a lawyer within days**, so the preservation letter goes out before the records expire.

## The courts and deadlines

Most Livonia truck cases are filed in the Third Judicial Circuit Court at the Coleman A. Young Municipal Center in Detroit. Cases against out-of-state carriers may be filed in or removed to the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan, which sits in Detroit. A crash on Eight Mile, or with an Oakland County defendant, may allow filing in the Oakland County Circuit Court (MCL 600.1629). Claims against the State go to the Court of Claims; smaller claims go to the 16th District Court on Five Mile Road. A fatal crash is brought by the estate through the Wayne County Probate Court; see our [Livonia wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death/livonia).

## Frequently asked questions

**How many truck accidents happen in Livonia?** Countywide, 3,916 heavy trucks and buses were involved in Wayne County crashes in 2024, and statewide 15,888 crashes involved a heavy truck or bus.

**I was rear-ended by a semi in I-96 traffic. Who pays?** Your own no-fault insurer pays your medical bills and lost wages first, regardless of fault. The carrier’s insurer pays for pain and suffering and losses beyond no-fault once a threshold injury is established; in a rear-end crash, fault is rarely the dispute, but the truck’s speed, the driver’s hours, and the following distance in the engine data decide how strong the claim is.

**A truck was stopped in the lane on Plymouth Road waiting to get into a dock, and I hit it. Is that my fault?** Not necessarily. Following too closely is usually the claim against the rear driver, but a truck stopped in a travel lane without warning, or a facility whose dock schedule routinely stacks trucks in the road, can carry fault too. The dock logs, the cameras, and the truck’s data decide it.

**I drive a truck for a Livonia company and was hurt in a crash. What are my claims?** Workers’ compensation through your employer, no-fault benefits coordinated with comp, and a lawsuit against any third party who caused the crash.

**Why does the lawyer need to act within days?** Because the records expire: six months for hours-of-service logs, three months for inspection reports, days or weeks for camera footage, and the truck’s electronic data can be cleared on repair.

**Is a truck case worth more than a car case?** The injuries are usually worse and the insurance is usually larger: a $750,000 minimum for a general freight carrier, against $50,000 for a minimally insured car.
