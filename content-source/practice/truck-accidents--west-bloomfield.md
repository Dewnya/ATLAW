## West Bloomfield, MI Truck Accident Lawyer

West Bloomfield Township has no interstate and no industrial park. Its heavy trucks are local, and they share the township’s roads with commuters, school buses, and residents:

- **Delivery trucks** supplying the stores and restaurants along Orchard Lake Road, Maple, Fourteen Mile, and Haggerty.
- **Construction and dump trucks, cement mixers, and equipment haulers** building and rebuilding homes on the lake roads and in the subdivisions.
- **Landscaping, tree-service, and snow-plowing trucks,** many of them pickups towing trailers loaded with mowers, brush, stone, or mulch.
- **Garbage and recycling trucks, fuel trucks, and package vans** on the residential streets.
- **Trucks towing boats** to and from the lakes.

In 2024, according to the Michigan State Police, 2,081 heavy trucks and buses were involved in crashes in Oakland County. Statewide, 110 people were killed and 3,613 injured in heavy truck and bus crashes. West Bloomfield recorded 1,223 crashes of all kinds, nearly all of them on county and local roads.

A truck crash is not a car crash with a larger vehicle:

- **The rules may be federal.** A commercial vehicle over 10,000 pounds is governed by federal and Michigan safety regulations, and a violation is evidence of negligence.
- **The insurance is commercial.** A business vehicle carries a commercial auto policy, and a regulated carrier must carry $750,000 or more.
- **The records expire.** Driver logs, inspection reports, and the truck’s electronic data can be lawfully discarded within months, and job-site records may never be kept at all.
- **The other side moves fast.** The company’s insurer will have an adjuster on the case within days.

## Where West Bloomfield truck crashes happen

**The retail corridors.** Orchard Lake Road, Maple, Fourteen Mile, and Haggerty carry box trucks and tractor-trailers to the shopping centers, grocery stores, and restaurants. The crashes are:

- wide right turns into a driveway or onto a cross street that catch a car on the inside of the turn
- slow left turns across oncoming traffic into a loading area
- trucks backing into and out of service drives
- rear-end crashes at the signals, and crashes at the Farmington Road and Maple roundabout, one of Oakland County’s ten highest-crash intersections in 2024
-

**The lake roads and construction sites.** Building and rebuilding homes on Lone Pine, Long Lake, Walnut Lake, Commerce, Cass Lake, and the roads around the lakes brings dump trucks, cement mixers, excavator haulers, and roll-off trucks onto two-lane roads with curves, hills, and short sight lines. The crashes are:

- a truck pulling out of a site or a steep driveway into the path of a car that cannot see it over a hill or around a curve
- a truck crossing the center line on a curve, or forcing an oncoming car off the road
- a truck backing out of a site without a spotter
- mud, gravel, and debris tracked onto the road from the site
- a truck parked in the lane at a site with no warning devices

When a general contractor or a homeowner controls the site, its practices on spotters, flaggers, staging, and cleanup can make it responsible along with the truck’s operator.

**Landscape trucks and trailers.** Spring and fall bring landscaping, tree-service, and lawn crews with pickups towing open trailers. The crashes are:

- a trailer that comes loose because the hitch, coupler, or safety chains failed
- mowers, brush, stone, or mulch falling or blowing from an open trailer, which violates Michigan’s load rules (MCL 257.720)
- a trailer whose lights or brakes do not work
- a crew truck parked partly in the lane while the crew works

The company that owns the truck and trailer is liable for its employee’s negligence. If the vehicle combination is over 10,000 pounds and used in commerce, the federal and Michigan safety rules apply to it. Many are not, and the case is then built on Michigan’s vehicle code and ordinary negligence.

**Seasonal weight restrictions.** In spring, as frost leaves the ground, the Road Commission for Oakland County imposes seasonal weight limits on many county roads. A heavy truck running overweight on a restricted road damages the road and takes longer to stop. A violation is evidence against the operator and the company.

**Garbage trucks, fuel trucks, and delivery vans.** Garbage and recycling trucks, fuel-oil and propane trucks, and package vans work the subdivision and lake streets. The crashes are backing crashes, crashes with vehicles stopped in the lane, and children and pedestrians struck by a truck pulling away. Delivery vans are often operated by contracted delivery companies rather than by the retailer named on the package. The van’s operator, its insurer, and the contract between them have to be identified.

M-10. The township’s few state-route crashes are on M-10 (Northwestern Highway), which ends at Orchard Lake Road in the township, where trucks from the freeway system enter the area.

**Whose road it is: almost never the township’s.** Michigan townships do not own or maintain public roads.

- **The Road Commission for Oakland County** owns Orchard Lake Road, Maple, the mile roads, the lake roads, and the public subdivision streets. A claim against it requires written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404).
- **MDOT** owns M-10. A road-defect claim against the State goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **Private roads** in some subdivisions and lake associations belong to the association or the owners.

A notice sent to West Bloomfield Township about a road defect is a notice to the wrong defendant.

## Who is responsible, and who pays

**The company, not just the driver.** A business is liable for its driver’s negligence in the course of employment. When the vehicle is a commercial motor vehicle, the motor carrier is also responsible for the driver’s compliance with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations, and Michigan applies those rules to intrastate carriers (MCL 480.11a). A local hauler, a construction company, or a landscaping business that never leaves Oakland County can be held to them.

These are the rules that decide truck cases most often:

| Rule | 49 CFR | What it means for the case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which vehicles are covered | 390.5 | A vehicle or combination over 10,000 lb used in commerce is a commercial motor vehicle. A pickup towing a heavy trailer can qualify. |
| Hours of service | Part 395 | 11 hours driving in a 14-hour window after 10 hours off; 60/70-hour weekly limits. Many local and short-haul drivers are exempt from logs but not from the limits. |
| Driver qualification | Part 391 | Medical certificate, driving record, background check; a CDL for heavier vehicles. Negligent hiring and retention are claims against the company. |
| Post-crash testing | Part 382 | For CDL drivers: alcohol within 8 hours and drugs within 32 hours after a fatal crash, or after an injury or tow-away crash with a citation. |
| Inspection and maintenance | Part 396 | Pre-trip inspections, defect reports, maintenance records. Brake, tire, hitch, and lighting cases are maintenance cases. |
| Cargo securement | 393.100–393.136 | Loads must be contained or secured so they cannot shift or fall. A spilled load is evidence of a violation. |
| Stopped vehicles; hazardous conditions; phones | 392.22, 392.14, 392.82 | Warning devices for a stopped truck; reduce speed or stop in snow, ice, and fog; no handheld phone use. |
| Minimum insurance | Part 387 | $750,000 general freight for a regulated interstate carrier; other carriers carry their own commercial limits. |

**The owner of the truck or trailer.** In Michigan, a vehicle’s owner is liable for its negligent operation by someone driving with the owner’s consent (MCL 257.401). A rented dump truck, a leased tractor, or a trailer owned by a different company from the truck that pulled it may each bring in another defendant, although a company in the business of renting or leasing vehicles is generally shielded from owner liability by federal law (49 USC 30106).

**Michigan no-fault still applies to you.** Your own auto insurer pays these benefits regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107):

- your medical bills
- 85 percent of lost wages, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- replacement services
- attendant care

Written notice is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)).

Your claim against the driver and the company is for pain and suffering and for losses beyond no-fault, including lost wages above the PIP cap. It requires a threshold injury (MCL 500.3135) and must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). Michigan’s comparative fault rule is modified, not pure:

- **More than 50 percent at fault:** you cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages.
- **50 percent or less:** your share reduces them (MCL 500.3135(2)(b); MCL 600.2959).

The noneconomic damages caps apply to medical malpractice and to product liability claims, such as a claim against the maker of a failed part (MCL 600.1483, 600.2946a), not to the claim against the driver and carrier.

A business’s commercial auto policy may be larger than a household policy, and some companies carry an excess or umbrella policy above it. If the coverage still falls short, your own underinsured motorist coverage may add to it. Your insurer’s written consent is needed before you accept the company’s limit. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

**Other defendants.**

- the general contractor or property owner whose site practices contributed
- the company that loaded the truck or trailer
- the maintenance shop that last worked on the brakes or hitch
- the manufacturer of a failed component, a defective hitch, or a defective guard ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability))
- for a government truck (the Road Commission, a bus a school district runs itself, or a township vehicle), the agency, under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405)

**If you were the driver or a crew member.** Workers’ compensation pays your benefits, and your employer usually cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). If you were injured in your employer’s truck, no-fault benefits generally come from the insurer of that truck (MCL 500.3114(3)). They are coordinated with comp, which is paid first (MCL 500.3109(1)). You can also sue a third party, such as another driver or a contractor whose site caused the crash. See our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).

## The evidence, and how fast it disappears

Regulated carriers must keep two kinds of records for a limited time:

- hours-of-service records, for six months (49 CFR 395.8)
- driver inspection reports, for three months (49 CFR 396.11)

A local contractor or landscaping company may keep far less. Other evidence goes faster:

- dash cameras, store cameras, and home doorbell cameras overwrite in days to weeks
- GPS and telematics data on a company’s trucks may be kept only as long as the company’s plan allows
- the truck’s engine control module can be cleared when the truck is repaired
- a spilled load or tracked-out gravel is cleaned up within hours
- a failed hitch or safety chain can be repaired or thrown away

The first act in a West Bloomfield truck case is a **preservation letter**. It goes to the company, its insurer, the owners of the truck and trailer, and, where the truck was working at a site, the general contractor and the property owner. It demands:

- dash camera footage, GPS and telematics data, and engine data
- the driver’s qualification file and any post-crash test results
- dispatch records, job tickets, and delivery or load records
- maintenance files for the truck and the trailer
- the truck, the trailer, the hitch, and the load, for inspection

Once a party knows a claim is coming, destroying that evidence exposes it to spoliation sanctions.

## What to do after a West Bloomfield truck crash

1. **Call 911 and get medical care the same day.** Henry Ford West Bloomfield Hospital, on Maple Road, is a Level III trauma center. The most severe injuries may go to a Level I center such as Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak.
2. **Photograph the truck.** Get these shots: the truck and any trailer; the company name and any USDOT number on the door; both plates, and the trailer’s plate; the load, and anything that fell from it; the hitch and safety chains, if a trailer came loose; and the construction site, driveway, or loading area if the truck was entering or leaving one.
3. **Get the police report.** Crashes in the township are worked by the West Bloomfield Police Department. A serious crash with a commercial vehicle may also produce a commercial vehicle inspection by the State Police.
4. **Do not speak to the company’s insurer or its adjuster.**
5. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** promptly.
6. **Call a lawyer within days,** so the preservation letter goes out before the footage, the data, and the trailer are gone.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many truck accidents happen in West Bloomfield?** The State’s county profile does not break out truck crashes by community. Countywide, 2,081 heavy trucks and buses were involved in Oakland County crashes in 2024. Statewide, 15,888 crashes involved a heavy truck or bus, and 110 people were killed.

**A dump truck pulled out of a construction site on a lake road and I hit it. Who is at fault?** It depends on the facts. A truck pulling onto the road must yield to traffic already on it, and a site without a flagger or spotter where the view was blocked can put responsibility on the contractor too. Your own speed matters as well. Photograph the site entrance, the hill or curve, and anything that blocked the view.

**A landscaping trailer came loose and hit my car. Who pays?** The company that owned the truck and trailer, through its commercial auto policy, and possibly the maker of a failed hitch. A trailer must be properly hitched with safety chains and working lights. Keep the trailer and hitch from being repaired or discarded until they are inspected.

**Mulch, stone, or brush flew off a truck and broke my windshield, and I crashed. Is that the truck’s fault?** Very likely. Michigan requires loads to be secured or covered so they cannot fall or blow from the vehicle. Get the company name and plate if you can; witnesses and doorbell cameras often help identify the truck.

**The truck was a pickup, not a semi. Do the federal trucking rules apply?** Sometimes. A pickup and trailer over 10,000 pounds used in a business can be a commercial motor vehicle under the federal rules, which Michigan applies to intrastate carriers. If not, the claim is built on Michigan’s vehicle code and ordinary negligence, and the company is still liable for its employee.

**Is there a cap on what I can recover from a trucking or construction company?** Not on pain and suffering against the driver or carrier. Michigan’s damages caps apply to medical malpractice and product liability claims. In practice, the limit is the insurance available: the company’s commercial policy, any excess or umbrella policy, and your own underinsured coverage.

**Why does the lawyer need to act within days?** Because the evidence expires. Regulated carriers keep logs for six months and inspection reports for three, and a local contractor may keep much less. Camera footage and telematics data may last only days or weeks, and a trailer or hitch can be repaired or thrown away.
