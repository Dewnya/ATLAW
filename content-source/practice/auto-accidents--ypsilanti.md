## Ypsilanti Car Accident Lawyer

A car crash in Ypsilanti is a Michigan no-fault claim, and the first question is which Ypsilanti it happened in:

- **The City of Ypsilanti:** downtown’s one-way grid, Depot Town, Eastern Michigan University, and the state routes that run through them, Washtenaw Avenue, Michigan Avenue, and Huron and Hamilton Streets.
- **Ypsilanti Charter Township:** Michigan Avenue and Ecorse Road east toward Wayne County, Whittaker, Textile, and Grove Roads to the south, Ford Lake, Willow Run, and I-94 with its interchanges.

In 2024, the Michigan State Police recorded **1,907 crashes** in the two together. **6 people were killed**, 40 were seriously injured, and 340 crashes injured someone. The county’s top crash intersection, Hamilton Street at Washtenaw Avenue, is in the city.

An Ypsilanti crash claim usually turns on four questions:

- **Fault on the grid, the corridor, or the ramp.**
- **Who pays your no-fault benefits.** For an EMU student or a pedestrian without a car, that may be a parent’s policy or, with none, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan.
- **The other driver’s insurance,** and how your own uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage fits with it.
- **Whether a public body is involved,** and which one: the City, the Road Commission, MDOT, TheRide, or Eastern Michigan University. The court and any notice deadline depend on which one.

The numbers show two different places:

- **The city’s crashes are on state routes.** 340 of its 838 crashes, about 4 in 10, were on state routes, and another 188 on U.S. routes. The streets that carry those routes through downtown are where the city’s crashes concentrate, and who owns them decides a road-defect claim.

## Where Ypsilanti crashes happen

**Hamilton and Washtenaw, and the downtown grid.** Hamilton Street at Washtenaw Avenue had 132 crashes in 2024, more than any other intersection in Washtenaw County. It is where Washtenaw Avenue comes in from the west and meets the one-way pairs that carry traffic through downtown. The crashes on the grid are:

- drivers turning the wrong way, or from the wrong lane, onto a one-way street
- lane changes into an occupied lane where one-way streets merge and split (MCL 257.642(1)(a))
- red-light and left-turn crashes (MCL 257.650)
- rear-end crashes in the stacking at the signals
- pedestrians struck in crosswalks near the Ypsilanti Transit Center, Depot Town, and campus (MCL 257.612)

**Michigan Avenue.** Through downtown, Michigan Avenue at Huron Street and at Prospect Street both made the county’s top ten. East of the city, in the township, it is a four- and five-lane road past strip retail, motels, and gas stations, with frequent driveways and long gaps between signals. The crashes are:

- left turns across traffic into driveways and side streets
- vehicles pulling out of driveways (MCL 257.652)
- pedestrians crossing between signals, often at night
- rear-end crashes at the signals

**Washtenaw Avenue and the EMU campus.** Washtenaw Avenue runs from Ann Arbor past the shopping centers in Pittsfield and Ypsilanti townships and into the city along Eastern Michigan’s campus. The crashes are left turns into the shopping centers, rear-end crashes in the turn lanes, and students on foot, on bikes, and on scooters struck at the campus crossings.

**I-94 and the interchanges.** I-94 runs along the edge of the city and through the township, with interchanges at Michigan Avenue, Huron Street, and Rawsonville Road. The township recorded 188 interstate crashes in 2024. The crashes are:

- rear-end and lane-change crashes in the backups at the ramps
- merge crashes on short ramps
- crashes in work zones
- truck crashes

**The township’s county roads.** Whittaker, Textile, Grove, Huron River Drive, and the subdivision roads south of I-94 are the township’s local roads, where it recorded 676 crashes in 2024. The crashes are:

- failure to yield at stop signs and unsignalized intersections
- head-on and run-off-road crashes on two-lane stretches
- deer: the township recorded 62 deer-involved crashes in 2024, against 11 in the city

**Drunk and drugged driving.** The township recorded 36 alcohol-involved and 18 drug-involved crashes in 2024, and the city 13 and 4. A bar, restaurant, or store that serves a visibly intoxicated person, or a minor, is liable for the injuries that person causes (see below).

## Whose road it is

Ypsilanti has four possible road owners, and a notice sent to the wrong one does not protect the claim:

- **In the city, the City of Ypsilanti** owns its local streets and sidewalks. A claim for a road, signal, or sidewalk defect requires written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1402a, 691.1404).
- **In the township, the Washtenaw County Road Commission** owns the public roads. Townships do not own roads. A claim requires written notice within 120 days. The township has the duty for sidewalks along those roads (MCL 691.1401, 691.1402a).
- **MDOT** owns I-94, US-12, and M-17, and any state trunklines through the city. A road-defect claim goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **Eastern Michigan University** owns its campus drives and lots. A claim against it is a claim against the State (see below).

## When the other vehicle belongs to EMU, TheRide, or a public agency

**Eastern Michigan University** is a constitutional state university. A crash with a University-owned vehicle or an EMU police car is a claim against the State, under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405). That changes three things:

- **The court.** The claim is filed in the Michigan Court of Claims and decided by a judge, not a jury.
- **The notice.** A verified notice, or the claim itself, must be filed with the Court of Claims within six months of the crash (MCL 600.6431). The Michigan Supreme Court has held that the notice applies to any claim against the State, wherever it is filed (*Christie v Wayne State University*, 2023). Missing it can end the claim, even though the ordinary deadline is three years.
- **The individual.** A claim against an individual University employee raises separate questions.

**TheRide** runs routes along Washtenaw Avenue and through the city and township from the Ypsilanti Transit Center downtown. A crash with a TheRide bus is a claim under the motor vehicle exception against the Ann Arbor Area Transportation Authority, in the circuit court. A bus passenger’s no-fault benefits follow the bus-passenger priority rules (MCL 500.3114(2)). See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

**City, Sheriff, and Road Commission vehicles** are claims under the same exception, against the agency that owns the vehicle. An individual officer or employee is personally liable only for gross negligence (MCL 691.1407(2)). Dash camera and body camera footage is overwritten on short schedules, so we request it by FOIA promptly.

## Fault is the case

In a rear-end crash, fault is rarely disputed. On a one-way grid, at a driveway on Michigan Avenue, or at a freeway merge, it often is. The other driver’s insurer may try to assign you half or more of the fault, and that share matters (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)):

- **More than 50 percent at fault:** you lose your claim for pain and suffering. It is barred, not merely “limited.”
- **50 percent or less:** your recovery is reduced by your share. At exactly 50 percent, you recover half.

The evidence that decides fault is perishable:

- the crash report from the Ypsilanti Police Department in the city, the Sheriff’s Office in the township, EMU police on campus, or the State Police on I-94
- the City’s or MDOT’s signal timing data
- cameras at gas stations, stores, motels, and apartment buildings along Michigan Avenue and Washtenaw Avenue, many overwritten within days
- TheRide’s bus cameras, if a bus was nearby
- the vehicles’ **event data recorders,** which store speed, braking, and throttle in the seconds before impact
- the other driver’s phone records, for a distracted-driving claim (see our [distracted driving page](/personal-injury/distracted-driving-accidents))
- witnesses

We move promptly to request the camera footage, signal data, and vehicle data.

## Who pays after an Ypsilanti crash

**Your no-fault (PIP) claim.** Your own auto insurer pays these benefits regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107):

- your medical expenses, up to the PIP level you chose
- 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- replacement services at $20 a day
- attendant care

**The PIP level is not the same as your liability limits.** Since July 2, 2020, Michigan drivers choose a PIP medical level for each policy: unlimited, $500,000, $250,000, $50,000 for some Medicaid enrollees, or an opt-out for some Medicare enrollees (MCL 500.3107c). That choice is separate from the liability limits that protect the other driver.

**Pedestrians, cyclists, and EMU students without a car.** Many Ypsilanti residents and EMU students walk, bike, or ride the bus. A person hit while walking, biking, or riding in someone else’s car claims no-fault benefits in this order (MCL 500.3114, 500.3115, 500.3172):

1. a policy in the person’s household, which for a student may be a parent’s policy if the student is still domiciled with the parent
2. if none applies and the person was riding in a car, the insurer of that car (MCL 500.3114(4)) 3. otherwise, including a person hit while walking or biking, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan, capped at $250,000 for medical benefits (MCL 500.3115(1))

Whether a student living in Ypsilanti is still domiciled with a parent depends on the facts.

**Out-of-state drivers and students.** A crash in Ypsilanti with an out-of-state driver, or involving a student from another state, still follows Michigan’s no-fault rules. Insurers that sell auto insurance in Michigan must provide Michigan benefits for a crash here.

**Scooters and e-bikes.** A scooter or e-bike rider hit by a car is generally treated like a pedestrian or cyclist for no-fault purposes. A rider who falls with no car involved generally has no no-fault claim.

The insurer must receive written notice within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). It must pay each bill within 30 days of reasonable proof, or owe 12 percent interest (MCL 500.3142). See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

**If you had no insurance.** A person who owned a car and was required to insure it, but did not, is generally barred from PIP benefits for their own injuries (MCL 500.3113(b)). A driver who was operating their own uninsured vehicle cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, whoever was at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(c)).

**Your claim against the at-fault driver.** For pain and suffering, you need a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment is an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life. It does not have to be permanent (MCL 500.3135(5); *McCormick v Carrier*, 2010).

You can also recover economic losses that no-fault does not cover (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)):

- lost wages beyond the three years or above the monthly cap
- medical bills above your chosen PIP level

The suit must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The mini tort claim recovers up to $3,000 in vehicle damage not covered by your policy (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)). If the at-fault driver’s insurer will not pay it, the claim is filed in the 14A-1 District Court for a crash in the city or the 14B District Court for a crash in the township.

**When the other driver has no insurance, or not enough.** Some drivers carry only the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)), and some carry nothing.

- **Against an uninsured driver:** your pain-and-suffering claim is worth what that driver can personally pay, unless you carry **uninsured motorist coverage**. A hit-and-run is usually a UM claim, with short notice terms in the policy.
- **Against an underinsured driver:** your own **underinsured motorist coverage** pays the difference up to your UIM limit, if you follow the steps in order:
 1. The at-fault driver’s insurer offers its limit.
 2. You notify your own insurer and ask for its written consent to settle.
 3. Only then do you accept the limit and sign the release.
 4. The UIM claim then proceeds.

Accepting without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under many policies. A student may be covered by UM or UIM under a parent’s policy as a resident relative. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Drunk drivers and the dram shop claim.** A bar, restaurant, or store that serves a visibly intoxicated person, or a minor, is liable for the injuries that person causes (MCL 436.1801). Written notice must go to the business within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, the drunk driver must be named and kept in the suit, and the suit must be filed within two years. See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

## What to do after an Ypsilanti crash

1. **Call 911 and get the police report.** Michigan requires a report for any crash with injury or $1,000 in damage (MCL 257.622). Note which agency responded. It tells you whether the crash was in the city or the township.
2. **Photograph the scene:** the vehicles, plates, the other driver’s license and insurance card, the signal, lane markings, and one-way signs, and the driveway or ramp. Note every business with a camera facing the scene. Footage is kept for days, not weeks.
3. **Get medical care the same day.** Trinity Health Ann Arbor, which has an Ypsilanti mailing address but sits in Superior Township, and University of Michigan Health in Ann Arbor are Level I trauma centers.
4. **If an EMU vehicle, a TheRide bus, or another public vehicle was involved,** write down the vehicle number and tell us at once. The six-month Court of Claims notice for an EMU vehicle is already running.
5. **Report to your own insurer, or your parents’ insurer if you are a student,** and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits promptly.
6. **Read the declarations pages** for the PIP medical level, UM/UIM, and collision coverage, including a parent’s policy.
7. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the other driver’s insurer, sign a release, or accept a policy-limits offer before your UIM insurer consents.
8. **Call a lawyer within days** if fault is disputed, the other driver is uninsured, a public body is involved, or a bar served the driver.

## The courts

- **Claims up to $25,000,** including mini tort claims, go to the 14A-1 District Court at 4133 Washtenaw Avenue in Pittsfield Township for a crash in the city, or the **14B District Court** at 7200 S. Huron River Drive for a crash in the township.
- **Injury suits over $25,000** go to the Washtenaw County Trial Court (22nd Circuit) at 101 East Huron Street in Ann Arbor, with a jury drawn from Washtenaw County.
- **Claims against EMU, MDOT, or the State** go to the Michigan Court of Claims.
- **Cases against out-of-state defendants** may be filed in, or removed to, the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan.
- **A fatal crash** is brought by the estate, opened through the Washtenaw County Probate Court. See our [Ypsilanti wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death).

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I know if my crash was in the city or the township?** The police report names the jurisdiction, and the responding agency is a clue: the Ypsilanti Police Department in the city, the Sheriff’s Office in the township. A mailing address that says “Ypsilanti” does not settle it. The answer decides who owns the road and which district court hears a small claim.

**I was hit at Hamilton and Washtenaw. Who is at fault?** It depends on the lanes and the signal. Hamilton and Washtenaw had more crashes than any other intersection in Washtenaw County in 2024, many of them lane-change and turning crashes where one-way streets meet. Signal data and nearby cameras usually decide it, and both are lost quickly.

**I’m an EMU student and I don’t have a car. Who pays my medical bills?** Possibly your parent’s auto policy, if you are still domiciled with your parent. If no household policy applies, you claim through the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan. Your claim for pain and suffering is against the driver.

**An EMU vehicle hit me. Is that different?** Yes. EMU is part of the State, so the claim goes to the Michigan Court of Claims, and a verified notice or the claim itself must be filed within six months of the crash. Your no-fault benefits are paid the usual way.

**A pothole on a township road caused my crash. Do I sue the township?** No. Townships do not own roads. A township road belongs to the Washtenaw County Road Commission, and written notice must reach it within 120 days. If the road is I-94, US-12, or M-17, the claim is against MDOT, with written notice filed in the Court of Claims within 120 days.

**How many crashes are there in Ypsilanti?** The State Police recorded 838 crashes in the city and 1,069 in the township in 2024, 1,907 in all. 6 people were killed and 40 were seriously injured.

**Is my PIP medical coverage the same as my liability limit?** Not necessarily. Your liability limits protect the other driver if you cause a crash. Your PIP medical level is a separate choice on the same policy, and it decides how much of your own medical care no-fault pays.

**How long do I have?** - Six months for a claim against EMU or the State, and 120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT. - 120 days for a notice to the City or the Road Commission. - One year to give your no-fault insurer written notice. - Two years to sue a bar, with a 120-day notice. - Three years to sue the at-fault driver.
