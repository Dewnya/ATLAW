## Novi, MI Personal Injury Lawyer

If you were hurt in Novi because someone else was careless, you have a claim under Michigan law, and its deadlines start the day you are hurt:

- **After a crash,** your no-fault insurer must have written notice within one year.
- A road or pathway defect needs written notice within 120 days to the City of Novi or the Road Commission for Oakland County. A defect on I-96, I-275, or M-5 needs written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days.
- **An injury in a City building, a school, or another public building** needs written notice to that agency within 120 days.
- **A claim against a bar or restaurant** that served a drunk driver needs written notice within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, and suit within two years.
- **A medical malpractice suit,** including claims about hospital care and many claims against nursing homes, requires 182 days’ notice first and must be filed within two years.
- **Most other claims,** including falls in stores and at the mall, dog bites, and crashes, allow three years.

A personal injury lawyer’s first job is to find out which of those deadlines apply to you. The second is to make sure none runs out before your claim is paid.

## What a Novi personal injury lawyer does

**The first week.** We calendar every deadline and send preservation letters to:

- the at-fault driver’s insurer
- a trucking company for its driver logs and electronic data, after a crash on I-96 or near the city’s warehouses
- MDOT, the Road Commission, or the City for signal timing records on Novi Road, Grand River, and the mile roads
- the City for Novi Police patrol-car video and dispatch records
- a school district or transit provider for vehicle records
- the mall, a store, a hotel, an event venue, an apartment complex, or a property manager for footage, incident reports, and snow-removal logs, which many overwrite or discard within days
- a snow-removal or maintenance contractor, which is often a separate defendant
- a nursing home or assisted living community for the resident’s chart, incident reports, and staffing records

We also obtain:

- the police report (the Novi Police Department in the city, the State Police on the freeways)
- the Novi Fire Department and ambulance run sheets
- the witnesses’ names

**The first month.** We identify every policy that may pay:

- your own auto policy, for no-fault benefits
- other household policies
- the at-fault party’s liability coverage
- your underinsured and uninsured motorist coverage
- a store’s, mall’s, hotel’s, venue’s, or management company’s commercial policy
- a contractor’s policy, for snow removal or maintenance
- umbrella policies
- the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan, where no auto policy exists (MCL 500.3172)

A pedestrian or cyclist hit by a car is covered by no-fault through the household policy first, then the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3114, 500.3115). For a workplace injury, we identify whether a third party caused it, because the employer itself usually cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). In Novi that often means a trucking company at a warehouse dock, an equipment manufacturer, a contractor on a construction site, or a property owner.

We file your Application for No-Fault Benefits inside the one-year window (MCL 500.3145(1)). We handle the insurer’s requests for statements, records, and examinations (MCL 500.3151). You stop talking to adjusters, and we do that instead.

**The treatment period.** The claim’s value is set mostly by the medical record, so we make sure it is complete and the bills are paid while you treat.

- **Henry Ford Providence Novi Hospital,** on Grand River near I-96 and Beck Road, is a Level II adult trauma center. The most severe injuries may go to a Level I center such as Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak.
- **The record:** we collect records from the hospital and from your own physicians and therapists.
- **Lost wages:** we document them with your employer. If you earn more than the PIP wage cap, the difference is part of the claim against the at-fault driver.

Where the medical care itself caused the harm, the claim becomes a malpractice claim with different rules. See our [Novi medical malpractice page](/personal-injury/medical-malpractice/novi).

**The demand, the suit, and the resolution.** When your condition has stabilized, we send a demand with the complete record and a valuation. The decision to accept any offer is yours (MRPC 1.2). If the insurer will not pay fair value, suit is filed in the right court:

- **52nd District Court, First Division,** at 48150 Grand River Avenue in Novi, for claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims. It serves Novi and several neighboring communities in western Oakland County.
- **Sixth Judicial Circuit Court** (Oakland County Circuit Court) at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac, for injury suits over $25,000, with a jury drawn from all of Oakland County. Many injury cases there go through case evaluation (MCR 2.403) or facilitation before trial.
- **The Third Circuit Court in Detroit,** where the injury happened on the Wayne County side of Eight Mile (MCL 600.1629).
- **The Court of Claims,** for claims against MDOT and the State.
- **The U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan,** where an out-of-state defendant removes the case.

At settlement, we resolve the liens before you are paid: health plans, Medicare, Medicaid, and workers’ compensation. Minors’ settlements and wrongful death distributions are approved by the Oakland County Probate Court or by the trial court (MCR 2.420; MCL 600.2922(6)). Compensation for physical injury is generally not taxable income (26 USC 104(a)(2)).

## Who the defendant is in Novi

Novi draws people from across the region to its mall, stores, hotels, restaurants, and event center, and it has a warehouse and distribution area. Most of its injury defendants are private businesses, not the government. Each one changes who you claim against.

- **Stores and the mall.** A fall in a store, a food court, a parking lot, or a parking structure at a shopping center is a premises claim against the owner, the tenant, or both, depending on who controlled the area. Snow and ice in the lots are often handled by a contractor, which can be a separate defendant. Since *Kandil-Elsayed v F & E Oil* (2023), an “open and obvious” hazard such as visible ice goes to comparative fault rather than ending the claim. The deadline is three years. See our [slip and fall](/personal-injury/slip-and-fall) and [premises liability](/personal-injury/premises-liability) pages.
- **Event venues and hotels.** An injury at a trade show, a concert, or a convention can involve the venue owner, the event organizer, and the contractor that built the booth or set the stage. A hotel guest hurt by a fall, a pool, an elevator, or a broken fixture has a premises claim against the hotel. A guest or patron assaulted by a third party may have a negligent-security claim, which requires showing the operator knew of a specific risk and failed to respond reasonably.
- **Warehouses, docks, and job sites.** A worker hurt at a warehouse or distribution center usually has a workers’ compensation claim against the employer and no suit against it (MCL 418.131). A third party can still be sued: a trucking company whose driver pulled away from the dock, a forklift or conveyor manufacturer, a general contractor or subcontractor on a construction site, or the owner of the building. See our [workers’ compensation](/personal-injury/workers-compensation) and [product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability) pages.
- **Apartment complexes, condominiums, and rentals.** A landlord must keep the premises and common areas fit for their intended use and in reasonable repair (MCL 554.139). A condominium association is responsible for the common elements under its bylaws and the Condominium Act. See our [tenant injury page](/personal-injury/tenant-injuries).
- **Dog owners.** An owner is liable for a bite when the person bitten was lawfully present and did not provoke the dog (MCL 287.351). Report the bite to the Novi Police Department. See our [dog bite page](/personal-injury/dog-bites).
- **The roads are split three ways.** MDOT owns I-96, I-275, and M-5, with written notice of a road defect filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). The Road Commission for Oakland County owns Haggerty, Eight Mile, Napier, Pontiac Trail, and Fourteen Mile, and the City of Novi owns the other public roads, including Novi Road and Grand River, and the subdivision streets. A claim against the Road Commission or the City requires written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404), and the notice must go to the agency that has jurisdiction over that stretch of road.
- **Sidewalks and pathways.** Novi has many miles of sidewalks and pathways along its roads. The City must keep public sidewalks in reasonable repair (MCL 691.1402a). A fall on a broken public sidewalk is a claim against the City, with three limits: the City must have known, or should have known, of the defect at least 30 days before the injury; a defect of less than two inches is presumed not to be actionable; and written notice is due within 120 days. Whether a given pathway counts as a “sidewalk” under the statute, and whether a Novi ordinance shifts any duty to the adjacent owner, has to be checked. Paths in a park are treated differently. See our [sidewalk injury page](/personal-injury/sidewalk-injuries).
- **City buildings, parks, and schools.** An injury caused by a dangerous or defective condition in a public building falls under the public building exception, which requires that the agency knew of the defect and failed to fix it, and written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1406). That covers buildings belonging to the City of Novi and to the Novi, Northville, Walled Lake, and South Lyon school districts that serve parts of the city. An injury on park grounds outside a building generally falls outside every exception.
- **Police and fire are the City’s.** A crash with a Novi Police car or fire truck is a claim against the City, under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405). EMS treatment claims are limited to gross negligence or willful misconduct (MCL 333.20965).
- **Buses.** A crash with a public transit bus, or a school bus a district runs itself, is a claim under the motor vehicle exception. Walled Lake’s buses are run by a private contractor, so a claim over one of them is an ordinary negligence claim. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

## When the other driver has no insurance, or not enough

In a serious Novi crash, the at-fault driver’s liability limit may be less than the claim is worth. If you carry **underinsured motorist coverage**, your own insurer pays the difference up to your UIM limit. The sequence matters:

1. The at-fault driver’s insurer offers its limit.
2. You notify your own insurer and ask for its written consent to settle.
3. Only then do you accept the limit and sign the release.
4. The UIM claim then proceeds.

Accepting without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under most policies. We also check umbrella policies, which often carry their own notice terms.

The freeways and the mall bring drivers from across the state and beyond, and some carry only the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)) or nothing. Against an uninsured driver, the claim for pain and suffering depends on **uninsured motorist coverage**. A hit-and-run is usually a UM claim, with short notice terms in the policy. Two rules cut the other way:

- An owner who was required to insure a car and did not is generally barred from PIP benefits (MCL 500.3113(b)).
- A driver who was operating their own uninsured vehicle cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, whoever was at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(c)).

See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

## What a Novi claim is worth, and what reduces it

A claim includes two kinds of damages:

- **Economic damages:** medical expenses, lost wages, lost earning capacity, and replacement services.
- **Noneconomic damages:** pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, and disfigurement.

There is no cap except in medical malpractice. In an auto case, noneconomic damages require a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment does not have to be permanent (*McCormick v Carrier*, 2010). A premises, dog bite, or dram shop case has no such threshold.

Your share of fault reduces the recovery, and above 50 percent it bars noneconomic damages (MCL 600.2959; in auto cases, MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). At exactly 50 percent, you recover half. Where more than one party caused the injury, each defendant generally pays only its own share of fault (MCL 600.2956, 600.6304). That is why the store, its tenant, and the snow-removal contractor all have to be identified and named. Michigan does not award punitive damages in ordinary injury cases. Exemplary damages for humiliation or outrage are available only in narrow circumstances.

You cannot sue the at-fault party’s liability insurer directly in Michigan. The claim is against the driver, owner, or business, and its insurer defends and pays.

The practical limit is often insurance:

- **The at-fault driver’s limit** defaults to $250,000/$500,000 unless the driver chose lower limits, down to the $50,000/$100,000 minimum (MCL 500.3009(1)).
- **Your UIM and umbrella coverage** fill the gap.
- **In a premises, workplace, or nursing home case,** the commercial policy of the property owner, contractor, or operator pays.
- **In a dram shop case,** the liquor liability policy adds to it. For a worker with a third-party claim, the workers’ compensation insurer has a lien on the recovery (MCL 418.827). How that lien is negotiated changes what you take home. Medicare, Medicaid, and health plan liens are negotiated the same way.

## What it costs

Michigan caps contingency fees in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery (MCR 8.121). Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted from the recovery first; the one-third fee is calculated on the remaining amount. You pay nothing up front and nothing if there is no recovery.

The agreement is in writing. Read how costs are handled if the case is lost, because that is where firms differ: filing fees, records, experts, and depositions. A promise that “you pay nothing unless we win” should say whether that includes costs, and whether “win” means the same thing as “settle.” A success rate quoted without saying which cases it counts tells you little.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in Novi?** Three years for most claims. Several deadlines are much shorter: - one year to notify your no-fault insurer after a crash - 120 days to notify the City or the Road Commission of a defect, or a public agency of a building defect - 120 days for a road-defect notice on I-96, I-275, or M-5 - 120 days for a dram shop notice after you hire a lawyer, and two years to sue the bar or restaurant - a 182-day notice and a two-year limit for malpractice

**I slipped on ice in a parking lot at a Novi shopping center. Who is responsible?** The owner or the tenant that controlled the lot, and often the snow-removal contractor. Since 2023, the fact that the ice was visible does not by itself end the claim; it goes to comparative fault. Photograph the ice, the lot, and your shoes, report the fall, and ask for the incident report.

**I was hurt at an event or a hotel in Novi. Who do I claim against?** It depends on what caused the injury: the venue or hotel owner for the building, the event organizer or a contractor for a booth, stage, or temporary setup, and the operator for security. Each may have its own insurer.

**I was hurt at work at a Novi warehouse. Can I sue?** Usually not your employer, which owes workers’ compensation. You may have a separate suit against a third party, such as a trucking company, an equipment manufacturer, a contractor, or the building owner.

**I tripped on a broken sidewalk or pathway in Novi. Is that the City’s fault?** Possibly, under the two-inch rule, with written notice within 120 days. Whether the path counts as a sidewalk, and who maintains it, has to be checked first. Photograph the defect with something that shows its height.

**Can I sue the other driver’s insurance company?** No. In Michigan, the claim is against the driver, and the driver’s insurer defends and pays. Your own no-fault insurer is a separate claim, for your medical bills and lost wages.

**What does a Novi personal injury attorney charge?** Michigan caps the fee in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery, with no fee if there is no recovery. Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted only from a recovery.

**Where would my Novi case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000 go to the 52-1 District Court on Grand River in Novi. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court in Pontiac. Claims against MDOT go to the Court of Claims.

**If I was partly at fault, do I still have a claim?** Yes, unless you were more than 50 percent at fault, which bars pain-and-suffering damages. Below that, your recovery is reduced by your share, and at exactly 50 percent you recover half. No-fault benefits are paid regardless of fault.

**Do I need a lawyer whose office is in Novi?** No. What matters is that the lawyer knows the courts your case will be in, the agencies, insurers, and property owners involved, and will meet you where you are.
