## The rules a Southfield facility must follow

**Federal regulations** (42 CFR Part 483) set the standard of care for every Medicare- and Medicaid-certified facility. A resident has the right to be free from abuse, neglect, exploitation, and restraints used for discipline or convenience (483.12). The facility must prevent pressure sores unless clinically unavoidable and treat existing ones (483.25(b)); keep the environment free of accident hazards and provide the supervision and devices needed to prevent falls (483.25(d)); maintain nutrition and hydration (483.25(g)); manage medications without significant error (483.45); and have enough staff to meet residents’ needs (483.35). The care plan the facility must write and follow for each resident (483.21) is the measure of whether it did.

**Michigan’s Public Health Code** gives every resident a bill of rights (MCL 333.20201): adequate and appropriate care, freedom from mental and physical abuse and from restraints except on a physician’s order, and notice to a family member or representative of changes in condition. Michigan licenses and inspects facilities through the Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs (LARA). A facility must report abuse and neglect to the State and investigate (MCL 333.21771); health professionals and others must report suspected abuse of a vulnerable adult to Adult Protective Services (MCL 400.11a); and abuse of a vulnerable adult is a crime (MCL 750.145m to 750.145r).

**Assisted living, memory care, homes for the aged, and adult foster care.** Many Southfield seniors live in assisted living or memory care communities, homes for the aged, or adult foster care homes rather than a certified nursing home. Those settings are licensed by LARA under different rules (MCL 400.701 et seq., 333.21301 et seq.), or in some cases are not licensed as care facilities at all, and they are not subject to 42 CFR Part 483 unless they include a certified skilled nursing unit. A claim against one is measured against the licensing rules, the resident’s service agreement, and ordinary negligence law. The first question is which kind of place it is.

## What neglect looks like, and where the proof is

- **Pressure sores**, especially on the tailbone, hips, and heels; a stage 3 or 4 sore that developed in the facility is treated under federal rules as the facility’s failure unless the clinical record shows it was unavoidable (42 CFR 483.25(b)(1)).
- **Falls and fractures**, especially in a resident whose care plan called for a two-person assist, a bed alarm, or a low bed. A visitor’s fall is a premises case; see our [Southfield slip and fall page](/personal-injury/slip-and-fall/southfield).
- **Dehydration, malnutrition, and weight loss.**
- **Medication errors** and sedation for staff convenience.
- **Elopement** by a resident with dementia, including from memory care units.
- **Infections and sepsis** from untreated wounds, catheters, or aspiration; and [brain injuries](/personal-injury/brain-injury) from unwitnessed falls.
- **Physical, sexual, and financial abuse** by staff, other residents, or visitors.

The proof is in the facility’s records: the care plan, nursing notes, incident reports, wound-care logs, weight charts, medication administration records, staffing schedules, and its report to the State. ## Who can bring the claim when the resident cannot

A resident who is competent brings the claim in their own name. Many residents with dementia cannot. A durable power of attorney may authorize the agent to hire a lawyer and pursue a claim, depending on its terms; a **patient advocate** designation covers medical decisions, not lawsuits. Where no document gives that authority, a family member petitions the **Oakland County Probate Court** to be appointed guardian or conservator (MCL 700.5303, 700.5401), and the conservator brings the claim. Any settlement for a protected person is approved by the court. If the resident has died, the estate’s personal representative brings the claim (see below).

Where a resident’s dementia was severe enough at the time of the injury that they could not understand their rights, Michigan law may extend the deadline (MCL 600.5851(1), (2)). That extension is fact-specific and contested; a family should not rely on it.

## Negligence or malpractice: the question that sets the deadline

Michigan courts decide whether a nursing home claim is ordinary negligence or medical malpractice by asking whether the conduct involved professional medical judgment and whether a jury would need expert testimony to evaluate it (*Bryant v Oakpointe Villa Nursing Centre*, Mich 2004). A nurse’s wound-treatment decision or a physician’s medication order is malpractice, governed by the rules on our [Southfield medical malpractice page](/personal-injury/medical-malpractice/southfield). Leaving a resident who could not swallow alone with a tray, ignoring a care plan that said “two-person assist,” or failing to supervise a resident known to wander is ordinary negligence any juror can judge.

|  | Ordinary negligence | Medical malpractice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deadline | 3 years (MCL 600.5805(2)) | 2 years (600.5805(8)), with the 6-month discovery rule (600.5838a) |

| Expert affidavit | Not required to file | Affidavit of Merit with the complaint (600.2912d) |

Many nursing home cases include both. We plead both, meet the malpractice requirements for the parts that need them, and preserve the three-year claim for the parts that do not. A family that waits past two years may have lost the malpractice claims; past three, ordinarily everything.

**Wrongful death.** If the resident died, the claim is brought by the estate’s personal representative under MCL 600.2922, through the Oakland County Probate Court for a Southfield resident, and follows the deadline of the underlying claim. See our [Southfield wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death/southfield).

**Medicaid and Medicare.** If Medicaid or Medicare paid for treatment of the injury, it has a right to be repaid from the recovery, and that repayment is resolved before the money is distributed. Michigan’s Medicaid estate recovery program is a separate claim against the resident’s probate estate after death.

## The arbitration clause

Many admission packets include an agreement to arbitrate. A certified facility may not require it as a condition of admission, must explain it in a way the resident or representative understands, and must allow rescission within 30 days (42 CFR 483.70(n)). An agreement signed by a family member without legal authority to bind the resident, such as a power of attorney or guardianship, is generally not enforceable against the resident or the estate. Assisted living agreements are not covered by the federal rule and must be read on their own terms. An enforceable agreement changes the forum, not the claim.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who do I call today about neglect in a Southfield nursing home?** Adult Protective Services at 855-444-3911, LARA at 800-882-6006, and the Long-Term Care Ombudsman at 866-485-9393. Call 911 if the resident is in danger.

**My mother has dementia and cannot make decisions. Can we still bring a claim?** Yes, through someone with legal authority: an agent under a power of attorney that covers claims, or a conservator appointed by the Oakland County Probate Court. We help the family get that authority if it is missing.

**How long do we have?** Two years for claims involving medical judgment, three years for ordinary negligence. Many cases include both; call before two years have passed.

**We signed an arbitration agreement at admission. Can we still sue?** Possibly. At a certified facility it cannot have been a condition of admission, the signer must have had authority to bind the resident, and it can be rescinded within 30 days. Even if it is enforceable, it moves the claim rather than ending it.

**My father is in assisted living, not a nursing home. Do the same rules apply?** No. Assisted living, memory care, homes for the aged, and adult foster care homes are licensed under different Michigan rules, or sometimes not as care facilities at all. A claim against one is measured against those rules, the service agreement, and ordinary negligence law.

**How do I check a facility’s record?** CMS Care Compare shows each certified nursing home’s star ratings, staffing, inspection results, and penalties. LARA’s inspection reports cover licensed assisted living and adult foster care homes.
