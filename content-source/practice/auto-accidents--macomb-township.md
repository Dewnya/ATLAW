## Macomb Township, MI Car Accident Lawyer

A car crash in Macomb Township is a Michigan no-fault claim on these roads:

- **Hall Road (M-59)** runs along the township’s southern edge, past the retail on both sides.
- **Romeo Plank, Garfield, Hayes, Card, and North Avenue** carry traffic north and south between Hall Road and the subdivisions.
- **21 Mile through 26 Mile** cross the township east and west. Some are five lanes, and some are still two-lane roads with ditches and no shoulders.

In 2024, the Michigan State Police recorded 1,422 crashes in Macomb Township. 5 people were killed, 15 were seriously injured, and 476 were injured in all. 531 of the crashes were on Hall Road, and 63 involved deer.

A Macomb Township crash claim usually turns on four questions:

- Who had the right of way at the signal, the Michigan left, or the roundabout?
- Did the other driver have insurance, and how much, and how does your own uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage fit with it?
- If the road played a part, is it Macomb County’s or the State’s? The township owns none of its roads.
- If a Sheriff’s car, a school bus, or a construction truck was involved, who is the defendant?

## Where Macomb Township crashes happen

**Hall Road (M-59).** The county’s retail corridor runs along the township’s southern edge, with signals at Hayes, Garfield, Romeo Plank, and Card. Left turns are made by **Michigan left**: a driver goes past the intersection and makes a U-turn at a median crossover. The crashes are:

- crashes at the crossovers, where a driver making the U-turn pulls into traffic in the far lanes
- red-light and late-yellow crashes at the signals
- rear-end crashes where traffic stacks up for a crossover or a signal
- driveway crashes at the strips, where a driver leaving a lot must yield to traffic on the road (MCL 257.652)
- lane changes into the next lane on a road with three or four lanes each way

In the State Police’s 2025 intersection data, Hall Road at Hayes had 67 crashes, seventh in Macomb County. Hall Road belongs to MDOT.

**Romeo Plank, Garfield, Hayes, Card, and North Avenue.** The north-south arterials carry the subdivisions to Hall Road. Some are widened, and some are still two lanes. They run past schools, the township hall, and subdivision entrances, with signals and, at some mile roads, roundabouts. The crashes are:

- intersection crashes at the signals
- **roundabout crashes,** where a driver entering fails to yield to traffic already in the circle, or changes lanes inside a two-lane roundabout
- rear-end crashes in the school-hour stop-and-go
- left turns into subdivision entrances across oncoming traffic (MCL 257.650)

**The mile roads, 21 through 26 Mile.** The east-west grid carries subdivision traffic to the arterials. Some segments are five lanes, some are two lanes with ditches and no shoulders, and some in the northern tier may still be gravel. The crashes are:

- run-off-road crashes into ditches, trees, and culverts
- head-on crashes on the two-lane stretches
- crashes at unsignalized crossings where a driver pulls out from a stop sign
- deer crashes, especially at dawn and dusk in the fall

**Work zones.** The township has been growing for a generation, and road widenings, new subdivisions, and utility work keep work zones on the mile roads and arterials. The crashes are rear-end crashes at lane shifts and flaggers, and crashes caused by confusing or missing signs. A road contractor that set up unsafe traffic control can be a defendant alongside the driver.

**School zones and buses.** Several school districts serve the township, including Chippewa Valley, Utica, L’Anse Creuse, and New Haven. Drivers must stop for a school bus with flashing red lights, in both directions on most roads (MCL 257.682). Crashes with school buses, and children struck near stops, are covered on our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

## Deer crashes

The State Police recorded 63 deer-involved crashes in Macomb Township in 2024, more than in Sterling Heights (50), Shelby Township (61), or Clinton Township (30). A deer crash is usually a one-vehicle crash, and the claim works differently:

- **Your medical bills and lost wages** are paid by your own no-fault insurer, as in any crash. That includes a crash where you swerved to miss a deer and hit a tree or went into a ditch.
- **Your car** is covered by your comprehensive coverage if you hit the deer. A crash into a tree or ditch after swerving is usually a collision claim instead.
- **There is usually no one to sue** for pain and suffering, because the deer is no one’s fault.
- **There may be a claim against another driver.** That includes a driver who hit you after you stopped for a deer, or who forced you off the road.
- **There may be a claim against the county** if a road defect, not the deer, caused the crash.

## Whose road it is: Macomb County or the State

Michigan townships do not own public roads. In Macomb Township:

- **MDOT** owns Hall Road (M-59). A road-defect claim against MDOT goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **Macomb County** (the Department of Roads) owns Romeo Plank, Garfield, Hayes, Card, North Avenue, the mile roads, and the public subdivision streets. A claim against the county requires written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404).
- **Private roads.** Some subdivision and condominium roads are private, owned and maintained by the association. A defect claim there is a premises claim against the association, not a highway claim.

A pothole or other defect in the improved portion of the road can make the agency a defendant alongside the other driver; signals, signs, sight obstructions, and shoulders generally fall outside the highway exception for State and county roads (Nawrocki v Macomb County Road Commission, Mich 2000; MCL 691.1402(1)). A notice sent to Macomb Township is sent to the wrong defendant.

## Fault is the case

In a rear-end crash, fault is rarely disputed. At a Michigan left, a roundabout, a subdivision entrance, or a two-lane mile road, it often is. The other driver’s insurer may try to assign you half or more of the fault, and that share matters (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)):

- **More than 50 percent at fault:** you lose your claim for pain and suffering.
- **50 percent or less:** your recovery is reduced by your share. At exactly 50 percent, you recover half.

The evidence that decides fault is perishable:

- the crash report from the Macomb County Sheriff’s Office, which provides the township’s police services, or from the State Police
- signal timing data from MDOT or the county
- cameras at the Hall Road stores and gas stations, and doorbell cameras at subdivision entrances, many overwritten within days
- the vehicles’ **event data recorders**, which store speed, braking, and throttle in the seconds before impact
- the other driver’s phone records, for a distracted-driving claim (see our [distracted driving page](/personal-injury/distracted-driving-accidents))
- witnesses, including other drivers

## Who pays after a Macomb Township crash

**Your no-fault (PIP) claim.** Your own auto insurer pays these benefits regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107):

- your medical expenses, up to the PIP level you chose
- 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- replacement services at $20 a day
- attendant care

**The PIP level is not the same as your liability limits.** Since July 2, 2020, Michigan drivers choose a PIP medical level for each policy: unlimited, $500,000, $250,000, $50,000 for some Medicaid enrollees, or an opt-out for some Medicare enrollees (MCL 500.3107c). That choice is separate from the $250,000/$500,000 liability limits that protect the other driver. The level on your declarations page decides how much of your medical care no-fault pays.

If you have no policy, a resident relative’s policy pays (MCL 500.3114). If none exists, an occupant claims from the insurer of the vehicle they were in (MCL 500.3114(4)), and then the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3172), capped at $250,000 for medical benefits. A pedestrian or cyclist hit by a car, with no household policy, claims through the Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3115).

The insurer must receive written notice within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). It must pay each bill within 30 days of reasonable proof, or owe 12 percent interest (MCL 500.3142). Providers’ charges are subject to the fee schedule in MCL 500.3157. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance) and our [Michigan no-fault guide](/resources/michigan-no-fault-guide).

**If you had no insurance.** A person who owned a car and was required to insure it, but did not, is generally barred from PIP benefits for their own injuries (MCL 500.3113(b)). A driver who was operating their own uninsured vehicle cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, whoever was at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(c)). A passenger or pedestrian who did not own an uninsured car may still be covered through a household policy or the Assigned Claims Plan.

**Your claim against the at-fault driver.** For pain and suffering, you need a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment is an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life. It does not have to be permanent (MCL 500.3135(5); *McCormick v Carrier*, 2010).

You can also recover economic losses that no-fault does not cover (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)):

- lost wages beyond the three years or above the monthly cap
- medical bills above your chosen PIP level

The suit must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The mini tort claim recovers up to $3,000 in vehicle damage not covered by your policy (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)). If the at-fault driver’s insurer will not pay it, the claim is filed in the 41A District Court.

**When the other driver has no insurance, or not enough.** Some drivers carry only the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)), and some carry nothing. Hall Road traffic comes from across the region.

- **Against an uninsured driver:** your pain-and-suffering claim is worth what that driver can personally pay, unless you carry **uninsured motorist coverage**. A hit-and-run is usually a UM claim, with short notice terms in the policy.
- **Against an underinsured driver:** your own **underinsured motorist coverage** pays the difference up to your UIM limit, if you follow the steps in order:
 1. The at-fault driver’s insurer offers its limit.
 2. You notify your own insurer and ask for its written consent to settle.
 3. Only then do you accept the limit and sign the release.
 4. The UIM claim then proceeds.

Accepting without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under most policies. We read every policy in the household before valuing the claim. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Drunk drivers and the dram shop claim.** A bar, restaurant, or store that serves a visibly intoxicated person, or a minor, is liable for the injuries that person causes (MCL 436.1801). The State Police recorded 33 alcohol-involved crashes in Macomb Township in 2024. Written notice must go to the business within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, the drunk driver must be named and kept in the suit, and the suit must be filed within two years. See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**Sheriff’s cars, fire trucks, county trucks, and buses.** All of these fall under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405):

- **Macomb County Sheriff’s patrol cars:** a claim against the county, which provides the township’s police under contract
- **Macomb Township Fire Department vehicles:** a claim against the township
- **Macomb County Department of Roads trucks:** a claim against the county
- **school buses:** a claim against the district that operates them
- SMART buses: a claim against SMART

Dash camera and dispatch records are overwritten on short schedules, so we request them by FOIA promptly. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

## What to do after a Macomb Township crash

1. **Call 911 and get the police report.** Michigan requires a report for any crash with injury or $1,000 in damage (MCL 257.622). Crashes in the township are worked by the Macomb County Sheriff’s Office.
2. **Photograph the scene.** Get the vehicles, plates, the other driver’s license and insurance card, the intersection, crossover, roundabout, or driveway, the signals, signs, and lane markings, and any work-zone signs. Note every store and house with a camera facing the scene. Footage is kept for days, not weeks.
3. **After a deer crash,** photograph the car, the deer if it is safe, the road, and any skid marks, and report the crash. Your insurer may require a police report for a comprehensive claim.
4. **Get medical care the same day.** Henry Ford Macomb Hospital in Clinton Township, just south of Hall Road, and McLaren Macomb in Mount Clemens are both Level II trauma centers.
5. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** promptly. If you have no policy, a lawyer can identify the insurer next in priority or the Assigned Claims Plan.
6. **Read your declarations page** for your PIP medical level, UM/UIM, collision, and comprehensive coverage.
7. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the other driver’s insurer, sign a release, or accept a policy-limits offer before your UIM insurer consents.
8. **Call a lawyer within days** if fault is disputed, the other driver is uninsured, or the road or a work zone played a part. The camera footage is the first evidence lost, and the 120-day notice to the county is already running.

## The courts

- **Claims up to $25,000,** including mini tort claims, go to the 41A District Court, which serves Macomb Township from its Shelby Township courthouse at 51660 Van Dyke.
- **Injury suits over $25,000** go to the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court (Macomb County Circuit Court) at 40 North Main Street in Mount Clemens, with a jury drawn from all of Macomb County.
- **Defect claims against MDOT** go to the Court of Claims.
- **A fatal crash** is brought by the estate, opened through the Macomb County Probate Court. See our [wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death).

## Frequently asked questions

**I was hit making a Michigan left on Hall Road. Who is at fault?** It depends on who had the right of way. A driver making the U-turn at a crossover must yield to traffic in the lanes being entered. A driver in those lanes who was speeding or ran a signal can carry fault too. Store cameras along Hall Road can settle it, but footage is kept only days.

**I was hit in a roundabout. Who is at fault?** Usually the driver entering the roundabout, who must yield to traffic already in the circle. In a two-lane roundabout, a driver who changes lanes or exits from the wrong lane can be at fault.

**I hit a deer on 24 Mile. Who pays?** Your own no-fault insurer pays your medical bills and lost wages. Your comprehensive coverage pays for the car. There is usually no one to sue for pain and suffering, unless another driver or a road defect caused the crash.

**The driver who hit me had no insurance. What now?** Your own no-fault insurer, a household member’s insurer, or the Assigned Claims Plan pays your medical bills and lost wages regardless of fault. Your pain-and-suffering claim depends on whether you carry uninsured motorist coverage.

**A pothole caused my crash. Do I file against Macomb Township?** No. Townships do not own public roads. - **Hall Road** belongs to MDOT, with notice filed in the Court of Claims within 120 days. - **Every other public road in the township** belongs to Macomb County, with written notice within 120 days. A notice sent to the township is sent to the wrong defendant. If a road contractor’s work zone contributed, the contractor can be sued as well.

**A Sheriff’s patrol car hit me. Who do I claim against?** Macomb County, which provides the township’s police under contract, under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity. The car’s video and dispatch records are obtained by FOIA and are overwritten on short schedules.

**How many crashes are there in Macomb Township?** The State Police recorded 1,422 crashes in Macomb Township in 2024. 5 people were killed, 15 were seriously injured, and 476 were injured in all. 531 of the crashes were on Hall Road, and 63 involved deer.

**Where would my Macomb Township case be filed?** Mini tort claims and claims up to $25,000 go to the 41A District Court. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court in Mount Clemens.

**How long do I have?** - One year to give your no-fault insurer written notice. - Three years to sue the at-fault driver. - 120 days for a notice to Macomb County. - 120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT. - Two years to sue a bar, with a 120-day notice.
