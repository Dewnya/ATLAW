## Dearborn Personal Injury Lawyer

If you were hurt in Dearborn because someone else was careless, you have a claim under Michigan law, and the claim has deadlines that start running the day you are hurt: one year to notify your no-fault insurer after a crash, 120 days to notify the City of Dearborn or Wayne County of a road or sidewalk defect, 120 days to notify the State for a defect on Michigan Avenue or Ford Road, 182 days’ notice before a malpractice suit, and three years for most everything else. ## What a Dearborn personal injury lawyer does

**The first week.** We calendar every deadline that applies and send preservation letters: to the at-fault driver’s insurer, to the trucking company for its logs and electronic data, to the City or SMART for vehicle video, to the store or landlord for footage and incident reports, to a bar for its receipts if a drunk driver was involved. We obtain the Dearborn Police report (the UD-10, purchased through CLEMIS), the Dearborn Fire Department EMS run sheet, and the witnesses’ names, and we photograph the scene while it still looks the way it did.

**The first month.** We identify every policy that may pay: your own auto policy for no-fault benefits, household policies, the at-fault party’s liability coverage, your uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage, the property owner’s policy, umbrella policies, and, where no auto policy exists, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3172). We file your Application for No-Fault Benefits inside the one-year window (MCL 500.3145(1)) and handle the insurer’s requests for statements, records, and examinations (MCL 500.3151). You stop talking to adjusters; we do that, in English or Arabic.

**The treatment period.** The value of the claim is set mostly by the medical record, so we make sure it is complete and the bills are paid while you treat, collecting records from Corewell Health Dearborn, Henry Ford Medical Center Fairlane, the Detroit hospitals, and your own physicians and therapists, and documenting lost wages with your employer, whether that is Ford, the Rouge, a Michigan Avenue business, or a hospital. **[CONFIRM: hospital list]**

**The demand, the suit, and the resolution.** When your condition has stabilized, we send a demand with the complete record and a valuation; the decision to accept any offer is yours (MRPC 1.2). If the insurer will not pay fair value, suit is filed: claims up to $25,000 in the 19th District Court at 16077 Michigan Avenue, a short drive from the office; injury suits over $25,000 in the Third Judicial Circuit Court in Detroit, which may send injury cases to case evaluation (MCR 2.403) or facilitation before trial. At settlement we resolve the liens (Medicaid, Medicare, health plan) before you are paid; minors’ settlements and wrongful death distributions are approved by the court (MCR 2.420; MCL 600.2922(6)). Compensation for physical injury is not taxable income (26 USC 104(a)(2)). **[ATTORNEY CHECK]**

## What a Dearborn claim is worth, and what reduces it

Economic damages (medical expenses, lost wages, lost earning capacity, replacement services) and noneconomic damages (pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, disfigurement), with no cap except in medical malpractice. Your own share of fault reduces the recovery and, above 50 percent, bars noneconomic damages (MCL 600.2959). In most Dearborn cases the practical limit is insurance: the at-fault driver’s policy, which on Michigan Avenue and Warren is more often the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)) than on the west side, and your own uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage, which we check in every household policy before valuing a claim.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in Dearborn?** Three years for most claims, but one year to notify your no-fault insurer after a crash, 120 days to notify the City or County of a sidewalk or road claim, 120 days for a defect on a state road, and a 182-day notice before any malpractice suit.

**What does a Dearborn personal injury lawyer charge?** One-third of the recovery, the Michigan cap for injury cases, with no fee if there is no recovery. Ask how costs are handled. **[CONFIRM]**

**Where would my case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000 in the 19th District Court on Michigan Avenue; injury suits over $25,000 in the Third Judicial Circuit Court in Detroit.

**Do you speak Arabic?** Yes, your consultation can be in Arabic. Consultations can also be accommodated in English, French, Mandarin, Urdu, and Spanish.

**Do I have to go to court?** Most Dearborn injury cases settle, many after suit is filed and before trial. Filing is usually part of getting a fair offer.

**How long will my case take?** A no-fault benefits claim can resolve in months; a disputed injury claim filed in the Third Circuit typically takes a year to two from filing.

**The insurer already offered me money. Should I take it?** Not before you know what the claim is worth and what the release gives up. An early offer is made because the insurer expects the claim to be worth more once the record is complete.

**I was hurt in Dearborn but live in Detroit or Dearborn Heights. Does that matter?** Venue depends on where the defendant lives or does business and where the injury happened (MCL 600.1629); where you live matters only as a fallback. The deadlines are the same.
