## Dearborn Slip and Fall Lawyer

For twenty years a fall on someone else’s property in Michigan was close to unwinnable, because a hazard that was “open and obvious” ended the case before it began. That rule is gone. Since the Michigan Supreme Court’s July 2023 decision in *Kandil-Elsayed v F & E Oil*, a Dearborn fall case turns on who had the duty to keep the place safe, what they knew about the hazard and when, what they did about it, and how much of the fall was your fault. ## Where Dearborn residents fall, and who is responsible

**The east-side business district.** Grocers, bakeries, restaurants, pharmacies, and shops along Michigan Avenue between Wyoming and Schaefer and along Warren: spills, recently mopped floors, ice at entrances, uneven thresholds, and potholes in small lots. You are an **invitee**, owed the highest duty: a property owner or possessor must exercise reasonable care under the circumstances, which may include inspecting for hazards, correcting dangerous conditions, or providing appropriate warnings. The case is decided on **notice**, actual (an employee saw or caused it) or constructive (it existed long enough that a reasonable inspection would have found it), and the footage, cleaning logs, and incident reports that prove it are held by the owner.

**Fairlane, Westborn, and the west side.** Fairlane Town Center, the Ford Road and Michigan Avenue retail corridors, and downtown West Dearborn: large-format stores and parking structures with extensive surveillance that is overwritten on a schedule, and management companies and tenants who each point to the other. The lease decides who maintained the floor, the lot, and the entrance; we request it with the footage.

**Rental housing.** Multi-family buildings along Michigan Avenue and Warren and the older east-side housing stock: broken porch steps, missing handrails, unlit stairwells, and ice on the only path from the lot to the door. Landlords must keep the premises and common areas fit for their intended use and in reasonable repair (MCL 554.139). **City sidewalks.** Dearborn’s ordinance places sidewalk maintenance and snow clearance on the adjacent property owner, but that duty alone does not make the owner liable to a pedestrian; an owner who made the sidewalk more dangerous can be (Dearborn Code, Secs. 13-71 to 13-74). ## The law after *Kandil-Elsayed*

Under the old rule (*Lugo v Ameritech*, 2001), an owner owed no duty to protect visitors from a danger an average person would have noticed, and insurers denied many fall claims on that basis. In *Kandil-Elsayed v F & E Oil* (Michigan Supreme Court, July 28, 2023), the Court held that whether a hazard was open and obvious no longer decides whether a duty existed; it is now a factor in whether the owner breached the duty of reasonable care and how much of the fault belongs to the person who fell, both jury questions. If the jury assigns you some fault, your recovery is reduced by that share; above half, you recover no pain-and-suffering damages (MCL 600.2959). A Dearborn store that left a spill on the floor for an hour cannot win by saying the spill was visible; the questions are what it knew, what it did, and whether you had a realistic alternative route.

## Snow and ice

An owner must take reasonable steps, within a reasonable time after snow or ice accumulates, to make walkways safe; what is reasonable depends on the property, and a store open at 9 a.m. after an overnight storm is expected to have salted its entrance. Black ice from refreezing meltwater, a leaking downspout, or a clogged drain can be a condition the owner created, and those cases are stronger than natural accumulation. Dearborn’s ordinance requires owners to clear sidewalks within 24 hours after a snowfall of three inches or more ends.

## What to do after a fall in Dearborn

1. **Get medical care the same day** and tell the provider where and how you fell. Corewell Health Dearborn Hospital is where many of the city’s fall injuries are treated. **[CONFIRM: hospital list]**
2. **Photograph the hazard immediately**, from several angles, with something for scale, before it is mopped, salted, or patched. Photograph your shoes.
3. **Report the fall** to the manager, the landlord, or the City, and get the incident report number. Do not give a recorded statement.
4. **Get witness names**, including employees, and note the time; it matters for constructive notice.
5. **Measure a sidewalk defect** with a ruler in the photograph.
6. **Keep the shoes** unworn and unwashed.
7. **Call a lawyer within days.** Footage is overwritten in 7 to 30 days; if the City, the Housing Commission, Henry Ford College, UM-Dearborn, or the State may be involved, the notice clock is already running.

## Frequently asked questions

**I fell on ice outside a store on Warren Avenue. The ice was obvious. Do I have a case?** Possibly. Since the 2023 decision, an obvious hazard no longer ends the case. The questions are whether the store should have salted or cleared it, whether you had a realistic alternative route, and how much of the fault is yours.

**I fell on a broken sidewalk in Dearborn. Who do I sue?** It depends on the defect and the location. The City is liable under the highway exception only for defects it knew or should have known about, with a presumption that defects under two inches are reasonable repair, and only if you serve written notice within 120 days. Dearborn’s ordinance also places sidewalk maintenance on the adjacent owner. We identify the right defendant before the notice runs.

**I fell at UM-Dearborn. Is that a claim against the City?** No. UM-Dearborn is a State institution, so the claim goes to the Court of Claims with 120 days' notice. A fall at Henry Ford College next door is a claim against the college district with 120 days’ notice.

**I fell on the stairs at my apartment. Can I sue my landlord?** Michigan landlords must keep common areas fit for their intended use and the premises in reasonable repair. A broken step, a missing handrail, or an unlit stairwell the landlord knew about is a claim.

**How long do I have?** Three years to sue a private owner; 120 days for written notice to the City, the Housing Commission, or Henry Ford College; six months for UM-Dearborn or the State. The footage disappears far sooner.
