## Novi, MI Medical Malpractice Lawyer

A medical malpractice claim in Michigan has rules no other injury claim has:

- a two-year deadline instead of three
- a written Notice of Intent, served on each provider at least 182 days before suit
- a sworn Affidavit of Merit from a qualified expert, filed with the complaint
- strict rules on who that expert can be
- a cap on pain-and-suffering damages

A case that misses any one of them can be lost before its merits are heard.

For a Novi patient, the first question is who the defendant is. The city’s hospital is **Henry Ford Providence Novi Hospital** at 47601 Grand River Avenue, near I-96 and Beck Road. Henry Ford Health describes it as a Level II Adult Trauma Center with 24-hour emergency care, and it provides obstetric and newborn care, cancer care, and inpatient rehabilitation. Many patients know it by an earlier name: Providence Park Hospital, St. John Providence Park, or Ascension Providence Novi.

The most serious injuries and illnesses may be transferred to a Level I center, such as Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak or Henry Ford Hospital in Detroit.

Day-to-day care happens in other places too:

- the medical office buildings on the hospital campus and along Grand River, Twelve Mile, and Haggerty
- urgent care clinics and outpatient surgery and imaging centers

- nursing homes, assisted living, and memory care communities

Health-system-employed physicians practice alongside independent practices and separate physician groups, and more than one health system has offices in the city. Each provider has its own legal entity, insurer, and notice address. A federally funded clinic is sued under federal law, on a different clock and in a different court.

## The rules, by the numbers

| Rule | Figure | Statute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deadline to file suit | 2 years from the act or omission | MCL 600.5805(8) |
| Discovery rule | 6 months from when the claim was or should have been discovered; never more than 6 years after the act (exceptions for retained objects, fraud, reproductive injury) | MCL 600.5838a(2) |
| Notice of Intent before suit | 182 days; the provider must respond in writing within 154 days | MCL 600.2912b |
| Affidavit of Merit | Filed with the complaint | MCL 600.2912d |
| Defendant’s Affidavit of Meritorious Defense | Within 91 days after the answer | MCL 600.2912e |
| Expert qualification | Same specialty; same board certification if the defendant is certified; majority of professional time in that specialty in the year before | MCL 600.2169 |
| 2026 cap on noneconomic damages | $596,400 (standard) / $1,065,000 (catastrophic) | MCL 600.1483 |
| Child injured before age 8 | Deadline runs to the 10th birthday | MCL 600.5851(7) |
| Federally funded provider | Administrative claim (SF-95) within 2 years; suit after denial or 6 months | 28 USC 2401(b), 2675 |

Economic damages are not capped. They include medical bills, lost earnings, and the cost of future care. Michigan does not award punitive damages in malpractice cases. The Affidavit of Merit is required by MCL 600.2912d, and MCL 600.2169 decides who is qualified to sign it.

**The two-year period and the discovery rule work together.** The two years run from the act or omission, not from when you learned of it. The six-month discovery rule helps only when the claim was not, and should not have been, discovered within those two years. It never extends a claim past six years from the act, except in the cases the statute lists. Waiting to “see how it turns out” can cost the claim.

## What malpractice is, and is not

A bad outcome is not malpractice. Malpractice is a bad outcome caused by a provider who failed to do what a reasonably competent provider in the same specialty would have done in the same circumstances (MCL 600.2912a). You must prove four things:

- the standard of care
- a breach of it
- that the breach more likely than not caused the injury
- damages

Michigan requires proof that the patient would have had a better outcome. A lost chance of a better outcome is compensable only where that chance was greater than 50 percent (MCL 600.2912a(2)). Before anything is filed, a qualified physician’s written review of the records decides whether a case exists. Many do not, and we say so.

## Novi providers, and who answers for each

**Henry Ford Providence Novi Hospital.** This is the city’s hospital, with its employed physicians, nurses, and staff. Its name and ownership have changed several times, most recently in 2024, when Ascension’s southeast Michigan hospitals came under Henry Ford Health. The correct legal entity for the Notice of Intent must be identified two ways:

- from current corporate filings
- from the name the hospital used at the time of care, not from today’s sign or letterhead

Under the Ascension name, the Novi hospital was a campus of a hospital that operated in more than one location. Care given before 2024 may involve an entity whose name includes neither “Novi” nor “Henry Ford,” and care before and after the transaction may involve different entities. A Notice of Intent served on the wrong entity can leave the right one unnoticed when the two years run out. Claims against the hospital and its employed providers are filed in the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court (Oakland County Circuit Court) in Pontiac.

**Emergency physicians and other contracted groups.** Many hospitals staff their emergency departments, anesthesia, radiology, and hospitalist services through separate physician groups. The Notice of Intent must name the group as well as the hospital. Where a patient came to the hospital for care and reasonably believed the doctor worked for it, Michigan law can hold the hospital responsible under ostensible agency (*Grewe v Mt Clemens General Hospital*, 1978).

**Transfers to a Level I center.** As a Level II center, the Novi hospital treats many trauma patients itself, but some major trauma, stroke, cardiac, neonatal, and complex surgical patients are transferred. A transfer case can involve two hospitals, two sets of physicians, and the transport between them. The question is which delay or decision caused the harm:

- the first hospital’s diagnosis
- the decision and timing of the transfer
- the transport
- the care after arrival

A transfer within the same health system still involves separate providers and may involve separate entities. A transfer to a hospital in Wayne County may raise a question of where to file (MCL 600.1629).

**Births and newborn care.** The Novi hospital delivers babies and cares for sick newborns. A birth injury case can involve the obstetrician’s practice, the hospital’s nurses, the anesthesiologist’s group, and a neonatal team, each a separate defendant. A child injured before age eight has until the tenth birthday to file, but the parents’ own claims, including for the child’s medical expenses before age 18, follow the ordinary two-year period.

**Medical office buildings, urgent care, surgery centers, and imaging centers.** This includes the primary-care and specialty practices on the hospital campus and along Grand River, Twelve Mile, and Haggerty; urgent care clinics; and outpatient surgery, dialysis, and imaging centers. Frequent claims are:

- a delayed diagnosis in an office practice
- a missed fracture or infection at urgent care
- a surgical complication at an outpatient center

The employer of each provider who touched the chart must be identified. A physician employed by one health system and an independent physician in the same building are different defendants with different insurers. Michigan rules apply to all of them.

**Federally funded community health centers.** Some Oakland County residents get primary, prenatal, and dental care at community health centers that receive federal Health Center Program funding. Providers at a funded center can be deemed federal employees for malpractice purposes. A claim against one follows the Federal Tort Claims Act, not Michigan procedure:

- an administrative claim on Standard Form 95 to the Department of Health and Human Services, within two years (28 USC 2401(b))
- no Notice of Intent
- suit in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan, tried to a judge without a jury

Identifying a deemed clinic early is the difference between a timely claim and none.

**Veterans.** Veterans treated at the John D. Dingell VA Medical Center in Detroit or a VA outpatient clinic have an FTCA claim on the same path. They may also qualify for disability compensation under 38 USC 1151 where the injury was caused by VA carelessness, negligence, or error, or by an event that was not reasonably foreseeable.

**EMS.** Novi’s emergency medical response is provided by the Novi Fire Department, with Advanced Life Support ambulance transport by a private contractor, Medstar. Claims against EMS personnel, and against the agency that employs them, public or private, including a private ambulance company handling a transfer between hospitals, are limited by the Emergency Medical Services Act to gross negligence or willful misconduct (MCL 333.20965(1)).

**Nursing homes, assisted living, and senior residences.** Novi has five Medicare-certified nursing homes, as well as rehabilitation centers, assisted living and memory care communities, and senior apartments. Their claims are sometimes malpractice and sometimes ordinary negligence. Which one it is decides whether the Notice of Intent, the two-year period, and the cap apply.

- A nurse’s wound-care decision is malpractice.
- Leaving a resident unsupervised against the care plan is usually negligence.

See our [Novi nursing home abuse page](/personal-injury/nursing-home-abuse).

**After hospital care: rehabilitation.** Many patients go from the hospital to an inpatient rehabilitation unit or a short-term rehabilitation center. A fall, a medication error, or a missed infection there is judged by the rehabilitation provider’s standard of care, and that provider may be a different entity from the hospital.

**Workplace clinics.** Occupational clinics and employer medical departments that treat injured workers are a separate case. A claim against a provider employed by your own employer may be barred by workers’ compensation exclusivity. An outside clinic or physician can be sued for malpractice. See our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).

## The Novi malpractice cases we see

- **Emergency department errors:** missed stroke, heart attack, sepsis, pulmonary embolism, and bleeds; discharge without a needed test; triage delays; and a delayed transfer to a Level I center.
- **Urgent care and clinic errors:** chest pain, abdominal pain, or a head injury sent home as minor; missed fractures, infections, and high-risk pregnancies.
- **Birth and newborn injuries:** delayed response to fetal distress, mismanaged shoulder dystocia, a delayed cesarean, errors in newborn care, and the [cerebral palsy](/personal-injury/cerebral-palsy), [brachial plexus](/personal-injury/erbs-palsy), and [hypoxic](/personal-injury/birth-asphyxia) injuries that follow.
- **Surgical and anesthesia errors,** including at outpatient surgery centers and in robotic-assisted procedures: wrong site, retained objects (which extend the limitations period), perforations, and airway and dosing failures causing [brain injury](/personal-injury/brain-injury).
- **Medication errors** in hospitals, pharmacies, and nursing homes.
- **Cancer misdiagnosis and delayed diagnosis** in office practices and imaging centers, where the “better outcome” causation standard is argued hardest.
- **Falls, pressure injuries, and missed decline** in older patients, in the hospital, in rehabilitation, and in nursing and assisted living settings.
- **Death from malpractice,** brought by the estate with the malpractice rules attached. See our [wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death).

**Injured in a crash, then harmed by the care.** Some malpractice cases here begin as crash cases. A person hurt on I-96 or Grand River is taken to the hospital, and a missed injury or a surgical error makes the outcome worse. That creates two claims with different rules: the crash claim against the driver, with a three-year deadline and no-fault benefits, and the malpractice claim against the provider, with a two-year deadline and the Notice of Intent. No-fault PIP benefits keep paying the medical bills either way. See our [Novi car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents/novi).

## How a Novi malpractice case proceeds

1. **The records,** including the electronic audit trail, requested from every provider promptly, under whatever name the provider used at the time of care. In a transfer case, that means both hospitals and the ambulance service.
2. **Expert review** by a physician in the defendant’s specialty, in writing, before anything is filed.
3. **Identify the defendants and the forum.** A hospital entity, physician group, surgery center, urgent care operator, or independent practice goes to the Sixth Circuit for Oakland County care. A hospital or provider in another county may raise a venue question. A deemed clinic or the VA follows the SF-95 route to federal court. EMS is subject to the EMS Act’s gross-negligence standard. Serving a Notice of Intent on a federal provider while the federal two-year clock runs can lose the case.
4. **The Notice of Intent** (MCL 600.2912b), served at least 182 days before suit, on every provider and entity, at the right legal name. A timely notice pauses the two-year period during the notice period (MCL 600.5856(c)). If the provider does not respond within 154 days, suit may be filed then.
5. **The complaint and Affidavit of Merit** (MCL 600.2912d). The defense then files its own affidavit (MCL 600.2912e).
6. **Discovery, case evaluation, and facilitation.** Cases that do not settle go to trial before a jury in the Sixth Circuit at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac. Two to four years is typical.
7. **Resolution.** Minors’ and estates’ settlements are approved by the court or the Oakland County Probate Court. Medicare, Medicaid, ERISA plan, health plan, and no-fault insurer liens are resolved before distribution. A large verdict is reduced to the cap on noneconomic damages unless the injury falls in a catastrophic category.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to sue a doctor or hospital in Novi?** Two years from the malpractice, with a six-month discovery rule capped at six years. The Notice of Intent must be served 182 days before suit, and the expert review comes first, so call well inside the first year.

**Does the two years start when I found out something went wrong?** No. It starts from the act or omission. Discovery matters only if you did not, and could not reasonably, discover the claim within the two years; then you have six months from discovery, but never more than six years from the act, except in the cases the statute lists.

**Is the Notice of Intent “six months” or 182 days?** 182 days, which is not the same as six calendar months. A notice served on the last day of a six-month count can be late. Count the days.

**My care was at “Providence Park” or “Ascension Providence Novi.” Does the name change matter?** It can. The Notice of Intent and the complaint must name the legal entity responsible for your care at the time it was given. Under the Ascension name, the Novi hospital was one campus of a multi-campus hospital, and after the 2024 change the entity may be different again. We identify it from the records and the corporate filings.

**I was treated at the Novi hospital and transferred to another hospital. Which hospital is responsible?** It depends on where the decision or delay that caused the harm happened: - the first hospital’s diagnosis and decision to transfer - the transport - the care after arrival. Each provider is a separate defendant with its own Notice of Intent.

**My doctor’s office is in a medical building on the hospital campus. Is the hospital responsible?** Not necessarily. A physician practice in a campus building may be employed by the hospital’s health system, by another health system, or by no one but itself. The employer is identified from the records and the practice’s legal filings before the notice goes out.

**I was hurt in a crash and then something went wrong at the hospital. Is that one case or two?** Usually two: a crash claim against the driver and a malpractice claim against the provider, with different deadlines and rules. Your no-fault insurer keeps paying the medical bills while both are pending.

**Is there a cap on what I can recover?** On pain-and-suffering damages only. In 2026, the cap is $596,400 in the standard case and $1,065,000 for the catastrophic permanent injuries the statute lists, adjusted each year. Medical bills, lost earnings, and future care are not capped.

**My child was injured at birth. How long do we have?** A child injured before age eight has until the tenth birthday to file. The parents’ own claims are subject to the ordinary two-year period, so the family should not wait.
