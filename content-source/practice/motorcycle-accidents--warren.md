## Warren Motorcycle Accident Lawyer

## Where Warren motorcycle crashes happen

**The left turn on Van Dyke, Mound, and the mile roads.** A driver turning left across four lanes of oncoming traffic, misjudging the speed of a motorcycle or not seeing it at all, is the recurring account in rider cases. Under Michigan law a driver must yield to oncoming traffic before turning left (MCL 257.650). The 11 Mile, 12 Mile, and 13 Mile intersections on Van Dyke, and the plant driveways on Mound, produce the same crash at different speeds.

**Groesbeck.** The diagonal across the east side meets the mile roads at sharp angles, so drivers looking for cross traffic look in the wrong direction and riders are hidden by the angle.

**Plant traffic.** Tractor-trailers turning wide into the gates on Mound and Van Dyke, and trucks stopped in travel lanes, are hazards a car can absorb and a motorcycle cannot. See our [Warren truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/warren).

**I-696 and Eight Mile.** Merging traffic at five interchanges in about six miles, and the Detroit-line traffic on Eight Mile, where the other driver is more likely to carry the $50,000 minimum or nothing. **[CONFIRM: current construction zones in Warren]**

**The road itself.** A single-vehicle crash caused by a pothole, a broken concrete joint, a pavement drop-off, or gravel is a claim against the agency with jurisdiction under the highway exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1402). In Warren that means three possible defendants:

- MDOT for Van Dyke, Groesbeck, Eight Mile, and I-696: Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404)
- **The Macomb County Department of Roads for Mound and many of the mile roads**: written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **The City of Warren for other streets**, with the City’s Engineering Division confirming which agency has a given street: written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).

A defect that was readily apparent for 30 days or more is presumed known to the agency (MCL 691.1403). Notice to the wrong agency is not notice. **[ATTORNEY CHECK: jurisdiction list; 1403 presumption]**

**Bars and restaurants.** 31 percent of Michigan’s 2024 rider deaths involved alcohol. A bar that over-served the driver who hit a rider can be liable under the dram shop act: written notice within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, and suit within two years (MCL 436.1801(3)). ## Who pays a Warren rider’s medical bills

**If a car or truck was involved, you have no-fault benefits, but not from your motorcycle insurer.** Under MCL 500.3114(5), your Personal Injury Protection benefits come, in order, from:

1. the insurer of the owner or registrant of the motor vehicle involved;
2. the insurer of that vehicle’s operator;
3. the motor vehicle insurer of the motorcycle’s operator;
4. the motor vehicle insurer of the motorcycle’s owner or registrant.

“Involved” does not require contact; a car that cut you off is involved even if it never touched you. If no insurer in that chain exists, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan pays (MCL 500.3172), with its $250,000 medical cap.

The benefits are the ones a driver would get under MCL 500.3107: medical expenses up to the PIP limit on the policy that pays, 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each October, $20 a day in replacement services, attendant care, and mileage. Written notice is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

The first medical limit is set by a policy you did not choose. Since the 2019 reform, the PIP medical level on the policy first in priority pays first; if the driver who hit you chose $250,000, that policy pays up to $250,000, and a lower-priority policy with more coverage, such as your own car policy, may then pay the rest.

**If no car or truck was involved, no-fault does not apply.** A single-vehicle crash on a pothole, a broken joint, or gravel produces no PIP claim. Your bills go to your health insurance and any medical coverage you bought on the motorcycle policy, and your injury claim is against whoever caused the hazard. Some summaries say motorcycles are simply “not included” in no-fault; that is true of the definition, but a rider hit by a car usually has a full PIP claim.

**Your motorcycle.** Your own collision or comprehensive coverage pays for the bike. The mini tort claim does not cover a motorcycle, because a motorcycle is not a “motor vehicle” under the no-fault act (*Nerat v Swacker*, 150 Mich App 61, 1986).

## Your claim against the driver who hit you

For pain and suffering and economic losses beyond no-fault, you sue the at-fault driver if your injury is a serious impairment of body function, permanent serious disfigurement, or death (MCL 500.3135). Motorcycle injuries clear that threshold more often than car injuries: fractures, road rash requiring grafts, torn ligaments, [brain injuries](/personal-injury/brain-injury), and [spinal cord injuries](/personal-injury/spinal-cord-injury). You also recover excess economic damages above the PIP limit that applied to you (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)). You have three years to sue (MCL 600.5805(2)); a fatal crash is brought by the estate (see our [Warren wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death/warren)).

**What the claim is worth depends on the other driver’s policy.** Many Warren drivers carry the $50,000 minimum, and some carry nothing. The recovery then turns on your own **uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage**, which must be on the motorcycle policy; many car policies exclude the insured while riding an owned motorcycle. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Comparative fault is the fight.** Above 50 percent at fault you recover no noneconomic damages; below that your award is reduced by your share (MCL 500.3135(2)(b), 600.2959). Insurers argue speed, lane position, lane splitting (illegal in Michigan), and “came out of nowhere.” The police report, witnesses, the other driver’s phone records, business and plant cameras along Van Dyke and Mound, and the rider’s own camera answer it.

## The helmet question

Since 2012, Michigan has let riders 21 and older ride without a helmet if they carry at least $20,000 in first-party medical coverage and have either held a motorcycle endorsement for two years or passed an approved safety course (MCL 257.658). If you met those conditions, riding helmetless was legal, so it was not a traffic violation. In a head-injury case the insurer will argue a helmet would have reduced the injury, which is a damages argument for the jury, not a bar; in a leg or shoulder case it is irrelevant. **[ATTORNEY CHECK]**

## What to do after a Warren motorcycle crash

1. **Get medical care the same day.** Henry Ford Warren Hospital on Twelve Mile (formerly Ascension Macomb-Oakland, Warren campus), Henry Ford Macomb, and McLaren Macomb treat many of the area’s injured. **[CONFIRM: hospital list and trauma designations]**
2. **Get the police report** from Warren Police, or the State Police on I-696; a Warren Police UD-10 is purchased through CLEMIS, and a State Police UD-10 at michigan.gov/tcps. If a car caused the crash without contact, make sure the report says so.
3. **Identify every vehicle involved**, including the one that never touched you.
4. **Photograph the bike, the scene, the road surface, your gear, and your injuries.** Do not repair or sell the bike.
5. **Read your motorcycle policy for first-party medical, UM/UIM, and collision coverage**, and your car policy’s PIP level.
6. **Apply for no-fault benefits with the right insurer within the first week**, and do not give a recorded statement to the other driver’s insurer.
7. **If a road defect was involved, call within days.** The 120-day notice is already running.

## Frequently asked questions

**A car turned left in front of me on Van Dyke and I went down. Do I have a claim?** Yes. A driver turning left must yield to oncoming traffic, and the car is “involved” under no-fault whether or not it touched you, so its owner’s insurer is first in line for your medical bills. Get the plate and witnesses before the car is gone.

**I hit a pothole on 12 Mile. Who do I notify?** Many of Warren’s mile roads are the Macomb County Department of Roads’, but some stretches are the City’s, and the City’s Engineering Division confirms which. Either way, written notice is due within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). A defect on Van Dyke or Groesbeck is MDOT’s, with notice filed in the Court of Claims within 120 days.

**Are motorcycles covered by no-fault?** A motorcycle is not a “motor vehicle” under the no-fault act, so a rider who crashes alone has no PIP claim. A rider hit by a car or truck does: from that vehicle’s insurer first, under the priority rule.

**I wasn’t wearing a helmet. Can I still recover?** Yes, if you were 21 or older with the required $20,000 medical coverage and either two years of endorsement or a safety course. The insurer can argue a helmet would have reduced a head injury, which goes to damages, not to whether you have a claim.

**How long do I have?** One year to notify the responsible no-fault insurer, three years to sue the at-fault driver, two years to sue a bar, and 120 days for a notice to the City, the Department of Roads, or the State.
