## Where Dearborn Heights injuries happen

**Telegraph Road (US-24).** Eight lanes from Joy Road to Van Born, the city’s spine and its dividing line, with signals at Joy, Warren, Ford, Cherry Hill, Michigan Avenue, Annapolis, and Van Born and retail and apartment frontage most of the way. Left-turn and red-light crashes at the signals, rear-end crashes in the stacked turn lanes, and pedestrian crashes between the complexes and the strips. Telegraph is MDOT’s.

**Ford Road (M-153).** Through the north end from the Southfield Freeway and the Dearborn line past the retail strips, the Ford Road and Telegraph intersection, and Inkster Road. The Ford and Telegraph and Ford and Beech Daly intersections carry retail traffic, with left-turn, red-light, and driveway crashes among the risks. Ford Road is MDOT’s.

**Warren Avenue and Joy Road.** The north end’s east-west streets through the Arab American community, lined with restaurants, bakeries, mosques, and markets, with the heaviest foot traffic in the city at every hour. Pedestrian crashes at the Warren crossings, parking-lot crashes, and the neighborhood crashes of dense residential streets. Warren and Joy are Wayne County’s.

**Michigan Avenue (US-12).** Across the city’s middle, with its industrial and commercial frontage, the 20th District Court, and the Inkster and Telegraph intersections. Intersection crashes and truck traffic to the Michigan Avenue industrial corridor. Michigan Avenue is MDOT’s. See our [Dearborn Heights truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/dearborn-heights).

**The south end.** Van Born, Pelham, Beech Daly, Annapolis, and Powers, with the Taylor and Allen Park retail at the edges and I-94 just south. Intersection and school-zone crashes, and the flood-prone Ecorse Creek neighborhoods.

**Hines Drive.** The Wayne County parkway along the Middle Rouge through the north end, a winding two-lane road through Hines Park with picnic areas, trails, and low spots that flood. Run-off-road and head-on crashes on the curves, bicycle and pedestrian crashes where the trail meets the drive, and park injuries. Hines Drive and the park are Wayne County’s; a road-defect claim is against the county with 120-day notice, and park injuries face the recreational-use and immunity defenses. See our [bicycle accident page](/personal-injury/bicycle-accidents).

**The neighborhoods.** Mid-century housing in both halves, with apartment complexes along Telegraph, Ford Road, and Van Born and a growing renter share. Falls on stairs and ice, rental-housing injuries under MCL 554.139, dog bites under MCL 287.351, and sidewalk injuries under the two-inch rule (MCL 691.1402a). ## What is different about a Dearborn Heights claim

**Coverage.** Dearborn Heights drivers carry a mix of insurance policies, and coverage limits can significantly affect recovery after a serious crash. A pain-and-suffering claim against the at-fault driver generally requires an injury meeting Michigan’s statutory threshold (MCL 500.3135). Recovery may depend on the driver’s insurance, available assets, and any uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage. Michigan’s default bodily-injury liability limits are $250,000 per person and $500,000 per accident, although lower limits may be selected. We review every policy in the household before we value a Dearborn Heights claim. See our [Dearborn Heights car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents/dearborn-heights), [no-fault insurance guide](about:blank), and [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Three road agencies.** Warren, Joy, Cherry Hill, Van Born, Outer Drive, Beech Daly, Inkster, and Pelham fall under Wayne County or city jurisdiction depending on the segment; Hines Drive is maintained by Wayne County. A crash caused by a qualifying defect in the traveled roadway may implicate the highway exception to governmental immunity, MCL 691.1402. Signal malfunctions and sight obstructions require separate legal analysis.

**Government vehicles.** Dearborn Heights Police and Fire, SMART buses on Telegraph, Ford Road, Michigan Avenue, and Warren, school buses from the Crestwood, Dearborn Heights District 7, Westwood, and Dearborn districts that serve parts of the city, and county road and parks vehicles are claims under the Governmental Tort Liability Act’s motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405). See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

**The 20th District and the Third Circuit.** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims for vehicle damage (MCL 500.3135(3)(e), up to $3,000), are filed in the 20th District Court at 25637 Michigan Avenue in Dearborn Heights. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Third Judicial Circuit Court at the Coleman A. Young Municipal Center in Detroit. Wrongful death cases may require estate proceedings in Wayne County Probate Court, while the lawsuit itself proceeds in the court with jurisdiction. Settlements involving minors require court approval under MCR 2.420.

**The hospitals.** Corewell Health Dearborn Hospital on Oakwood Boulevard and Garden City Hospital on Inkster Road serve injured Dearborn Heights residents; Henry Ford Medical Center–Fairlane, Corewell Health Taylor, and Detroit’s Level I trauma centers also provide care, depending on the injury and treatment needs.

**Language.** Like Dearborn, Dearborn Heights’ north end is home to a large Arabic-speaking community. ATLAW can assist clients in Arabic and English, with additional language support available in French, Mandarin, Urdu, and Spanish.

## Deadlines that apply to Dearborn Heights claims

| Claim | Deadline | Statute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Negligence (crash, fall, dog bite) | 3 years from injury | MCL 600.5805(2) |
| Written notice to your no-fault insurer | 1 year from the crash | MCL 500.3145(1) |
| Road or sidewalk defect against the City of Dearborn Heights or Wayne County (incl. Hines Drive) | Written notice within 120 days | MCL 691.1404 |
| Defect on Telegraph, Ford Road, or Michigan Avenue (MDOT) | Written notice within 120 days, filed in the Court of Claims | MCL 691.1404 |
| Dram shop (bar that served a drunk driver) | Suit generally within two years; written notice to defendants within 120 days after entering an attorney-client relationship to pursue a dram shop claim, subject to statutory exceptions | MCL 436.1801 |
| Medical malpractice | 2 years, 182-day notice of intent first | MCL 600.5805(8), 600.2912b |
| Injured person under 18 | Generally, one year after turning 18 if the ordinary limitation period has expired; special rules apply | MCL 600.5851(1) |

## Frequently asked questions

**Where is ATLAW’s office, and how far is it from Dearborn Heights?** ATLAW’s office is at 3 Parklane Boulevard in Dearborn, a short drive from Dearborn Heights. Free surface parking is available.

**Where are the crashes in Dearborn Heights?** At the Telegraph signals from Joy to Van Born, on Ford Road from the Southfield Freeway to Inkster (including Ford at Mercury Drive and Ford at Telegraph), on Michigan Avenue, and at the Warren Avenue crossings. In 2024, Dearborn Heights recorded 1,251 traffic crashes, 385 people injured, and no traffic fatalities. Wayne County as a whole recorded 50,355 crashes, 16,458 injuries, and 177 deaths in 2024.

**I was hit crossing Warren Avenue or Ford Road. Who pays?** A pedestrian injured by a motor vehicle may qualify for no-fault benefits through an applicable personal or household auto policy. If no such coverage applies, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan may provide benefits, subject to statutory eligibility and coverage rules (MCL 500.3114 and 500.3115). Your pain-and-suffering claim against the driver requires a threshold injury and depends on the driver’s coverage and your own UM/UIM. See our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents).

**I was hurt on Hines Drive or in Hines Park. Who do I claim against?** For a crash with another driver, the driver, through no-fault and a threshold claim. For a road defect on Hines Drive, Wayne County, with written notice within 120 days. For an injury in the park itself, the county, subject to the recreational-use and governmental-immunity defenses that make park claims difficult.

**Where would my Dearborn Heights injury case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, in the 20th District Court on Michigan Avenue in Dearborn Heights; injury suits over $25,000 in the Third Judicial Circuit Court in Detroit.

**I was hurt because of a signal problem or pothole in Dearborn Heights. Who do I claim against?** On Telegraph, Ford Road, or Michigan Avenue, a qualifying highway-defect claim against MDOT generally requires notice within 120 days, filed in the Court of Claims. On Warren, Joy, Cherry Hill, Van Born, Outer Drive, Beech Daly, Inkster, or Pelham, the responsible agency depends on the specific road segment and may be Wayne County or the City of Dearborn Heights. On a local street, the City of Dearborn Heights, with the same 120 days.

**Do you speak Arabic?**  Yes. ATLAW can assist clients in Arabic and English. We also offer language support in French, Mandarin, Urdu, and Spanish.

**How much does it cost to hire you?** Nothing up front, and no fee unless we recover money for you. Michigan caps contingency fees in personal injury cases at one-third of the recovery (MCR 8.121).

**How long do I have?** Three years for most injury suits, one year to notify your no-fault insurer in writing, and as little as 120 days for a claim against the City of Dearborn Heights or Wayne County.
