## Where Southfield crashes happen, and whose road it is

**Telegraph Road (US-24).** Eight lanes from Eight Mile to Thirteen Mile. The 12 Mile intersection is third on the state’s 2025 list and first in Oakland County, and Southfield Road at 10 Mile is also in the county’s top ten. Left-turn crashes across four lanes of oncoming traffic, red-light crashes, and pedestrian crashes between the apartment complexes and the retail on both sides (see our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents)).

**Northwestern Highway and the Lodge (M-10).** The diagonal from the Lodge’s end through the Town Center and the office corridor to Twelve Mile, with the I-696 interchange in the middle. Merging crashes at the office-tower driveways and rear-end crashes in the Lodge’s stop-and-go.

**I-696.** Interchanges at Telegraph, Northwestern, Southfield Road, Greenfield, and Evergreen within four miles, and the reconstruction between Lahser Road and I-75 that has put lane closures and work-zone speed limits on the Southfield stretch. Heavy trucks share the work zone; see our [Southfield truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/southfield). A crash in a work zone carries enhanced penalties for the at-fault driver (MCL 257.601b) and is strong fault evidence in the civil case. **[CONFIRM: current I-696 project status; ATTORNEY CHECK: 257.601b]**

**The mile roads and the arterials.** Eight Mile (the Detroit line), Nine through Thirteen Mile, Evergreen, Lahser, Inkster, Greenfield, and Southfield Road: intersection crashes at the signals and pedestrian crashes near schools and apartment complexes.

**Three road agencies.** Telegraph, Northwestern, the Lodge, Eight Mile, and I-696 are MDOT’s; a road-defect claim goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). Lahser is also the Road Commission’s, and Evergreen and the local streets are the City of Southfield’s; both take written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404). A pothole or other defect in the roadbed can make the agency a defendant alongside the other driver; on State and county roads, signals, signs, and sight obstructions generally fall outside the highway exception. Your own auto insurer pays your medical expenses up to the PIP level you chose, 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each October, replacement services at $20 a day, and attendant care, regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107). If you have no policy, a resident relative’s policy pays (MCL 500.3114); if none exists, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3172), capped at $250,000 for medical benefits. The insurer must receive written notice within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)) and must pay each bill within 30 days of reasonable proof or owe 12 percent interest (MCL 500.3142). See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance) and [Michigan no-fault guide](about:blank); passengers are covered on our [passenger injury page](/personal-injury/passenger-injury).

**Your claim against the at-fault driver.** For pain and suffering and losses beyond no-fault, you need a threshold injury under MCL 500.3135: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement. Three years to sue (MCL 600.5805(2)); recovery reduced by your share of fault and barred above 50 percent (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). The mini tort recovers up to $3,000 in vehicle damage (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)), filed in the 46th District Court if the insurer will not pay it.

**The Southfield insurance picture.** Two kinds of drivers share Southfield’s roads. Commuters to the Northwestern towers and residents of the northern neighborhoods more often carry high liability limits and umbrella policies; drivers crossing Eight Mile more often carry the $50,000 minimum or nothing. Since the 2019 reform, many drivers on both sides also chose reduced PIP medical levels, so medical bills above your chosen level become part of your claim against the at-fault driver (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)). Your own uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage often decides what a Southfield claim is worth. Where the other driver carries high limits and you carry UIM, the sequence of settlement and your insurer’s consent matter; settling with the at-fault driver without your UIM insurer’s consent can forfeit the UIM claim. **[ATTORNEY CHECK: UIM consent-to-settle]**

**Drunk drivers and dram shop.** A crash caused by a drunk driver adds a claim against the bar or store that served a visibly intoxicated person, with written notice to the retailer within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, and suit within two years (MCL 436.1801(3)). Southfield’s 83 alcohol-involved crashes make this a recurring Southfield claim. See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

## What to do after a Southfield crash

1. **Call 911 and get the Southfield Police report.** Michigan requires a report for any crash with injury or $1,000 in damage (MCL 257.622). Southfield Police crash reports (UD-10s) are sold online through Oakland County’s CLEMIS system or at the Police Records Bureau.
2. **Photograph the vehicles, plates, the other driver’s license and insurance card, the signals, and the road**, and get witness names. Business and parking-structure cameras along Telegraph and Northwestern overwrite in days.
3. **Get medical care the same day.** Henry Ford Providence Southfield Hospital (formerly Ascension Providence) on Nine Mile treats many of Southfield’s seriously injured; Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak is the county’s Level I center; Henry Ford Providence Southfield is a Level II trauma center and Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital is Level I (State list, July 31, 2026).
4. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** within the first week.
5. **Read your declarations page** for PIP level, UM/UIM, and collision coverage.
6. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the other driver’s insurer or sign a release.
7. **If alcohol was involved, call within weeks**; the bar’s receipts and video do not keep, and the suit must be filed within two years.

## The courts

Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, are filed in the 46th District Court at 26000 Evergreen Road, which serves Southfield and Lathrup Village. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court (Oakland County Circuit Court) at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac. A crash on Eight Mile, or with a Wayne County defendant, may allow filing in the Third Circuit in Detroit (MCL 600.1629). Defect claims against MDOT go to the Court of Claims. A fatal crash is brought by the estate in the Oakland County Probate Court; see our [Southfield wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death/southfield). **[ATTORNEY CHECK: venue]**

## Frequently asked questions

**How dangerous is Telegraph Road in Southfield?** Telegraph at 12 Mile recorded 121 crashes in 2025, the third-highest count of any intersection in Michigan according to the Michigan State Police. The 10 Mile, 11 Mile, and Northwestern intersections are also high-crash locations.

**How many car accidents happen in Southfield each year?**  Southfield recorded 3,313 crashes, 945 injuries, and 10 deaths in 2024. Oakland County as a whole recorded 35,951 crashes, 9,139 injuries, and 68 deaths in 2024.

**I was hit in the I-696 construction zone. Does that change the case?** It can. Work-zone violations carry enhanced penalties and are strong evidence of fault, and a contractor’s traffic-control failures can make the contractor a defendant. Photograph the signs, barrels, and lane markings that day.

**The driver who hit me had no insurance. What now?** Your no-fault benefits are unaffected: your own insurer, a household member’s, or the Assigned Claims Plan pays your medical bills and lost wages. Your pain-and-suffering claim depends on your uninsured motorist coverage.

**Where would my Southfield case be filed?** Mini tort and claims up to $25,000 in the 46th District Court on Evergreen Road; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court in Pontiac, or in some cases the Third Circuit in Detroit.

**The other driver was drunk. Can I sue the bar?** If the bar or store served a visibly intoxicated person who then caused the crash, yes, under Michigan’s dram shop act, but only if written notice is given within 120 days after you hire a lawyer and suit is filed within two years.

**How long do I have?** One year to give your no-fault insurer written notice, three years to sue the at-fault driver, 120 days for a notice to the City, the RCOC, or the State, and two years to sue a bar, with notice to the bar within 120 days after you hire a lawyer.
