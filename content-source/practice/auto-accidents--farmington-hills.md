## Where Farmington Hills crashes happen, and whose road it is

**The Orchard Lake roundabout at 14 Mile.** A multilane roundabout at the city’s northern edge, on the West Bloomfield line. The crashes are drivers entering without yielding to traffic already circulating, drivers in the inner lane exiting across the outer lane, lane changes inside the circle, and rear-ends at the yield lines. Michigan’s rules of the road require an entering driver to yield to traffic in the roundabout, and in a two-lane roundabout the lane markings and signs before the entry decide which lane may exit where (MCL 257.649(6), 257.642(1)). Because the speeds are low, the injuries are usually less severe, but neck, back, and shoulder injuries in a sideswipe can still meet the threshold for a pain-and-suffering claim.

**Orchard Lake Road between 12 Mile and 14 Mile.** Orchard Lake carries the commute and the retail traffic between I-696 and the roundabout. The crashes are left turns across oncoming lanes, drivers pulling out of retail and office driveways, and rear-ends at the signals. At 12 Mile, the signalized intersection near the retail and offices produces red-light and angle crashes; it recorded 16 injuries in 2024 on 65 crashes.

**The I-696/I-96/I-275/M-5 interchange.** Four freeways meet at the city’s southwest corner, with ramps to Twelve Mile, Orchard Lake, Grand River, and Haggerty. Merge, weave, and lane-change crashes at the ramps; rear-end crashes in stop-and-go traffic on I-696 and I-96; and heavy trucks bound for the Novi and Wixom distribution corridor. See our [Farmington Hills truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/farmington-hills).

**Twelve Mile, Grand River, and the arterials.** Twelve Mile from Orchard Lake to Haggerty carries the office and engineering campuses, with crashes at the campus driveways and the Halsted and Haggerty signals at shift times. Grand River (M-5’s surface continuation) carries the retail strips and the hospital, with left-turn and driveway crashes. Middlebelt, Inkster, Farmington Road, Halsted, and the mile roads from Eight to Fourteen carry signal crashes and pedestrian and bicycle crashes near the schools and parks (see our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents) and [bicycle accident page](/personal-injury/bicycle-accidents)).

**Three road agencies.** I-696, I-96, I-275, M-5, and Grand River are MDOT’s; a road-defect claim against MDOT goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). Orchard Lake, Middlebelt, Inkster, Farmington Road, Halsted, Haggerty, and the mile roads, including Eight Mile, are generally the Road Commission for Oakland County’s, and subdivision streets the City of Farmington Hills’; both take written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404). A pothole or other defect in the roadbed can make the agency a defendant alongside the other driver; on State and county roads, signals, signs, and sight obstructions generally fall outside the highway exception. Notice to the wrong agency is not notice.

## Fault is the case

In a rear-end crash, fault is rarely disputed. In a roundabout, a Michigan-left crossover, or a signal crash on Orchard Lake, it is disputed often, and the other driver’s insurer may try to assign you half or more of the fault. That number matters: if you are found more than 50 percent at fault, you lose your claim for pain and suffering, and below that your recovery is reduced by your share (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). At exactly 50 percent you recover half. The evidence that decides it is perishable:

- the Farmington Hills Police report and the officer’s diagram, including which lane each car was in at the roundabout;
- **signal timing data** and any traffic-camera footage held by the Road Commission for Oakland County or MDOT;
- business, campus, and parking-lot cameras along Orchard Lake, Twelve Mile, and Grand River, many overwritten within days;
- the vehicles’ **event data recorders**, which store speed, braking, and throttle in the seconds before impact;
- photographs of the roundabout’s lane markings and signs as they were that day;
- witnesses in the other lanes.

We move promptly to request the camera footage, signal data, and vehicle data. Signal records are public records released under FOIA (MCL 15.231 et seq.).

## Who pays after a Farmington Hills crash

**Your no-fault (PIP) claim.** Your own auto insurer pays your medical expenses up to the PIP level you chose, 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year, replacement services at $20 a day, and attendant care, regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107). If you have no policy, a resident relative’s policy pays (MCL 500.3114); if none exists, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan does (MCL 500.3172), capped at $250,000 for medical benefits. The insurer must receive written notice within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)) and must pay each bill within 30 days of reasonable proof or owe 12 percent interest (MCL 500.3142). Since the 2019 reform, PIP medical coverage is no longer unlimited unless you chose it, so the level on your declarations page matters. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance) and [Michigan no-fault guide](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

**Your claim against the at-fault driver.** For pain and suffering, you need a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment is an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life; it does not have to be permanent (MCL 500.3135(5); *McCormick v Carrier*, 2010). You can also recover lost wages beyond the three years and medical bills above your chosen PIP level (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)), which matters for the engineers and professionals on the Twelve Mile corridor whose wages exceed the monthly cap. The suit must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The mini tort recovers up to $3,000 in vehicle damage not covered by your policy (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)), filed in the 47th District Court if the at-fault driver’s insurer will not pay it.

**Coverage on the other side, and UIM sequencing.** Many Farmington Hills drivers carry liability limits well above the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)), and freeway traffic from across the region brings drivers who carry only the minimum or nothing. When the at-fault driver’s limit is too small for the injury, your own **underinsured motorist coverage** pays the difference, but most policies require your insurer’s written consent before you accept the at-fault driver’s limit; settling without it can forfeit the UIM claim. We read every policy in the household, including umbrella coverage, before valuing the claim. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Drunk drivers and dram shop.** Oakland County recorded 871 alcohol-involved crashes in 2024. A crash caused by a drunk driver leaving a Grand River or Orchard Lake restaurant or bar adds a claim against the business that served a visibly intoxicated person or a minor. Written notice must go to the retailer within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, the drunk driver must be named and kept in the suit, and the suit must be filed within two years, not three (MCL 436.1801(3), (4)). See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**Government vehicles.** Farmington Hills Police and Fire, SMART buses on Grand River, Orchard Lake, and Twelve Mile, Farmington Public Schools buses, and Road Commission trucks fall under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405). See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

## What to do after a Farmington Hills crash

1. **Call 911 and get the police report.** Michigan requires a report for any crash with injury or $1,000 in damage (MCL 257.622). City streets and arterials are generally worked by the Farmington Hills Police; freeway crashes may be worked by the Michigan State Police. **[VERIFY: Farmington Hills Police report request process]**
2. **Photograph the vehicles, plates, the other driver’s license and insurance card, the signals or roundabout signs, and the lane markings.** Note every business with a camera facing the intersection; footage is kept for days, not weeks.
3. **Get medical care the same day.** Corewell Health Farmington Hills Hospital on Grand River treats many of the city’s injured; Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak, a Level I trauma center, Henry Ford Providence Novi Hospital, and Trinity Health Livonia Hospital treat others.
4. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** within the first week.
5. **Read your declarations page** for PIP level, UM/UIM, umbrella, and collision coverage.
6. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the other driver’s insurer, sign a release, or accept a policy-limits offer before your UIM insurer consents.
7. **Call a lawyer within days** if fault is disputed; the camera footage and signal data are the first evidence lost.

## The courts

Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, are filed in the 47th District Court at 31605 W. Eleven Mile Road, which serves Farmington Hills and Farmington. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac. A crash on Eight Mile or Haggerty at the Wayne County line, or with a Wayne County defendant, may allow filing in the Third Circuit in Detroit instead (MCL 600.1629). Defect claims against MDOT go to the Court of Claims. A fatal crash is brought by the estate through the Oakland County Probate Court; see our [Farmington Hills wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death/farmington-hills).

## Frequently asked questions

**Which Farmington Hills intersection has the most crashes?** In the State Police data for 2024, the roundabout at Orchard Lake Road and 14 Mile, with 109 crashes and 5 injuries, sixth among all Michigan intersections and second in Oakland County. Orchard Lake at 12 Mile, with 65 crashes and 16 injuries, was seventh in the county.

**How many car accidents happen in Farmington Hills each year?** Farmington Hills recorded 2,672 crashes, 540 injuries, and 3 deaths in 2024.

**I was hit in the Orchard Lake roundabout. Who is at fault?** Usually the driver who entered without yielding to traffic already in the roundabout, or who changed lanes or exited across another lane. Lane position is the key fact, so the police diagram, photographs of the lane markings, and any cameras matter.

**The other driver’s insurer offered their policy limit. Should I take it?** Not before checking your own underinsured motorist coverage and getting your insurer’s written consent to settle. Accepting the limit without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under most policies.

**I was hit by a driver leaving a Farmington Hills restaurant. Can I sue the restaurant?** If it served a visibly intoxicated person who then caused the crash, yes, under the dram shop act, but notice must go out within 120 days after you hire a lawyer and the suit must be filed within two years.

**Where would my Farmington Hills case be filed?** Mini tort and claims up to $25,000 in the 47th District Court on Eleven Mile; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court in Pontiac, or in some Eight Mile or Wayne County cases the Third Circuit in Detroit.

**How long do I have?** One year to give your no-fault insurer written notice, three years to sue the at-fault driver, two years to sue a bar, 120 days for a notice to the City or the Road Commission, and 120 days to file notice of a road-defect claim against MDOT.
