## Pittsfield Township Personal Injury Lawyer

If you were hurt in Pittsfield Charter Township because someone else was careless, you have a claim, and its deadlines start the day you are hurt:

- A road defect needs written notice within 120 days to the Washtenaw County Road Commission for a county road, or written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days for I-94, US-23, Michigan Avenue (US-12), or Washtenaw Avenue (M-17).
- **A sidewalk, pathway, or township building defect** needs written notice to the township within 120 days.
- **A claim against the University of Michigan or the State,** including care at University of Michigan Health, needs a verified notice filed with the Court of Claims within six months.
- **After a crash,** your no-fault insurer must have written notice within one year.

## Who the defendant is in Pittsfield Township

- **The Washtenaw County Road Commission.** The township owns no roads. Carpenter, Packard, Ellsworth, State Street, Platt, Hogback, and the subdivision streets are county roads. A road defect is a claim against the Road Commission, with written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404). A road agency is liable for failing to keep the roadway in reasonable repair, not for how it was designed.
- **MDOT and the State.** I-94, US-23, US-12 (Michigan Avenue), and M-17 (Washtenaw Avenue east of US-23) are state highways. A road-defect claim goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404); other claims against the State need a verified notice within six months (MCL 600.6431). The Michigan Supreme Court has held that the notice requirement applies to any claim against the State, wherever it is filed (*Christie v Wayne State University*, 2023).
- **Pittsfield Charter Township.** The township has the duty for sidewalks and pathways along the roads, with written notice within 120 days. A sidewalk claim requires that the township knew, or should have known, of the defect at least 30 days before the injury, and a defect under two inches is presumed to be in reasonable repair (MCL 691.1402a). A dangerous condition in a township building falls under the public building exception, and a crash with a township vehicle under the motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405, 691.1406). An injury on a path or field in a township park, such as Lillie Park, is limited by governmental immunity unless an exception applies. A notice sent to the township about a road, or to the Road Commission about a sidewalk, is sent to the wrong defendant.
- **The University of Michigan.** A claim against the University, including malpractice at University of Michigan Health or a crash with a University vehicle, is a claim against the State in the Court of Claims, with the six-month notice.
- **TheRide.** A crash with a TheRide bus on Washtenaw Avenue, Carpenter Road, State Street, or Packard Road is a claim against the Ann Arbor Area Transportation Authority under the motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405).
- **Landlords and apartment complexes.** The township has many apartment and condominium complexes, many of them along Carpenter, Ellsworth, and Packard. A landlord must keep the premises and common areas fit for their intended use and in reasonable repair, and comply with health and safety laws (MCL 554.139). That covers: stairs, railings, and balconies; lighting in stairwells, walks, and parking areas; wiring; smoke and carbon monoxide detectors; and snow and ice the landlord controls.

A lease clause that waives the landlord’s liability for its own negligence is not enforceable. Earlier complaints, work orders, and inspection records are often strong evidence of what the landlord knew. A guest at a party in an apartment may have a premises claim too. In a condominium, the association usually controls the common walks, stairs, and lots. See our [Pittsfield Township slip and fall page](https://atlawgroup.com/personal-injury/slip-and-fall/pittsfield-township) and our [tenant injury page](https://atlawgroup.com/personal-injury/tenant-injuries).

- **Stores, shopping centers, and parking lots.** The township’s retail is concentrated on Carpenter, Ellsworth, and Washtenaw Avenue. A fall in a store, a restaurant, or a parking lot is a premises claim against the owner, the tenant, or both, depending on who controlled the area. In a shopping center, the owner and its management company usually control the common walks and lot, and a snow-removal contractor can be a separate defendant. Since *Kandil-Elsayed v F & E Oil* (2023), an “open and obvious” hazard such as visible ice goes to comparative fault rather than ending the claim.
- **Bars, stores, and service to minors.** A bar, restaurant, or store that serves a visibly intoxicated person, or anyone under 21, is liable for the injuries that person causes (MCL 436.1801). The State Police recorded 34 alcohol-involved crashes in the township in 2024. The claim needs written notice within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, the intoxicated person named and kept in the suit, and suit within two years. Michigan generally does not hold a private social host liable for serving adults, but furnishing alcohol to minors can create liability. See our [drunk driving accident page](https://atlawgroup.com/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).
- **Dog owners.** An owner is liable for a bite when the person bitten was lawfully present and did not provoke the dog (MCL 287.351). The township’s animal ordinance may add a separate violation, such as a leash requirement, which can be evidence in a non-bite injury. See our [dog bite page](https://atlawgroup.com/personal-injury/dog-bites).
- **Employers and third parties.** Warehouses and light industry, retail, the schools, and construction on the freeways and corridors are major workplaces. Workers’ compensation is the remedy against the employer (MCL 418.131), but a contractor, property owner, or equipment maker who caused the injury can be sued, and the comp insurer is repaid from that recovery (MCL 418.827). See our [workers’ compensation page](https://atlawgroup.com/personal-injury/workers-compensation).
- **Schools.** The township is served by more than one school district. A district is a governmental agency. A school bus crash falls under the motor vehicle exception, and a dangerous condition in a school building under the public building exception, with written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1404, 691.1405, 691.1406).
- **Nursing homes and senior communities.** A claim against a nursing home may be ordinary negligence, with a three-year deadline, or medical malpractice, with two years and a 182-day notice. ## When the other driver has no insurance, or not enough

Some drivers carry only the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)), and some carry nothing.

- **Against an uninsured driver,** the claim for pain and suffering depends on your own **uninsured motorist coverage**. Without it, the claim is worth what the driver can personally pay. A hit-and-run is usually a UM claim, with short notice terms in the policy.
- **Against an underinsured driver,** your own **underinsured motorist coverage** pays the difference up to your UIM limit, if you follow the steps in order:
 1. The at-fault driver’s insurer offers its limit.
 2. You notify your own insurer and ask for its written consent to settle.
 3. Only then do you accept the limit and sign the release.
 4. The UIM claim then proceeds.

Accepting without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under many policies.

If you have no auto insurance of your own and no household policy, your no-fault benefits may come from the Assigned Claims Plan, with its $250,000 medical cap. That is often the case for a pedestrian or cyclist who does not own a car. A bus passenger with no household policy is paid by the bus’s insurer (MCL 500.3114(2)). Two rules cut the other way:

- An owner who was required to insure a car and did not is generally barred from PIP benefits (MCL 500.3113(b)).
- A driver who was operating their own uninsured vehicle cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, whoever was at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(c)).

## What a Pittsfield Township claim is worth, and what reduces it

A claim includes two kinds of damages:

- **Economic damages:** medical expenses, lost wages, lost earning capacity, and replacement services.
- **Noneconomic damages:** pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, and disfigurement.

There is no cap except in medical malpractice and product liability cases (MCL 600.1483, 600.2946a). In an auto case, noneconomic damages require a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment does not have to be permanent (*McCormick v Carrier*, 2010). Vehicle damage up to $3,000 can be recovered from the at-fault driver through the mini tort claim, without a threshold (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)). A premises, dog bite, or dram shop case has no threshold.

**Comparative fault.**

- **More than 50 percent at fault:** noneconomic damages are barred (MCL 600.2959; in auto cases, MCL 500.3135(2)(b)).
- **50 percent or less:** your recovery is reduced by your share. At exactly 50 percent, you recover half.
- **Economic damages** are reduced by your share but not barred.

Whether your share is above 50 percent is often the whole dispute.

Where more than one party caused the injury, each defendant generally pays only its own share of fault (MCL 600.2956, 600.6304). That is why the landlord, the property manager, the shopping center owner, the snow-removal contractor, and the bar all have to be identified and named. Michigan does not award punitive damages in ordinary injury cases.

You cannot sue the at-fault party’s liability insurer directly in Michigan. The claim is against the driver, owner, or business, and its insurer defends and pays.

The practical limit is often insurance:

- **The at-fault driver’s limit** defaults to 500,000 unless the driver chose lower limits, down to the 100,000 minimum (MCL 500.3009(1)).
- **Your UM and UIM coverage** fills the gap.
- **In a premises, workplace, or nursing home case,** the commercial policy of the landlord, owner, contractor, or operator pays, and those policies are often larger than an auto policy.
- **In a dram shop case,** the bar’s liquor liability policy adds to it.

 For a worker with a third-party claim, the workers’ compensation insurer has a lien on the recovery (MCL 418.827). Medicare, Medicaid, and health plan liens are negotiated the same way.

## What it costs

Michigan caps contingency fees in personal injury cases at one-third of the net recovery (MCR 8.121). A fee above one-third of an injury recovery exceeds that cap. You pay nothing up front and nothing if there is no recovery. Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted from the recovery first; the one-third fee is calculated on the remaining amount.

The agreement is in writing. Read how costs are handled if the case is lost, because that is where firms differ: filing fees, records, experts, and depositions. A promise that “you pay nothing unless we win” should say whether that includes costs, and whether “win” means the same thing as “settle.” A “success rate” or a list of verdicts tells you about past cases, not yours, and says nothing unless it says when, where, and what the case was.

## Frequently asked questions


**How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in Pittsfield Township?** Three years for most claims. Several deadlines are much shorter: - six months for a claim against the University of Michigan or the State, and 120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT - 120 days for a notice to the Road Commission of a road defect, or to the township of a sidewalk, pathway, or building defect - one year to notify your no-fault insurer after a crash - 120 days for a dram shop notice after you hire a lawyer, and two years to sue the bar - a 182-day notice and a two-year limit for malpractice - two years for a federal claim about VA care

**My address says Ann Arbor. Is my claim a Pittsfield Township claim?** Possibly. Most township residents have an Ann Arbor, Ypsilanti, or Saline mailing address. The police or incident report names the jurisdiction, and the responding agency is a clue: the township’s Department of Public Safety in the township, the Ann Arbor police in the city. The answer decides who owns the road and which district court hears a small claim.

**I fell on the stairs at my apartment complex. Is the landlord responsible?** Usually, if the stairs or railing were in disrepair. A landlord must keep the premises and common areas in reasonable repair and fit for their use, and a lease clause waiving that duty is not enforceable. Photograph the defect, report it in writing, and keep earlier complaints.

**I tripped on a township pathway. Who is responsible?** Usually the township, which has the duty for sidewalks and pathways along the roads, with written notice within 120 days. A defect in the road itself is the Road Commission’s.

**Can I sue over a dangerous intersection on Carpenter Road?** Sometimes. A road agency is liable for failing to keep the roadway itself in reasonable repair, not for its design. The claim needs written notice within 120 days for a county road, or 120 days, filed with the Court of Claims, for a state highway.

**Can I sue the other driver’s insurance company?** No. In Michigan, the claim is against the driver, and the driver’s insurer defends and pays. Your own no-fault insurer is a separate claim, for your medical bills and lost wages.

**What does a Pittsfield Township personal injury attorney charge?** Michigan caps the fee in personal injury cases at one-third of the net recovery, with no fee if there is no recovery. Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted only from a recovery.

**If I was partly at fault, do I still have a claim?** Yes, unless you were more than 50 percent at fault, which bars pain-and-suffering damages. Below that, your recovery is reduced by your share, and at exactly 50 percent you recover half. No-fault benefits are paid regardless of fault.

**Do I need a lawyer whose office is in Pittsfield Township?** No. What matters is that the lawyer knows the courts your case will be in, the agencies, insurers, and property owners involved, and will meet you where you are.
