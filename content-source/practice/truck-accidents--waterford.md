Waterford Township’s freight moves on two state highways. ## Where Waterford truck crashes happen

**Dixie Highway and the heavy haulers.** Michigan allows trucks on designated state routes to weigh up to 164,000 pounds, about twice the federal Interstate limit, on eleven axles or more; the multi-axle aggregate trucks called gravel trains are the familiar example. A loaded gravel train needs far more distance to stop than a car, and its lift axles, brakes, and load have to be maintained and adjusted to carry that weight legally. The crashes on Dixie are rear-end crashes when traffic stops at the Walton, Sashabaw, Scott Lake, and Andersonville signals, trucks turning wide across lanes into yards and dealership lots, and lost or spilled loads: gravel, stone, or equipment that strikes the cars behind. An unsecured load is a violation of the federal cargo securement rules (49 CFR 393.100 and following) and of Michigan law (MCL 257.720). Farther up the corridor, Dixie at I-75 in Springfield Township ranked fourth in Oakland County for crashes in 2024.

**M-59 and the retail deliveries.** The stores and restaurants along Highland Road take deliveries daily. Tractor-trailers turn across several lanes into store lots. A car that strikes the side or rear of a trailer can go under it; that is an **underride** crash, among the most lethal in trucking. Trailers must carry rear impact guards that meet federal standards (49 CFR 393.86), and a missing, damaged, or substandard guard is evidence against the carrier and the trailer’s owner. When a store’s dock layout or delivery schedule puts trucks in the road, the property owner may share responsibility with the carrier.

**The lake roads.** Dump trucks, concrete and construction trucks, waste haulers, and delivery vans use Walton, Sashabaw, Williams Lake, Pontiac Lake, Elizabeth Lake, Cass Lake, and the subdivision streets, which were not built for them: two lanes, curves and hills, and driveways on short sight lines. The crashes are trucks crossing the center line on a curve, backing crashes in residential streets, and pedestrians and cyclists struck by a turning truck whose driver could not see them. A crash with a smaller commercial vehicle, such as a delivery van, may fall outside some federal rules but is still a claim against the employer for its driver’s negligence.

**Whose road it is: never the township’s.** Michigan townships do not own public roads. Dixie Highway, Telegraph (US-24), and M-59 are MDOT’s; a road-defect claim against the State goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). The county roads, including Walton, Sashabaw, Crescent Lake, Williams Lake, Pontiac Lake, Elizabeth Lake, Cass Lake, and Airport Road, are the Road Commission for Oakland County’s, with 120 days’ notice (MCL 691.1404).

## Who is responsible, and who pays

**The carrier, not just the driver.** The motor carrier is responsible for its drivers’ compliance with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations and is liable for a driver’s negligence in the course of employment. Michigan applies the federal rules to intrastate carriers (MCL 480.11a), so a local aggregate or construction hauler that never leaves Oakland County is held to the same standards as an interstate carrier. The rules that decide truck cases most often:

| Rule | 49 CFR | What it means for the case |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Driver qualification | Part 391 | CDL, medical certificate, driving record, background check; negligent hiring and retention are claims against the carrier. |  |
| Post-crash testing | Part 382 | Alcohol within 8 hours and drugs within 32 after a fatal crash, or an injury or tow-away crash with a citation. |  |
| Inspection and maintenance | Part 396 | Pre-trip inspections, defect reports, maintenance records; brake, tire, lift-axle, lighting, and reflective-tape cases are maintenance cases. |  |
| Cargo securement | 393.100–393.136 | Loads must be contained or secured so they cannot shift or fall; a spilled load is evidence of a violation. |  |
| Equipment: rear impact guards, lighting, reflectors | Part 393 | Underride guards (393.86) and conspicuity marking; a trailer that cannot be seen at night or lacks a working guard is a defective trailer. |  |

**The owner of the truck or trailer.** In Michigan, a vehicle’s owner is liable for its negligent operation by someone driving with the owner’s consent (MCL 257.401). When the tractor, the trailer, and the driver belong to different companies, which is common in freight and in aggregate hauling, each may be a defendant, although a company in the business of renting or leasing vehicles is generally shielded from owner liability by federal law (49 USC 30106).

**Michigan no-fault still applies to you.** Your own auto insurer pays your medical bills, 85 percent of lost wages up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year, replacement services, and attendant care regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107), with written notice due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). The Michigan Assigned Claims Plan pays if no household policy applies (MCL 500.3172). Your claim against the driver and the carrier for pain and suffering and losses beyond no-fault requires a threshold injury (MCL 500.3135) and must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). If you were more than 50 percent at fault, you cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, and below that your share reduces them (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). The difference from a car case is the money available: the carrier’s $750,000-plus policy, often with excess coverage above it. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

**Other defendants.** The shipper, pit, or broker that loaded the truck or hired an unsafe carrier; the property owner whose dock operations contributed; the maintenance contractor; the manufacturer of a failed component or a defective guard ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability)); and, for a government truck (MDOT, the Road Commission, Waterford Township, SMART, or a school bus), the agency under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405).

**If you were the truck driver.** Workers’ compensation pays your benefits, and your employer usually cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). If you were injured in your employer’s truck, no-fault benefits generally come from the insurer of that truck (MCL 500.3114(3)), coordinated with comp, which is paid first (MCL 500.3109(1)). You can also sue a third party, such as another driver or a property owner whose dock operation caused the crash. See our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).

## The evidence, and how fast it disappears

Carriers must keep hours-of-service records for six months (49 CFR 395.8) and driver inspection reports for three months (49 CFR 396.11); after that the regulations no longer require them. Business and yard cameras along Dixie and M-59, MDOT cameras, and dash cameras overwrite in days to weeks. The truck’s engine control module and event data recorder can be cleared when it is repaired and returned to service; a spilled load is swept up within hours; and a damaged rear guard can be replaced before anyone measures it.

The first act in a Waterford truck case is a **preservation letter**: to the carrier, its insurer, the tractor and trailer owners, the shipper, pit, or broker, and, where the crash happened at or near a store or yard, the property owner. It demands the ELD and engine data, dash camera footage, the driver’s qualification file and post-crash test results, dispatch records, bills of lading and scale tickets showing the load’s weight, maintenance files, and the truck and trailer themselves for inspection, including the brakes, lift axles, load securement, and rear guard. Once a party knows a claim is coming, destroying that evidence exposes it to spoliation sanctions. 

## What to do after a Waterford truck crash

1. **Call 911 and get medical care the same day.** McLaren Oakland and Trinity Health Oakland in Pontiac are both Level II trauma centers; the most serious injuries may go to Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak, a Level I center.
2. **Photograph the truck**: the cab, the trailer, the USDOT number and carrier name on the door, both plates, the load and anything that fell from it, the rear guard and reflective tape, and the driveway or lot if the truck was entering or leaving one.
3. **Get the police report.** Most township crashes are worked by the Waterford Township Police Department; some by the Michigan State Police or the Oakland County Sheriff’s Office. A serious crash may produce a separate commercial vehicle inspection report from the State Police. **[VERIFY: commercial vehicle enforcement; report request process]**
4. **Do not speak to the carrier’s insurer or its investigator.**
5. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** within the first week.
6. **Call a lawyer within days**, so the preservation letter goes out before the records expire and the truck is repaired.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many truck accidents happen in Waterford?** The State does not publish truck crash figures by township. Countywide, 2,081 heavy trucks and buses were involved in Oakland County crashes in 2024, and statewide 15,888 crashes involved a heavy truck or bus.

**Gravel fell off a truck on Dixie Highway and hit my car. Who pays?** The carrier, and possibly the company that loaded it. Federal and Michigan rules require loads to be secured so they cannot fall, and a load that falls is evidence the rule was broken. Your own insurer pays for your injuries under no-fault and your collision coverage pays for the car; the carrier’s insurer pays for losses beyond that. Write down the truck’s company name and USDOT number if you can.

**My car went under the back of a trailer on M-59. Is that the truck’s fault?** It may be partly or wholly. Trailers must carry rear impact guards built to federal standards, and a guard that is missing, bent, or too high to stop a car is evidence against the carrier and the trailer’s owner. The trailer has to be inspected before it is repaired.

**Why are the trucks on Dixie Highway so heavy?** Michigan allows trucks on designated routes to weigh up to 164,000 pounds on eleven axles or more, about twice the federal Interstate limit. Those trucks are legal only if their axles, brakes, and loads are set up and maintained for that weight, which is why the weight tickets and maintenance records matter in a case.

**I was rear-ended by a semi on M-59. Who pays?** Your own no-fault insurer pays your medical bills and lost wages first, regardless of fault. The carrier’s insurer pays for pain and suffering and losses beyond no-fault once a threshold injury is established.

**I drive a truck and was hurt in a crash in Waterford. What are my claims?** Workers’ compensation through your employer, no-fault benefits coordinated with comp, and a lawsuit against any third party who caused the crash.

**Why does the lawyer need to act within days?** Because the records expire: six months for hours-of-service logs, three months for inspection reports, days or weeks for camera footage, and the truck’s electronic data, the spilled load, and the trailer’s damage can disappear on cleanup and repair.
