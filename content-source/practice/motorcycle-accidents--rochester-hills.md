## Where Rochester Hills motorcycle crashes happen

**The left turn.** The recurring account in rider cases is a driver turning left across oncoming traffic who misjudges the speed of a motorcycle or does not see it at all. Under Michigan law, a driver must yield to oncoming traffic before turning left (MCL 257.650). Rochester Hills produces that crash in two places every day:

- the signals on Rochester Road, Adams, Crooks, and Livernois
- the shopping-center driveways and subdivision entrances between them

Drivers pulling out from a side street across the lanes produce its mirror image.

**M-59.** M-59 is a freeway through the city. At its interchanges, including Adams, Crooks, and Rochester Road, drivers merging or changing lanes for an exit often fail to check for a motorcycle in the next lane, and rear-end crashes in the backups throw a rider in a way a car would absorb.

- curves with limited sight lines

- pavement seams and patched potholes

**Deer.** Oakland County recorded 2,024 deer-involved crashes in 2024. For a rider, a deer strike is often a serious injury crash, and it raises a no-fault problem covered below: if no car or truck was involved, there is no PIP claim.

**The road itself.** A single-vehicle crash caused by a pothole, a broken concrete joint, a pavement drop-off, or gravel is a claim against the agency with jurisdiction, under the highway exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1402). In Rochester Hills there are four possible defendants:

- MDOT for M-59, and for Rochester Road (M-150) from M-59 north to Tienken: written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404; *Champine v Department of Transportation*, Mich 2022).
- The Road Commission for Oakland County for many major roads, including Adams, Crooks, Livernois, Avon, Tienken, Walton, and Rochester Road south of M-59: written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- The City of Rochester Hills for Hamlin, John R, other City roads, and the subdivision streets: written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- **Oakland University** for its campus roads: a claim against the State, in the Court of Claims, with written notice of a road defect within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).

A defect that was readily apparent to an ordinary observer for 30 days or more is conclusively presumed known to the agency (MCL 691.1403). Notice to the wrong agency is not notice. Loose gravel is not always a defect an agency is liable for, so the notice should go out while the facts are checked.

**Bars and restaurants.** 31 percent of Michigan’s 2024 rider deaths involved alcohol. A bar that over-served the driver who hit a rider can be liable under the dram shop act. Written notice is due within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, and suit within two years (MCL 436.1801(3)). See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

## Who pays a Rochester Hills rider’s medical bills

**If a car or truck was involved, you have no-fault benefits, but not from your motorcycle insurer.** Under MCL 500.3114(5), your Personal Injury Protection benefits come, in order, from:

1. the insurer of the owner or registrant of the motor vehicle involved
2. the insurer of that vehicle’s operator
3. the motor vehicle insurer of the motorcycle’s operator
4. the motor vehicle insurer of the motorcycle’s owner or registrant

Priority follows the vehicles’ insurers, not who was at fault. “Involved” does not require contact; a car that cut you off is involved even if it never touched you. ; If no insurer in that chain exists, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan pays (MCL 500.3172), with its $250,000 medical cap, unless you owned the motorcycle and it was uninsured (MCL 500.3113(b)).

The benefits are the ones a driver would get under MCL 500.3107:

- medical expenses, up to the PIP limit on the policy that pays
- 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- $20 a day in replacement services
- attendant care
- mileage

Written notice is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

The first medical limit is set by a policy you did not choose. Since the 2019 reform, the PIP medical level on the policy first in priority pays first; if the driver who hit you chose $250,000, that policy pays up to $250,000, and a lower-priority policy with more coverage, such as your own car policy, may then pay the rest.

**If no car or truck was involved, no-fault does not apply.** Several common crashes produce no PIP claim:

- a deer strike on Tienken
- a slide on gravel at a driveway
- a pothole on a county road
- a crash avoiding a deer with no other vehicle in the picture

Your bills go to your health insurance and to any medical coverage you bought on the motorcycle policy. Any injury claim is against whoever caused the hazard, if anyone did.

**Your motorcycle.** Your own collision or comprehensive coverage pays for the bike. If another driver was at fault, you also have the mini tort claim against that driver for up to $3,000 (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)). If the insurer will not pay it, the mini tort claim is filed in the 52-3 District Court.

## Your claim against the driver who hit you

For pain and suffering and economic losses beyond no-fault, you can sue the at-fault driver if your injury meets the threshold: serious impairment of body function, permanent serious disfigurement, or death (MCL 500.3135). A serious impairment is an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life. It does not have to be permanent (*McCormick v Carrier*, 2010).

Motorcycle injuries clear that threshold more often than car injuries do. Common examples include:

- fractures
- road rash requiring grafts
- torn ligaments
- [brain injuries](/personal-injury/brain-injury)
- [spinal cord injuries](/personal-injury/spinal-cord-injury)

You also recover excess economic damages above the PIP limit that applied to you (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)). You have three years to sue (MCL 600.5805(2)). A fatal crash is brought by the estate; see our [Rochester Hills wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death).

**What the claim is worth depends on the policies.** The default liability limit is $250,000 per person, but some drivers carry the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)) and some carry nothing. The recovery above the other driver’s limit turns on your own **uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage**. That coverage must be on the motorcycle policy, because many car policies exclude the insured while riding an owned motorcycle. Your UIM insurer’s written consent is needed before you accept the at-fault driver’s limit. See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Comparative fault is the fight.** Michigan’s rule is modified comparative fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(b), 600.2959):

- **More than 50 percent at fault:** you recover no noneconomic damages.
- **50 percent or less:** your award is reduced by your share.
- **Exactly 50 percent:** you recover half.

Economic damages beyond no-fault are reduced by your share as well. Insurers argue speed, lane position, lane splitting (illegal in Michigan), and “came out of nowhere.” The evidence that answers them:

- the Sheriff’s report and witnesses
- the other driver’s phone records
- store and gas-station cameras along Rochester Road and M-59
- the rider’s own camera

## The helmet question

Since 2012, Michigan has let riders 21 and older ride without a helmet if they meet two conditions (MCL 257.658):

- They carry at least $20,000 in first-party medical coverage.
- They have either held a motorcycle endorsement for two years or passed an approved safety course.

Riders under 21 must wear a helmet. If you met those conditions, riding without a helmet was legal, so it was not a traffic violation. In a head-injury case, the insurer will argue a helmet would have reduced the injury. That is a damages and causation argument for the jury, not a bar to the claim. In a leg or shoulder case, it is irrelevant.

## What to do after a Rochester Hills motorcycle crash

1. **Get medical care the same day.** Henry Ford Rochester Hospital, on University Drive, describes itself as a Level III adult trauma center. The most serious injuries may go to McLaren Oakland or Trinity Health Oakland in Pontiac, both Level II trauma centers, or to Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak, the county’s Level I adult trauma center.
2. **Get the police report.** Crashes in the city are worked by the Oakland County Sheriff’s Office, which polices Rochester Hills under contract, and freeway crashes may be worked by the State Police. If a car caused the crash without contact, make sure the report says so.
3. **Identify every vehicle involved,** including the one that never touched you.
4. **Photograph the bike, the scene, the road surface, your gear, and your injuries.** If a deer was involved, photograph where it happened and any other vehicle nearby. Do not repair or sell the bike.
5. **Read your motorcycle policy** for first-party medical, UM/UIM, collision, and comprehensive coverage, and check your car policy’s PIP level.
6. **Apply for no-fault benefits with the right insurer within the first week.** Do not give a recorded statement to the other driver’s insurer.
7. **If a road defect was involved, call within days.** The 120-day notice is already running.

## Frequently asked questions

**A car turned left in front of me on Rochester Road and I went down. Do I have a claim?** Yes. A driver turning left must yield to oncoming traffic. The car is “involved” under no-fault whether or not it touched you, so its owner’s insurer is first in line for your medical bills. Get the plate and witnesses before the car is gone.

**I hit a deer on Tienken. Does no-fault pay my medical bills?** Usually not. A motorcycle is not a “motor vehicle” under the no-fault act, and if no car or truck was involved, there is no PIP claim. Your health insurance and any medical coverage on your motorcycle policy pay, and your comprehensive coverage pays for the bike. If a car was involved, for example one that braked or swerved into you, the answer may be different.

**Does the at-fault driver’s insurance pay my medical bills?** Not because the driver was at fault. Under the rider priority rule, the insurer of the car involved pays first, whoever caused the crash. Fault matters for your separate claim for pain and suffering.

**I hit a pothole on a Rochester Hills road. Who do I notify?** It depends on the road. City roads, such as Hamlin and John R, belong to the City, with written notice within 120 days. County roads, such as Adams, Crooks, Livernois, Avon, Tienken, Walton, and Rochester Road south of M-59, belong to the Road Commission for Oakland County, also within 120 days. M-59, and Rochester Road north of M-59, belong to MDOT, with written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days. The notice must state the exact location, the defect, the injury, and the witnesses.

**I wasn’t wearing a helmet. Can I still recover?** Yes, if you meet all three conditions: - You were 21 or older. - You carried the required $20,000 in medical coverage. - You had either two years of endorsement or a safety course. The insurer can argue a helmet would have reduced a head injury, but that goes to damages, not to whether you have a claim.

**How long do I have?** - One year to notify the responsible no-fault insurer. - Three years to sue the at-fault driver. - Two years to sue a bar. - 120 days for a notice to the City or the Road Commission. - Six months for a claim against MDOT or the university.
