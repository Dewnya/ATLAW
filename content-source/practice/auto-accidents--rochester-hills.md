## Rochester Hills, MI Car Accident Lawyer

A car crash in Rochester Hills is a Michigan no-fault claim on a road system with these main parts:

- **M-59** runs across the city as a freeway, from I-75 to the Macomb County line.
- **Rochester Road** carries the northeast county’s retail and commuter traffic from the Troy line to downtown Rochester.
- **Adams, Crooks, Livernois, and John R** run north and south.
- **Auburn, Hamlin, Avon, Tienken, Walton, and South Boulevard** run east and west, crossed by the Clinton River and Paint Creek trails.

## Where Rochester Hills crashes happen

**M-59.** M-59 is a freeway through the city, with interchanges including Adams, Crooks, and Rochester Road, and the approach to I-75.

- **On the freeway:** merge and weave crashes at the ramps, and rear-end crashes in the backups.

M-59 belongs to MDOT. See our [Rochester Hills truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/rochester-hills).

**Rochester Road.** This is the city’s main north-south arterial, from South Boulevard and the Troy line, through the M-59 interchange and the retail strips at Auburn and Hamlin, to the Avon and Tienken signals and downtown Rochester. The crashes are:

- left turns across traffic (MCL 257.650)
- rear-end crashes in the stacked turn lanes
- driveway crashes at the shopping centers
- pedestrian crashes near the restaurants

From M-59 north to Tienken, Rochester Road is M-150 and belongs to MDOT; south of M-59 it is a Road Commission road.

**Livernois, Hamlin, and the mile roads.** Livernois at Hamlin was the city’s highest-crash intersection in 2024. Crooks, Adams, John R, Auburn, Avon, Tienken, and Walton carry neighborhood and commuter traffic to M-59 and Rochester Road. The crashes are:

- signal and left-turn crashes
- lane changes into an occupied lane (MCL 257.642(1)(a))
- crashes at subdivision entrances
- crashes with cyclists and walkers where the Clinton River Trail and Paint Creek Trail cross the roads at grade

See our [bicycle accident page](/personal-injury/bicycle-accidents) and our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents).

**Oakland University.** The campus sits on the city’s western side along Walton Boulevard, extending into Auburn Hills. Its traffic includes pedestrian crossings, campus roads, parking structures, and event traffic. A crash on the university’s own roads, or with a university vehicle, is a claim against the State, because Oakland University is a state institution. That claim goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice within six months for a vehicle claim (MCL 600.6431), or 120 days for a road defect (MCL 691.1404)

**Deer.** Oakland County recorded 2,024 deer-involved crashes in 2024, and the wooded corridors along the Clinton River, Paint Creek, and the city’s northern and eastern edges produce them. A deer crash is a no-fault claim:

- Your own insurer pays your medical bills and lost wages.
- Comprehensive coverage usually pays for damage from hitting the deer; collision coverage applies if you swerved and hit something else.

There is usually no one to sue. The exception is a crash where another driver swerved into you or caused a chain-reaction collision. **Comprehensive coverage usually pays for damage from hitting the deer; collision coverage applies if you swerved and hit something else**

**Whose road it is: the State, the county, the City, or the university.** Rochester Hills is a city, and it maintains a number of its own major roads as well as its subdivision streets:

- MDOT owns M-59, and Rochester Road (M-150) from M-59 north to Tienken. A road-defect claim against MDOT goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
- The Road Commission for Oakland County owns many of the major roads, including Adams, Crooks, Livernois, Avon, Tienken, Walton, and Rochester Road south of M-59.
- The City of Rochester Hills owns some major roads, including Hamlin and John R, as well as the subdivision streets.
- **Oakland University** owns its campus roads. A claim against it is a claim against the State.

A claim against the Road Commission or the City requires written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404). A pothole or other defect in the road can make the agency a defendant alongside the other driver; on State and county roads, signals, signs, and sight obstructions generally fall outside the highway exception (*Nawrocki v Macomb County Road Commission*, Mich 2000). The notice must go to the agency that actually has jurisdiction over that stretch of road, or it is no notice. The City also has the duty to keep public sidewalks and pathways in repair (MCL 691.1402a). See our [sidewalk injury page](/personal-injury/sidewalk-injuries).

## Fault is the case

In a rear-end crash, fault is rarely disputed. At an M-59 ramp, a Rochester Road left turn, or a subdivision entrance, it often is, and the other driver’s insurer may try to assign you half or more of the fault. That number matters:

- **More than 50 percent at fault:** you lose your claim for pain and suffering.
- **50 percent or less:** your recovery is reduced by your share. At exactly 50 percent, you recover half.

The rule is in MCL 500.3135(2)(b). The evidence that decides fault is perishable:

- the Oakland County Sheriff’s Office crash report and the deputy’s diagram (the Sheriff polices Rochester Hills under contract)
- **signal timing data** from MDOT, the Road Commission, or the City
- store, gas-station, business, and residential cameras, many overwritten within days
- the vehicles’ **event data recorders**, which store speed, braking, and throttle in the seconds before impact
- the other driver’s phone records, for a distracted-driving claim (see our [distracted driving page](/personal-injury/distracted-driving-accidents))
- witnesses

We move promptly to request the camera footage, signal data, and vehicle data.

## Who pays after a Rochester Hills crash

**Your no-fault (PIP) claim.** Your own auto insurer pays these benefits regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107):

- your medical expenses, up to the PIP level you chose
- 85 percent of lost wages for up to three years, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year
- replacement services at $20 a day
- attendant care

If you have no policy, a resident relative’s policy pays (MCL 500.3114). If none exists, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan pays (MCL 500.3172), capped at $250,000 for medical benefits. A pedestrian or cyclist hit by a car, with no household policy, claims through the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3115).

The insurer must receive written notice within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). It must pay each bill within 30 days of reasonable proof, or owe 12 percent interest (MCL 500.3142). Since the 2019 reform, PIP medical coverage is no longer unlimited unless you chose it, so the level on your declarations page matters. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance) and [Michigan no-fault guide](about:blank).

**Your claim against the at-fault driver.** For pain and suffering, you need a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A serious impairment is an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life. It does not have to be permanent (MCL 500.3135(5); *McCormick v Carrier*, 2010).

You can also recover two kinds of economic loss beyond no-fault (MCL 500.3135(3)(c)):

- lost wages beyond the three years or above the monthly cap
- medical bills above your chosen PIP level

The suit must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The mini tort claim recovers up to $3,000 in vehicle damage not covered by your policy (MCL 500.3135(3)(e)). It is filed in the 52nd District Court, Third Division, if the at-fault driver’s insurer will not pay it.

**Two policies, in the right order.** In a serious crash, the at-fault driver’s liability limit, often $250,000 or more, may still be less than the claim is worth. If you carry **underinsured motorist coverage**, your own insurer pays the difference up to your UIM limit. The order matters, and a mistake can forfeit the second policy:

1. The at-fault driver’s insurer offers its limit.
2. You notify your own insurer of the offer and ask for its written consent to settle. Most UIM policies require that consent, and many give your insurer a short period to respond or to pay the limit itself.
3. Only after consent do you accept the limit and sign the release.
4. Then the UIM claim proceeds against your own insurer, which can contest the value of the claim.

Accepting the liability limit without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under most policies. We read every policy in the household, including umbrella coverage, before valuing the claim.

**When the other driver has no insurance, or the minimum.** Some drivers carry the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5))

- **Against an uninsured driver:** your pain-and-suffering claim is worth what that driver can personally pay, unless you carry **uninsured motorist coverage**.
- **A hit-and-run:** usually a UM claim, and the policy’s notice terms are often short.
- **If you were uninsured yourself:** an owner who was required to insure a car and did not is generally barred from PIP benefits (MCL 500.3113(b)). A driver who was operating their own uninsured vehicle cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, whoever was at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(c)).

See our [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**Drunk drivers and dram shop.** Oakland County recorded 871 alcohol-involved crashes in 2024. A crash caused by a drunk driver leaving a bar or restaurant on Rochester Road or in downtown Rochester can add a claim against the business, if it served a visibly intoxicated person or a minor. That claim has its own rules (MCL 436.1801(3), (4)):

- Written notice must go to the business within 120 days after you hire a lawyer.
- The drunk driver must be named and kept in the suit.
- The suit must be filed within two years, not three.

See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**Sheriff’s cars, fire trucks, and buses.** Rochester Hills is policed by the Oakland County Sheriff’s Office under contract, so a crash with a patrol car in the city is a claim against Oakland County, not the City. Several other government vehicles fall under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405):

- Rochester Hills Fire Department apparatus and City vehicles
- SMART buses
- Rochester Community Schools and Avondale buses
- Oakland University vehicles, which are the State’s

## What to do after a Rochester Hills crash

1. **Call 911 and get the police report.** Michigan requires a report for any crash with injury or $1,000 in damage (MCL 257.622). Crashes in the city are worked by the Oakland County Sheriff’s Office. Freeway crashes may be worked by the Michigan State Police. **[VERIFY: report request process at the Rochester Hills substation]**
2. **Photograph everything.** Get the vehicles, the plates, the other driver’s license and insurance card, the signals, and the lane markings. Note every business with a camera facing the scene, because footage is kept for days, not weeks.
3. **Get medical care the same day.** Henry Ford Rochester Hospital, on University Drive, describes itself as a Level III adult trauma center. The most serious injuries may go to McLaren Oakland or Trinity Health Oakland in Pontiac, both Level II trauma centers, or to Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak, the county’s Level I adult trauma center. Henry Ford Rochester Hospital, on University Drive, describes itself as a Level III adult trauma center.
4. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** within the first week.
5. **Read your declarations page** for PIP level, UM/UIM, umbrella, and collision coverage.
6. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the other driver’s insurer, sign a release, or accept a policy-limits offer before your UIM insurer consents.
7. **Call a lawyer within days** if fault is disputed, the other driver’s limit looks too low, or the road played a part. The camera footage and signal data are the first evidence lost, and the 120-day notice to the City or the Road Commission is already running.

## The courts

- **Claims up to $25,000,** including mini tort claims, go to the 52nd District Court, Third Division, at 700 Barclay Circle in Rochester Hills.
- **Injury suits over $25,000** go to the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac, with a jury drawn from all of Oakland County. A crash on Dequindre, the county line, may have happened in Macomb County, which would put the case in the Sixteenth Circuit (MCL 600.1629).
- **Defect claims against MDOT, and claims against Oakland University,** go to the Court of Claims.
- **A fatal crash** is brought by the estate through the Oakland County Probate Court. See our [Rochester Hills wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death).

## Frequently asked questions

**The other driver’s insurer offered its $250,000 policy limit. Should I take it?** Not before checking your own underinsured motorist coverage and getting your insurer’s written consent to settle. Accepting the limit without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under most policies. Done in the right order, you can recover both.

**Which Rochester Hills intersections have the most crashes?** In the State Police data for 2024, Livernois at Hamlin had 60 crashes, tenth among Oakland County intersections. The city as a whole recorded 1,986 crashes, 2 deaths, and 30 serious injuries.

**I hit a deer on Tienken Road. Who pays?** Your own no-fault insurer pays your medical bills and lost wages. Comprehensive coverage usually pays for the car if you hit the deer; collision applies if you swerved into something else. There is usually no one to sue unless another driver caused the crash.

**I was hit by a Sheriff’s patrol car in Rochester Hills. Who do I claim against?** Oakland County, because the Sheriff’s Office polices the city under contract. The claim proceeds under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity. The patrol car’s video and dispatch records should be requested right away.

**A pothole caused my crash. Do I file against the City of Rochester Hills?** It depends on the road. M-59, and Rochester Road north of M-59, belong to MDOT, with written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days. Some major roads belong to the City and some to the Road Commission for Oakland County, and each requires written notice within 120 days. Campus roads belong to Oakland University, with written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days.

**I was hit while crossing at Oakland University. Who pays?** Your own household’s auto policy pays your medical bills. If you have none, the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan pays. (MCL 500.3115.) Your claim against the driver is an ordinary claim. A claim against the university itself goes to the Court of Claims, with a six-month notice.

**Where would my Rochester Hills case be filed?** Mini tort claims and claims up to $25,000 go to the 52-3 District Court at Barclay Circle. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court in Pontiac.

**How long do I have?** - One year to give your no-fault insurer written notice. - Three years to sue the at-fault driver. - Two years to sue a bar. - 120 days for a notice to the City or the Road Commission. - 120 days for a road-defect notice against MDOT or the university. - Six months for other claims against the university.
