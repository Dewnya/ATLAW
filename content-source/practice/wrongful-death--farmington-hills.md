## Farmington Hills Wrongful Death Lawyer

When someone dies in Farmington Hills because another person or company was careless, Michigan’s Wrongful Death Act (MCL 600.2922) gives the family a claim, but not in the way many families expect. The claim belongs to the estate, not to the spouse or children directly. It must be brought by a personal representative appointed by the probate court. The recovery is divided among family members by a judge. And the deadline depends on what caused the death: a crash at the I-696 interchange or on Orchard Lake Road, a truck on I-96, a driver leaving a Grand River restaurant, a death on a job site, a hospital error, a nursing home. Some of those deadlines are 120 days. This page explains how the process works for a Farmington Hills family and what to do in the first weeks.

## How a Farmington Hills wrongful death claim works

**The estate.** Only the personal representative can bring the claim (MCL 600.2922(2)). Where the person lived in Farmington Hills, the estate is opened in the Oakland County Probate Court at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac. If there is a will naming a personal representative, that person petitions for appointment; if not, a family member, usually the spouse or an adult child, petitions under Michigan’s priority rules (MCL 700.3203). The court issues letters of authority, and the personal representative then has standing to hire a lawyer for the estate and file. We file the petition as the first legal step.

**Notice to the family.** Within 30 days after filing suit, the personal representative must notify in writing every person who may share in the recovery: the spouse, children, descendants, parents, grandparents, brothers and sisters, the children of the deceased’s spouse, and anyone named in the will (MCL 600.2922(2)–(4)). The list is not limited to dependents; a parent or adult child who was not financially dependent can still share for the loss of companionship. The Act does not rank them in a fixed order of priority; the court divides the recovery according to each person’s loss. A person named in the will may share in the recovery, but does not bring the claim; only the personal representative does.

**The court.** The suit is the injury claim the person would have had, brought by the estate against whoever caused the death. Death claims are filed in circuit court, not the 47th District Court on Eleven Mile, which hears civil cases only up to $25,000. For a death in Farmington Hills that is usually the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court (Oakland County Circuit Court) at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac, on the same campus as the probate court. Where the crash was on Eight Mile at the Livonia line, or a defendant is in Wayne County, the Third Circuit in Detroit may also be a proper venue (MCL 600.1629). Claims against the State go to the Court of Claims.

**The damages** (MCL 600.2922(6)): medical and hospital expenses from the injury to the death; funeral and burial expenses; the person’s **conscious pain and suffering** between the injury and death; **loss of financial support** over a working life; and **loss of society and companionship** for each family member. Michigan places no cap on these except in medical malpractice cases. Michigan does not award punitive damages in wrongful death cases. No-fault survivor’s loss benefits, described below, are a separate insurance claim, not part of these damages.

**Comparative fault.** If the person who died was partly at fault, the damages are reduced by that share. If their fault was greater than the combined fault of everyone else, the estate cannot recover noneconomic damages, but can still recover reduced economic damages (MCL 600.2959). In an auto case, the no-fault act bars noneconomic damages at more than 50 percent fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(b)).

**The distribution hearing.** After a settlement or verdict, the personal representative proposes a division among the eligible family members, with notice to each. If they agree, the court approves it; if not, the court decides based on each person’s relationship and loss (MCL 600.2922(6)). Fees and costs are approved at the same hearing. Because a judge divides the money, no family member should sign anything with an insurer; a release signed by one relative does not settle the estate’s claim.

## The causes of Farmington Hills wrongful death claims

**Crashes** at the I-696/I-96/I-275/M-5 interchange, on Orchard Lake, Grand River, Twelve Mile, Middlebelt, and the mile roads. A wrongful death claim after a crash does not step outside no-fault: the claim against the at-fault driver still requires a death, which meets the threshold (MCL 500.3135(1)), and no-fault benefits are paid alongside it. The at-fault driver’s liability insurer pays, subject to policy limits that may be the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)); the deceased’s own underinsured motorist coverage, umbrella policies, and the policy of an employer whose driver was working are checked as well. Separately, **no-fault survivor’s loss benefits** (MCL 500.3108) are paid regardless of fault by the deceased’s own insurer, or the insurer that would have paid their PIP, for up to three years after the death: the economic support the person provided, up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each year, $20 a day in replacement services, and funeral expenses up to the policy limit, at least $1,750 and up to $5,000 (MCL 500.3107(1)(a)). For a family that lost a high earner, survivor’s loss stops at the cap, and the support above it is part of the wrongful death claim. Written notice is due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)); do not wait for the lawsuit. See our [Farmington Hills car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents/farmington-hills) and, for pedestrian deaths, our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents).

**Truck and motorcycle deaths.** A fatal crash with a tractor-trailer on I-696, I-96, or I-275 brings the federal trucking rules, the carrier’s policy of $750,000 or more, and evidence that can be overwritten within days; see our [Farmington Hills truck accident page](/personal-injury/truck-accidents/farmington-hills). A fatal motorcycle crash follows the rider priority rules; see our [Farmington Hills motorcycle accident page](/personal-injury/motorcycle-accidents/farmington-hills).

**Drunk driving.** The driver, and under the dram shop act (MCL 436.1801) the bar, restaurant, or store that served a visibly intoxicated person or a minor. The dram shop claim has its own rules: written notice to every defendant within 120 days after the family hires a lawyer, the intoxicated driver must be named and kept in the suit, and the suit must be filed within **two years**, not three. The criminal case proceeds separately; the family’s claim does not wait for it. See our [drunk driving accident page](/personal-injury/drunk-driving-accidents).

**Deaths on the job.** Farmington Hills’ office and engineering campuses, light-industrial parks, and construction sites, including the building and road work around them, are where workplace deaths happen. When a worker dies on the job, the employer owes workers’ compensation death benefits to dependents, up to 500 weeks plus burial (MCL 418.321, 418.345), and generally cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). There are two important openings:

- **A third party** whose negligence caused the death can be sued in a wrongful death action: a contractor or subcontractor on the site, a property owner, a trucking company, or the manufacturer of a machine with a missing guard or a defective safety device ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability)).
- **The employer itself** can be sued only under the narrow intentional tort exception, where the employer had actual knowledge that an injury was certain to occur and willfully disregarded it (MCL 418.131(1)).

The MIOSHA investigation file is one of the first records requested. See our [Farmington Hills workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).

**Hospital and medical negligence** at Corewell Health Farmington Hills Hospital on Grand River, Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak, Henry Ford Providence Novi, Trinity Health Livonia, or the medical offices along Twelve Mile, Grand River, and Northwestern Highway: a wrongful death claim with the malpractice rules attached, a 182-day Notice of Intent (MCL 600.2912b), an Affidavit of Merit (MCL 600.2912d), a two-year period (MCL 600.5805(8), 600.5838a), and the cap (MCL 600.1483). See our [Farmington Hills medical malpractice page](/personal-injury/medical-malpractice).

**Nursing homes and premises.** Farmington Hills has two Medicare-certified nursing homes and many assisted living and memory care communities; a death from neglect there may be ordinary negligence or malpractice, which changes the deadline (see our [nursing home abuse page](/personal-injury/nursing-home-abuse)). Deaths from falls, fires, and carbon monoxide in apartments, condominiums, and homes follow premises law and, for tenants, MCL 554.139 (see our [Farmington Hills slip and fall page](/personal-injury/slip-and-fall)).

**Deaths involving a government agency.** I-696, I-96, I-275, M-5, and Grand River are MDOT’s; Orchard Lake, Middlebelt, Inkster, Farmington Road, Halsted, Haggerty, and the mile roads, including Eight Mile, are generally the Road Commission for Oakland County’s; subdivision streets are the City of Farmington Hills’. A defect claim against the City or the Road Commission needs written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404); a road-defect claim against MDOT needs written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404); other claims against the State, such as a State vehicle, need notice within six months (MCL 600.6431). A City, Road Commission, or SMART vehicle falls under the motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405). An individual government employee is liable only for gross negligence (MCL 691.1407(2)).

## Deadlines for Farmington Hills wrongful death claims

| Claim | Deadline | Statute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crash, fall, fire, product, and premises claims | 3 years, generally from the injury that caused the death | MCL 600.5805(2), 600.5827, 600.2922 |
| Medical malpractice | Notice of Intent, then 2 years, with a 6-month discovery rule and 6-year repose | MCL 600.2912b, 600.5805(8), 600.5838a |
| Savings provision for the estate | Up to 2 years after letters of authority, but no more than 3 years after the ordinary period ran | MCL 600.5852 |
| No-fault survivor’s loss benefits | Written notice within 1 year | MCL 500.3145(1) |
| Dram shop claim | Notice within 120 days of hiring a lawyer; suit within 2 years | MCL 436.1801(3) |
| Road defect: City of Farmington Hills or the Road Commission | Written notice within 120 days | MCL 691.1404 |
| Claim against MDOT or the State | 120 days for a road or building defect; 6 months for other claims, such as a State vehicle | MCL 691.1404, 600.6431 |
| Workers’ compensation death benefits | Claim within 2 years of the death | MCL 418.381(1) |

When the injury and the death happen on the same day, the difference between “from the injury” and “from the death” does not matter. When a person lingers for weeks or months, the ordinary period may already be running before the estate exists, which is what the savings provision is for. It adds only a limited period and courts apply it strictly. Open the estate and file inside the ordinary deadline.

## What the family should do in the first weeks

1. **Get the death certificate and request the Oakland County Medical Examiner’s report**, along with the Farmington Hills Fire Department EMS run sheet and the hospital record. If the family’s customs call for prompt burial, tell the Medical Examiner’s office at once, or call us.
2. **Keep everything**: the vehicle, the phone, the helmet, the work gear, the medication bottles. Do not authorize salvage of a vehicle until a lawyer has seen it.
3. **Notify the no-fault insurer in writing** if a vehicle was involved; survivor’s loss benefits begin on the claim, not on the lawsuit.
4. **Do not give a recorded statement** to any insurer and do not sign any document, however it is described. An insurer offering to “cover the funeral” in exchange for a signature may be settling the claim for the price of a funeral.
5. **Open the estate.** The probate petition is the first legal step; the wrongful death suit cannot be filed until letters issue.
6. **Call within days** if the death involved a city, county, or state road or vehicle, a bar, or an employer. Those notice periods are short.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who can file a wrongful death lawsuit in Farmington Hills?** Only the personal representative of the estate, appointed by the Oakland County Probate Court for a Farmington Hills resident. A spouse, child, or parent can be appointed, but must be appointed before the claim can be filed.

**Who receives the money?** The spouse, children, descendants, parents, grandparents, siblings, the children of the deceased’s spouse, and anyone named in the will may share, whether or not they were financially dependent. The probate court divides the recovery according to each person’s loss after notice to each of them.

**How long do we have?** Generally three years from the injury that caused the death for crash, fall, and premises claims, with a limited extension for the estate; two years with a 182-day notice for medical malpractice; two years for a dram shop claim; one year to claim no-fault survivor’s loss benefits; and 120 days to notify the City or the Road Commission of a road defect.

**My husband died in a crash. Are survivor’s loss benefits part of the lawsuit?** No. Survivor’s loss is paid by the no-fault insurer regardless of fault, up to a monthly cap, for up to three years. The wrongful death claim against the at-fault driver is separate and covers the losses survivor’s loss does not, including support above the cap and the family’s loss of companionship.

**My mother died after a fall in a Farmington Hills assisted living community. Is that malpractice?** It depends on whether the failure was a medical judgment or ordinary care, such as leaving a fall-risk resident unattended. Malpractice carries a 182-day notice and a two-year limit; ordinary negligence carries three years. Treat it as the shorter deadline until a lawyer has reviewed the records.

**Can we get punitive damages?** Michigan does not award punitive damages in wrongful death cases. The family recovers the losses the Act lists, including loss of companionship, which has no cap outside medical malpractice.

**Is there a lawsuit if the driver was charged with a crime?** Yes. The criminal case and the family’s claim are separate. The family does not have to wait for the criminal case, and the civil deadlines keep running.
