## Sterling Heights Personal Injury Lawyer

If you were hurt in Sterling Heights because someone else was careless, you have a claim under Michigan law, and its deadlines start the day you are hurt. After a crash, your no-fault insurer must have written notice within one year. A road or sidewalk defect claim against the City of Sterling Heights or the Macomb County Department of Roads needs written notice within 120 days; a defect on Van Dyke, Hall Road, or the M-53 freeway needs written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days. A claim against a bar that served a drunk driver must be filed within two years. A malpractice suit requires 182 days’ notice first. Most other claims allow three years. A personal injury lawyer’s first job is to find out which of those apply to you; the second is to make sure none runs out before your claim is paid.

## What a Sterling Heights personal injury lawyer does

**The first week.** We calendar every deadline and send preservation letters:

- to the at-fault driver’s insurer;
- to a trucking company for its driver logs and electronic data;
- to MDOT or the Department of Roads for signal timing records;
- to SMART or the City for vehicle records;
- to a plant, store, or apartment complex for footage and incident reports, which many overwrite within days;
- to a bar for its receipts if a drunk driver was involved.

We obtain the police report (Sterling Heights Police reports through LexisNexis BuyCrash; State Police reports through michigan.gov/tcps)

**The first month.** We identify every policy that may pay: your own auto policy for no-fault benefits, household policies, the at-fault party’s liability coverage, your uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage, a property owner’s commercial policy, umbrella policies, and the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan where no auto policy exists (MCL 500.3172). For a workplace injury, we identify whether a third party, such as a contractor, a vendor’s driver, or an equipment manufacturer, caused it, because the employer itself usually cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). We file your Application for No-Fault Benefits inside the one-year window (MCL 500.3145(1)) and handle the insurer’s requests for statements, records, and examinations (MCL 500.3151). You stop talking to adjusters; we do that.

**The treatment period.** The claim’s value is set mostly by the medical record, so we make sure it is complete and the bills are paid while you treat. We collect records from Henry Ford Macomb, McLaren Macomb, Corewell Health Troy, Henry Ford Medical Center – Sterling Heights, and your own physicians and therapists, and document lost wages with your employer, including overtime and shift premiums that plant workers often lose.

**The demand, the suit, and the resolution.** When your condition has stabilized, we send a demand with the complete record and a valuation. The decision to accept any offer is yours (MRPC 1.2). If the insurer will not pay fair value, suit is filed in the right court:

- **41A District Court**, Sterling Heights division, at 40111 Dodge Park Road, for claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims.
- **Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court** at 40 N. Main Street in Mount Clemens for injury suits over $25,000. The court may send injury cases to case evaluation (MCR 2.403) or facilitation before trial.
- **Oakland County Circuit Court** where the defendant lives or does business in Oakland County, or the crash was on Dequindre at the Troy line (MCL 600.1629).

At settlement we resolve the liens, including health plans, Medicare, Medicaid, and workers’ compensation, before you are paid. Minors’ settlements and wrongful death distributions are approved by the Macomb County Probate Court or the trial court (MCR 2.420; MCL 600.2922(6)). Compensation for physical injury is generally not taxable income (26 USC 104(a)(2)).

## What a Sterling Heights claim is worth, and what reduces it

A claim includes economic damages (medical expenses, lost wages, lost earning capacity, replacement services) and noneconomic damages (pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, disfigurement). There is no cap except in medical malpractice. In an auto case, noneconomic damages require a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). Your share of fault reduces the recovery, and above 50 percent it bars noneconomic damages (MCL 600.2959; in auto cases MCL 500.3135(2)(b)).

In many Sterling Heights cases the practical limit is insurance: the at-fault driver’s policy, often the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)); your own underinsured motorist coverage; and, in a premises or workplace case, the commercial policy of the property owner or contractor. Where UIM is involved, settling with the at-fault driver without your own insurer’s consent can forfeit the UIM claim. For a plant worker with a third-party claim, the workers’ compensation insurer has a lien on the recovery (MCL 418.827), and how that lien is negotiated changes what you take home.

## What it costs

Michigan caps contingency fees in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery (MCR 8.121). Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted from the recovery first; the one-third fee is calculated on the remaining amount. You pay nothing up front and nothing if there is no recovery. The agreement is in writing; read how costs (filing fees, records, experts, depositions) are handled if the case is lost, because that is where firms differ.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in Sterling Heights?** Three years for most claims. But you have one year to notify your no-fault insurer after a crash, 120 days to notify the City or the Department of Roads of a defect, 120 days to file notice of a defect on a state road, two years to sue a bar, and a 182-day notice before any malpractice suit.

**What does a Sterling Heights personal injury lawyer charge?** Michigan caps the fee in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery, with no fee if there is no recovery. Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted only from a recovery.

**Where would my Sterling Heights case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000 in the 41A District Court on Dodge Park Road; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court in Mount Clemens, or in some Dequindre cases the Oakland County Circuit Court.

**I was hurt at a plant in Sterling Heights. Do I need a workers’ comp lawyer or an injury lawyer?** Possibly both claims. Workers’ compensation pays your benefits regardless of fault, and your employer usually cannot be sued. If a contractor, a vendor’s driver, or a defective machine caused the injury, you also have an injury claim against that party, and the comp insurer is repaid from it.

**Do I need a lawyer whose office is in Sterling Heights?** No. What matters is that the lawyer knows the courts your case will be in, the road agencies, employers, and insurers involved, and will meet you where you are.

**How long will my case take?** A no-fault benefits claim can resolve in months. A disputed injury claim in the Sixteenth Circuit often takes a year or two from filing.

**The insurer already offered me money. Should I take it?** Not before you know what the claim is worth and what the release gives up, and not before your UIM insurer has consented if UIM may apply.
