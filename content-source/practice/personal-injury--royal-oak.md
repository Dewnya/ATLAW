## Royal Oak, MI Personal Injury Lawyer

If you were hurt in Royal Oak because someone else was careless, you have a claim under Michigan law, and its deadlines start the day you are hurt:

- **After a crash,** your no-fault insurer must have written notice within one year.
- **A claim against a bar** that served a drunk driver needs written notice within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, and suit within two years.
- A road or sidewalk defect needs written notice within 120 days to the City of Royal Oak or the Road Commission for Oakland County. A defect on Woodward or I-696 needs written notice filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days.
- **An injury in a City building, a City parking structure, a school, or a county building** needs written notice to that agency within 120 days.
- **A medical malpractice suit,** including claims about care at the hospital and many claims against nursing homes, requires 182 days’ notice first and must be filed within two years.
- **Most other claims** allow three years.

A personal injury lawyer’s first job is to find out which of those deadlines apply to you. The second is to make sure none runs out before your claim is paid.

## Who the defendant is in Royal Oak

Royal Oak is a city with its own police and fire departments, a downtown of bars and venues, City parking structures, and roads split among three agencies. Each of those changes who you claim against.

- **The bar that served the drunk driver.** A bar, restaurant, or store that served a visibly intoxicated person, or a minor, is liable for the injuries that person causes (MCL 436.1801). The claim has strict rules: written notice within 120 days after you hire a lawyer, the drunk driver named and kept in the suit, and suit within two years. The bar’s liquor liability policy is a second source of recovery. Every bar must carry at least $50,000 in coverage (MCL 436.1803(1)).
- **Venues and bars, for what happens inside.** A patron hurt in a fight, a crowd surge, or a fall at a bar, club, or concert venue may have a premises or negligent-security claim against the operator. That requires showing the operator knew of a specific risk and failed to respond reasonably.
- **The roads are split three ways.** MDOT owns Woodward, I-696, and I-75, with written notice of a road defect filed with the Court of Claims within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). The Road Commission for Oakland County owns Ten, Twelve, and Fourteen Mile, and the City of Royal Oak owns the other streets, including Eleven and Thirteen Mile. A claim against the Road Commission or the City requires written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1402, 691.1404), and the notice must go to the agency that has jurisdiction.
- **Sidewalks: the City and possibly the adjacent owner.** The City must keep public sidewalks in reasonable repair (MCL 691.1402a). A fall on a broken public sidewalk is a claim against the City, with three limits: the City must have known, or should have known, of the defect at least 30 days before the injury; a defect of less than two inches is presumed not to be actionable; and written notice is due within 120 days. Royal Oak’s ordinance puts sidewalk repair on the adjacent property owner (City Code § 650-5), but that duty runs to the City and does not by itself make the owner liable to a pedestrian (*Levendoski v Geisenhaver*, Mich 1965).
- **City parking structures and buildings.** An injury caused by a dangerous or defective condition in a City-owned parking structure or building falls under the public building exception. That exception requires that the City knew of the defect and failed to fix it, and it requires written notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1406). A privately owned structure or lot is an ordinary premises claim with a three-year deadline.
- **Schools, the college, and county buildings.** The public building exception and the 120-day notice also apply to injuries in buildings belonging to Royal Oak Schools, Oakland Community College’s Royal Oak campus, and Oakland County.
- **Police and fire are the City’s.** A crash with a Royal Oak Police car, a fire truck, or an ambulance is a claim against the City, under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405). EMS treatment claims are limited to gross negligence or willful misconduct (MCL 333.20965).
- **Buses.** A crash with a SMART bus on Woodward or at the Royal Oak Transit Center is a claim under the motor vehicle exception. Royal Oak Schools’ buses are run by a private contractor, so a claim over one of them is an ordinary negligence claim. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).
- **The Detroit Zoo.** The Zoo sits at the city’s southern tip. It is operated by the Detroit Zoological Society on land the City of Detroit owns, so who is sued for an injury there depends on what caused it.
- **Private property owners** are ordinary defendants, with a three-year deadline. That includes stores, restaurants, apartment buildings, and the new mixed-use buildings downtown. A private landlord must keep the premises and common areas in reasonable repair (MCL 554.139). Since *Kandil-Elsayed v F & E Oil* (2023), an “open and obvious” hazard goes to comparative fault rather than ending the claim.

## When the other driver has no insurance, or not enough

In a serious Royal Oak crash, the at-fault driver’s liability limit may be less than the claim is worth. If you carry **underinsured motorist coverage**, your own insurer pays the difference up to your UIM limit. The sequence matters:

1. The at-fault driver’s insurer offers its limit.
2. You notify your own insurer and ask for its written consent to settle.
3. Only then do you accept the limit and sign the release.
4. The UIM claim then proceeds.

Accepting without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under most policies.

Downtown traffic comes from across the region, and some drivers carry only the $50,000 opt-down minimum (MCL 500.3009(5)) or nothing. Against an uninsured driver, the claim for pain and suffering depends on **uninsured motorist coverage**. A hit-and-run is usually a UM claim, with short notice terms in the policy. Two rules cut the other way:

- An owner who was required to insure a car and did not is generally barred from PIP benefits (MCL 500.3113(b)).
- A driver who was operating their own uninsured vehicle cannot recover pain-and-suffering damages, whoever was at fault (MCL 500.3135(2)(c))

## What a Royal Oak claim is worth, and what reduces it

A claim includes two kinds of damages:

- **Economic damages:** medical expenses, lost wages, lost earning capacity, and replacement services.
- **Noneconomic damages:** pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, and disfigurement.

There is no cap except in medical malpractice. In an auto case, noneconomic damages require a threshold injury: death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement (MCL 500.3135(1)). A premises, dram shop, or dog bite case has no such threshold.

Your share of fault reduces the recovery, and above 50 percent it bars noneconomic damages (MCL 600.2959; in auto cases, MCL 500.3135(2)(b)). At exactly 50 percent, you recover half. Where more than one party caused the injury, each defendant generally pays only its own share of fault (MCL 600.2956, 600.6304). That is why the bar, the driver, and any property owner all have to be identified and named. Michigan does not award punitive damages in ordinary injury cases. Exemplary damages for humiliation or outrage are available only in narrow circumstances.

The practical limit is often insurance:

- **The at-fault driver’s limit** defaults to $250,000/$500,000 unless the driver chose lower limits, down to the $50,000/$100,000 minimum (MCL 500.3009(1)).
- **Your UIM and umbrella coverage** fill the gap.
- **In a dram shop case,** the bar’s liquor liability policy adds to it.
- **In a premises, workplace, or nursing home case,** the commercial policy of the property owner, contractor, or nursing home operator pays.

For a worker with a third-party claim, the workers’ compensation insurer has a lien on the recovery (MCL 418.827). How that lien is negotiated changes what you take home. Medicare, Medicaid, and health plan liens are negotiated the same way.

## What it costs

Michigan caps contingency fees in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery (MCR 8.121). Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted from the recovery first; the one-third fee is calculated on the remaining amount.

The agreement is in writing. Read how costs are handled if the case is lost, because that is where firms differ: filing fees, records, experts, and depositions. A promise that “you pay nothing unless we win” should say whether that includes costs, and whether “win” means the same thing as “settle.”

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in Royal Oak?** Three years for most claims. Several deadlines are much shorter: - one year to notify your no-fault insurer after a crash - 120 days for a dram shop notice to a bar after you hire a lawyer, and two years to sue it - 120 days to notify the City or the Road Commission of a defect - 120 days for a road-defect notice on Woodward or I-696 - a 182-day notice and a two-year limit for malpractice

**A drunk driver who had been at a downtown bar hit me. Who pays?** Your own no-fault insurer pays your medical bills and lost wages. Your claim for pain and suffering is against the driver and, if the bar served the driver while visibly intoxicated, against the bar too. The bar’s policy is a second source of recovery. Act fast, because the 120-day notice and the bar’s records are on short clocks.

**I fell in a downtown parking structure. Who is responsible?** It depends on who owns it. In a City-owned structure, the claim is against the City under the public building exception, with written notice within 120 days. In a privately owned structure, it is an ordinary premises claim with a three-year deadline.

**I tripped on a broken sidewalk in Royal Oak. Is that the City’s fault?** Possibly the City’s, under the two-inch rule with 120-day notice. The adjacent owner must repair the sidewalk under Royal Oak’s ordinance, but that does not by itself make the owner liable to you. Photograph the defect with something that shows its height.

**A Royal Oak police car crashed into me. Who do I claim against?** The City of Royal Oak, under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity. Request the patrol car’s video and dispatch records right away.

**What does a Royal Oak personal injury attorney charge?** Michigan caps the fee in injury cases at one-third of the net recovery, with no fee if there is no recovery. Case costs are advanced by the firm and deducted only from a recovery.

**Where would my Royal Oak case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000 go to the 44th District Court on Eleven Mile. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court in Pontiac. Claims against MDOT go to the Court of Claims.

**If I was partly at fault, do I still have a claim?** Yes, unless you were more than 50 percent at fault, which bars pain-and-suffering damages. Below that, your recovery is reduced by your share, and at exactly 50 percent you recover half. No-fault benefits are paid regardless of fault.

**Do I need a lawyer whose office is in Royal Oak?** No. What matters is that the lawyer knows the courts your case will be in, the agencies, insurers, and property owners involved, and will meet you where you are.
