## Where Warren truck crashes happen

**Mound Road and the plant gates.** Mound is the plant corridor, rebuilt north of I-696 in the Innovate Mound project, finished in December 2023; the stretch from Eight Mile to I-696 is still awaiting funding. When the gate layout, the plant’s traffic control, or a delivery schedule that stacks trucks in the road contributed, the plant or its logistics provider may share responsibility with the carrier. **[ATTORNEY CHECK: premises owner liability for off-site queuing]**

**Van Dyke and Groesbeck.** Through-freight and deliveries on eight lanes of Van Dyke past Warren Truck and the Tech Center, with the 11 Mile intersection second on the state’s list for all crashes; and Groesbeck’s diagonal across the east side, where skewed intersections at the mile roads make wide turns by trucks harder to judge.

**I-696.** Interchanges at Dequindre, Mound, Van Dyke, Hoover, and Schoenherr within about six miles. Merging trucks, lane changes into a car’s blind spot, and rear-end crashes into stopped traffic at the back of a queue. The Macomb stretch was rebuilt in 2018–2019. MDOT’s current work is in Oakland County, with work from I-75 to Dequindre planned for 2027.

**Eight Mile and the mile roads.** Freight along the Detroit line and box trucks serving the retail and industrial parcels along Nine through Fourteen Mile: turning and backing crashes that injure drivers of smaller vehicles, cyclists, and pedestrians (see our [pedestrian accident page](/personal-injury/pedestrian-accidents)).

**Whose road it is.** Van Dyke, Groesbeck, Eight Mile, and I-696 are MDOT’s; a road-defect claim against the State goes to the Court of Claims, with written notice filed there within 120 days (MCL 691.1404). Mound and many of the mile roads, such as 10 Mile and 14 Mile, are the Macomb County Department of Roads', and other streets the City of Warren’s, also with 120 days' notice; the City’s Engineering Division confirms which agency has a given street.

## Who is responsible, and who pays

**The carrier, not just the driver.** The motor carrier is responsible for its drivers’ compliance with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations and is liable for a driver’s negligence in the course of employment. Michigan applies the federal rules to intrastate carriers (MCL 480.11a), so a local parts hauler running between suppliers is held to largely the same standards as an interstate carrier. MCL 480.11a adopts the federal parts for intrastate carriers. MCL 480.12d keeps a few intrastate differences, such as an 18-year-old minimum age and State medical waivers. The rules that decide truck cases most often:

| Rule | 49 CFR | What it means for the case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hours of service | Part 395 | 11 hours driving in a 14-hour window after 10 off; 60/70-hour weekly limits; recorded on an electronic logging device. Waiting time at a plant gate counts as on-duty time. |
| Driver qualification | Part 391 | CDL, medical certificate, driving record, background check; negligent hiring and retention are claims against the carrier. |
| Post-crash testing | Part 382 | Alcohol within 8 hours and drugs within 32 after a fatal crash, or an injury or tow-away crash with a citation. |
| Inspection and maintenance | Part 396 | Pre-trip inspections, defect reports, maintenance records; brake and tire cases are maintenance cases. |
| Cargo securement | Part 393 | Securement standards; shifted steel coils, stamped parts, and racks cause rollovers and spilled loads. |
| Handheld phone ban; hazardous conditions | 392.82, 392.14 | No handheld phone use; reduce speed or stop in snow, ice, and fog. |
| Minimum insurance | Part 387 | $750,000 general freight; $1 million oil; $5 million hazardous materials. |

**The owner of the truck or trailer.** In Michigan, a vehicle’s owner is liable for its negligent operation by someone driving with the owner’s consent (MCL 257.401). When the tractor, the trailer, and the driver belong to different companies, which is common in plant freight, each may be a defendant, although a company in the business of renting or leasing vehicles is generally not liable for the lessee’s driver unless it was itself negligent (49 USC 30106).

**Michigan no-fault still applies to you.** Your own auto insurer pays your medical bills, 85 percent of lost wages up to the monthly cap DIFS sets each October, replacement services, and attendant care regardless of fault (MCL 500.3107), with written notice due within one year (MCL 500.3145(1)). The Michigan Assigned Claims Plan pays if no household policy applies (MCL 500.3172). Your claim against the driver and the carrier for pain and suffering and losses beyond no-fault requires a threshold injury (MCL 500.3135) and must be filed within three years (MCL 600.5805(2)). The difference from a car case is the money available: the carrier’s $750,000-plus policy, often with excess coverage above it. See our [no-fault insurance claim page](/personal-injury/car-accident-insurance).

**Other defendants.** The shipper or broker that loaded the trailer or hired an unsafe carrier; the plant or logistics provider whose gate operations contributed; the maintenance contractor; the manufacturer of a failed component ([product liability](/personal-injury/product-liability)); and, for a government truck (City of Warren, the Department of Roads, MDOT, SMART), the agency under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity (MCL 691.1405). A crash with a vehicle driven by a federal employee from the Arsenal is a claim against the United States under the Federal Tort Claims Act, with an administrative claim due within two years (28 USC 2401(b), 2675). A truck driven by a private defense contractor is not a federal vehicle; the contractor is sued directly. **[ATTORNEY CHECK: FTCA and contractor distinction]**

**If you were the truck driver.** Workers’ compensation pays your benefits, and your employer usually cannot be sued (MCL 418.131). If you were injured in your employer’s truck, no-fault benefits generally come from the insurer of that truck (MCL 500.3114(3)), coordinated with comp, which is paid first (MCL 500.3109(1)). You can also sue a third party, such as another driver or a plant whose gate operation caused the crash. See our [Warren workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation/warren).

## The evidence, and how fast it disappears

Carriers must keep hours-of-service records for six months (49 CFR 395.8) and driver inspection reports for three months (49 CFR 396.11); after that they may lawfully be destroyed. Plant-gate cameras, MDOT freeway cameras, business cameras along Mound and Van Dyke, and dash cameras overwrite in days to weeks. The truck’s engine control module and event data recorder can be cleared when it is repaired and returned to service.

The first act in a Warren truck case is a **preservation letter**: to the carrier, its insurer, the tractor and trailer owners, the shipper or broker, and, where the crash happened at or near a plant, the plant and its logistics provider. It demands the ELD and engine data, dash camera footage, the driver’s qualification file and post-crash test results, dispatch records and bills of lading, the gate’s appointment and arrival logs, maintenance files, and the truck itself for inspection. A party that destroys evidence after receiving the letter faces spoliation sanctions. **[ATTORNEY CHECK]**  **[CONFIRM: firm’s preservation-letter practice]**

## What to do after a Warren truck crash

1. **Call 911 and get medical care the same day.** Henry Ford Warren Hospital on Twelve Mile (formerly Ascension Macomb-Oakland, Warren campus), Henry Ford Macomb, and McLaren Macomb treat many of the area’s injured. **[CONFIRM: hospital list and trauma designations]**
2. **Photograph the truck**: the cab, the trailer, the USDOT number and carrier name on the door, both plates, the load, and the gate or driveway if the truck was entering or leaving one.
3. **Get the police report.** Warren Police or the Michigan State Police respond; a Warren Police UD-10 is purchased through CLEMIS, and a State Police UD-10 at michigan.gov/tcps. A serious crash may produce a separate MSP Commercial Vehicle Enforcement inspection report.
4. **Do not speak to the carrier’s insurer or its investigator.**
5. **Report to your own insurer and file the Application for No-Fault Benefits** within the first week.
6. **Call a lawyer within days**, so the preservation letter goes out before the records expire.

## The courts and deadlines

Most Warren truck cases are filed in the Sixteenth Judicial Circuit Court at 40 N. Main Street in Mount Clemens. Cases against out-of-state carriers may be filed in or removed to the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan, and claims against the United States are heard there. A crash on Eight Mile or Dequindre, or with a Wayne or Oakland County defendant, may allow filing in those counties (MCL 600.1629). Claims against the State go to the Court of Claims; smaller claims go to the 37th District Court on Common Road. A fatal crash is brought by the estate; see our [Warren wrongful death page](/personal-injury/wrongful-death/warren).

## Frequently asked questions

**How many truck accidents happen in Warren?** Countywide, 1,257 heavy trucks and buses were involved in Macomb County crashes in 2024.

**A truck stopped in the lane outside a plant gate and I hit it. Is that my fault?** Not necessarily. Following too closely is usually the claim against the rear driver, but a truck stopped in a travel lane without warning, or a plant whose gate operation routinely stacks trucks in the road, can carry fault too. The gate logs, the cameras, and the truck’s data decide it.

**Who pays my medical bills after a truck crash in Warren?** Your own no-fault insurer, regardless of fault, after you file an Application for No-Fault Benefits; the Assigned Claims Plan if no household policy applies. The carrier’s insurer pays for pain and suffering and losses beyond no-fault once fault and a threshold injury are established.

**I drive a truck for a Warren supplier and was hurt in a crash. What are my claims?** Workers’ compensation through your employer, no-fault benefits coordinated with comp, and a lawsuit against any third party who caused the crash.

**Why does the lawyer need to act within days?** Because the records expire: six months for hours-of-service logs, three months for inspection reports, days or weeks for camera footage, and the truck’s electronic data can be cleared on repair.

**Is a truck case worth more than a car case?** The injuries are usually worse and the insurance is usually larger: a $750,000 minimum for a general freight carrier, against $50,000 for a minimally insured car.
