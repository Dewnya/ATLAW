## Oakland County Personal Injury Lawyers

Oakland County is Michigan’s second most populous county, about 1.27 million people in 62 cities, villages, and townships, and the state’s second-busiest county for traffic injuries. In 2024, according to the Michigan State Police, 35,951 crashes were reported in Oakland County; 9,139 people were injured in them, 509 seriously, and 68 were killed. The county’s freeway network, its 1,400 lakes, its concentration of hospitals and senior-care facilities, and one of the largest county road agencies in the country each shape the injury claims that come out of it.

## Where Oakland County injuries happen

**The freeways.** I-75 from the Detroit line through Troy, Auburn Hills, and Clarkston, rebuilt between Eight Mile and M-59 in a modernization project that finished in 2023; I-696 across the south of the county from Southfield to St. Clair Shores; I-96 and I-275 through Novi and Farmington Hills; M-59 (Highland Road and Hall Road) across the center; M-10 (the Lodge) into Southfield; and M-5 toward Commerce. Major interchanges in Oakland County include I-75/I-696, I-75/M-59 in Auburn Hills, I-696/Telegraph, and I-696/Woodward. These busy junctions present merging, lane-changing, and congestion-related driving risks.

**The arterials.** Telegraph Road (US-24) from Southfield to Pontiac, Woodward Avenue (M-1) from Ferndale to Pontiac, Orchard Lake Road, Big Beaver and Maple in Troy and Birmingham, 12 Mile and 14 Mile, Grand River, Haggerty, Rochester Road, Lapeer Road (M-24), and Dixie Highway. These are the intersection, left-turn, and rear-end crashes, and the pedestrian crashes in the downtowns of Royal Oak, Ferndale, Birmingham, and Pontiac.

**The lakes and the trails.** Oakland County has more inland lakes than any county in Michigan, and boating, jet-ski, and dock injuries on Cass, Orchard, Walled, Union, and Lake Orion produce claims under Michigan’s Marine Safety Act (MCL 324.80101 and following) and general negligence law; snowmobile and ORV injuries have their own statutes. The county’s paved trail network and the Paint Creek and Clinton River trails produce bicycle and pedestrian injuries that are not motor-vehicle cases and fall outside no-fault.

**The senior-care facilities.** Oakland County has a large concentration of nursing homes, assisted-living facilities, and memory-care units. Falls, pressure injuries, medication errors, and neglect in those facilities are a steady source of claims; see our [nursing home abuse page](/personal-injury/nursing-home-abuse/oakland-county).

**The hospitals.** The county’s hospitals are high-volume, and medical negligence claims arising from care in them are governed by Michigan’s medical malpractice statutes, including the 182-day notice of intent (MCL 600.2912b) and the 2026 noneconomic damage caps of $596,400 and $1,065,000 (MCL 600.1483); see our [medical malpractice page](/personal-injury/medical-malpractice/oakland-county).

## What is different about an Oakland County claim

**More insurance, more often.** The amount of insurance available after an Oakland County crash depends on the policies involved, including the at-fault driver’s liability limits and any uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage carried by the injured person. Those coverage choices change the arithmetic: a serious-injury claim may involve liability coverage above the $50,000 per person statutory minimum under MCL 500.3009(1), and underinsured motorist coverage may provide additional protection when the at-fault driver’s insurance is insufficient. It does not eliminate the problem; minimum-limits and uninsured drivers are on every Oakland County road, and the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MCL 500.3172) still handles Oakland claims every week. See our [uninsured and underinsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**The Road Commission for Oakland County.** Unlike Wayne County, where the county’s public services department and the City of Detroit maintain most roads, Oakland County’s county roads are maintained by a separate agency, the Road Commission for Oakland County (RCOC), which maintains about 2,700 miles of road, one of the largest county road systems in the United States. A claim for a road defect on a county road is a claim against the RCOC under the highway exception to governmental immunity, MCL 691.1402, with written notice within 120 days under 691.1404; state trunklines (I-75, M-59, Telegraph, Woodward) fall under MDOT’s jurisdiction, and qualifying highway-defect claims generally require notice within 120 days under MCL 691.1404, with the notice filed in the Court of Claims as prescribed by the statute; and city streets belong to the 62 municipalities. Sidewalk falls are governed by the two-inch rule in 691.1402a. Identifying the right agency early is often decisive.

**SMART and school buses.** SMART operates the fixed-route and paratransit buses in Oakland County; Oakland County has no city-owned transit system comparable to DDOT. A claim for a SMART bus crash proceeds under the motor vehicle exception to governmental immunity, MCL 691.1405. School bus crashes are claims against the school district under the same exception. See our [bus accident page](/personal-injury/bus-accidents).

**Venue.** Under MCL 600.1629, venue for a personal injury lawsuit follows a statutory order of priority, generally beginning with the county where the original injury occurred and where a defendant resides, conducts business, or maintains a registered office. A crash in Troy between two Oakland County residents goes to the Sixth Circuit in Pontiac; a crash in Southfield involving a Detroit driver requires applying the statutory venue hierarchy rather than assuming either Oakland or Wayne County can be chosen freely. Where the case is filed affects the jury pool, the judge, the court’s scheduling, and the facilitation practices, and it is a decision made with the client, not by default.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many car accidents happen in Oakland County?** 35,951 in 2024, according to the Michigan State Police, with 9,139 people injured and 68 killed, about 12 percent of Michigan’s crashes.

**Where would my Oakland County injury lawsuit be filed?** Most are filed in the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac, which hears civil cases over $25,000 from all 62 Oakland County communities. Smaller claims go to the district court for the community where the defendant lives or the injury occurred. Some cases can be filed in Wayne or Macomb instead, depending on where the defendant lives or does business.

**I was hurt on a county road in Oakland County because of a pothole or a missing sign. Who do I claim against?** Probably the Road Commission for Oakland County, which maintains county roads, under the highway exception to governmental immunity, and written notice must reach it within 120 days of the injury. If the road was a state trunkline such as M-59, Telegraph, or Woodward, a qualifying highway-defect claim against MDOT generally requires notice within 120 days under MCL 691.1404, filed as required for claims against the state. A lawyer can identify the agency from the location in a day.

**Do you have an office in Oakland County?** Our office is in Dearborn. We serve clients throughout Oakland County and offer consultations by phone or video. Home or hospital visits can also be accommodated when needed.

**I was injured at a nursing home in Oakland County. Is that a medical malpractice case?** Sometimes. A nursing-home injury may involve ordinary negligence or medical malpractice, depending on the conduct alleged. Under Bryant v. Oakpointe Villa Nursing Centre, Inc. (2004), the distinction depends on whether evaluating the challenged conduct requires professional medical judgment beyond ordinary knowledge and experience. Ordinary negligence generally has a three-year filing deadline, while medical malpractice generally has a two-year deadline and additional procedural requirements, including pre-suit notice. Many cases involve both, and the distinction has been litigated repeatedly in Michigan; see our [nursing home abuse page](/personal-injury/nursing-home-abuse/oakland-county).

**The other driver had a high-limit policy. Does that mean my case is worth more?** It means the ceiling is higher. The value still depends on the injury, whether it meets the serious-impairment threshold in MCL 500.3135, your share of fault, and the medical record. A large policy is paid in full only when the injury justifies it.

**How much does it cost to hire you?** Nothing up front, and no fee unless we recover money for you. Michigan caps contingency fees in personal injury cases at one-third of the recovery (MCR 8.121).

**How long do I have?** Three years for most injury suits, one year to notify your no-fault insurer in writing, and as little as 120 days for a claim against the RCOC or a city. See the table above.
