## Personal Injury Law Firm Serving West Bloomfield, MI

West Bloomfield is a charter township of about 66,500 people in the lake country of central Oakland County, with Pine, Orchard, Cass, Walnut, Union, and a dozen smaller lakes inside or along its borders, Henry Ford West Bloomfield Hospital on Maple, and the Orchard Lake Road retail corridor running north through its center. Its roads are two-lane and five-lane county arterials winding between the lakes: Orchard Lake, Maple, Fourteen Mile, Lone Pine, Long Lake, Walnut Lake, Commerce, Pontiac Trail, Haggerty, Farmington, Middlebelt, Inkster, Drake, and Halsted. And it is a township, which means it owns none of those roads. In 2024, West Bloomfield Township recorded 1,223 traffic crashes, with 317 people injured and no traffic fatalities.

Those facts shape a West Bloomfield injury claim. The lakes produce boating, dock, and winter injuries that no-fault does not cover. In serious crash cases, available liability insurance and the client’s own underinsured motorist coverage may both affect recovery. And the township’s lack of road jurisdiction means a signal, sight-line, or pavement claim goes to the Road Commission for Oakland County or MDOT, with a 120-day notice requirement for qualifying highway-defect claims. The responsible agency must be identified by the location and type of defect; West Bloomfield Township may also have liability for its own facilities, vehicles, and qualifying sidewalks.

## What is different about a West Bloomfield claim

**Coverage, and sequencing.** Michigan’s default bodily-injury liability limits are $250,000 per person and $500,000 per accident under MCL 500.3009(1), although drivers may select lower limits. Some drivers also carry umbrella policies or underinsured motorist coverage. The at-fault driver’s liability policy and any applicable umbrella coverage may provide compensation. The client’s own UIM coverage may provide additional recovery, subject to policy limits, offsets, exhaustion provisions, consent-to-settle requirements, and other conditions. Settling without obtaining consent when the UIM policy requires it can jeopardize the UIM claim. An insurer determined under Michigan’s no-fault priority rules may pay covered medical expenses and wage-loss benefits regardless of fault, subject to applicable coverage, limits, and exclusions (MCL 500.3107); the claim against the at-fault driver requires a threshold injury (MCL 500.3135). See our [West Bloomfield car accident page](/personal-injury/auto-accidents), [no-fault insurance guide](/resources/michigan-no-fault-guide), and [uninsured motorist page](/personal-injury/uninsured-motorist-claims).

**The roads are not the township’s.** Under Michigan law, townships generally do not have jurisdiction over public roads; West Bloomfield’s public county roads are under the Road Commission for Oakland County, while nearby M-5 and Telegraph are MDOT routes. A crash caused by a defect in the traveled roadway may implicate the highway exception to governmental immunity, MCL 691.1402. Signal malfunctions and sight obstructions require separate legal analysis. For a defect on an RCOC road, notice must reach the responsible road agency rather than relying on notice to the township.

**The township’s own agencies.** West Bloomfield Police and Fire, the township’s buildings, parks, and lake access sites, and its vehicles. A crash involving a township-owned vehicle may fall under the motor vehicle exception (MCL 691.1405); an injury caused by a qualifying defect in a township public building may fall under the public building exception (MCL 691.1406), with a 120-day notice requirement. Crashes involving school buses from West Bloomfield, Walled Lake, Farmington, Bloomfield Hills, and Birmingham schools, or Road Commission trucks, may implicate MCL 691.1405 when its requirements are satisfied.

**The 48th District and the Sixth Circuit.** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims for vehicle damage (MCL 500.3135(3)(e), up to $3,000), are filed in the 48th District Court at 4280 Telegraph Road in Bloomfield Township, which serves West Bloomfield, Bloomfield Township, Bloomfield Hills, Birmingham, Keego Harbor, Orchard Lake Village, and Sylvan Lake. Injury suits over $25,000 go to the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court at 1200 N. Telegraph Road in Pontiac. Wrongful death cases may require estate proceedings in Oakland County Probate Court, while the lawsuit itself proceeds in the court with jurisdiction. Settlements involving minors require court approval under MCR 2.420.

**The hospitals.** Henry Ford West Bloomfield Hospital on Maple treats injured West Bloomfield residents; Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital in Royal Oak, the county’s Level I trauma center, and Corewell Health Farmington Hills also treat patients with serious injuries.

## Frequently asked questions

**The other driver’s insurer offered their $500,000 policy limit. Should I take it?** Not before checking for an umbrella policy above it and your own underinsured motorist coverage, and getting your insurer’s written consent to settle. Accepting the liability limit without consent can forfeit the UIM claim under most policies. A lawyer can sequence the claims so you recover from each layer.

**I was hurt because of a bad signal or pothole in West Bloomfield. Do I sue the township?** Usually not for a defect in a county-maintained road. West Bloomfield Township does not own or maintain its public county-road network. Nearby M-5 and Telegraph are MDOT routes; qualifying state highway-defect claims generally require written notice within 120 days, filed in the Court of Claims. For a qualifying defect on an RCOC road, notice must be directed to the responsible road agency.

**I was injured in a boating accident on Pine Lake, Orchard Lake, or Cass Lake. Is that a personal injury case?** Yes. Boat operators generally owe a duty to operate with reasonable care, the Marine Safety Act sets operating rules whose violation is evidence of negligence, and available insurance may include watercraft liability, homeowners, or club coverage, depending on the policy. No-fault does not apply, so the claim is a straight negligence claim with a three-year deadline; if the operator was intoxicated, a licensed bar or club that unlawfully served a visibly intoxicated person may also be liable under Michigan’s dram shop law, which has a special 120-day notice requirement.

**I was hurt in a crash at the Orchard Lake Road roundabout. Who is at fault?** Usually the driver entering, who must yield to traffic already in the roundabout, but these crashes are often disputed, and the yield signs, the lane markings, and the sight lines matter. Photograph the roundabout, get the witnesses, and ask for any nearby camera footage within days.

**Where would my West Bloomfield injury case be filed?** Claims up to $25,000, including mini tort claims, in the 48th District Court on Telegraph in Bloomfield Township; injury suits over $25,000 in the Sixth Judicial Circuit Court in Pontiac.

**My parent was injured in a West Bloomfield senior facility. Is that malpractice or negligence?** It depends on who did what. A fall during a transfer or a pressure injury from inadequate turning may involve ordinary negligence or medical malpractice, depending on whether the alleged failure requires professional medical judgment. Ordinary negligence generally has a three-year deadline; medical malpractice generally has a two-year deadline and requires advance notice of intent. Treat the shorter deadline as the real one.

**How much does it cost to hire you?** Nothing up front, and no fee unless we recover money for you. Michigan caps contingency fees in personal injury cases at one-third of the recovery (MCR 8.121).

**How long do I have?** Three years for most injury suits, one year to notify your no-fault insurer in writing, and as little as 120 days for a claim against the Road Commission or the township.
