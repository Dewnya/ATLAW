## Southfield Social Security Disability Lawyer

About two in three first applications for Social Security Disability are denied, and many of the people denied are in fact disabled. They are denied because the application did not answer the question Social Security actually asks: not “are you sick?” but “can you do any job that exists in significant numbers in the national economy, given your age, education, and work history?” For a Southfield claimant, the answer often turns on what kind of work they did. Southfield is an office city: insurance, health care administration, engineering, auto supplier offices, and call centers along Northwestern Highway, Telegraph, and the Town Center. A claimant whose past work was sedentary has a harder path at the last two steps of the test than one who did heavy labor, because Social Security will ask whether they can still do a desk job. Winning that claim means proving, with medical evidence, why they cannot.

## The two programs

**Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI)** is for people who worked and paid Social Security taxes: generally 40 work credits, 20 of them in the ten years before disability, fewer for younger workers. The benefit is based on your earnings record, with Medicare after 24 months of entitlement. Because the benefit tracks earnings, a Southfield professional with a long, well-paid career often has a substantial SSDI benefit, and the back pay matters accordingly. See our [SSDI page](/disability/social-security-disability-insurance).

**Supplemental Security Income (SSI)** is for people with little income and few assets, whether or not they worked: $994 a month for an individual in 2026 and $1,029 in 2027, reduced by other income, with a $2,000 asset limit (home and one vehicle excluded) and Michigan Medicaid. Some claimants qualify for both. See our [SSI page](/disability/supplemental-security-income).

## The five-step test, as it applies to a Southfield claim

Social Security uses a five-step sequential evaluation (20 CFR 404.1520), deciding the claim at the first step where the answer is clear.

1. **Are you working above the substantial gainful activity level?** Earning more than $1,690 a month in 2026 ($1,770 in 2027) generally ends the claim at step one. Part-time or accommodated work below that level does not.
2. **Is your impairment severe and expected to last 12 months or result in death?** A diagnosis is not enough; the record must show functional limits.
3. **Does your condition meet or equal a Listing?** Meeting one wins the claim here. See our [qualifying impairments page](/disability/qualifying-impairments); some conditions are fast-tracked under [Compassionate Allowances](/disability/compassionate-allowances).
4. **Can you do your past relevant work?** Social Security assesses your residual functional capacity (RFC), what you can still do despite your limitations, and compares it to the jobs you held in the past five years. If you did a desk job, a physical limitation alone may not end your ability to do it. The claim then depends on limits that affect any job: the need to lie down or take unscheduled breaks, absences from treatment or flare-ups, the inability to sit for long periods, limits on using the hands for typing, or concentration and memory deficits from pain, medication, or a mental health condition.
5. **Can you do any other work?** Using your RFC, age, education, and work experience under the Medical-Vocational Guidelines (the “grids”). Age matters: at 50 and above, the grids favor claimants limited to sedentary work who cannot transfer their skills. Skilled office work can produce transferable skills, so the vocational evidence about what your skills were, and whether they transfer to other sedentary work, is often contested at the hearing.

## Back pay and the fee

SSDI benefits begin five full months after the established onset date; SSI is not paid for months before the application. SSDI back pay reaches to the onset date, up to 12 months before the application, and the onset date your lawyer proves sets the amount. The fee is set by federal law: 25 percent of back benefits, capped at the current limit, and nothing if the claim is not approved (42 USC 406(a)). Social Security pays it directly from back pay; ongoing monthly benefits are not subject to it. Any costs for obtaining records are explained in the written fee agreement at the start of the case.

## How a Southfield disability claim interacts with other claims

- **An employer’s long-term disability plan.** Many Southfield office employers provide group LTD insurance. Many plans require you to apply for SSDI, offset the SSDI award dollar for dollar, and may claim repayment from your SSDI back pay. An LTD denial has its own 180-day ERISA appeal deadline (29 CFR 2560.503-1), and the LTD file and the Social Security file should tell the same story.
- **Workers’ compensation** offsets SSDI (42 USC 424a); see our [workers’ compensation page](/personal-injury/workers-compensation).
- **A personal injury settlement** does not reduce SSDI but can affect SSI; see our [Southfield personal injury lawyer page](/personal-injury/southfield).
- **VA disability** is evidence Social Security must consider but does not bind it.

## What to do if you are applying or were denied

1. **Apply as soon as you stop working** or drop below the SGA level; SSDI back pay reaches back no more than 12 months before the application.
2. **If denied, appeal within 60 days.** Do not file a new application.
3. **Keep treating** and tell every provider how your condition limits you day to day; gaps in treatment are a common reason claims are denied.
4. **Keep your employer’s records**: the last job description, accommodation requests, leave records, and any LTD file.
5. **Attend the consultative examination** and tell us what happened in it.
6. **Call a lawyer before the hearing is scheduled**, not after the notice arrives. See our [how to win your claim page](/disability/winning-your-disability-claim).

## Frequently asked questions

**I had a desk job. Can I still get disability?** Yes, if the medical evidence shows you cannot sustain even sedentary work, for example because of the need for unscheduled breaks, frequent absences, limits on sitting or on using your hands, or problems with concentration. Age 50 and above changes the analysis in your favor if your skills do not transfer.

**My employer’s long-term disability insurer told me to apply for Social Security. Why?** Because many LTD plans reduce your LTD benefit by the SSDI award and may ask you to repay overlapping months from your back pay. Read the plan before you sign a repayment agreement, and make sure both claims describe your limits the same way.

**How long do I have to appeal?** Sixty days from the date on the denial notice, plus five days for mailing, at every stage.

**Where will my hearing be?** Usually through the Livonia hearing office, which is assigned the Redford field office that serves Southfield ZIP codes; Oak Park or Detroit if a different field office handles your claim. In person, by video, or by phone; you can ask to appear in person.

**What changes in 2027?** From January 2027, the SGA limit rises to $1,770 a month, the trial work month to $1,270, and the SSI federal benefit to $1,029 for an individual, with a 3.6 percent increase in benefits.

**What does a disability lawyer cost?** Twenty-five percent of your back benefits, capped by federal law, paid by Social Security from the back pay. Nothing if you do not win.
